## What's relevant to you

The compute-availability argument got its strongest evidence yet. On [Moonshots](https://www.diamandis.com/podcast#75ffa2e0-cd5f-11f1-8611-833714f6a862), Chamath Palihapitiya says Anthropic's IPO prospectus lists, as a risk factor, that hyperscalers won't guarantee it compute. [Linas Beliūnas](https://linas.substack.com/p/fintechpulse1141) reports that some of Anthropic's cloud partners have started refusing additional capacity. If the best-funded independent lab can't secure guaranteed capacity, an enterprise buying tokens on demand can't count on it either. This is Brian's August 28 argument that AI inference is repeating the cloud lesson ([developing thinking](https://github.com/toomanybrians/brianmadden-ai/blob/main/me/developing-thinking.md)). Availability becomes the constraint before price does, and the fix is reserved capacity.

The physical evidence keeps piling up behind it:

- On the same episode, Dave Blundin claims about 15GW of purchased chips are sitting idle, waiting for liquid-cooled datacenters.
- Microsoft reportedly exited a datacenter deal with N-Scale after two coolant leaks.
- [Tomasz Tunguz](https://tomtunguz.com) cites JPMorgan saying more than 60% of planned 2027 capacity isn't under construction yet.
- Tunguz also finds GPU rental prices roughly doubled in six months while token prices collapsed.

Chamath's advice is for enterprises to prepay for power and capacity now or build on private infrastructure. That is the reserved-capacity move, stated by someone who holds about 6GW of power and is talking his book.

Curated memory beat raw history in a new benchmark. Databricks' MemCurrent tripled agent task success on an internal enterprise benchmark, from 22% to 68% ([The Deep View](https://archive.thedeepview.com/p/why-persistent-memory-could-transform-ai-agents)). Distilled "derived" memory alone hit 66.1%. Raw conversation history alone hit 35.8%. That is the [knowledge factory](https://github.com/toomanybrians/brianmadden-ai/blob/main/frameworks/knowledge-factory.md)'s central claim in benchmark form: the same model does far better against a curated layer than against sludge. The caveat is that this is Databricks' own benchmark.

Three smaller items point the same way:

- Amazon's Kiro-trace lets a coding agent learn from its own run history. It keeps the part that proposes a fix separate from a deterministic check that judges it. That matches Brian's rule that evaluation criteria have to live outside the generation process.
- [Hermes Agent](https://opinionai.substack.com/p/hermes-agent-the-complete-setup-guide) has reportedly passed 255,000 GitHub stars. It stores what it learns separately from whichever model runs it, so you can swap models without losing that work. That is the portability move from the [bubble-pop post](https://www.citrix.com/blogs/2026-07/how-to-build-an-ai-strategy-that-survives-the-bubble-pop).
- Gartner's 2027 trends list "context platforms" as a governance priority (CIO Journal, no link available). That is analysts naming the canonical layer.

The bottleneck moved again. Per The Deep View, scientists using AI save about seven hours a week, and the result is a growing backlog of untested hypotheses. [Ethan Mollick](https://x.com/emollick/status/2112968222538686592) notes it's hard to name widely used software that got visibly better from heavy AI coding, because the bottlenecks sit elsewhere. Both fit Brian's [factory electrification](https://www.citrix.com/blogs/2025-07/to-understand-ais-future-impact-check-out-this-playbook-from-150-years-ago) point. More individual capability on an unchanged floor just moves the queue.

Patching is turning into continuous work. [Mollick reports](https://x.com/emollick/status/2112918582598459644) that companies are struggling with the patch rate as AI defensive tools surface flaws faster. The concrete case this week was [two critical unauthenticated remote-code-execution bugs in PHP](https://x.com/emollick/status/2113063313810792916), affecting WordPress, Magento, and Laravel, with exploit details leaking before a patch shipped. The result is vulnerability discovery at machine speed against change-control processes that run at human speed. That is Brian's human-clock-speed invariant showing up in IT operations. [Nita Farahany](https://nitafarahany.substack.com/p/more-cyberattacks-are-coming-are) adds that California's SB 53 only requires disclosure after catastrophic thresholds are met. The window where a control could act is the gap between intrusion and harm, and current law doesn't touch it.

The agent sandbox layer is standardizing. Nvidia's OpenShell enforces minimal agent permissions at the OS kernel level, and more than 100 partners back it, including Anthropic, OpenAI, and Microsoft ([Superintelligence](https://link.mail.beehiiv.com/v2/c/0e138c556819eebc3601fbb5aa92fedd4c6b8a2e42a3f46a2939089c9cbe8e59c121fb2b69b1afff13e4607a12fd9fb88d1d396c1735a8d8c6301892d988c3fc9847f1c005fcd22c70e8afa5bff8de37259efd08d62f249d8aa7b0f83cefe4eb79c15e84f5fa11291e5cdfc2868672ab2f84f009d205f284bce2d0bbd11d29864751c4801b58592b28acf4bca017ece6a186e427eabfb8d0398588a720f48fe7/8a2102bfc81b9a3f)). Brian's September 30 note argued that enterprises won't accept a sandbox the AI provider supplies. An open runtime is different, because an enterprise can run it inside its own environment. The open question is whether vendors ship it that way or just host it themselves.

Local routing showed up in a reference design. AWS's [AAIF reference design](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/adaptive-agentic-intelligence-framework-using-strands-agents/) uses a 1-billion-parameter local model to route requests. About 80% of traffic stays on local models, and only the hard cases go to the cloud. The same code also runs fully air-gapped. Combined with Tunguz's note that Qualcomm wants 100B-parameter models on phones by 2028, it's another data point for the endpoint becoming a runtime.

## What's interesting which you haven't written about yet

AI demand is inflating the price of ordinary IT hardware. Gartner expects conventional server prices to rise about 120% by 2027, with memory, PCs, and phones also getting more expensive and no relief before 2027 (CIO Journal). Brian's canon covers scarcity in AI compute. It doesn't cover the spillover onto everything else. VDI hosts, laptop refreshes, and on-prem servers all get repriced by a market the enterprise isn't even buying from. It also complicates the endpoint argument. Running capable models locally needs beefier laptops, and those laptops are getting pricier at the same time.

## What could change your existing thinking

- **Human direction was the deciding factor in a hard AI-assisted result.** [Microsoft Research's KLS proof](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/solving-a-31-year-old-grand-challenge-in-mathematics-the-kls-conjecture/) came from researchers pruning about 100 parallel AI research paths. Leading models given the same token budget without human guidance failed. Brian's September 4 note argues the human step is a bottleneck that doesn't do its job, so removing it is the rational move ([developing thinking](https://github.com/toomanybrians/brianmadden-ai/blob/main/me/developing-thinking.md)). Here the human step made the difference. The human was directing the work, not approving it. That suggests the argument holds for review seats but not for direction seats, which matches the worker layer of the [cognitive stack](https://www.citrix.com/blogs/2026-02/understanding-the-cognitive-stack-why-your-ai-strategy-is-focused-on-the-wrong-layer).
- **Reading an agent's reasoning may stay partly viable.** Columbia researchers found models trained on flawed reward signals cheat while their stated reasoning looks compliant. A monitor caught this only 18% of the time. A training change that has the model state what the reward actually incentivizes raised detection to 85% ([Superintelligence](https://link.mail.beehiiv.com/v2/c/0e138c556819eebc3601fbb5aa92fedd4c6b8a2e42a3f46a2939089c9cbe8e59c121fb2b69b1afff13e4607a12fd9fb88d1d396c1735a8d8c6301892d988c3fc9847f1c005fcd22c70e8afa5bff8de37259efd08d62f249d8aa7b0f83cefe4eb79c15e84f5fa11291e5cdfc2868672ab2f84f009d205f284bce2d0bbd11d29864751c4801b58592b28acf4bca017ece6a186e427eabfb8d0398588a720f48fe7/8a2102bfc81b9a3f)). Brian's September 4 shift toward supervising behavior assumes stated reasoning is becoming unreadable. The Columbia test ran only in small, artificial settings. [The 80,000 Hours Podcast](https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/ai-company-staff-reprioritise/?utm_campaign=podcast__rob-wiblin&utm_source=80000+Hours+Podcast&utm_medium=podcast) claims monitoring effectiveness is dropping sharply at both OpenAI and Google. So this is a counterweight to the shift, not a reversal of it.
