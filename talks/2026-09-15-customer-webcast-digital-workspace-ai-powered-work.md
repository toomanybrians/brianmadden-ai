# From digital workspace to AI-powered work

## Talk details

**Event:** Customer webcast (customer name withheld at Brian's request — see note below)
**Date:** September 15, 2026
**Format:** Solo webcast keynote with slides, ~35 minutes
**Speaker:** Brian Madden, VP & Futurist, Citrix
**Substack post (with the slide deck attached):** https://www.brianmadden.ai/p/speech-transcript-from-digital-workspace

**Abstract:** Most companies right now are stuck trying to figure out an AI strategy — which model to bet on, what to let AI see — and Brian argues that's the wrong first question, because it's trying to design a solution before you've done the one thing that actually has to come first: get visibility into the AI that's already operating in your company, unofficially and officially, from every direction. Walks through three waves — the AI that's already there (a configuration exercise using tools you already own, not a migration), the AI knowledge layer that visibility unlocks (the knowledge factory, built backward from outputs rather than forward from raw inputs), and AI becoming portable to the device and to the customer's own region via open-weight models. Closes with where Citrix fits (delivering, governing, and securing whatever "existing work" runs on this decade, same as the last 37 years) and a pitch for his own second brain as the individual-scale version of the same knowledge-factory idea.

## Key arguments

- **The real question isn't "what's our AI strategy," it's "how do we get useful AI without losing control"** of the systems, data, and workflows the business already runs on. Strategy-first thinking tries to design the solution before establishing the one prerequisite: visibility into what AI is already doing in the company.
- **AI did not wait for your strategy.** It's already in the building, from every direction at once — official pilots and department-level projects, personal AI accounts used for work (official or not), an AI copilot embedded in nearly every SaaS tool now, and ad hoc experiments running in every team. The only real choice left is whether you can see it. You cannot transform what you can't see.
- **Wave 1 (the AI that's already there) is a configuration exercise, not a migration.** It reuses the exact playbook already run for decades — access, identity, governance, control — just pointed at AI instead of only at human workers. Nothing about existing systems or processes has to change to get this visibility.
- **Agent identity is the one genuinely new problem in that playbook.** Most AI today operates with the same rights as the human worker running it, which is unsafe by default — an AI agent should not inherit a worker's own access.
- **"Visibility is the new security."** Every governance control point is also a visibility point — instrument the estate for AI governance, and the side effect is visibility into how the work actually happens underneath it.
- **Wave 2 (the AI knowledge layer) fails without Wave 1 first.** Pointing AI straight at the raw pile of inputs — docs, PDFs, Slack, source code — and asking it to generate finished outputs produces hallucination-filled garbage regardless of model quality, because the actual judgment (the invisible 80%) was never in those inputs to begin with. The fix is a managed, canonical middle layer built backward from specific outputs, not forward from the slurry — what Citrix runs internally as its "knowledge factory."
- **Forward-deployed engineers (FDEs)** are effectively a rebrand of what business-transformation consultants did in the 1990s, and every major AI lab and hyperscaler spent this summer racing to build FDE armies. The actual work doesn't require hiring outsiders — it requires people who already understand the business, which most companies already employ.
- **Wave 3 (AI everywhere) makes capability portable in two directions at once**: down to the device (Brian runs a ~27B local model on his own laptop, roughly Sonnet/Opus-class, slow but real) and into the customer's own region via open-weight models, removing dependence on a single foreign frontier-model host.
- **Citrix's role hasn't changed in 37 years** — deliver, govern, and secure whatever "existing work" happens to run on, decade to decade. AI is just the newest thing riding rails that DOS apps, client-server, web apps, and cloud rode before it.
