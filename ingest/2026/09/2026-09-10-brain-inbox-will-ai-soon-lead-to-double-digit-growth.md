# Will AI soon lead to double-digit growth?

## Insights

- Reframes AI growth debate around "levels vs. rates": being 2x richer in 15 years already implies ~4.7% annual growth, so 4-5% growth is not modest — double-digit forecasts imply 100x wealth within a generation, not incremental improvement.
- A standard economic growth model (task-based Solow model) can mathematically produce AI-driven explosive growth (10-20%+ annual) once automation removes labor as a bottleneck — but this requires specific assumptions to hold, not just capability advances.
- Argues capability explosion ≠ automation of most economic tasks: only ~1/3 of the economy is remote/cognitive work; the rest (mining, construction, healthcare, care work) requires physical robots that remain far from cost-competitive with human labor.
- Central economic mechanism working against explosive growth: as automated goods get cheaper, spending shifts toward whatever remains scarce and human (Baumol's cost disease) — "messy jobs" (bundled, locally-accountable work), "relational goods" (where human presence is the product, e.g. therapists, teachers), and physical inputs (energy, land, materials) all resist full automation and cap growth.
- Additional constraints identified: demand-side limits (capital owners spend less of their income than workers, so automation-driven income shifts could suppress demand), AI-driven cyber incidents that destroy value and slow deployment, and the fact that "automating R&D" requires assumptions (fully replacing human researchers) not currently supported by data — research productivity has been falling for decades.
- Notes GDP itself may understate AI's welfare impact (cheap/free AI tools shrink their own measured GDP share), but argues this doesn't change the growth-rate forecast — concludes a realistic 10-15 year baseline is 4-5% growth, not double digits, while acknowledging this would still represent a massive increase in living standards.

## Quote

> Capabilities, deployment and investment feed on each other, and safety failures make this loop work in reverse.
