# Millennium Pastimes

## Insights

- Frontier AI models reportedly solved or made "substantial progress" on multiple Millennium Prize math problems within days of each other, compressing what was expected to take 1,000 years into roughly a week.
- Terence Tao's core argument (as characterized here): struggling with a problem is what builds mathematical understanding, so AI-generated proofs deliver the result while eliminating the value-generating process — humanity gains solutions but loses competence, mirroring how AI use in schools boosts performance while weakening underlying skill.
- The piece describes "proof abundance" as a distinct risk: if AI generates huge volumes of true statements/proofs, truth becomes commodified and uninterpretable without human sense-making — someone still has to act as the "bridge" between raw output and human decisions, so that role can't be automated away without leaving humans out of the loop entirely.
- Draws a historical parallel to mathematician William Thurston, who was so prolific in his field that he discouraged others from entering it ("killed foliations" through success) — the argument is that AI now does this across every field simultaneously, and unlike a person, it never leaves or retires.
- Frames the loss for mathematicians as more than losing a specific unsolved problem — it threatens "ground projects" (referencing Bernard Williams), i.e., life-organizing pursuits that give someone reason to keep working in a field for decades.
- Twenty-five Fields medalists (including Tao) published a declaration warning that rapid AI-driven mass production of true/false mathematical statements could "destroy fertile ground instead of breathing life into new ideas" — used with a fallow-field/agriculture analogy about needing unharvested space for a discipline to regenerate.
- Suggests a possible future split between "mathematics-as-research" (dominated by machines) and "mathematics-as-human-practice" (an amateur/sport-like pursuit for humans), analogous to how humans still run track despite cars, or chess/speedrunning separating human and AI competition.

## Quote

> AI does not merely threaten to solve the problems they hoped to solve... it threatens to remove the problems around which they might have built a life.
