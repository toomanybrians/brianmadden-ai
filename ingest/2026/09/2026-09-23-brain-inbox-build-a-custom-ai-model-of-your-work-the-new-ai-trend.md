# Build a Custom AI Model of Your Work: The New AI Trend

## Insights

- Some startups and researchers are moving away from full dependence on frontier AI labs (OpenAI, Anthropic) by post-training open-weight models on their own proprietary work examples, rather than building foundation models from scratch.
- The strategic risk framed here: outsourcing "intelligence" to a single external API creates exposure to price changes, model deprecation, access restrictions, or geopolitical shifts between companies/countries — even if the business itself remains under your control.
- Analogy drawn to early internet infrastructure: hosting/servers seemed like boring commodity infrastructure until businesses moved online and whoever controlled that infrastructure became strategically valuable — the piece suggests AI model ownership could follow the same trajectory.
- Harvey (legal AI) is cited as an advanced real-world example: its "Tenet" model starts from Moonshot AI's open-weight Kimi K3 and is post-trained specifically for legal work, with Harvey stating a goal of eventually letting law firms "own their intelligence."
- The practical recommendation for smaller organizations: don't try to build a general-purpose model — instead take an open-weight model and narrowly train it on one recurring task (e.g., research→newsletter draft, meeting notes→weekly report, customer email→approved response, bug description→company-style code).
- Value comes not from a model "knowing everything" but from becoming unusually proficient at one specific, repeated workflow task particular to the organization.

## Quote

> Your business may still belong to you. But its intelligence does not.
