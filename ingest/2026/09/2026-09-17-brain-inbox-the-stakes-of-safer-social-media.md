# The Stakes of Safer Social Media

## Insights

- Panelists framed AI's rapid development as compounding, not separate from, the harms already caused by social media's attention-optimization design on adolescent mental health.
- A founder cited concern that young people who build their sense of identity around skills or career paths may have those things "replaced by AI" shortly after entering the workforce, undermining identity formation.
- One panelist argued algorithmic engagement models are especially damaging to young users because youth lack a stable sense of self and are more susceptible to targeted, "profound"-seeming content.
- Proposed interventions included mandatory tech education in schools (analogized to sex ed) and giving young people direct representation in tech policy-making processes.
- The piece frames a generational split: older millennials/Gen X "grew up alongside" social media's development, while Gen Z is entering vulnerable developmental years into an already-mature social media landscape plus a newly normalized AI presence.
- News of an Anthropic whistleblower report is referenced as an example of the "doom loop" of unchecked AI development causing real emotional distress even among informed adults.
