# 🧗 Opus 5.5 Climbs to the Top, GPT-6 Gets Much Cheaper

## Insights

- Claude Opus 5.5 now tops Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index (58 vs. 53 for Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra) while running about 40% cheaper than its predecessor Opus 5, reversing the usual pattern of higher capability costing more.
- Epoch AI measures the cost of a fixed level of AI performance falling ~13x per year (47%/quarter) over three years — faster than electricity, batteries, or DNA sequencing ever declined — with price drops steepest (~75x/year) right after a capability debuts and slowing over time.
- OpenAI cut GPT-6 Sol and Luna API prices in half same-day as Anthropic's release; Sol at xhigh setting reportedly beats Claude Opus 5 at max effort for 9% of Opus 5's per-task cost on OpenAI's internal business-workflow benchmark.
- Falling inference costs are already enabling new applications: Alibaba's Qwen launched phone-based agents (planner, mobile-use agent with 90% task success, image generator), and Anthropic used Claude to speed up 30+ open-source biology/protein-modeling programs ~4x, cutting protein-design compute cost from ~$10,000 to ~$150 per target.
- Both labs caveat their own benchmark claims: Anthropic says "benchmark margins have become a less reliable guide to real-world differences" at this performance level, and safety guardrails route some cybersecurity/biology tasks to older, less-restricted models, complicating apples-to-apples comparisons.
- a16z is opening a free, non-degree one-year "Horowitz Andreessen Academy" ($42M, 50 students) placing recent high-schoolers inside AI companies (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta, Nvidia, Palantir) with compute credits instead of traditional coursework — positioned as an alternative to college credentialing.
