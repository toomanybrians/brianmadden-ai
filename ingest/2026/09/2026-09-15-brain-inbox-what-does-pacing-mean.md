# What Does Pacing Mean?

## Insights

- Dario Amodei's "pace the frontier" proposal calls for external auditors, industry-wide coordination to slow innovation, and international coordination among allied nations — but doesn't specify what pace actually means in practice.
- Five distinct camps back "pacing" for different reasons with no shared definition of speed: interpretability researchers (worried alignment science lags capability), labor advocates (protecting workers, e.g. Sanders' proposed data-center construction moratorium), economists (viewing AI-driven GDP growth as the route to managing national debt), geopolitical strategists (racing China), and a "regulatory capture" camp skeptical that safety talk is cover for entrenching incumbents.
- Anthropic's own alignment lead reportedly estimates greater than 10% odds AI causes catastrophic harm within a decade, with no concrete plan in place; internal interpretability work still only explains "a tiny fraction" of what happens inside frontier models.
- Empirical pacing signals cited: the gap between when a frontier model launches and when a rival matches it has stretched from 41 days (Jan 2025) to several months; the lag between open and closed models widened from 3 to 4 months.
- A prior attempt to govern pace via a hard compute threshold (10^26 FLOPS, 2023 executive order) failed as a mechanism — it was revoked before any model crossed it, and with training compute growing ~5x/year, a fixed threshold becomes a floor rather than a ceiling almost immediately.
- The piece frames the unresolved core question as not just "how fast should AI develop" but "who gets to decide the pace" — no camp has offered a concrete, dated speed target except the economic camp's GDP-growth framing.

## Quote

> The question is more than how fast. It is who decides.
