# You can’t transform the AI you can’t see

## Insights

- Companies typically frame AI adoption as a top-down strategy problem — forming committees, evaluating platforms, running pilots — while actual AI usage has already spread through the organization from every direction, ahead of any formal decision.
- Shadow AI usage takes many concrete forms: personal accounts on unmanaged devices, AI features embedded in existing SaaS tools, department-level pilots using independent token sources, and IT-issued licenses (e.g., Copilot) whose actual usage and value are unclear even to IT itself.
- Even where security guardrails are in place, workers route around them by photographing screens and running the content through personal AI subscriptions.
- The piece argues the right starting questions aren't about platform selection ("which AI should we standardize on?") but about visibility and control: what AI is currently running, what data it can access, whose identity/credentials it operates under, and who has oversight of it.
- The core problem framed is one of visibility/governance lagging far behind actual deployment, not a lack of strategic decision-making per se.
