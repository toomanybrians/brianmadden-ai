# How to Seize the Means of Tech Production

## Insights

- Unionization efforts are quietly building across multiple major San Francisco tech companies, with organizers deliberately anonymized due to ongoing illegal retaliatory firing risks.
- A new book, *Against Tech Oligarchy* by Clarissa Redwine and JS Tan, chronicles firsthand tech unionization efforts (Kickstarter, Microsoft, Twitter) and argues collective action remains viable despite setbacks.
- Kickstarter's 2020 unionization (the first white-collar tech workforce to unionize in US history) followed illegal retaliatory firings of organizers, which backfired and galvanized support rather than suppressing it.
- The authors argue tech's founding ethos ("move fast and break things," "don't be evil") was designed to obscure a widening gap between workers' idealism and the industry's actual function serving oligarchic interests (e.g., cloud contracts with ICE, Pentagon, Chevron).
- Twitter's employee-driven push to deplatform Trump after Jan 6 built union momentum, but Musk's acquisition and restructuring dissolved it — illustrating how ownership change can erase labor organizing gains.
- The authors argue tech strikes require different tactics than traditional labor action, since AI-era tech work is often designed to function without the humans who built it — making "walking off the job" an ineffective strike model.

## Quote

> "We need to test, and we need to see how the boss responds so we can learn how our strikes cannot be undermined in advance." — JS Tan
