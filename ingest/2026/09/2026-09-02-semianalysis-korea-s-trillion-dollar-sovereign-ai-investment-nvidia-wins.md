# Korea’s Trillion-Dollar Sovereign AI Investment: Nvidia Wins, Hynix Loses

## Insights

- Frontier AI access is increasingly gated by US labs and government policy — models have been banned, delayed, or safety-restricted (cyber/bio safeguards), and the piece speculates OpenAI/Anthropic may eventually stop offering API access to their most capable models entirely.
- Open source is not a reliable escape hatch: licenses are getting more restrictive (e.g. Moonshot requiring separate agreements for high-revenue commercial use of Kimi K3), and labs could stop open-sourcing future models, leaving adopters stuck on stale capability for high-value, security-sensitive use cases.
- Korea's government ran a Squid-Game-style elimination tournament (5 consortiums → contestants judged every 6 months on benchmarks/expert review/user testing) to build a sovereign frontier model, subsidizing compute, data, and researcher recruitment rather than picking a national champion upfront.
- A small, ~30-person startup (Motif Technologies) trained a model for ~$15M total compute cost that outperformed larger chaebol-backed teams and beat the two leading American open-source models on Artificial Analysis' Intelligence Index — then was controversially eliminated from the tournament despite topping benchmarks, reportedly due to opaque "expert review" concerns about tech transfer risk.
- The piece argues training a near-SOTA open-source model from scratch is far cheaper than commonly assumed, putting it within reach of nation-states' budgets.
- Korea is committing ~$919B to build 8.4GW of AI datacenter capacity by 2029 (18.4GW by 2035), framed by the president as a legacy project comparable to Korea's 1990s broadband buildout; much of this capacity may ultimately be sold to Anthropic/OpenAI rather than used for sovereign models.
- Nvidia is positioned as the biggest structural backer of open source and sovereign AI because it needs a diverse ecosystem of customers/complements beyond just OpenAI/Anthropic and hyperscalers (most of whom are building competing chips), using sovereign AI deals and financial engineering (e.g. compute as an "investable asset class") to broaden demand.
