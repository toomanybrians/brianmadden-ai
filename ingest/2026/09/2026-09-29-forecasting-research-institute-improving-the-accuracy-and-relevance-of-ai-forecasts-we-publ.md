# Improving the Accuracy and Relevance of AI Forecasts We Publish

## Insights

- Median forecasts from experts, superforecasters, and the public have systematically underestimated near-term AI capability progress (e.g., benchmarks) and AI company revenue growth, though results are mixed on other adoption indicators.
- A recent example: LEAP experts and superforecasters gave only 10% and 5.4% median odds that a Millennium Prize Problem would be solved with AI assistance by end of 2027 — shortly before OpenAI reportedly settled the Navier–Stokes problem.
- FRI plans to break out a "fast AI progress" subsample of forecasters who put more weight on near-term transformative AI, since this group has so far been more accurate on capability questions; multiple methods for defining this subsample (relative ranking, professional expertise, absolute threshold criteria) are still being tested.
- A separate "AI risk expert" subsample (people working on catastrophic AI risk mitigation) is being added, but their AI-progress forecasts weren't dramatically faster than other experts' — this group is not a good proxy for the "fast progress" worldview.
- FRI intends to add regularly updated LLM forecasts (citing ForecastBench results showing top LLMs at parity with superforecasters on some question types) as a supplement to human forecasts, partly to solve the problem of survey answers going stale (e.g., Anthropic's reported revenue jumped from $47B to $65B within days of a survey closing).
- Once enough questions resolve (~16 more expected by end of 2026), FRI plans to identify and highlight the most historically accurate individual LEAP forecasters, and to weight rationale quality and allow forecasters to update after seeing peers' reasoning.

## Quote

> Our findings suggest that median forecasts from these groups are not reliable estimates for predicting the trajectory of AI on many questions.
