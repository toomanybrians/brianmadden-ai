# The Overhang

## Insights

- Mollick argues current frontier models (GPT-6 Astra, Fable 5.1) are already capable of "weeks worth of human work" when properly guided, yet most users barely tap this capability — a "capability overhang" between what models can do and what people actually use them for.
- Demonstrated examples: an AI converting the text-only game Zork into a full 3D action-adventure (inventing visuals/monsters not specified in original text), and reconstructing Umberto Eco's 27,000-slot personal library in 3D from video/photos/catalogs alone, marking books as certain/guess/unknown.
- AI is shown exercising judgment and creativity once considered uniquely human — e.g., autonomously using Blender, writing scripts with jokes, generating voice/music/sound, and building a video trailer with minimal human direction beyond feedback.
- Proposes four human "advantages" that remain valuable for working with AI: deep knowledge (expert intuition to spot errors/mismanage AI output), wide knowledge (breadth across fields/traditions to know what to ask for), taste (selecting/discarding among abundant AI output now that production is cheap), and agency (willingness to experimentally probe unmapped capabilities).
- Cites Anthropic research suggesting domain expertise correlates with getting both better and more output from AI.
- Frames the change AI brings as inevitable but not fixed in form — argues society needs models of AI-human collaboration that "enhance, rather than only replace" human labor, and that debates over pausing/regulating future frontier models don't erase the transformative capability already sitting unused in current systems.

## Quote

> The capability overhang, the gap between what these models can do and what almost anyone is doing with them, is an opportunity.
