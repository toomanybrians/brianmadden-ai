# AI’s Greatest Risk To Humanity? It’s Not Robots Gone Rogue.

## Insights

- Argues that the recent wave of AI "existential risk" claims (whistleblower warnings, Anthropic's own 10%-doom estimate, Amodei's manifesto) may function as cover for industry consolidation, letting incumbents like Anthropic and OpenAI use safety rhetoric to sideline cheaper open-source competitors ahead of their IPOs.
- Notes that Anthropic has stated it is profitable except for the cost of training models, framing the IPO push and safety narrative as tied to urgent capital needs.
- Draws a historical line from IQ testing and the statistical/pattern-recognition roots of machine learning to eugenics and colonial ideology, suggesting today's "superintelligence" framing echoes past justifications for domination.
- Cites Cory Doctorow's take on the Hugging Face incident: it wasn't an emergent AI threat but "autonomous malicious software" poorly monitored — a mundane engineering failure, not a supernatural one.
- Points to a documented real-world harm (Anthropic's partnership with Palantir, per AI Institute researcher Heidy Khlaaf) as evidence that AI-enabled harm is already happening via military use, not just a future hypothetical.
- Contends that sci-fi "AI will destroy humanity" narratives distract from present material problems (healthcare costs, wildfires, data-center pollution) and reinforce the idea that ordinary people lack the intelligence or agency to challenge those "in control."
- Suggests embodied, relational intelligence (grief, intimacy, lived experience) is categorically different from what LLMs produce, since AI models descriptions of experience without having lived it.

## Quote

> The true danger of AI, perhaps, is the deceptive messaging that leads us to believe we do not have agency or freedom in its system.
