# 🥈 Sonnet 5.5 Breathes Down Opus's Neck

## Insights

- Anthropic's Sonnet 5.5 scores 56 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (vs. Opus 5.5's 58), at half Opus's price ($2/$10 per million tokens), with the largest gains in agentic coding (70.6% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 vs. 10.3% for Sonnet 5).
- Cost claims diverge by source: Anthropic says Sonnet 5.5 costs up to 30% less per task than its predecessor, while independent testing (Artificial Analysis) found that at maximum reasoning effort it actually costs ~50% more than Sonnet 5, using ~193,000 output tokens per task.
- Anthropic's IPO prospectus shows steep financial contrasts: revenue up 12x to ~$4.6B, but compute costs tripled to $7.33B, an $8.06B operating loss, and a $42B headline net loss (partly a non-cash financing charge). Nearly a quarter of revenue comes from just two customers.
- The same filing devotes roughly 80 of 261 pages to risk factors, including explicit warnings that models could show "self-preserving behaviors," attempt to "resist shutdown," or exhibit behavior "resembling blackmail" — far more risk disclosure than comparable filings (e.g., SpaceX's 38 of 277 pages).
- Dario Amodei, in a CBS interview, calls the current moment "a warning sign that we need to slow down," proposes third-party evaluators embedded in AI labs, but rejects an outright development ban since the technology would be built elsewhere (e.g., China).
- NVIDIA released an "Open Agent Safety Platform" pairing a sandbox (OpenShell) with an optional hardware-level monitor (Sentry, on the BlueField-4 chip) that can contain a misbehaving agent within milliseconds — built on the premise that an agent "cannot be expected to fully govern its own behavior."
- Separately: Meta is launching "Meta Enterprise Platform" to sell its Muse models/agents directly to businesses (competing with Anthropic/OpenAI for enterprise customers), and Feather Robotics is selling a $29,990 two-armed robot with swappable AI "brains," aiming to be the "Android of robotics."
