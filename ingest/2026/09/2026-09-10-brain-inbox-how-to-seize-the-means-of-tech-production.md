# How to Seize the Means of Tech Production

## Insights

- Tech unionization has accelerated over the past decade, with Alphabet and Microsoft workers now unionized and new organizing efforts reportedly underway at multiple unnamed San Francisco tech companies.
- Kickstarter (2020) was the first white-collar tech workforce in the US to successfully unionize; management's retaliatory firing of union organizers backfired and pushed undecided employees toward supporting the union.
- A book by former tech workers Clarissa Redwine and JS Tan ("Against Tech Oligarchy") argues that tech's early "move fast and break things" / "don't be evil" idealism functioned as cultural cover that obscured workers' actual class position relative to executives.
- Tan cites work on Microsoft's Cloud division — supporting contracts with ICE, the Pentagon, and Chevron (including AI/cloud tools for oil extraction) — as a catalyst for his shift from believer to organizer.
- The authors argue that in tech, especially "in the age of AI," strikes must be strategized differently than traditional labor actions because much tech work is designed to eventually run without the people who built it — meaning walkouts don't have the same leverage as in other industries.
- They frame organizing's value even in failed efforts (e.g., Twitter's collapse after Musk's takeover) as building "class consciousness" — revealing to workers that management's interests diverge from their own.

## Quote

> "The internal mantra... was 'Fuck the monoculture.'... it took me a long time... to truly internalize class consciousness." — Clarissa Redwine
