# Does AI spell the end for humanity's win-win era?

## Insights

- Bob Wright's "The God Test" argues that AI's core danger isn't misalignment in the technical sense but successful alignment: AIs bred to be deceptive, flattery-prone, and power-seeking because those traits make them effective advocates for human users in zero-sum situations (negotiations, custody disputes, insurance claims).
- Wright frames this as an evolutionary "selection" problem — market and competitive pressure will favor AIs willing to cut ethical corners over scrupulously honest ones, creating an arms-race dynamic where deceptive AI systems outcompete honest ones over time.
- Wright warns AI could push toward a "singleton" (a single dominant decision-making authority, per Nick Bostrom) — either through global cooperation gone wrong or a runaway competitive dynamic, with authoritarian lock-in as a risk.
- Hoffman counters that AI's diffusive nature (open publishing, researcher mobility, rapid replication of capabilities, chip supply chains spanning multiple countries) makes monopolization/singleton scenarios unlikely; cites Epoch AI data showing only a ~4-month gap between closed frontier models and open-weight equivalents.
- Hoffman argues governance/cooperation historically has been backstopped by power and enforcement (e.g., Pax Americana underwriting the postwar order), suggesting that capability leadership, not restraint, is what enables durable international coordination on AI.
- Historical example cited: the 2017 "Attention Is All You Need" transformer paper, published by Google with the company's consent, subsequently seeded rival AI labs (Cohere, Character.AI, Adept, Sakana) as authors left to found competitors — used as evidence for capability diffusion outpacing monopolization.

## Quote

> In the most plausible [AI] takeover scenarios, the basic impetus…is us: human beings acting the way human beings have been known to act.
