# The current balance of power in open models

## Insights

- Chinese open-weight models (GLM-5.3, Kimi K3, DeepSeek) now clearly lead American open-weight models (Llama, Nemotron, Thinking Machines' Inkling) on capability benchmarks, having overtaken the US around 18 months prior; American open-weight models lag the closed US frontier by 6-9 months, while Chinese open models trail it by only 2-5 months.
- Adoption data shows the shift is structural, not just benchmark noise: Hugging Face downloads favor China ~2:1, OpenRouter usage has flipped from ~70% to over 80% Chinese-model share, and Chinese models dominate ~95%+ of inference on platforms like OpenCode.
- Academic research is quietly consolidating around Chinese models — Qwen alone is now mentioned in ~30% of top ML arXiv papers, with all Chinese open models combined appearing in ~40% of papers versus ~30% for US models, reversing Llama's earlier dominance.
- Real companies (Cursor, Harvey, DoorDash, Airbnb, Perplexity) are building products on Chinese open-weight models for cost, flexibility, and customization reasons, with a new pattern emerging of US startups signing enterprise agreements directly with Chinese model labs.
- Distillation from closed US models only accounts for an estimated 1-2 months of the Chinese open-model lead — the gap is mainly driven by faster release cadence, narrower task focus, and increasing willingness to pay for high-quality agentic training data.
- Restricting access to strong Chinese open models to manage security risk (e.g., cybersecurity) would primarily harm American businesses that depend on them, since open software can't be effectively gatekept from bad actors anyway.

## Quote

> The leading Chinese labs do not appear to be meaningfully challenged, as they expand their enterprise and research adoption globally.
