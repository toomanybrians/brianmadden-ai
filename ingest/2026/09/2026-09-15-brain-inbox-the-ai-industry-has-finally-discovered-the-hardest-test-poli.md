# The AI Industry Has Finally Discovered the Hardest Test: Politics

## Insights

- Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published an essay ("We Must Pace the Frontier") arguing the AI industry should slow capability gains until safety is better understood, citing loss of full control over the technology (e.g., the Hugging Face hack, a possible AI-derived Navier-Stokes solution) as evidence.
- Amodei's proposal has three stages: independent evaluators embedded inside AI companies (Anthropic has committed; OpenAI followed), coordination among democratic-world AI labs, and eventual international coordination including China.
- The essay landed amid a backdrop that made it well-timed: safety concerns going mainstream after a high-profile resignation/tweet from an Anthropic employee, a string of real-world AI-related security incidents, and a frontier landscape effectively dominated by two US companies (Anthropic, OpenAI), meaning a slowdown wouldn't meaningfully cede ground to China.
- Industry leaders (Altman, Musk, Hassabis) voiced general support, but government leaders rejected the pacing idea: Trump said "whoever wins AI wins" and dismissed the proposal, while Xi Jinping/Chinese state media framed it as a Cold War move to entrench US tech hegemony and exclude China from AI governance.
- The author frames the standoff as industry caution vs. state ambition ("Oppenheimer vs Truman"), arguing that even sincere safety intentions from AI leaders are unlikely to overcome national-level competitive incentives — technology confers power, but not more power than political power itself.
- The author states he takes Amodei's stated concerns at face value as genuine (not merely IPO-timed marketing), while still planning to identify structural flaws in the pacing proposal that Amodei himself reportedly acknowledges.

## Quote

> Unfortunately for Amodei, technology is power but not more so than power itself.
