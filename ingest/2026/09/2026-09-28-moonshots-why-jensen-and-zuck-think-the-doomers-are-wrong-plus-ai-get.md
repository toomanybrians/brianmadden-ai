# Why Jensen and Zuck think the doomers are wrong (plus AI get’s a rebrand)  | #294 MOONSHOTS Live

## Insights

- Jensen Huang and Zuckerberg publicly pushed back on "doomer" rhetoric (Hinton's ~10% extinction-risk estimate, Dario Amodei's warnings); Huang argued such predictions aren't grounded in science and accused slowdown advocates of narrative-building to deflect responsibility.
- Panelists framed AI danger as primarily a misuse/infrastructure problem (bad security protocols, humans deliberately or accidentally misusing AI) rather than a risk of AI "breaking out" and killing everyone on its own — arguing these distinct risks are being conflated.
- The US reportedly rebranded "AGI" to "superintelligence" around the Xi-Trump summit and rejected any global AI-control scheme; panel speculated this is partly a deliberate strategy to dodge regulatory/political backlash (referenced as pushback against "Bernie Sanders"-style panic) by becoming "a moving target."
- Speakers described a split between consumer-facing AI products (e.g., Meta's "Muse" assistant, framed as Meta's critical distribution play to avoid irrelevance) and frontier/recursively self-improving models — arguing raw intelligence and everyday usefulness are now different axes of competition.
- Frontier labs' simultaneous safety rhetoric and rapid capability releases (new models days after "pause" calls, labs opening wet labs for AI-driven biology) was called out by the panel itself as revealed-preference hypocrisy — labs don't act as if they believe their own doom warnings.
- A published "Millennium Problems for Biology" list (origin of life, cryopreservation, limb regrowth, etc.) was discussed as a template for turning hard biology problems into verifiable, AI-tractable search targets, with claims that math is already "solved" and biology is next.
