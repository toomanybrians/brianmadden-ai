# Executive Briefing: Where Your AI Money Actually Goes (+ the Token Saver Guide and the 15 Changes I Use)

## Insights

- The piece reframes AI's enterprise value away from "cost of the AI line item" and toward a hidden prior cost: the expense of understanding a customer's problem, coordinating a response, and delivering it — a cost that shaped which customers got a human, a form, a custom response, or a "no."
- Argues that if AI is truly consequential, customers should get something categorically different — a better result, a lower price, or more attention than the business could previously afford — not just a new feature bolted onto the same invoice/pricing structure.
- Points out that many existing business structures (service tiers, minimum account sizes, standardized/generic offers) were rational answers to the historical cost of giving customers real attention, and that constraint is now loosening.
- Raises a competitive-dynamics question: efficiency gains from AI don't automatically become profit — competitors can redirect the same savings into a better customer offer, so the gain is contested between margin capture and competitive repositioning.
- Cites an anecdote (via Salesforce's Rohan Kumar at Dreamforce) where a customer challenges the disconnect between "AI changes everything" messaging and products that look largely unchanged, used to motivate the deeper cost-structure argument.
- Notes that better AI agents can paradoxically increase bills, and suggests this should prompt companies to reconsider which work needs to exist at all rather than just how to manage agents.

## Quote

> An AI announcement ought to lead somewhere beyond a new feature on the same invoice.
