# 🌍 Trump and China Resist Amodei's Slowdown

## Insights

- Dario Amodei's proposal to slow AI capability advances (so safety measures can keep pace) has backing from Sam Altman and Elon Musk, but is being rejected by both the US and Chinese governments — Trump argues the US must stay ahead ("whoever wins AI wins"), while China's foreign ministry and state press frame the proposal's export/access restrictions as a hostile containment tactic.
- Amodei's concrete first step is independent evaluators embedded inside AI labs with ongoing access and public reporting; Anthropic says it will do this unilaterally even without a broader international agreement, with eventual hope for verifiable China-inclusive arrangements.
- Notably, Amodei's own proposal pairs safety-slowdown rhetoric with US-competitiveness measures (export controls, model-weight protection, anti-distillation rules), which is part of why China reads it as strategic rather than purely safety-driven.
- Yann LeCun dismisses the alarm as a repeat of 2019's GPT-2 withholding controversy, signaling continued split among prominent AI figures on how much danger current systems pose.
- A separate research paper proposes a five-level framework for recursive self-improvement (from fixed improvement recipes up to systems that improve their own improvement process), explicitly framed as the technical concern underlying Amodei's warning — though "sustained, general self-improvement" is described as still an open research problem.
- Separately, an Epoch AI analysis finds reasoning models improving on a capability index roughly twice as fast per year as non-reasoning models (14 vs. 6 points/year), offered as one measurable proxy in the broader slowdown debate, though explicitly not a measure of danger or self-improvement.
- On the business side, OpenAI has ruled out a 2026 IPO citing safety concerns, while Anthropic is preparing a possible autumn public listing, touting gross margins above 80% versus OpenAI's reported 33% and Microsoft Cloud's 66%.
