# Why I still haven’t bought into true RSI

## Insights

- Author distinguishes "true RSI" (recursive self-improvement expanding peak intelligence) from "lossy self-improvement" — his baseline view that automatable research is too narrow, gains diminish with more parallel agents, and resource/political bottlenecks limit acceleration regardless of AI's help.
- Argues frontier labs' internal culture (thousands of concurrent agents, competitive SF environment) amplifies AI-risk anxiety, and that jumping from that anxiety to extinction-risk conclusions is more emotional/quasi-religious than evidence-based.
- Cites forecasts from Charlie O'Neill, Beren Millidge, and John Schulman (via a Dwarkesh podcast) on timelines for: a drop-in remote worker (~1-3 years depending on tool access), 10x AI-researcher productivity uplift (~2-10 years), and AI surpassing top human experts across all computer work (~3-10 years).
- Contends current RSI-like effects are concentrated in efficiency gains (cheaper inference, faster software engineering, log monitoring, experiment management) rather than expanding peak intelligence — and that peak intelligence is the "hardest exponential" to accelerate.
- Notes post-training remains resistant to automation because it requires human judgment on how models should behave in many domains, and errors there often don't surface in benchmarks (per Schulman).
- Points to Anthropic's Claude system card language stating internal AI usage maintains current progress rate but shows no "dramatic acceleration" beyond it, as evidence against strong RSI claims.
- Suggests the real near-term transformation is inference-time compute scaling and diffusion into AI research/related work — economically significant but distinct from true recursive self-improvement.

## Quote

> Lossy self-improvement remains my baseline on the trajectory of progress, and the increased discussion of extinction risk seems very misplaced.
