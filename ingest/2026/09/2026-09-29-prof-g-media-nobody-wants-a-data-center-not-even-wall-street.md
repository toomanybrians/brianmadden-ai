# Nobody Wants a Data Center. Not Even Wall Street.

## Insights

- Wall Street's appetite for data center investment is cooling: SB Energy's IPO (targeted at a $50B market cap) was delayed after bankers couldn't find enough buyers, despite backing from SoftBank.
- SB Energy has zero operational data centers and a $439B contract backlog, but only 9% of those contracts have broken ground — and most contracts include completion-time clauses letting customers exit or get discounts if projects are delayed over a year.
- Community opposition to data centers has surged: more than 840 opposition groups exist in the U.S. now, up from fewer than 50 at the start of 2024, compounding grid-connection and power-equipment supply constraints.
- An estimated 30–50% of data center capacity planned to come online this year will be delayed, and other infrastructure-adjacent companies (Aggreko, a modular power supplier; Holtec, a nuclear tech firm) have also pulled or delayed IPOs citing data-center-development uncertainty.
- Sam Altman has pushed OpenAI's IPO back to 2027, cited alongside these infrastructure delays as part of a broader pattern of caution around AI-buildout-linked offerings.
- Second-order effects are hitting Big Tech directly: Oracle sent a force majeure notice on a data center project (seeking payment-delay options if not operational by 2028), sending Oracle stock down 4% and financing partner Blue Owl down 5%, with related debt trading below 90 cents on the dollar.
