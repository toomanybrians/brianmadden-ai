# Using personal agents for professional networking

## Insights

- Author built a personal AI agent system ("Grok Bot," anchored by a persona named "Ari") to support professional networking, explicitly framed as amplifying human helpfulness rather than automating or replacing relational effort.
- The system runs four steps: nudging reconnection with contacts (scanning iMessage/Gmail/Granola into Notion), manually noting how to help specific people, analyzing shared links to build an "interest graph" per contact, and surfacing serendipitous recommendations via a daily "heartbeat" routine.
- The approach is scoped narrowly to ~150 AI-related contacts (explicitly likened to Dunbar's number), not applied to the author's full network.
- Author reports a low action rate on AI suggestions (roughly 1 in 30), framing this as evidence the system is appropriately human-in-the-loop rather than over-automated — and states a too-high hit rate would be "scary."
- Estimates that 90% of actual relational reminders/ideas still originate from personal memory/judgment, not the AI layer.
- Frames the core design principle as a values check: any agent behavior that could read as "creepy" surveillance rather than genuine helpfulness is discarded.

## Quote

> The greatest skill in the AI era is to know how to use the technology in the most human way possible.
