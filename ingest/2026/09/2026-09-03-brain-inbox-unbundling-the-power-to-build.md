# Unbundling the Power to Build

## Insights

- OpenAI frames a shift from "how much faster can AI help someone do their job" to "how much work can one person reliably direct" — coordinating multiple parallel agents rather than just accelerating individual tasks.
- Cited Codex usage data (Dec 2025–May 2026): 80% of sampled users made at least one request estimated at 30+ minutes of human-equivalent work, and over 25% made requests estimated at 8+ hours of human work.
- Introduces the term "Participation Economy" — the idea that AI lets individuals direct work once requiring an entire organization, potentially enabling more people to launch products/businesses without large teams or capital.
- Warns of a coming "conversion gap": a divide not over access to AI itself, but between those equipped (tools, skills, freedom) to convert AI access into actual output, income, or new ventures.
- Separately, Argonne/OpenAI researchers describe building an "Atlas of the Machine-Accessible Frontier" to map which scientific problems AI can already help solve versus which still require new experiments or human-led inquiry — explicitly cautioning against assuming "more AI" is always the right investment.
- OpenAI's own AI Sentiment Tracker shows US public sentiment toward AI dropped sharply, from -5 to -30 on a three-month rolling average, partly driven by a poll finding 70% of adults view AI as "mostly bad for society."

## Quote

> The goal, then, shouldn't simply be AI everywhere – it should be more people with the tools, skills, and freedom to build.
