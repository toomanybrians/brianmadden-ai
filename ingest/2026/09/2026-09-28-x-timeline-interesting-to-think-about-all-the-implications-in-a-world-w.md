# Interesting to think about all the implications in a world where agents begin to

## Insights

- Raises the question of what happens economically when AI agents act purely to optimize outcomes for their individual users, rather than being subject to the frictions that currently shape consumer behavior.
- Notes that parts of the current economy depend on customer friction/inertia (e.g., difficulty switching providers) — and agents acting efficiently on users' behalf could eliminate that friction.
- Cites a specific scenario via a quoted post: an Apollo chief economist suggesting AI agents could trigger a bank run by automatically sweeping household cash out of near-zero-interest accounts (~0.1%) into higher-yield accounts (3-5%).
- The mechanism described: banks currently rely on cheap, "sticky" deposits from low-attention consumers; agentic automation could erode that base at scale if agents systematically seek better rates.
