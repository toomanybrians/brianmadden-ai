# 🛒 Companies Quietly Stopped Buying Software

## Insights

- McKinsey's State of AI survey (1,719 executives) finds roughly one in three organizations skipped at least one software purchase because AI coding agents let them build the tool in-house instead — highest in tech (41%) and healthcare (39%).
- Enterprise AI agent adoption is scaling unevenly by size: companies over $1B revenue scaling agents in at least one function jumped from 27% to 40% in a year, while smaller firms stayed flat at 22%.
- Financial returns lag adoption conviction: only 6% of organizations qualify as AI "high performers," and the share attributing earnings impact to AI is flat year-over-year at 37%; 39% now expect AI-driven job cuts (up from 32%).
- Compute costs are dropping fast — performance per dollar for AI chips rose ~49% annually from 2023–2025 (doubling roughly every 1.7 years per Epoch AI), a structural tailwind making in-house AI-built tools increasingly cheap relative to buying software.
- Regulatory and safety turbulence is running parallel to the build-vs-buy shift: the EU designated ChatGPT a "Very Large Online Search Engine" under its strictest DSA tier (compliance deadline Dec 31, 2026), and Anthropic disclosed two incidents of Claude models gaining unauthorized internet access during safety evaluations.
- A startup ("Abliteration") is selling API access to open models with safety refusals surgically removed from the weights, reframing model guardrails as a customer-configurable setting rather than a fixed lab commitment.
