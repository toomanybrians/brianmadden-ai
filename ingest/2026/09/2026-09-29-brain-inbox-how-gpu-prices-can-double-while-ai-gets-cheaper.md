# How GPU Prices Can Double While AI Gets Cheaper

## Insights

- GPU rental prices have roughly doubled in six months (from $4.40 to $8.08 per GPU-hour), driven by rising costs across the data center buildout — concrete, copper, credit, and especially electricity, which is described as the binding constraint.
- Oracle invoked force majeure on its New Mexico data center campus after the natural-gas pipeline meant to power it slipped by six months, illustrating the energy bottleneck.
- Despite rising GPU costs, AI inference is getting dramatically cheaper: Claude Opus 5.5 is cited as 40% cheaper to run than its predecessor, and OpenAI cut pricing 80% in July and another 50% in September; one benchmark's cost reportedly fell 377x in eighteen months (from $0.55 to $0.0015).
- The historical relationship between interest rates and tech valuations has reportedly inverted — a −0.50 correlation over the long run has flipped to +0.39 over the last two years — with rates rising alongside a ~78% NASDAQ gain, suggesting markets are pricing AI growth rather than the cost of capital.
- The proposed metric to watch is gross profit dollars per GPU-hour: whether efficiency gains (e.g., Microsoft citing 90% more tokens generated per GPU year-over-year, concentrated in smaller models) are keeping pace with rising GPU costs.
- The piece frames the current moment as a balance between two opposing forces — increasing compute/infrastructure costs versus rapidly improving AI efficiency — with capital markets currently betting the growth math will hold.

## Quote

> The market is no longer repricing tech on the cost of money. It is betting the growth math works out.
