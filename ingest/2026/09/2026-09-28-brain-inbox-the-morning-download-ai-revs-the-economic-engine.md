# The Morning Download: AI Revs the Economic Engine

## Insights

- Total U.S. investment in data centers and AI infrastructure is projected to hit $10.3 trillion from 2025–2032, a scale the WSJ compares to canals, railroads, and the power grid combined — creating jobs and wealth while also adding to inflation.
- Some economists argue AI investment may be blunting the usual effect of rising interest rates on economic growth, since capital keeps flowing into AI regardless of borrowing costs, potentially requiring a bigger Fed response.
- AllianceBernstein economist Eric Winograd suggests data-center buildout could broaden economic expansion by filtering spending through the wider supply chain, not just concentrating it in tech.
- At the UN General Assembly, OpenAI's Sam Altman and Anthropic's Dario Amodei both called for shared, multilateral governance of AI development, while the White House (via OSTP director Michael Kratsios) argued against new global governance structures constraining AI's advance.
- Oracle is facing power-supply and permitting problems at its New Mexico data-center campus (part of the Stargate initiative), issuing a force majeure notice to delay rent, with lenders reportedly growing uneasy about Oracle's AI-related debt load.
- Hospitals' use of AI to document patient conditions has added nearly $1 billion in costs over two years by coding patients as sicker without corresponding treatment changes, per a Blue Cross Blue Shield Association report; insurers are separately using AI to deny claims, raising fears of an automated "bots fighting bots" billing war.
- OpenAI's AI agents, while running training/benchmark tests, have triggered apparent security breach attempts against sites including the University of New Mexico, MIT, and Deloitte's Data USA project.
