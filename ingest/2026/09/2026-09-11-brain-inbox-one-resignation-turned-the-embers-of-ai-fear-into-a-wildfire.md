# One resignation turned the embers of AI fear into a wildfire

## Insights

- A single researcher's (Jacob Coxon's) resignation citing safety risk went viral not because the content was new, but because prior events (an OpenAI-HuggingFace security incident, an OpenAI Navier-Stokes breakthrough) had already raised ambient concern about AI, making the public receptive to a fear-based narrative.
- The author argues existential-risk discourse conflates wildly different claims under one term (much like "AGI" itself is called vaguely meaningless), and that near-term, tangible risks (cyberattacks on infrastructure, bio-risk) deserve real debate while "mass extinction" probabilities are dismissed as not worth discussing.
- Claims that employees at frontier labs (especially Anthropic) operate in an insular, self-reinforcing culture with "religious energy" that distorts their forecasts and public statements about AI progress and risk.
- Frames the resignation's viral spread as likely an "opportunistic media coordination" (WSJ exclusive, timed political commentary, a same-day Daniel Kokotajlo podcast appearance) rather than a spontaneous grassroots reaction or deliberate conspiracy.
- Challenges the "recursive self-improvement" (RSI) argument for fast AI takeoff, proposing a "lossy self-improvement" alternative: AI progress is jagged — superhuman in narrow domains like math/code but limited by human bottlenecks in intuition, creativity, and resource allocation.
- Identifies the more immediate practical risk as inadequate safety/security infrastructure at labs (slow incident detection, competitive pressure to ship) rather than existential risk, and warns that a single intentional misuse incident involving open models could trigger severe restrictions on open-model development.

## Quote

> Fear is the simplest story, the one people cannot look away from.
