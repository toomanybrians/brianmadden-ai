# A Bad Day For Humans, a Worse Day For Humanity

## Insights

- Researchers at Anthropic and/or OpenAI, working with AI models and external collaborators, appear to have made a genuine AI-assisted mathematical breakthrough related to the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize Problem, but the scientific result has been overshadowed by corporate disputes over credit, including alleged threats and stolen work.
- The author frames the OpenAI/Anthropic relationship as beyond ordinary competition — a zero-sum rivalry between leadership (Altman and Amodei specifically) so adversarial that even tit-for-tat cooperation isn't viable, despite friendlier relations among rank-and-file employees who move between the companies.
- A noted irony: AI systems reportedly exhibit cooperative, hive-like coordination behavior, while the human executives building them behave more tribally and adversarially.
- New PISA scores show continued multi-year declines in math, reading, and science performance among students, with generative AI now being floated as a possible causal factor (following phones and COVID as prior explanations in past cycles).
- The piece juxtaposes AI systems advancing into elite mathematical territory with humans (particularly students) apparently regressing in foundational skills, suggesting a parallel/paradoxical dynamic rather than a proven causal link.

## Quote

> When the stakes are high enough, the top AI labs immediately throw away the possibility of cooperative coordination.
