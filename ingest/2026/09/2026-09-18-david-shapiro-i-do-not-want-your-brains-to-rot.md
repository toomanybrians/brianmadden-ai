# 🧠 I do not want your brains to rot

## Insights

- Distinguishes "cognitive offloading" (a low-cost, strategic delegation of tasks) from "cognitive surrender" (an uncritical abdication of reasoning itself), framing them as different in kind, not degree.
- Argues AI's power and appeal make widespread cognitive surrender more likely than with prior tools.
- Cites an unpeer-reviewed paper claiming a "cognitive divergence" is underway: measured attention spans and reading levels were already declining before AI's rise.
- The argument extends this trend: advanced AI invites people to delegate increasingly small, simple tasks, which erodes the practices that sustain cognitive capacity over time.
- Raises an open question about the tradeoff between productivity gains from AI use and a possible loss of selfhood or cognitive identity.

## Quote

> Cognitive offloading is a strategic delegation that costs nothing. Cognitive surrender is something different.
