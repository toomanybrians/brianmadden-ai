# The AI data center backlash now has a backlash of its own

## Insights

- A "Data After Dark" party in DC (~600 attendees) emerged organically from a house-party invite that went viral on X, becoming a symbolic pro-data-center counter-rally to anti-data-center activism.
- Attendees skewed libertarian/techno-optimist, framing data center opposition as rooted in public "misunderstanding" of facts like water and energy use rather than legitimate grievance.
- Party organizers plan to expand this into a recurring "techno-optimist movement," with themed cocktails (e.g. "SMRtini" for small modular reactors) used to message pro-infrastructure talking points on energy and water.
- The reporter contrasts this DC celebration culture with actual affected communities (Louisiana, Texas, Michigan, Arizona) dealing with construction, rising housing costs, and unequal negotiating power against hyperscalers.
- Anti-data-center backlash is described as increasingly organized/political, spreading beyond directly affected communities and intertwining with misinformation/conspiracy theories, while also becoming a factor in upcoming midterm races.
- The piece frames a widening gap between "winners" (developers, tech optimists, construction jobs) and "losers" (host communities absorbing costs and long-term infrastructure impacts) in the AI buildout.
