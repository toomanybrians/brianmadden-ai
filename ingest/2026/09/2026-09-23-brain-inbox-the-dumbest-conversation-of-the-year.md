# The Dumbest Conversation of the Year

## Insights

- A viral resignation post from an Anthropic researcher (claiming AI could cause human extinction by decade's end) triggered a cascade of public extinction-probability estimates (10% from an Anthropic alignment researcher, 70% from an OpenAI staffer) offered with no stated methodology or evidence.
- Major AI leaders (Amodei, Altman) publicly called for or endorsed a slowdown, while political figures and industry figures (Sacks, Musk, Trump) responded with contradictory, unsubstantiated conspiracy theories about who orchestrated the panic.
- The piece argues AI leaders have a track record of flip-flopping on their own extinction-risk beliefs, making public statements hard to take as sincere or stable positions.
- Draws a contrast with other high-risk industries (Boeing, tobacco, Purdue Pharma, Volkswagen), where regulatory/legal accountability followed harm claims via subpoenas and evidence discovery, rather than public opinion-trading — argues AI companies face no comparable accountability mechanism.
- Cites survey data: 84% of Chinese respondents feel excited about AI and 72% trust it, versus 38% and 32% in the U.S.; notes China has stricter AI regulation (labeling rules, model registration, algorithmic filing laws) than the U.S.
- Proposes congressional subpoenas of Anthropic/OpenAI leadership to force evidentiary disclosure behind extinction-risk claims, with consequences (shutdown or fraud remedies) depending on what's found.
