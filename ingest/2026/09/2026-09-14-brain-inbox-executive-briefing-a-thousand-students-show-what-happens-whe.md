# Executive Briefing: A Thousand Students Show What Happens When You Take the AI Away

## Insights

- Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei's essay "We Must Pace the Frontier" (published Sept 12) calls for deliberately slowing the most capable AI systems so safety work can catch up, proposing outside evaluators and coordination among democratic nations.
- Sam Altman committed OpenAI to accepting outside evaluators as well, though implementation details remain undefined and it's unclear if companies will actually agree to slow development pace.
- Anthropic reported (Sept 10) that a Yemen-based engineering cell used Claude to help develop guided weapons software and conduct AI-assisted surveillance targeting dissidents; the account did not confirm a working weapon was actually fielded.
- Security researchers at "Calif" used AI assistance to find a WeChat vulnerability, demonstrated a cross-account spreading attack, and worked with Tencent to fix it before public disclosure — framed as a controlled demonstration, not evidence of a real-world outbreak.
- Reuters reported preparations for AI safety talks ahead of a possible Sept 24 Trump-Xi summit, though a White House official disputed that any AI-specific meeting was planned for mid-September.
- The author argues great-power competition itself increases net AI risk: governments convinced they must "win" have incentive to move faster, share less, and treat rivals' progress as threat — and separately notes AI's value as a family/accessibility aid, arguing workplace gains alone undersell what "abundance" from AI could mean.

## Quote

> Governments that believe they must win at almost any cost have reasons to move faster, share less, and interpret another country's progress as a threat.
