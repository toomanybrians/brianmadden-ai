# Your Agents Should Have Names

## Insights

- Miessler distinguishes today's "agents" (scheduled tasks/loops with no persistent memory or goals) from a future generation of autonomous, persistent agents that function more like specialized team members (e.g., one for cyber, one for physical security, one for finances, one for health).
- He proposes "personified" agents — named entities (e.g., "Chris" for cybersecurity) with consistent personality, full contextual knowledge of the person/business, and an ongoing relational history — arguing this is a structural shift in what the agent *is*, not merely a UI/interaction-style change.
- Even in a multi-agent, personified future, he expects a "main agent" would retain top-level permissions, cross-domain knowledge, and overall coordination authority.
- He references Dwarkesh Patel's framing of this trend as effectively increasing the "population" of active entities on Earth — each person/org having named agents that themselves manage teams of sub-agents with real purpose, not just background scripts.
- He lays out four requirements for this progression: persistent memory, consistent personality, alignment, and capable tools — noting these map onto his existing "Personal AI Maturity Model" axes.
- He raises an unresolved tension: a maximally capable single agent might make personified multi-agent setups "superfluous," but if personified agents genuinely gain efficacy from embedded goals/simulated feelings, that raises ethical discomfort about casually creating and deleting them.

## Quote

> The same thing that makes Chris good at his job is the thing that makes deleting Chris feel like something.
