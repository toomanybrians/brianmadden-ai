# Evaluators must be able to report important findings outside the agreed scope. E

## Insights

- Argues that third-party AI evaluators/auditors must retain the ability to report significant findings even when those findings fall outside the scope originally agreed upon with the company being evaluated.
- Frames this as necessary because evaluators will inevitably encounter issues nobody anticipated when scope was defined in advance.
- Sets a minimum bar: any finding meeting the company's own internal safety-incident threshold must be disclosed, regardless of scope agreements.
- Calls for "meta-transparency" — public disclosure of the evaluation agreement itself, including scope, what access was granted or denied, and any redactions made.
- Positions this transparency as protecting both evaluators and companies: it defends evaluators' independence while shielding companies from accusations of hiding unfavorable results via redaction.
