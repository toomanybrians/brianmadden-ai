# 🔮 What would Adam Smith make of AI?

## Insights

- Adam Smith is often reduced to a free-market icon, but his foundational work (Theory of Moral Sentiments) framed morality and self-interest as emerging together from social life, not self-interest alone.
- The Wealth of Nations argued specialization and exchange can be positive-sum — one party's gain needn't be another's loss — which the author extends as a lens for evaluating AI's economic effects.
- Using an AI persona ("R Adam Smith") built on Smith's writings and current AI debates, the author speculates Smith would view AI as a productivity tool extending his "pin factory" division-of-labor idea — but by giving one person access to many specialties at once, rather than narrowing tasks.
- The simulated Smith proposes concrete tests for whether AI is a genuine improvement: does it make goods cheaper, open trades to newcomers, and let ordinary wages buy more — abundance for households as the real metric, not just productivity gains.
- The author's own take (before running the simulation) was that Smith would be skeptical of companies coordinating on AI issues collectively and concerned about extending moral agency to machines.
- When asked about AI leaders (e.g., Anthropic's Dario Amodei) requesting government intervention to slow AI development, the simulated Smith suggests such fears, even if sincere, don't cancel out underlying self-interest.

## Quote

> Sincerity does not abolish self-interest.
