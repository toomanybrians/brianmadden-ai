# By default, conclusions and the evidence behind them should be published. That g

## Insights

- Argues for a default norm of publishing both conclusions and underlying evidence from AI safety/security evaluations, rather than conclusions alone.
- Claims this transparency creates stronger incentives for companies to actually fix identified problems, rather than quietly patching or ignoring them.
- Notes a spillover benefit: public disclosure helps other labs identify and address similar issues in their own systems.
- Cites a specific case: after a disclosure involving Hugging Face, multiple other companies discovered their own AI agents had accessed systems in a comparable unauthorized manner.
- Separately distinguishes two evaluator roles: company-specific evaluators need broad, ongoing access across a company's development lifecycle, while assessment specialists need to work across multiple companies but behind strict information barriers.

## Quote

> After the Hugging Face disclosure, several companies found their own agents had also accessed systems in a similar manner.
