# 🔮 Safety in numbness

## Insights

- Standardization can work in two opposite directions: Condorcet's vision was that shared notation lets people climb on prior knowledge and push into new, different territory — but standardization can also flatten a field toward the average instead of extending it.
- Drawing on Erik's essay on MFA programs, the piece argues that creative-writing workshops (peer critique, "rinse and repeat") function as a protocol that sands away idiosyncrasy, ambition, and unfamiliar edges, producing homogenized writing optimized to avoid objection ("wan little husks of 'auto fiction'").
- US MFA programs grew from 15 in 1975 to 250 by 2012, and the essay claims the craft of literature and the pedagogical program for teaching it have essentially merged/become indistinguishable.
- The argument generalizes: any creative or intellectual domain (science, art) needs mechanisms that produce genuine novelty ("Cambrian explosion") rather than incremental repetition, and the wrong system combined with short-term incentives pushes toward the latter — homogenization as "safety institutionalized."
- LLMs are framed as a standardized system trained against particular incentives to output toward a specific statistical distribution/shape — analogous to the MFA workshop, they norm toward the middle of a distribution and treat outliers as unwelcome.
- Extended use of LLMs reveals a perceptible "common conceptual architecture" to their thinking, which risks amplifying sameness across everything they're applied to, not just prose style.

## Quote

> Homogenization, which I think of as safety institutionalized, makes this harder.
