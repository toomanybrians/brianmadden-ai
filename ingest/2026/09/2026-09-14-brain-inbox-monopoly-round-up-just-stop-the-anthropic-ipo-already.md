# Monopoly Round-Up: Just Stop the Anthropic IPO Already

## Insights

- Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei publicly called for antitrust laws not to apply to major AI firms, framing it as necessary to let the industry collectively "pace the frontier" (slow development) to reduce extinction risk; Sam Altman agreed. This came just before Anthropic's planned IPO.
- Reporting (The Information) indicates OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google have held backchannel talks about forming an AI standards organization, which the author frames as a mechanism to coordinate a cartel.
- Financial detail cited from the FT: Anthropic's gross margins are reportedly above 80%, but that figure excludes model-training costs and stock compensation — the actual cash bleed driver is the recurring cost of developing newer models to stay ahead of competitors, especially cheaper Chinese open-source models.
- Argument advanced: "pacing the frontier" / seeking an antitrust exemption could functionally let Anthropic and OpenAI cut model-development spending, making their financials look better ahead of an IPO (echoing a comment attributed to Jim Cramer), rather than being purely about safety.
- Because AI labs are major buyers of hyperscaler/cloud/chip capacity, reduced spending by OpenAI/Anthropic could ripple through Nvidia, Broadcom, and cloud providers, given circular financing arrangements in the sector.
- Frames the current policy debate as a contest between two camps: "AI doomers" seeking new legal frameworks/antitrust exemptions and industry self-regulation, versus a "rule of law" camp (citing Lina Khan, Cory Doctorow) arguing AI agents/products should simply be subject to existing product-safety, tort, and unfair-practices law without new exemptions.
- Notes the SEC technically has authority to block an IPO by refusing to clear a company's S-1 registration statement if risk disclosures are deemed inadequate.

## Quote

> These developments, to Amodei, are inevitable, no human is responsible, though we must all act quickly.
