# Unsupervised Learning NO. 541

## Insights

- Miessler frames a core enterprise-AI question via "Watch the Plot": the real metric for AI's value should be whether it increases human happiness and reduces suffering, not raw capability gains — and he argues that's largely unproven so far.
- Anish Acharya (via Lenny's podcast) argues AI is turning company functions into repeatable "loops," and that career advancement is shifting from owning tasks to supervising loops, with smaller teams gaining more autonomy.
- Enrique Dans argues AI cannot optimize what it cannot structurally understand — it needs an explicit organizational model (objects, links, rules, actions), not just more context, before it can meaningfully improve how a company works.
- A cybersecurity essay (Sylvain Kalache) warns that as AI absorbs routine incident response, engineers lose the hands-on experience that normally teaches them how systems fail — raising a skills-erosion risk as AI takes over operational work.
- Miessler's "Peak Human Readership" essay claims humans may have already passed the point of reading anything requiring sustained focus, predicting that the overwhelming majority of future content will be AI-made and AI-consumed, with human-authored/human-read content shrinking to a tiny niche.
- Separately, a WIRED piece is flagged (title only) describing AI's use in hiring as creating an "infinite doom loop" in the job market.
