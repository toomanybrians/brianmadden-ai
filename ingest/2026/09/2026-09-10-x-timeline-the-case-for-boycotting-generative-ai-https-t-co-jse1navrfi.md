# The case for boycotting Generative AI: https://t.co/jse1naVrFi https://t.co/z1lu

## Insights

- Marcus argues frontier AI labs are knowingly building systems they themselves acknowledge could be catastrophic, citing an Anthropic employee's public claim of >10% chance AI could kill all humans within a decade and no working alignment plan.
- He distinguishes between "p(doom)" (extinction risk, which he rates low, citing human resilience/diversity) and "p(dystopia)" (catastrophic but non-extinction harm from AI-generated pathogens, disinformation-fueled wars, infrastructure hacks), which he rates high.
- His core technical objection: probabilistic LLMs are inherently unreliable/non-deterministic ("bulls in a china shop"), unlike classical deterministic AI (search, recommendation, routing engines), which never behaves unpredictably; giving unreliable LLMs internet access compounds the risk.
- He proposes not a permanent ban but a temporary halt on building/deploying "incorrigible AI" (systems that can't be trusted or aligned) until technical reliability and political oversight improve.
- He's lost faith in government response (citing an opaque White House policy and unchecked release of a model that reduces monitorability) and instead calls for a consumer boycott of generative AI tools to hit companies financially and force safety action.
- Addresses the "China will build it anyway" counterargument by suggesting a US-China deal is possible, arguing the CCP is unlikely to actually want out-of-control recursively self-improving AI.
- Quotes Cal Newport's claim that there's no real commercial justification for pursuing unstable frontier AI — it's ideological (a "deterministic belief in a world made whole... by AI"), not commercially necessary.

## Quote

> Taking a supposed 10% chance at catastrophe with no clear path to a tremendously positive outcome to offset it is insane.
