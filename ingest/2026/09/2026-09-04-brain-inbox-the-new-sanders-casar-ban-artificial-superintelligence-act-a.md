# The new Sanders-Casar Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act – and why I oppose it

## Insights

- Gary Marcus opposes the Sanders-Casar "Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act" as drafted, arguing a permanent, unilateral ban on superhuman AI research is too broad and would put the US at a competitive disadvantage.
- He distinguishes between a temporary pause (which he could support, possibly lasting years, until regulation and alignment are better understood) and a permanent ban, which he sees as the wrong mechanism.
- He reiterates support for a dedicated AI regulatory agency, referencing his own 2023 testimony to the US Senate on this point.
- He criticizes the bill for being naive about the technical complexity of benchmarking AI capability thresholds, and warns it could let authoritarian actors game the rules.
- He argues the bill overemphasizes speculative future risks (e.g., AI that "plans to disempower humanity") at the expense of addressing current, more immediate AI risks — citing Dr. Angela Rasmussen's similar critique.
- He proposes an alternative framing (borrowed from Anthony Aguirre) that would allow narrow/sub-frontier AI development to continue while requiring developers of more general, autonomous systems to prove alignment/control/oversight to an independent authority — a capability-triggered gate rather than a blanket ban.
