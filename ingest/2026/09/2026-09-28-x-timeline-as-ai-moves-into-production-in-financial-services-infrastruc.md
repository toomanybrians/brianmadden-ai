# As #AI moves into production in financial services, infrastructure and governanc

## Insights

- Financial services firms have largely moved past AI experimentation into production, with nearly two-thirds piloting or deploying top-priority use cases in data analysis, fraud detection, workflow automation, and customer experience.
- Firms that succeed treat AI as business transformation wired into core workflows and risk controls, not as isolated IT experiments — an "execution gap" often separates ambitious AI strategy from operational reality (ownership, governance, funding, data readiness).
- Data remains a major bottleneck: legacy data was structured for human/compliance use, not for AI systems needing vector databases, lineage, and multimodal handling — and AI's probabilistic nature requires continuous monitoring for model/data drift rather than one-time builds.
- Infrastructure built for batch analytics over decades doesn't support real-time, GPU-bound inference needs; many institutions overestimate their technical readiness despite confidence, and "project-grade" infrastructure will lose to "enterprise-grade" approaches.
- ROI measurement should extend beyond cost savings to decision quality, speed, risk reduction, and "organizational velocity" — how fast teams can act on new information — which is harder to quantify but potentially more strategically significant.
- Moving from AI assistants to autonomous AI agents is described as a governance upgrade, not just a technology upgrade, requiring audit trails, clear permissioning, defined dependencies, and controls that tighten under stress; firms that shortcut governance early will struggle to earn trust for more autonomous deployment later.
