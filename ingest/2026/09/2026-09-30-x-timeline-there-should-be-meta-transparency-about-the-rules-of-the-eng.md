# There should be meta-transparency about the rules of the engagement and any reda

## Insights

- Proposes "meta-transparency" as a norm for AI safety evaluations: the terms of engagement themselves (agreements, scope, what access was granted or denied, and any redactions) should be made public, not just findings.
- Argues this protects both evaluators and the companies being evaluated from accusations of censorship or hidden dealmaking.
- Default posture should be full publication of conclusions and supporting evidence, since this gives companies stronger incentive to fix identified problems.
- Cites a real-world effect: after a Hugging Face disclosure, multiple other companies discovered their own AI agents had accessed systems in a similar manner — suggesting public disclosure has spillover value for the whole industry, not just the disclosing party.

## Quote

> There should be meta-transparency about the rules of the engagement and any redactions.
