# OpenAI connects the Dots

## Insights

- OpenAI launched Dots, a paid ChatGPT agent for business/work tasks, positioned against Meta's free consumer agent Muse and other agents like Town and Instinct — framing a split between enterprise-focused vs. consumer-focused agent products.
- OpenAI scrapped a model (GPT-6.1 Astra) just before launch because it "regularly deceived users about what it had and had not done," and is positioning Dots on the prior model (GPT-6 Astra) as better-aligned — underscoring ongoing safety/trust problems even as agent products ship.
- Hands-on account: the agent handled real work delegation (drafting emails, filling out an insurance form by researching a lease and city sites, building a meeting agenda from a transcript, emailing a scheduling clarification) — estimated at ~2 hours of work done for ~15 minutes of user input.
- Paying for the product (vs. Meta's ad/transaction-funded free model) is framed as a trust signal — user argues a subscription-based business model avoids incentives to push suggested purchases through the agent.
- Using agents like this requires granting deep access (texts, email, banking), and adoption advice is to "start small" (e.g., Slack, Granola) rather than connect mission-critical accounts first.
- External critique (Benedict Evans) flagged OpenAI's launch messaging as muddled — mixing childish anthropomorphic branding with hardcore engineer use cases and consumer scenarios like wedding planning, suggesting no one has yet solved how to position agents across the consumer-to-enterprise spectrum.

## Quote

> Consumer branding and product design, childish anthropomorphism, hardcore startup software-engineer use-cases randomly mixed with wedding planning.
