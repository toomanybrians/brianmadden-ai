# Re-Founding Incumbents for the AI Era with Sequence Holdings Co-Founder and CEO Michael Lee

## Insights

- Sequence Holdings' thesis: acquire incumbents rather than build startups where the incumbent holds structural advantages (brand, scale, regulation, network effects), then "re-found" them with AI/frontier engineering rather than compete against them.
- The firm frames AI transformation as needing a culture shift where engineers become the "celebrated persona" inside acquired companies, contrasting with PE firms (e.g., Blackstone) where investors hold that status — argues incumbents can't recruit/retain AI talent without this cultural reorientation.
- Prefers "organizational physics" that are centralized/dense (e.g., bank underwriting, brokerage carriers) over distributed roll-ups, because shared technology investments amortize better across centralized operations than fragmented ones needing standardization across many sites.
- Case study: a community bank investment cut average consumer loan underwriting time by 94% and end-to-end loan processing from 30 to 11 days, allowing doubled loan volume in one quarter with a smaller (not larger) underwriting team — achieved via attrition/reassignment, not layoffs.
- Describes an "Atlas" platform (data ontology, agent builder, orchestration/"Lattice," application builder/"artifacts") built to be ~80% reusable across industries and ~20% vertical-specific customization.
- Argues change management/employee anxiety, not the engineering itself, is the harder problem in AI-driven organizational redesign; emphasizes framing AI as elevating existing roles rather than replacing people.
- Uses a permanent holding-company structure (vs. fund/services model) to align capital duration with long-term operational transformation, deliberately doing very few deals ("one per year") rather than scaling deal volume.

## Quote

> "In a world where you believe that alpha comes from engineering and AI, you need to create a culture whereby the celebrated persona is the engineer."
