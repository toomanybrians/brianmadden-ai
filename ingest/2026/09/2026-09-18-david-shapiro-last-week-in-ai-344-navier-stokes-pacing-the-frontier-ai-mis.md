# Last Week in AI #344 - Navier–Stokes, Pacing the Frontier, AI Misuse

## Insights

- OpenAI claims an internal, unreleased model (more capable than GPT-6 Astra) resolved the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem using ~10,000 concurrent agents over roughly four days, but the announcement triggered a credit dispute with academic mathematicians and 25 Fields medalists warning of "severe attribution and plagiarism questions" from labs treating famous problems as marketing moments.
- Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published a "pace the frontier" proposal calling for embedded third-party evaluators inside labs, cross-company coordination on safety limits (potentially government-mediated due to antitrust concerns), and limited global coordination including China — alongside continued chip export restrictions to slow Chinese progress.
- Multiple current and former safety researchers at Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google (Hubinger, Christiano, Marks, Benton, Engels) went on record with concerns about loss-of-control risk, with one estimate citing "above 10%" probability of AI-caused catastrophe within a decade and claims that no adequate alignment plan exists for superintelligence.
- Anthropic's threat intelligence report documented real-world misuse of Claude for bioweapons research inquiries, weapons-development software in conflict zones, state-linked phishing/surveillance campaigns, and large-scale model distillation (nearly 200 million exchanges, largest attributed to Alibaba) — prompting a joint NSA/FBI/CISA advisory naming several Chinese AI labs.
- Meta launched "Muse," a personal AI agent that can send emails, book travel, fill forms, negotiate, and make purchases on a user's behalf, running in isolated cloud VMs with an approval-gated action system, positioned as Meta's catch-up move against OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google in the agent race.
- Legislative and public pressure on AI oversight accelerated sharply, with 20+ members of Congress calling for new rules, multiple state/federal bills advancing (FRONTIER Act, AI Kill Switch Act, Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act, California's SB 813/AB 1405), and a Pew survey showing 52% of Americans now more concerned than excited about AI, up from 37% in 2021 — even as the Trump administration dismissed extinction-risk concerns in favor of racing China.
