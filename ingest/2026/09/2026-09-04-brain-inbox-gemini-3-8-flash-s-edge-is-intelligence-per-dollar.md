# ⚙️ Gemini 3.8 Flash’s edge is intelligence per dollar

## Insights

- Google released Gemini 3.8 Flash (its third Flash-series release in six weeks), pricing it at $0.75/million input and $3.75/million output tokens — roughly 4-5x cheaper than Anthropic's Opus 5 ($5/$25) and OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol ($4/$20), while matching predecessor Flash's speed/cost.
- Gemini 3.8 Flash trails Opus 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol on general knowledge-work, long-horizon coding, and computer-use benchmarks, but beats both on domain-specific tasks (legal, finance, biology) and agentic terminal coding.
- Google reportedly scrapped internal Gemini 3.5 Pro candidates because they didn't meaningfully outperform Flash models, suggesting a strategic pivot toward efficiency/cost over frontier capability as rivals (Anthropic, OpenAI) push heavier, pricier models.
- CrowdStrike's new Agentic Identity Provider gives AI agents verifiable, non-spoofable cryptographic identities, registers them in one directory, and enforces short-lived, task-scoped permissions with full traceability of agent-to-agent handoffs — framed as necessary since AI agents are estimated to outnumber humans roughly 90:1 in enterprise environments.
- Dataiku CEO Florian Douetteau argues enterprise AI creation costs are falling toward zero, so value will increasingly accrue to governance and judgment: companies will buy ~90% of their agents and build only the differentiated 10%, making "agent management" an emerging discipline.
- Douetteau frames the core enterprise dilemma as speed vs. control: moving too slowly cedes cost advantage to competitors, but moving too fast without governance risks a major AI failure becoming a crisis.

## Quote

> "Most research has AI agents outnumbering humans somewhere around 90 to 1." — AJ Shipley, CrowdStrike chief product officer
