# Job hugging is mostly a private white-collar thing

## Insights

- "Job hugging" (low quits rates) is not economy-wide; it's concentrated almost entirely in private white-collar sectors (finance, insurance, real estate, information, professional/business services — labeled FIIPB), where quits sit at the 13th percentile of 25-year history, 28% below 2019 levels.
- Government, education, and health show the opposite pattern — quits are near-normal/high (71st percentile), because these sectors are still adding jobs at scale, with health care leading employment growth 2023–2025.
- The driver isn't sentiment or fear alone but job scarcity: FIIPB employment peaked in early 2023 and has since fallen roughly 1.5 million jobs short of its pre-pandemic trend path; net hiring in the sector has flipped negative.
- Layoffs remain historically low across all sectors (no 2001- or 2008-style layoff spike), meaning companies are managing headcount reduction through hiring freezes and attrition rather than firing — one exception: FIIPB layoffs have been quietly rising through 2025, the one metric moving the "wrong way."
- For employers this dynamic is currently favorable (lower turnover costs, less pressure to raise pay); for workers it's unfavorable, since job-switching is historically the primary route to raises and promotions — a channel that's now largely closed in white-collar sectors.
- Hiring-rate data mostly just mirrors the quits data, since most hiring is replacement hiring for vacated seats — when nobody quits, there's little to hire for.

## Quote

> People do not quit when there is nowhere to go.
