# The AI Bubble Isn’t Where You Think It Is

## Insights

- Model intelligence-per-dollar has fallen sharply: a capability band that cost ~$37.50 per million tokens (GPT-4 era) is now available for ~$0.04 per million tokens (Gemma 4 E4B), roughly a 940× cost reduction over three years.
- Narrower, structured-decision approaches (cited example: "JEV") reportedly achieve up to 193.6× faster and 444.6× cheaper performance than general-purpose LLMs on evaluated workflows by avoiding one-size-fits-all model use.
- Independent research trends (Stateful Swarms, Latent Reasoning, fractal embeddings) are all oriented toward driving down AI cost/compute needs through better reasoning infrastructure and information organization rather than bigger models.
- Simultaneously, five major cloud/tech companies (Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Oracle) are projected to spend ~$750B on capex in 2026 (~38% of their combined revenue), increasingly locked into multi-year contracts, debt, leases, and guarantees.
- This creates a structural tension: falling per-token costs vs. massive fixed infrastructure bets — the piece frames two scenarios (compute demand outgrows efficiency gains and pays off, or falling prices/shifting workloads cause assets to underperform expectations, triggering a correction).
- The piece frames the real analytical question not as "will AI boom" but as "who bears the risk when repricing happens" — drawing an analogy to how dot-com era winners and losers (Pets.com vs. Cisco vs. Amazon) diverged sharply despite the internet's overall success.

## Quote

> The same band of intelligence got roughly 940× cheaper.
