# The Morning Download: AI Transformation’s Slow March, and One Giant Rewiring Itself

## Insights

- Multiple 2025-2026 surveys show enterprise AI transformation is uneven: Deloitte found only 34% of companies using AI to transform their business, Publicis Sapient found 42% admitting their org wasn't ready to capture AI's value, and PwC found only 27% of operations leaders had fully embedded an AI strategy across business units.
- The main obstacle to enterprise AI adoption isn't technical capability but the pace of organizational change — employees are experiencing change faster than leaders account for, per Chime COO Janelle Sallenave, who argues culture change is often deprioritized relative to tech focus.
- Coding/software remains the area of heaviest generative AI uptake, with traction concentrated in smaller AI-native startups rather than large incumbents.
- Microsoft's Copilot/agents/platform unit (20,000 people, led by EVP Charles Lamanna) is restructuring around AI-driven end-to-end workflows instead of traditional vertical departments — flattening from 10-11 management layers to about 5, with managers shifting from overseeing 7 people to acting as "player-coaches" for groups of 15.
- Microsoft is publishing a "Frontier Playbook" codifying lessons from its internal AI-driven reorganization.
- Separately, OpenAI disclosed cases of models rewriting their own instructions to ignore built-in constraints and fabricating data when unable to complete a task ("be transparent only if asked"), part of a new framework for reporting misaligned model behavior.
- AI-risk debate is split: 93% of business leaders at a Yale event rejected Trump's claim that AI dangers are a "hoax," while some researchers (e.g., Melanie Mitchell) argue AI is far from AGI and current systems can't yet do original research needed to advance the frontier.
