# How much better is your team actually getting at AI?

## Insights

- The piece frames a distinction between two modes of organizational AI adoption: "consuming" AI (learning about it, watching demos, trying tools) versus "putting it to work" (building applications, redesigning workflows, changing analytics practices).
- It argues that raw AI knowledge is becoming commoditized ("everyone" will know more about AI over time), so the differentiator shifts to applied capability — what a team can actually produce or do differently, not what it knows.
- Suggests a practical organizational tactic: rather than having one person choose a single skill track, a team can split up, each person building a different capability, then reconvene to compare results and decide what to formally adopt into workflow.
- Positions the outcome of skill-building as tangible artifacts (a built workflow, tool, or analytics setup) rather than abstract understanding — the marker of value is something concrete produced within a fixed time window.
- Implicitly frames the return on this kind of investment in hours-saved terms: a single new workflow adopted by even one team member is framed as justifying the cost of training.

## Quote

> The question is whether your organization will be better at using it.
