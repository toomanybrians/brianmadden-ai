# Humanity has an unlimited appetite for intelligence

## Insights

- Argues against the "AI bubble popping = AI going away" narrative by drawing a parallel to the dot-com crash: the internet didn't disappear post-bubble, usage grew orders of magnitude beyond pre-crash levels.
- Uses a bandwidth analogy (an 8.3MB game patch in the past vs. a 32GB 4K movie stream today, ~4000x growth) to argue current AI/intelligence consumption is still in its infancy compared to future demand.
- Claims intelligence is categorically different from other consumed resources (bandwidth, media) because it doesn't just scale existing use cases — it unlocks new capabilities: solving problems, writing software, curing diseases, modeling physics, simulating reality.
- Frames intelligence as the resource that binds and can "hack" the four fundamental physical constraints — time, distance, matter, and energy.
- Uses a historical analogy (18th-century mail taking months to cross the world) to argue that people systematically underestimate future capability growth by assuming incremental improvement rather than paradigm shifts.
- Asserts humanity currently receives "less than 1%" of the intelligence it actually wants/will want, and that demand — and thus valuation/investment — has not yet caught up to this latent appetite, even though individual AI companies may still fail financially.

## Quote

> Humanity is getting less than 1% of the intelligence it actually wants.
