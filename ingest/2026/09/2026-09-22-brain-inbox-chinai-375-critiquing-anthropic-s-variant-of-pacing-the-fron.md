# ChinAI #375: Critiquing Anthropic’s Variant of Pacing the Frontier

## Insights

- Critiques Dario Amodei's "We Must Pace the Frontier" essay for diverging from the original multi-signatory "pacing the frontier" statement by adding a goal of maximizing U.S. AI lead over China, which most signatories didn't endorse.
- Points out a logical inconsistency: if slowing U.S. frontier labs would let China catch up via distillation or model theft (as Anthropic argues), then cracking down on distillation/theft should also slow China's pace if the U.S. slows down too — undermining the "must race ahead" logic.
- Notes that Anthropic/Dario's predicted timeline for when AI becomes "geopolitically most important" has repeatedly shifted later (2026 → 2028 → 2029-2031), despite claims of accelerating recursive self-improvement, which the author calls contradictory.
- Argues AI leadership should be understood as a "diffusion marathon" — how effectively a country adopts AI across economic sectors for productivity gains — rather than a single breakthrough moment, drawing on historical patterns of general-purpose technology adoption (predicting real productivity payoffs not until 2040s-2050s).
- Contends that meaningful U.S.-China AI safety cooperation is more likely to emerge from lower-level technical exchanges and industry associations (citing Cold War nuclear safety cooperation and aviation/nuclear industry associations as templates) than from top-down political agreements between leaders.
- Criticizes AI lab leaders and EA-aligned researchers for claiming moral authority on AI governance while being personally enriched and disconnected from those affected by their decisions, contrasting this with a biblical reference to inclusion of marginalized people.
