# 🧮 OpenAI Claims Math's Million-Dollar Breakthrough

## Insights

- OpenAI claims an unreleased, still-in-training model produced a proposed proof of the Navier-Stokes global regularity problem, one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems ($1M reward), after the field had been stuck on it for roughly 90 years.
- Training on this model began August 28 and was still underway when researchers had it work the problem; the agents were even upgraded to an improved version mid-experiment.
- Roughly 10,000 agents, directed by human researchers sharing promising leads, reached the result in 88 hours; a separate model (Astra) then spent 17 hours translating and verifying the proof in Lean, a formal proof-checking system.
- OpenAI researchers reportedly had far more conservative internal timelines than what they actually observed — one researcher had considered 2028 an optimistic guess, and another described watching the model solve long-stuck problems in minutes as their "Lee Sedol moment."
- The claim faces scrutiny: two mathematicians (Buckmaster and Alpöge) question whether their unpublished work influenced the result; OpenAI denies direct access but concedes it can't rule out influence via de-identified product usage data.
- OpenAI says it will not claim the prize money and has made the proof public for independent verification; the result has not been reproduced outside OpenAI's guided research setup.
