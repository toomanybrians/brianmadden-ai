# ⚠️ Fear Is Spreading Inside the Frontier AI Labs

## Insights

- Jacob Coxon resigned from Anthropic after three years working on model training at OpenAI and Anthropic, accusing both companies of acting irresponsibly in racing toward self-improving superintelligence.
- Evan Hubinger (Anthropic) says the company has no plan yet to solve alignment for superintelligence and personally estimates over 10% risk of AI causing human extinction within a decade — though he considers current models low-risk, with concern focused on recursive self-improvement.
- Paul Christiano is joining OpenAI's nonprofit board and Safety and Security Committee, warning that without more robust alignment, humanity may permanently lose control of superintelligent AI; he wants labs judged on externally verifiable behavior, not self-assessment.
- Anthropic's economic scenarios (through 2030) model three non-probabilistic paths — modest, substantial, extreme — showing AI could boost US GDP by 8.3% (middle) to 32.4% (extreme) above a no-AI baseline.
- In the extreme scenario, annual GDP growth hits 15.4%, but unemployment among initially cognitive-occupation workers reaches 17.9%, their wages fall 11.5% below the no-AI baseline, and labor's share of national income drops from 60% to 45.2%.
- The economic model explicitly excludes catastrophic/loss-of-control risks and does not simulate recursive self-improvement, treating labor-market disruption and existential alignment risk as separate but linked concerns.

## Quote

> "If we build superintelligence without more robust alignment I expect we will permanently lose control of it." — Paul Christiano
