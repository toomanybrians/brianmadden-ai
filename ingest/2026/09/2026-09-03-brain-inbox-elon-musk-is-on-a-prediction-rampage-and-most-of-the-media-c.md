# Elon Musk is on a prediction rampage, and most of the media can’t seem to figure out what to do about it.

## Insights

- Musk's April 2024 prediction claimed AI smarter than any human by "end of next year" (2025) and that AI compute would exceed all human compute within 5 years — neither has materialized, and Marcus notes Musk simply pushed the same prediction's date back to 2027 rather than revising the claim itself.
- Marcus argues AI still fails at basic tasks many humans handle reliably (not fabricating information) and at tasks only the most skilled humans can do (Pulitzer-caliber writing); of ten sample challenges posed to Miles Brundage in late 2024, roughly zero have been solved by AI since.
- Rodney Brooks, asked about Musk's humanoid-robot productivity predictions, called them a "hallucination," stating no deployed humanoid robot is even 1% as productive as a human, with negligible year-over-year improvement.
- The piece criticizes tech journalism (specifically The Information and TIME's Altman interview) for reporting CEO predictions uncritically without checking track records — contrasting this with political journalism's more adversarial norms.
- Cites a specific factual gap: Altman claimed thirteen months prior that GPT-5 could do anything a PhD could, a claim the interviewing journalist did not verify or challenge.
- Notes a few exceptions to uncritical tech coverage: The Economist's Zanny Minton Beddoes challenging Musk, and The New Yorker's Ronan Farrow challenging Altman.
