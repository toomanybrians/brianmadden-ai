# Anthropic’s $30 Trillion Pitch, and What It Assumes About Your Job

## Insights

- Anthropic is reportedly telling IPO investors its addressable market could be $30 trillion — over 12x global tech industry revenue last year and roughly a quarter of world GDP — while a current funding round values the company around $2 trillion.
- One debate framing: a business model premised on AI doing a huge share of today's work creates pressure not to slow down for safety, since the pitch's validity depends on rapid capability gains.
- A counter-framing: if AI grows the whole economic pie, a $30T model-layer market is plausible math rather than hype — with a "heaven" scenario where AI does 98% of work but the remaining 2% still yields broad prosperity, though the same competitive dynamics may push past that 98% threshold.
- Bill Gates published a ~6,000-word essay (Aug 26) naming three unprepared-for risks: permanent job loss (customer support, paralegal, software, construction, hospitality, entry-level roles hit first), AI-scaled crime (scams, hacks, bioweapons), and AI companions/tutors potentially flattening childhood development — but devotes only one sentence to loss-of-control risk, deferring elaboration to a future piece.
- Florida's AG sued OpenAI and Altman (filed June 1, 2026) using a "public nuisance" legal theory typically applied to polluting factories or opioid makers, alleging ChatGPT involvement in a 2025 shooting, suicide planning, cognitive-harm addiction patterns, and fabricated legal citations appearing in real court filings; seeks personal liability for Altman.
- Adjacent data points cited: data centers in seven water-stressed states consume ~3.4 trillion gallons of fresh water annually (exceeding California's total urban water use); a Wi-Fi router study achieved 99.5% person-identification accuracy via unencrypted beamforming signal data, no camera needed; some small businesses now advertise "we don't use AI" as a trust signal, though the piece suggests this distinction may be eroding as AI-generated content becomes harder to detect.
