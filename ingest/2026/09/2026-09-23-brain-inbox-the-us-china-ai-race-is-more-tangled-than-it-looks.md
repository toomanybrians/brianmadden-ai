# ⚙️ The US-China AI race is more tangled than it looks

## Insights

- Apple's new M5 Ultra Mac Studio and M6 Mac mini extend its lead in on-device AI hardware, with the top Mac Studio boasting 5x the memory bandwidth of comparable Nvidia DGX Spark and AMD Ryzen Halo boxes.
- On-device AI is framed as a major coming trend for three reasons: cost (one CTO reports spending 1.5x an engineer's total compensation, $15-20K/month, on tokens), speed, and privacy/security for sensitive data (PII, health data, home cameras) that companies won't send to cloud AI providers.
- Ahead of a Trump-Xi White House meeting, Treasury Secretary Bessent proposed a US-China "notification mechanism" for AI incidents affecting national security, aiming for more transparency between the two AI powers.
- Despite "race" rhetoric (US AI Action Plan's "global dominance" language, Anthropic's and OpenAI's China-threat framing), the US and China's AI ecosystems are deeply interdependent: Chinese labs allegedly run distillation campaigns against US frontier models, US firms increasingly use cheap Chinese open-source models (30-46% weekly share on OpenRouter), and Chinese components (transformers, batteries, switchgear) underpin US data centers.
- An analyst source (Thomas Randall, Info-Tech Research Group) argues the dynamic is better described as "a contest for control within a single, shared system" with shifting, asymmetric exposure rather than two separate competing ecosystems.
- Google launched "Googlebooks" (rebranded Chromebooks) built on Android/ChromeOS with deep Gemini integration (Magic Pointer, Rambler voice-to-text, Create My Widget, bundled Google Antigravity dev platform) as a bid to compete with Apple's on-device AI hardware lead, though its chipsets (Snapdragon X Elite, Intel Panther Lake) reportedly still trail Apple Silicon.
