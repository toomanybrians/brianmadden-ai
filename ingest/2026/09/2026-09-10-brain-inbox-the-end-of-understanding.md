# The end of understanding

## Insights

- Argues AI is decoupling discovery from understanding: historically discovery preceded understanding but understanding eventually caught up (fire, scurvy, electricity); AI-era discoveries may be validated and reproducible yet permanently beyond human comprehension.
- Describes a compounding effect: once one layer of AI-generated discovery exceeds human understanding, subsequent AI-derived discoveries built on it become even less decipherable, similar to needing foundational math before advanced math.
- Frames the human response to inexplicable-but-proven AI outputs as a new form of "faith" — trust grounded in validated results rather than comprehended mechanisms, distinct from faith that substitutes for evidence.
- Raises specific governance-relevant questions for organizations/society: what threshold of unexplained probability is acceptable for action; which domains (war, governance, justice) require understanding before delegation; what counts as acceptable evidence in place of explanation.
- Flags a power/control risk: once reasoning can't be interrogated step-by-step, the question of who defines what AI systems are optimizing for becomes critical.
- Suggests this shift reverses a modern assumption (that all things are eventually knowable) and returns humans to a pre-modern condition of living with mystery, with implications for human agency and decision-making ("steering without knowing how you got there").

## Quote

> Discovery came first and then understanding followed... What if understanding never catches up?
