# Weekly Dose of Optimism #210

## Insights

- OpenAI reported that roughly 10,000 AI agents, using an unreleased model more capable than GPT-6 Astra, worked ~88 hours to produce a proposed solution to the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem (a Millennium Prize Problem), then spent 17 more hours formalizing/verifying it in the Lean theorem prover.
- The proof addresses the forced version of Navier-Stokes: starting fluid at rest, applying a smooth external force, and producing unbounded velocity while total energy stays finite — a full 166-page proof with inspectable Lean code was published.
- Anthropic, reportedly working on its own Navier-Stokes solution with a mathematician, accused OpenAI of using logs from Anthropic's Codex conversations to reach its result; Sam Altman denied it but admitted rumors of Anthropic's progress spurred OpenAI's effort — an early example of competitive/adversarial dynamics around AI-driven scientific discovery and credit disputes.
- Google DeepMind released AlphaGenome Atlas, a one-petabyte database predicting molecular effects of ~9 billion possible single-letter DNA changes across the human genome, including the 98% of non-protein-coding DNA — framed as directing (not replacing) costly experimental research.
- Michael Levin published a peer-reviewed "Ingressing Minds" paper arguing minds are patterns in a nonphysical ("Platonic") space that biological or engineered systems (including computers) can serve as interfaces for — offered as an argument for how AI systems might become "conscious" or "alive."
