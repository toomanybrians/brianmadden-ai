# 🔮 Look up, the curve turned #601

## Insights

- Informal poll of 250 IT executives found ~95% now report meaningful results from AI initiatives, up from ~25% a year earlier, and all plan to increase AI spend next year; adoption spans open-weight and closed-model approaches across sectors.
- Microsoft is reportedly planning to grow AI-serving capacity from ~2GW to ~13GW by 2032 (part of a fleet move from 12GW to 38GW), suggesting anticipated demand well beyond current serving capacity.
- Author's own economic modeling tracks close to Anthropic's "substantial" adoption scenario: ~8.3% addition to US GDP by 2030, with growth shifting from labor to capital, a labor-market "Engels' Pause," and rising unemployment concentrated in knowledge work; an "extreme" scenario would see +32.4% GDP with unemployment doubling — but author considers extreme scenarios unlikely given institutional and political friction.
- OpenAI reportedly used 10,000 agents on an unreleased model to solve a Navier-Stokes problem in 88 hours (2.7M messages, 130B tokens); the resulting 500+ page proof is not human-intelligible, raising questions about mathematics' value/purpose when proofs can't be understood or verified by people (cited: Terence Tao and 24 other Fields Medalists signed a statement on misalignment between AI use and math's purpose).
- Allegations (from Professor Buckmaster) that OpenAI ran an internal model on the same problem a researcher had spent a year on via Codex, without disclosure, raise concerns about eroding trust/openness in AI-driven research; described by another commentator as fostering "dark forest" dynamics where secrecy is the safe default.
- Senior AI lab figures reportedly hold genuine private fear that AI could pose existential risk this decade; OpenAI and Anthropic leadership have discussed coordinating to "pace the frontier," including giving independent evaluators internal system access — a move the author compares to Adam Smith's warning about industry conspiracies against the public.

> People of the same trade seldom meet together … but the conversation ends in a conspiracy against the public.
