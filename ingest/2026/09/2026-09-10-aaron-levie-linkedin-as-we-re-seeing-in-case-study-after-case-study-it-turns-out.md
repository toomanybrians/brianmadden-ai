# As we're seeing in case study after case study, it turns out that the amount of

## Insights

- The value created between raw AI model capability and actual end-user workflows is larger than most people assumed — the "last mile" integration work is itself a major value driver, not just the model.
- Agent representation must vary by business process: sometimes a chat interface, sometimes a background agent embedded in a deterministic workflow, with many other variants depending on domain needs.
- Enterprise workflows each connect to different systems and data types (life sciences, financial, legal, etc.), requiring contextual, domain-specific approaches to data access and UX.
- Change management is domain-specific and non-trivial — deploying AI at a bank requires different talent, language, and process reengineering than at a law firm.
- Multi-model flexibility allows tuning workflows for cost/performance tradeoffs, and post-training models for specific tasks can extract gains beyond what frontier models offer out of the box.
- Domain-specific evals are treated as a core bottleneck: AI is "basically not useful" without evaluation, and the long tail of tasks makes a single universal eval system nearly impossible.
- Pricing models need industry-specific abstractions beyond simple token-based pricing to match how different verticals actually consume AI.

## Quote

> AI is basically not useful if it can't be evaluated.
