# The Jobs That Never Arrived

## Insights

- Since April 2023, finance/insurance/information/professional-business-services (FIIPB) employment has shed 700,000 jobs while the rest of the economy added 4.4 million — a divergence unprecedented outside recessions in 35+ years of data.
- Two independent counterfactual models (a 2005-2019 linear trend, and a GDP-based hiring model) each find over a million "missing" jobs in FIIPB — 1.5M vs. trend, 2.3M vs. what GDP growth would have predicted — with the gap opening entirely in the last 2.5 years, no recession involved.
- The shortfall is concentrated in professional/business services (software services, corporate HQ, back-office work), not finance or Wall Street; business support services (call centers, document processing) has lost over a quarter of its jobs since 2019 despite economic growth.
- The mechanism looks like hiring freezes rather than layoffs: JOLTS data show hiring rates well below pre-pandemic levels while layoff rates remain normal — roles simply aren't opening, attrition isn't backfilled, junior positions are quietly dropped.
- Author (labor economist) attributes this to AI, older automation, and offshoring combined — inseparable in the data — plus the *expectation* of better tools discouraging hiring now, occurring alongside record corporate profit share of GDP.
- Young college graduates are the first visible casualties: for the first time since 1996, 22-26 year olds with a bachelor's degree now have higher unemployment (6.1%, up 2 points since 2019) than those with only some college; author predicts the next recession will convert this gradual labor-saving into permanent job losses, as happened to manufacturing in 2001/2008.

## Quote

> Those jobs didn't disappear. They just never arrived.
