# Agents Promise to Remake the Web. As Instinct & Muse Take Off, We’ll Begin to See How it Looks.

## Insights

- Personal AI agents (Meta's Muse, startup Instinct) have moved from concept to mass-market consumer products almost overnight, with Instinct reportedly seeking a $10B valuation just after a $2.5B round, signaling investor conviction the category is a "Next Big Thing."
- The infrastructure and business norms of online commerce aren't built for agent-mediated transactions: agents get blocked by CAPTCHAs, Amazon has banned Muse outright, and restaurants are being "swarmed" by booking agents to the point of considering bans.
- A "consumer inertia" stock sector thesis emerged this week — banks, brokerages, carriers, OpenTable, DoorDash, and subscription media (NYT down 15%) all sold off on fears that agents will let consumers switch providers or bypass intermediaries frictionlessly.
- Partnerships (e.g., Shopify-Muse, Expedia-Muse) are proliferating but aren't necessarily protective — Expedia's stock fell anyway on disintermediation fears, suggesting integration deals don't neutralize the perceived existential threat.
- Deeper structural questions are unresolved: whether platforms will treat agents differently from humans, whether agents will need traditional intermediaries at all if they can access underlying data directly (e.g., via Amadeus/Sabre), and who controls the agent's choices on the user's behalf.
- The piece frames this as a potential power shift away from platforms toward individuals, though it treats the current market reaction (the "SaaS-pocalypse" for consumer inertia stocks) as likely overblown, since services are usually stickier than they appear.
