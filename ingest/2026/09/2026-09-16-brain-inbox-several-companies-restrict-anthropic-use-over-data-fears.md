# 🔒 Several Companies Restrict Anthropic Use Over Data Fears

## Insights

- Nvidia, Palantir, and Booz Allen are restricting specific uses of Anthropic's Fable model over data-retention concerns, not cutting ties with Anthropic entirely — the restrictions are scoped to sensitive work.
- The core dispute is over "zero data retention" (ZDR): Anthropic's current option lets eligible customers hold usage logs on their own servers, but the rollout is phased through fall 2026 and the guarantee can be revoked — customers want a permanent, irrevocable commitment instead.
- Anthropic's Fable required rolling 30-day usage logs for security purposes; this logging is separate from model training — both Anthropic and OpenAI say enterprise inputs/outputs and usage metadata are excluded from training by default unless customers opt in.
- One large utility abandoned a Fable trial for core power infrastructure specifically because it couldn't secure a permanent ZDR commitment; Nvidia now reserves Fable for lower-sensitivity work and uses its own Nemotron models internally for sensitive projects.
- Competitors are positioning themselves around this trust gap: Microsoft pitches isolated environments, Palantir sells itself as a protective layer between customers and model providers — both have commercial incentive to sell into the anxiety.
- The newsletter frames this as a broader structural tension: as AI labs increasingly sell applications (not just models) that compete with their customers' core business, data-control demands become a commercial/competitive issue as much as a security one.

> "A customer can accept a model's capabilities and still refuse to give it the information that makes it useful."
