# ChinAI #374: China’s first AI-generated longform TV series

## Insights

- Mango Excellent Media premiered "The Later Journey to the West" on Aug 31 in prime time — described as the first fully AI-generated entertainment on mainstream provincial satellite TV, with no live-action footage; all visuals generated by ByteDance's Seedance 2.0/2.5 models.
- Production took ~6 months from March planning to premiere, versus 1-2 years for traditional TV dramas; a ~100-person crew (including 15 concept artists) worked in "co-creation" with the AI rather than replacing human roles outright.
- Per-episode budget was ~900,000 RMB, with compute only ~1/4 of costs; teams had to ration compute budgets and cap generation attempts per scene, and the first two episodes required a full remake.
- A subtle character-design flaw (an incomplete tail on the adult-monkey model) caused the AI to fundamentally misread the character as "a human in prosthetics" rather than "an actual monkey," producing wooden expressions — showing how small spec details can cascade into large behavioral/logic errors in AI-generated content.
- Technical limits persist for longform AI video: subtle facial expression, complex movement, and consistency across long sequences remain weak; the production is framed by its creators as proof of feasibility, not profitability.
- Audience reception was mixed-to-negative in comments, with complaints that the show lacked "Eastern aesthetic" and felt too Western/3D-modeled, and comparisons unfavorable to lower-budget alternatives — despite the project reportedly driving a 13.3-billion RMB rise in Mango's market value in five days.
