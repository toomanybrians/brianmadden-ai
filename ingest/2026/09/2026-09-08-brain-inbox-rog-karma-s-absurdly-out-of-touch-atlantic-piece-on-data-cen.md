# Rogé Karma’s Absurdly Out-of-Touch Atlantic Piece on Data Centers

## Insights

- The piece critiques a Rogé Karma (The Atlantic) argument that anti-data-center activism is a "distraction" from the need for a global AI nonproliferation treaty aimed at catastrophic AI risks.
- Author argues AI companies' "please slow us down" statements are more plausibly a fundraising/hype tactic than a genuine safety plea, citing Big Tech's track record of exploiting or ignoring regulation.
- Contends that publicized AI "rogue behavior" incidents (e.g., UK AI Security Institute, OpenAI/Hugging Face) were actually failures of human oversight and weak safeguards, not autonomous AI action — media coverage anthropomorphized the models.
- Cites Gallup polling: 61% of Americans oppose a data center being built in their area, with top reasons being resource strain (50%), quality-of-life (22%), cost (20%), and pollution (16%); only 4% cited fear of AI/robot takeover.
- Disputes Karma's framing that "local environmental concerns" are analogous to the early anti-nuclear movement, arguing the anti-nuclear movement originated from mass-casualty weapons concerns, not local siting issues.
- Frames the broader critique as: elite AI commentators dismiss concrete community harms (water use, grid strain, noise, cost) as trivial compared to speculative catastrophic-risk narratives.
