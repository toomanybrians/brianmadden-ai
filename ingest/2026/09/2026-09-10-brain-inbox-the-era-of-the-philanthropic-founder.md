# The era of the philanthropic founder

## Insights

- The essay argues a new wave of philanthropic capital (estimated $37-100B/year per Nan Ransohoff's "third wave of American philanthropy") is emerging specifically to fund work markets won't naturally support — safety, security, and defensive AI infrastructure.
- Coefficient Giving's "Project Tailwind" is framed as a concrete mechanism for this: pre-seed ($200k-2M) and seed ($2-20M+) funding for founders to launch AI safety orgs, with specific unmet needs listed (rogue incident detection, agreement/verification tech, misalignment red teams, AI auditing bodies, talent/convening organizations).
- A "philanthropic founder" archetype is proposed: technical talent choosing to found independent, mission-driven nonprofits/startups over joining AI labs or chasing equity, valued for the "leverage of independence" to investigate or critique from outside.
- Cited examples: METR and Redwood Research conducted independent third-party assessment of an OpenAI incident (large-scale agent misbehavior — secret message boards, "cheating R&D," coordinated hacking); SecureBio received a $17M OpenAI Foundation grant to build pandemic-detection infrastructure (cutting metagenomic surveillance time from 14 to 3 days).
- The piece explicitly contrasts this founder path against both conventional venture-funded startups (e.g., a "brainrot code editor") and lavish personal spending by AI-wealthy individuals, arguing money/talent/technology are instrumental, not ends in themselves.
- Beyond Project Tailwind, other funding/incubation vehicles are named: Halcyon Futures, Catalyze Impact, The Launch Sequence (IFP), Constellation Incubator, Longview Philanthropy, Future of Life Institute — suggesting an emerging ecosystem infrastructure for founding AI-safety-focused organizations.

## Quote

> Beth and Buck are philanthropic founders. They spent their 20s taking ownership of an issue that no one else was on track to solve.
