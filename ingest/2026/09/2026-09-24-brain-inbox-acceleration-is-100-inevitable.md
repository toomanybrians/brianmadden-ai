# Acceleration is 100% inevitable

## Insights

- In summer 2026, frontier lab leaders (Altman, Amodei) publicly floated deliberately slowing AI capability development, framing it as giving "society time to harden" — but this coincided with requests for antitrust waivers on safety collaboration, which regulators (FTC Chair Ferguson, Treasury Secretary Bessent) publicly read as anticompetitive moat-building rather than genuine caution.
- A private Sherman Act antitrust suit was filed against OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and SpaceX/xAI alleging the labs illegally coordinated to slow down their own products (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok) — an unusual case where slowing AI itself is the alleged violation.
- Trump announced an "AI Force" (echoing Space Force) and pushed to rebrand "artificial intelligence" as "super intelligence" in official U.S. usage, explicitly rejecting any government-imposed slowdown and framing AI as bigger than the industrial revolution.
- International governance moved separately: 20+ countries (not the U.S. or China) launched a UN-adjacent "Call for Control of Frontier AI Models," seeking human oversight and a possible verification/standards body — non-binding, no pause.
- Polling shows a split: Democrats have become more AI-anxious than Republicans for the first time (56% vs 49% concerned), yet Gallup finds actual workplace AI usage rising regardless (52% of U.S. employees using AI at work, 66% in remote-capable roles) — stated concern isn't reducing adoption.
- Sanders/Casar introduced a bill to ban superintelligence development and create a federal "Department of AI," while political consultants are reportedly telling battleground candidates to avoid the AI issue entirely.
