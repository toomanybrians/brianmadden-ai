# ⚙️ AI slowdown talk is creating strange allies

## Insights

- Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published "We Must Pace the Frontier," proposing a three-part framework (including government intervention) to address AI risks, citing the OpenAI-Hugging Face breach as an example.
- Sam Altman told OpenAI staff he was open to slowing frontier AI development; Elon Musk also publicly agreed with calls to pace AI, producing unusual alignment among rival CEOs.
- Markets reacted negatively to slowdown talk: Intel fell 6%, Nvidia over 3%, SoftBank 10%, and ASML over 5%, reflecting investor fear that regulation or pacing would squeeze AI-related profits.
- The White House and China's government both pushed back against slowdown calls — Trump argued only "a strong and smart president" should regulate AI, while a Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs spokesperson called Amodei's essay "fearmongering."
- Steve Bannon and Bernie Sanders jointly appeared at a "Pro-Human Assembly" urging more AI regulation, illustrating left-right convergence on the issue even as tech and government leaders split along different lines.
- Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI reportedly discussed creating an AI industry standards body even before the latest controversy, an idea traced partly to Demis Hassabis' earlier safety essay; White House advisor David Sacks countered that Anthropic and OpenAI hold a "duopoly on frontier intelligence" and are calling for a slowdown from a position of dominance.
- The piece frames AI pacing as commonly presented as a binary (unfettered progress vs. slowing down to prevent catastrophic harm) and argues this framing is a false, overly simplistic choice that obscures a broad spectrum of non-mutually-exclusive policy options.
