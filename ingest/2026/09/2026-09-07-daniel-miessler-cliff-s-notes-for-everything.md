# Cliff's Notes for Everything

## Insights

- Draws a distinction between "moving work" (getting a task done, outcome-focused) and "lifting work" (doing the work itself for the experience/growth), using reading fiction as the clearest example of the latter.
- Argues that Cliff's Notes-style summaries give factual content but strip out the "grappling" or "struggle" with material that is where the actual human value lies.
- Proposes that AI functions like "Cliff's Notes for everything" — highly efficient, high-quality distillation that nonetheless is not equivalent to doing/experiencing the work directly.
- Raises an open question about AI-assisted work (e.g., coding with Claude Code): whether users are getting the "Cliff's Notes" version of the task or genuinely "enjoying the journey" of building something.
- Suggests the risk isn't AI efficiency itself, but people being confused about which mode (outcome vs. experience) they're actually in for a given task, and warns that this confusion is harmful.
- Frames this as an unresolved, still-developing line of thinking rather than a settled framework.

## Quote

> It's almost as if AI has become Cliff's Notes for everything.
