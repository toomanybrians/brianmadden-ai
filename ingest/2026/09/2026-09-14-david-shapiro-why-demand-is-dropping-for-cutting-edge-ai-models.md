# ⚙️ Why demand is dropping for cutting edge AI models

## Insights

- Ramp's monthly AI Index shows business AI adoption growth decelerating (56.1% of US businesses in August, up less than 0.5 points month-over-month) and per-employee AI spend falling nearly 10% ($7,976 to $7,205 among top adopters).
- Mid-tier, cheaper models (e.g., Claude Sonnet, GPT-5.6 Terra) are driving most enterprise usage growth, while frontier "heavyweight" models (Opus, Fable, Sol) fell to 45% of token share from a 53% peak, as some companies impose company-wide defaults favoring standard models for cost/performance reasons.
- Effective price per million tokens has dropped 41% to $0.68 from a 2026 peak of $1.15, even as top frontier models remain priced far higher ($10/million input, $50/million output tokens).
- Open-source and Chinese model adoption remains marginal in the enterprise (6.4% of AI-spending businesses), so falling costs are attributed to price competition among mainstream labs, not a shift to alternative model ecosystems.
- Separately, multiple AI safety researchers at Anthropic and OpenAI (Jacob Coxon, Evan Hubinger, Paul Christiano, Mrinank Sharma) have publicly warned that alignment/safety work is not keeping pace with model capability advances, with one estimating >10% odds of AI causing catastrophic harm within a decade.
- Apple is introducing passive, "always listening" AI features on Apple Watch (Live Rewind, Siri Recap) that transcribe/summarize conversations on-device, raising questions about consent and surveillance norms even as Apple emphasizes on-device privacy safeguards.

## Quote

> "We've heard from businesses who are imposing company-wide defaults that reduce usage of frontier models." — Ara Kharazian, Ramp
