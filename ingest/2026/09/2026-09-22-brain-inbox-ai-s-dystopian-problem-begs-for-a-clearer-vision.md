# ⚙️ AI's dystopian problem begs for a clearer vision

## Insights

- Surveys from Gallup and Pew show 60–70% of Americans hold negative views of AI; the piece frames this as a bigger problem for the industry than the safety debate itself.
- Argues the core issue is a "vision gap" — AI leaders haven't offered a compelling, believable narrative of benefit, only platitudes (curing disease, abundance, UBI) that don't land with the public.
- Claims people find dystopian narratives (AI-driven job automation by "billionaire business owners") more believable than utopian ones because they track with observed behavior, while positive futures require imagination people generally lack.
- Cites Dario Amodei's "Machines of Loving Grace" as an attempt at a positive vision (spreading expertise to poorer communities, gains in agriculture/education/infrastructure) but argues Amodei is too academic to be the needed "Steve Jobs-level" communicator; names Jensen Huang and Fei-Fei Li as closer to that role.
- Separately reports: Typesafe AI's new "Jev" model claims ~238x cheaper input tokens and two-orders-of-magnitude faster response times than GPT-6 Astra/Claude Fable 5.1 by replacing sequential generation with parallel computation — positioned as machine-to-machine (not chat) AI, aimed at enterprise workflows/agentic tasks rather than human-in-the-loop use.
- Notes broader enterprise cost pushback against "tokenmaxxing," with neolabs like Jev and Pathway challenging frontier labs' scaling-cost assumptions just as OpenAI and Anthropic pursue trillion-dollar IPOs.
