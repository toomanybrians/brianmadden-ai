# 📈 Monday data: More AI numbers, more clarity?

## Insights

- Analysis of S&P 500 Q2/Q3 2026 earnings calls finds only 33% of companies made any quantified AI statement, and just 15% quantified AI's actual business impact — up from 9% a year earlier, but still a small minority.
- Quantified claims skew toward cost/productivity gains (average ~47% boost, 24% of companies) rather than revenue/demand impact (average ~40% boost, 18% of companies), though both are rising at similar rates and the ranges are wide (20-60% and 20-83% respectively).
- Companies increasingly describe AI initiatives using "deployment" language (up from 7% to 12% since early 2025) while "pilot" language stays under 4% throughout — suggesting firms avoid publicly framing efforts as still experimental, which obscures how many initiatives are actually early-stage.
- Terminology is shifting fast: "agentic" mentions rose from 9% to 24% of calls in under two years while "generative AI" mentions fell from 13% to 5%; "copilot" stayed flat around 3%.
- Overall picture: AI adoption and self-reported measurement are both increasing but from a low base, leaving open whether this reflects early-stage rollout or that most companies simply don't yet have reportable results.

## Quote

> Take with a pinch of salt, and work with the ranges (20-60% for cost/productivity; 20-83% for revenue/demand).
