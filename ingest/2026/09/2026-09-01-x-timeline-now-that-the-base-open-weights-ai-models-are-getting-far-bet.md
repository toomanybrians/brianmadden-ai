# Now that the base open weights AI models are getting far better, and post traini

## Insights

- As open-weight base AI models improve and post-training infrastructure matures and commercializes, new business opportunities emerge for companies sitting on large proprietary datasets to build their own models.
- One such opportunity is licensing proprietary data to external model builders, rather than only building in-house.
- Thomson Reuters is cited as a concrete example: it built an LLM ("Thomson") starting from a Qwen3.5-397B base, then continually trained it on 175 years of proprietary legal, tax, accounting, and news data.
- The Thomson Reuters project reportedly cost $40M, illustrating the capital investment required even when starting from an open-weight base model rather than training from scratch.
- The framing suggests a shift where domain-specific proprietary data becomes a differentiator once base model capability is no longer the bottleneck.
