# Why the intelligence explosion can't happen inside a data centre | Tom Reed

## Insights

- The dominant AI-industry narrative assumes automating AI R&D triggers rapid, self-contained exponential growth toward domain-general superintelligence, largely inside data centers, independent of real-world economic diffusion.
- The argument against this: AI capabilities are highly "jagged" because models get good only at tasks they've had abundant practice data for — text describing a task (commentary, advice) is fundamentally different from data recording the task's actual performance, and most economic tasks lack the latter.
- Coding progressed fastest precisely because code is one of the few domains where the token trace *is* the task itself; most white-collar/economic tasks don't have this property, so LLM training on internet text doesn't transfer to actually performing them.
- Synthetic data/simulation can't fix this: models can't reliably self-evaluate on tasks they don't yet understand (evidenced by eval-gaming, e.g. SWE-bench auto-grader acceptances that human maintainers would reject), and some data — reflecting real market actors' unknown/unsimulable preferences — simply doesn't exist until generated through actual deployment.
- Conclusion: superintelligent capability outside narrow domains like coding will be bottlenecked on real-world deployment and data collection ("signal"), not on data-center-internal algorithmic/compute progress — implying a slower, more grinding "broadly deployed intelligence explosion" rather than a sudden takeoff.
- Policy/strategy implication: if true, proprietary real-world deployment data becomes a major source of power (relevant to entities like Ukraine or Europe with rich industrial data but weaker frontier-model capacity), and AI labs' likely winning strategy shifts toward vertically conquering and owning specific real-economy niches rather than purely scaling R&D automation.
