# Palmer Luckey: Autonomous Weapons Are Ancient and Why Anduril Won't Build Humanoids  | Moonshots Live | EP #295

## Insights

- Luckey argues autonomous weapons aren't a new Pandora's box — fully autonomous kill decisions (Aegis destroyer systems, CIWS/C-RAM, even Vietnam-era over-the-horizon missiles) have existed for decades; what's new is public attention, not the underlying capability.
- His core rationale for forward (on-device) autonomy over centralized C2: centralized command-and-control creates a single point of failure — jam/hack/destroy the central node and the whole distributed force collapses, versus degrading gracefully if individual units are autonomous.
- Anduril deliberately avoids humanoid robots, small quadcopters, and space launch — not from doubt in those markets but from a stated strategy of only building things "that wouldn't exist otherwise," ceding well-covered categories to other builders.
- He frames defense hardware as inherently anti-convergence: unlike consumer/auto industries that share platforms for efficiency, defense pushes toward extreme specialization (e.g., a submarine built only to dive deep vs. a generalized robot) because performance edges (even ~5%) justify dedicated design.
- On AI risk: dismisses "AI doom" as overweighted by newly-anxious commentators without deep threat-modeling history; his actual worry is competent-but-not-superintelligent AI being weaponized by "evil people," not autonomous AI itself causing harm.
- Predicts AI-driven economics will replicate the industrialization pattern (like agriculture/textiles) across all sectors — collapsing costs of resource extraction and processing broadly, not just automating white-collar tasks.
- On founder work in the AI era: argues AI accelerates technical/product work but doesn't touch the "bullertshit" core of founding — legal accountability, people management, fundraising, final-call decisions — predicting roughly a decade before AI meaningfully encroaches there.

## Quote

> I'm very, very worried about evil people using moderately competent AI to do evil things.
