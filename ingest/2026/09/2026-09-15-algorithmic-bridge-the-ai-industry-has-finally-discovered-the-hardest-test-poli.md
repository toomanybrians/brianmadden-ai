# The AI Industry Has Finally Discovered the Hardest Test: Politics

## Insights

- Dario Amodei's essay "We Must Pace the Frontier" calls for slowing AI capability gains until safety is better understood, citing AI's own role in accelerating its advancement and cases like the Hugging Face hack as evidence companies aren't fully in control.
- His proposed path has three stages: independent evaluators embedded inside AI companies (Anthropic and OpenAI have both committed to this), coordination among American/democratic-world labs, and eventual international coordination including China.
- Industry leaders (Altman, Musk, Hassabis) publicly backed the proposal, but government response diverged sharply: Trump rejected any slowdown ("whoever wins AI wins"), while Xi Jinping framed it as a "Cold War playbook" to preserve US tech hegemony and exclude China.
- The author argues the timing worked in Amodei's favor due to three converging factors: safety concerns going mainstream (referencing a resignation/tweet from an Anthropic employee), real-world harm from recent hacks, and the fact that frontier progress is currently dominated by just two American companies, making China's objections easier to dismiss domestically.
- The piece frames the standoff as industry (fearful, favoring caution) vs. governments (fearless, favoring competitive advantage), predicting the pacing proposal likely won't be adopted because political/national power incentives override it.
- The author states he takes Amodei's stated motives at face value as genuine even while acknowledging Anthropic's commercial incentives (investors, profit, upcoming IPO) — arguing sincerity and self-interest aren't mutually exclusive — but notes the plan still has flaws that Amodei himself recognizes.
