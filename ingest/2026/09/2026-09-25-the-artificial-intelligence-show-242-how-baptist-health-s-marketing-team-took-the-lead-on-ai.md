# #242: How Baptist Health’s Marketing Team Took the Lead on AI Transformation

## Insights

- Baptist Health's marketing/comms team began its AI transformation after a talk from an outside speaker triggered a "eureka" (or "holy bleep") moment — a recognition that generative AI represented a seismic shift to knowledge work, not just a new tool.
- The team built a deliberate, staged adoption model rather than a broad tool rollout: structured learning syllabus tied to performance evaluations, then peer support via designated "AI Sherpas," weekly learning labs, office hours, an AI council, and short pilots to decide whether a tool should go to everyone, a subgroup, or no one.
- Adoption speed varied by individual, and the traits that predicted early AI adoption (curiosity, entrepreneurial fearlessness, resourcefulness) didn't correlate with prior marketing or tech background — one leading "Sherpa" came from a restaurant entrepreneurship background.
- Concrete AI-driven changes: an online-listings-management workflow that took ~10 hours/week manually now takes ~2 minutes via an automated dashboard; creative services shifted to self-service templated design (Adobe Express) for account teams, reducing bottlenecks, freelancer costs, and internal team burden.
- Leadership initially misread employee resistance to AI as reluctance when it was often "pride of ownership" in craft/expertise; addressing this required visible leadership support, empathy, and framing adoption as a shared journey rather than pure mandate.
- In year two, friction shifted from literacy to integration: security/governance review layers in a regulated healthcare environment slow down tool connection work, creating tension with eager staff; the leader advocates bifurcating "slow" clinical AI governance from a "fast track" for corporate functions like marketing to retain motivated talent.
- Redesigning workflows and org structures around AI (vs. bolting AI onto existing human-centric roles) is described as harder than expected — the team is still working out where humans fit once workflows are redesigned agent-first rather than person-first.

## Quote

> "I think I misinterpreted reluctance and resistance... as that was actually pride."
