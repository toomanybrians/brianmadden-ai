# I Have Been Writing About AI For 6 Years: Something Changed This Summer

## Insights

- Scott Aaronson (quoted in the piece) argues a threshold has been crossed in the past year: skepticism about AI capability now requires more intellectual effort to sustain than acceptance does, given "front-page news" results.
- AI's impact is framed as radically uneven ("spiky") across multiple axes at once — access, compute, model quality, technical skill, willingness to delegate, domain familiarity — so two people can experience completely different realities of the same technology simultaneously.
- Erik Hoel notes there has been no "move 37" moment in writing (unlike Go/AlphaGo) six years after GPT-3, but has since shifted from confident skepticism to genuine uncertainty about whether traits like taste or creativity are truly resistant to AI, and has begun publicly supporting a ban on superintelligence.
- The OpenAI Navier-Stokes proof is cited as a case where AI produced a technically correct but largely unreadable 166-page solution that mathematicians say humanity has "learned very little" from — raising the prospect that AI could solve problems while erasing humans' role as sense-makers.
- DeepSeek kernel engineer Shengyu Liu's essay is used as a firsthand account of AI automating away work someone loved: he expects to keep a job but lose the specific craft (writing kernels) that gave his work meaning, describing this as a distinct grief from job loss itself.
- The piece pushes back on "post-work aristocrat" arguments (attributed to Alex Imas and Clara Collier) that meaning can simply relocate to leisure/relationships once work is automated, arguing this doesn't address people for whom work and identity were fused.

## Quote

> You're no longer being asked to believe in arguments and extrapolations, but only in the front-page news. — Scott Aaronson (as quoted by Alberto Romero)
