# AI researchers debate how close we are to recursive self-improvement

## Insights

- Researchers disagree on what would stop rapid recursive self-improvement (RSI): candidates include a persistent "sim-to-real" gap where benchmark gains don't translate to real-world productivity, models remaining bottlenecked by weak judgment/self-checking even as raw capability rises, or a repeating cycle where each new model feels like AGI briefly before its weaknesses become apparent.
- A key distinction raised: RL/distillation can rapidly optimize against a well-specified objective (e.g., lowering pre-training loss), but there's no clear mechanism for AI to generate genuinely new paradigms or objectives — "just thinking doesn't necessarily buy you the right objective in the first place."
- Chinese labs' models reportedly match or beat some Western frontier models partly through router/proxy services that collect real user prompts from blocked US models, giving them a realistic prompt distribution for distillation — argued to matter more than access to hard RL environments alone.
- Distillation is framed as a natural counterforce to market consolidation: RL-learned behaviors are "a small number of bits" and easy to copy once you have realistic trajectories, so frontier labs can't easily maintain durable moats even with continual learning.
- The panel expects the "last job for humans" in AI R&D to be defining objectives/values (constitutions, model specs, what "helpful" means) rather than technical execution, since that requires judgment calls that resist full automation.
- One proposed future training loop: automated AI researchers trained on a mix of verifiable tasks (e.g., beating a game, hitting a loss target), human/LLM-judged fuzzier tasks, and continuous "diffing" of the last few months of human-found bugs/improvements turned into new training environments — raising the question of whether this can ever fully exclude humans from the loop.

## Quote

> Just thinking doesn't necessarily buy you the right objective in the first place.
