# Pluralistic: Itch scratching (25 Sep 2026)

## Insights

- Draws a distinction between "scratching your own itch" (self-built tools, from bash scripts to Filemaker databases) and having someone who cares about you build/tailor a tool for you — argues the latter, done well, beats pure DIY.
- Treats vibe-coding as a direct descendant of the low-code/no-code tradition (Logo, Hypercard, Visual Basic, Scratch): valuable specifically for people building tools for their own personal use, where imperfect/inefficient code doesn't matter because they can just redo it if it breaks.
- Flags a real risk: non-programmers who vibe-code apps for themselves may be unable to assess the code's quality, creating security holes or unintended exposure of sensitive data.
- Explicitly rejects vibe-coding for building tools *for others to use*, distinguishing "building for yourself" (low stakes, iterative) from "building for other people" (requires expertise, care, accountability).
- Frames the current backlash against "slop code" (floods of low-quality AI-generated PRs/bug reports in open source) as a breakdown of the traditional care/reciprocity relationship between developers and users, not merely an efficiency complaint.
- Argues genuine expertise (a contractor, a skilled craftsperson, an attentive open-source maintainer) can surface needs a person "didn't even know they had" — something self-directed tool-building or self-service AI generation can't replicate.

## Quote

> Slop's not merely annoying or time-wasting: it's a betrayal of the love and care that goes into writing and maintaining code for others.
