# How Accurate Have AI Progress Forecasts Been So Far?

## Insights

- Across FRI's forecasting studies since mid-2022, both domain experts and superforecasters have systematically and dramatically underestimated AI progress on capability benchmarks (math, coding, IMO gold, FrontierMath, LiveCodeBench, METR time horizons) — the underestimation pattern holds from 2022 forecasts through spring 2026 ones, suggesting forecasters aren't correcting even as they gain more data.
- AI economic adoption metrics (company revenue, ARR) have been underestimated even more severely than capability benchmarks — e.g., forecasters predicted $20-25B ARR for a leading AI company by end of 2026, while actual figures were tracking toward $100B+.
- Notable exceptions to underestimation exist: forecasters overestimated autonomous vehicle adoption rates (predicted 7.3% of US ride-hailing trips by 2027 vs. ~2.5% projected) and overestimated how much LLMs would help non-experts complete dangerous biology lab tasks (predicted 22-40% completion vs. actual 5.2%).
- Forecasts on "resource" proxies for AI growth — electricity/data-center buildout, price of compute — have tracked much closer to accurate than benchmark or adoption forecasts, suggesting infrastructure trends are more predictable than capability jumps or usage/revenue growth.
- The methodology itself is structurally biased toward finding underestimation: it's easy to confirm a forecast was "too low" once reality crosses it, but confirming a forecast was "too high" requires waiting for the resolution date to fully pass, so overestimates are systematically harder to detect early.
- One interpretive theory offered: benchmarks may be easier to "teach to the test" than forecasters assumed, meaning fast benchmark progress might not translate to equally fast real-world capability or impact — implying benchmark underestimation and real-world-impact forecasting accuracy could diverge.

## Quote

> Superforecasters assigned an average probability of just 9.7% to the observed outcomes across these four AI benchmarks, compared to 24.6% from domain experts.
