# Wisconsin versus AI Goliath Part III: If Not Here, Then Where?

## Insights

- Sam Altman, in a TIME interview, framed data center siting as a matter of finding "land that people don't have much use for" in sparsely populated areas, arguing results can be moved anywhere so location flexibility solves community opposition.
- Author reports firsthand accounts from two Wisconsin towns (Wisconsin Rapids, Port Washington) where residents formed opposition groups; complaints escalated from basic questions about utility costs and noise to residents being ignored, laughed at, arrested, or threatened.
- Environmental impact reports were reportedly avoided, leaving residents without answers on how data centers affect utility bills, noise, water, and local ecosystems before construction proceeds.
- Companies promise local hiring and reinvestment, but the piece claims data centers (built by Vantage, Oracle, OpenAI, and others) aren't hiring locally even for construction jobs, contradicting community-benefit messaging.
- "Closed-loop" water cooling systems are characterized as a PR term rather than proven technology; cites a Meta data center in Cheyenne, Wyoming where a closed-loop system reportedly contaminated the city water supply with bacteria after a fill-and-flush process.
- Broader pattern described: local/state/federal governments removing regulatory barriers to fast-track data center construction (e.g., the Stargate Project) before environmental and safety impacts can be studied, leaving resource-use consequences (energy, water) at an unprecedented and poorly understood scale.

## Quote

> There's a lot of places with not many people around, and land that people don't have much use for. — Sam Altman
