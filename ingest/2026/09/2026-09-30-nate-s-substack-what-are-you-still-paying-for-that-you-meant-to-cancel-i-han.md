## Insights

- The author used Meta's new AI assistant, Muse, to audit personal subscriptions, which identified $5,350/year in recurring spend and canceled $1,285/year of it so far, framing this as "future spending avoided" rather than a refund.
- Muse reached #1 on the US free iPhone chart ten days after its September 8 launch, which the author argues signals AI adoption driven by concrete task value rather than technical interest (e.g., model benchmarks).
- The piece frames unreviewed subscriptions as a business model: companies profit from customer inaction/inertia, and an assistant that actively manages purchasing decisions disrupts that dynamic.
- Amazon reportedly blocked Muse from its store, which the author connects to Amazon's $68.6B advertising business being built on controlling the point of purchase decision.
- The author raises a trust/incentive concern: an assistant offered for free needs another revenue source, and if that source is monetizing purchase recommendations, it could shape the "advice" the assistant gives — the same assistant that helps you cancel subscriptions may later be positioned to help you buy.
