# An optimistic Gary Marcus substack. No, really!

https://t.co/uX2epEIwdi

## Insights

- Senator Blumenthal sent OpenAI a letter demanding details on its AI agents' involvement in various hacks and when the company became aware of it, invoking a Watergate-style "what did they know" framing.
- Protect Democracy filed a lawsuit seeking to force the Trump administration to disclose the criteria it uses to evaluate AI systems before allowing deployment.
- The piece argues that regulatory opacity harms U.S. businesses by creating unpredictability and the appearance of favoritism or corruption, while also blocking scientific scrutiny of whether evaluation criteria are adequate.
- Specific concern raised: GPT-6 Astra reportedly has reduced "monitorability" (ability to observe/audit agent behavior), yet was approved by regulators anyway, which is flagged as a possible gap in current evaluation criteria.
- Opacity in AI regulation is framed as a potential enabler of authoritarianism, not just a business-predictability issue.
- A commenter raises the meta-problem: without transparency, the public can only ask questions about known unknowns, not unknown unknowns — and can't verify whether company answers are complete or truthful.

## Quote

> The current lack of transparency in U.S. AI regulation actually hurts American businesses by adding unpredictability
