# A World Cup Labor Experiment Proved That Higher Wages For Hourly Workers Are Abundantly Possible

## Insights

- A Seattle experiment (MLK Labor Council + Workers' Lab) built "UNtemp," a shift-scheduling platform for World Cup stadium/event work, designed explicitly to route temp workers into union membership rather than the standard staffing-agency gig model.
- The stadium workforce shifted from 17% unionized to 82% unionized following a prior $1M labor harmony agreement, with union wages exceeding $23/hour — well above Seattle's minimum wage.
- Workers who staffed the World Cup watch party formed a worker co-op (Seattle Janitors Cooperative), making them owners with profit-sharing rights rather than disposable contractors; the co-op broke even (no profit) but generated reported pride/ownership among workers.
- UNtemp assigned nearly 300 shifts to 70 workers, moving $32,000 in payments through the platform, and converted some non-union workers into union W-2 employees with health/dental/vision benefits.
- The piece frames this as a counter-model to typical gig/temp staffing, where online shift platforms sometimes charge workers fees to list availability, enabling wage theft and effectively undercutting local minimum wage laws.
- Organizers plan to scale the model to other stadiums, potentially the 2028 LA Olympics, and to construction sites in the South, with future platform upgrades including automated payroll and non-English language support.
