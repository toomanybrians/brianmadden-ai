# Daily Briefing: September 3, 2026

## Insights

- OpenAI's ChatGPT Health integration with Epic went live with read-only clinician access covering 325M+ patient records; the Pentagon has 1.7M of 3M DoD personnel already enrolled in ChatGPT/Gemini access — both framed as full rollouts, not pilots, with AI sitting between worker and system of record while the underlying database/backend stays untouched.
- A five-year self-tracked writing log (Tomasz Tunguz) shows edit volume held flat (median 136 line edits/piece) even with AI drafting alongside nightly, but quality rose — weakest pieces improved nearly twice as fast as best pieces, suggesting AI raises the floor more than the ceiling.
- Token costs fell from $2.07 to $0.97 per million tokens (May–August) and new budget-tier models beat a shortlist published just five days earlier — capabilities-up/costs-down trend continuing with no sign of slowdown.
- OpenAI is cutting off Cursor's model access on November 12 following SpaceX's acquisition of Cursor — a case of model access revoked for competitive/ownership reasons unrelated to price, safety, or capability, raising portability risk beyond data portability alone.
- OpenAI rated its upcoming Astra model "Critical" for cybersecurity risk (perfect ExploitBench score, two unassisted zero-days found and chained); part of the capability gain reportedly comes from "recurrent depth," a technique that loops computation internally rather than producing readable chain-of-thought, though OpenAI says it limited the technique to preserve legibility in this case.
- Goldman Sachs reportedly flags law, consulting, and accounting as most at-risk professional services not because AI replaces firms, but because AI-fluent junior staff are already outperforming senior partners who haven't adopted the tools — described as an inversion of the seniority ladder rather than a generic middle-compression.
- A cited Harvard physics tutor study found AI tutors that personalize and sequence problems based on prior student work more than doubled learning gains, a larger effect than MOOCs achieved, attributed to personalization MOOC content lacked.
