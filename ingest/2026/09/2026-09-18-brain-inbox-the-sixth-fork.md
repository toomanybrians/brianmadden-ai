# The Sixth Fork

## Insights

- Diamandis frames AI adoption as a binary "fork": despite GPT-3.5-level query costs falling ~280x (from $20 to $0.07 per million tokens, Nov 2022–Oct 2024), a Pew survey found 65% of American workers report barely using AI in their jobs — suggesting a widening gap between tool accessibility and actual usage.
- He adds a sixth "fork" to his original five (Creators vs. Consumers, Longevity Escape Velocity, Brain-Computer Interfaces, Earth vs. Stars, Digital Consciousness): genetic optimization, covering embryo screening and gene editing.
- Embryo screening is already commercial: Nucleus Genomics analyzes IVF embryos across 2,000+ conditions/traits, reporting that selecting among five embryos could cut predicted relative risk for Type 1 diabetes ~67%, Alzheimer's ~55%, breast cancer ~42%, and shows a ~10.8 IQ-point spread between highest- and lowest-scoring embryos — predictions, not measured outcomes.
- Gene editing has moved into approved medicine (FDA-approved CRISPR treatment for sickle cell disease), and a gene-editing treatment tailored to an infant's specific mutation was developed and delivered in roughly six months in 2025 — illustrated as evidence of increasingly precise biological intervention.
- Diamandis raises the equity concern directly: if genetic advantages become available only to families who can afford them, inequality could become "embedded in biology," while still stating his own lean toward pursuing genetic optimization for his children.
- He proposes a three-question framework for each "fork" (where do I stand today; what one action moves me toward my preferred side; who is already navigating this well) and is promoting a "6 Forks Future Readiness Assessment" tool tied to this framing.
