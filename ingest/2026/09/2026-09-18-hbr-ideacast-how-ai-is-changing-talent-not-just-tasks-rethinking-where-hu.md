# How AI Is Changing Talent, Not Just Tasks: Rethinking Where Human Judgment Matters Most

## Insights

- Paula Goldman (Salesforce's chief ethical and humane use officer) argues there's a current opportunity to redesign work around complementary strengths of humans and AI, rather than just using AI to cut costs.
- The piece frames "human in the loop" as an outdated model for AI oversight, proposing instead that humans should be "at the helm" — a shift in framing from passive review to active direction.
- AI is expected to reshape entry-level jobs specifically, which raises questions about how future leaders get developed if the traditional junior-role training ground changes or disappears.
- Companies are urged to rethink internal mobility and talent development pipelines as AI absorbs more routine tasks, rather than treating job redesign as an afterthought.
- Leaders need explicit criteria for deciding which tasks to delegate to AI versus where human judgment remains essential, framed as a strategic (not just operational) decision.
- Goldman warns that treating AI primarily as a cost-cutting tool risks organizations missing its larger potential for restructuring how work and leadership function.
