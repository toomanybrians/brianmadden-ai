# Muse Is Asking for Everything

## Insights

- Meta launched Muse, a personal AI agent (web, iOS, Android, WhatsApp) that connects to email, calendar, payments, health apps, smart home, and shopping, running in its own virtual machine — a broader personal-data footprint than any prior Meta consumer product, arriving two weeks after an $18B settlement over social media claims.
- OpenAI reports its research group now runs 3.1 "agent-workdays" per human workday, meeting a goal Sam Altman set the prior October — but OpenAI itself defined the metric, measured it, and graded it, and more than half of successful long agent runs still required human intervention.
- OpenAI's next stated target is a fully autonomous AI researcher by March 2028, a goal the company says it doesn't yet know how to reach safely.
- At IFA Berlin, humanoid robots from Chinese firms (Unitree, MagicLab) mostly performed novelty acts (dancing, backflips, sandwich assembly); the one robot with an actual industrial job — Xiaomi's CyberOne — fastens nuts on an EV line at 98% accuracy, while XPeng started up a separate humanoid production line in Guangzhou targeting mass output by year-end.
- Chip supply and AI demand are consolidating: Qualcomm gave Amazon a 25-million-share warrant tied to Amazon committing up to $60B in Qualcomm hardware purchases for AWS custom inference silicon, while Mistral raised a record €3B (Europe's largest equity round) led by Samsung, with Nvidia already a partner — vendors increasingly hold equity stakes in their own customers.
- Quick hits: The Intercept published FOIA documents showing OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and xAI each hold $200M-ceiling Pentagon AI contracts from July 2025, including embedded engineers and reported use of Anthropic technology in target identification for Iran airstrikes; separately, Anthropic quit the ITI trade group after it lobbied to strip chip export-control provisions from the defense bill.
