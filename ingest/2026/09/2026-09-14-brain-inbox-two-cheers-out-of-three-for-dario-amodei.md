# Two cheers (out of three) for Dario Amodei

## Insights

- Dario Amodei's essay proposes "pacing" AI development, endorsed by Sam Altman and Elon Musk, with Marcus supportive in principle but skeptical of intent — suspecting it may be pre-emptive positioning against real regulation rather than genuine self-restraint.
- Amodei's preferred mechanism is regulation targeting all frontier AI companies plus "embedded evaluators" partnering with government; critics (e.g., Angela Rasmussen, Gillian Hadfield) argue this "partnership" framing lets companies retain influence over the regulatory process rather than submit to independent oversight.
- Amodei's reliance on the evaluator group METR is flagged as a potential regulatory-capture risk, since METR is described as closely tied to the AI industry itself, undermining claims of independent evaluation.
- The essay's use of China as a competitive threat is seen as self-serving — a rhetorical device to justify continued acceleration despite safety rhetoric, and as hypocritical given Anthropic's own reliance on distilling others' work.
- Marcus argues "pacing" sidesteps two more direct policy tools: liability/criminal accountability for companies deploying poorly-controlled AI agents, and product recalls for harmful AI systems already in use.
- Despite the criticism, Amodei's call for transparency is highlighted as the strongest and most credible part of the proposal, aligning with long-standing demands from researchers like Deb Raji, Meg Mitchell, and Timnit Gebru.
