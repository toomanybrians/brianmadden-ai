# The Boy Who Cried AI

## Insights

- The author describes AI criticism as having become so pervasive and indiscriminate that it's difficult to distinguish rigorous scientific argument from generalized cultural grievance.
- A pattern of treating "every new model" as dangerous and "every new capability" as a crisis leads to warning fatigue, where distinct concerns blur into an undifferentiated backdrop of alarm.
- The author admits to a personal coping response: discounting nearly all AI warnings wholesale, including ones that might be legitimate — a direct cost of alarm oversaturation.
- The piece frames this dynamic through the "Boy Who Cried Wolf" fable: the moral isn't that the threat is fake, but that repeated false or exaggerated alarms erode the credibility needed when a real threat arrives.
- The author explicitly states they are not an AI expert and are struggling to determine which voices in the AI-risk discourse to trust.

## Quote

> The lesson of The Boy Who Cried Wolf isn't that wolves don't exist. It's that false alarms destroy credibility.
