# The Harness Margin Opportunity

## Insights

- A Berkeley study (HarnessTax, 21 model-harness pairs across SWE-bench Lite and Terminal-Bench 2.0) found that swapping the "harness" — the orchestration system controlling an AI agent — around the same model can cut cost per resolved task by up to 71% with no accuracy loss (one measured case: GPT-5.6 Sol on Claude Code vs. Pi, $1.540 to $0.441 per task).
- Harnesses work by compressing repeatable workflow steps into deterministic code or skills, reserving expensive state-of-the-art model calls only for steps that actually require them.
- In a hypothetical example, two startups bidding on the same $250k AI-associate contract diverge sharply in gross margin (38% vs. 75%) purely based on harness efficiency, despite identical revenue and non-inference costs.
- The cost advantage from a good harness is inseparable from a data/knowledge moat: knowing which tasks can safely route to cheaper models requires having observed thousands of prior task executions — competitors can't easily copy this.
- Margin efficiency compounds into growth capacity and product usability: the inefficient competitor must ration usage as volume grows (becoming a worse product exactly when demand is highest), while the efficient one can say yes to everything and recoup customer acquisition cost roughly twice as fast.
- The author frames effective harnesses as requiring three ingredients: deep customer/task understanding, a relevant evaluation suite, and an automated system for iterative improvement ("hill climbing") — positioned as the basis of a new category of defensible software companies.
