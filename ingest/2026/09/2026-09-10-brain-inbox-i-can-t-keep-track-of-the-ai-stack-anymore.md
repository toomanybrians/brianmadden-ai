# I can’t keep track of the AI stack anymore

## Insights

- The AI technology stack (chips/infrastructure → models → software/applications) is breaking down as companies expand aggressively across layers instead of staying specialized, unlike the traditionally more stable martech landscape (which grew in product count but kept clearer category lines).
- The underlying driver: it's still unclear which layer of the AI stack will become the most valuable or defensible business, which layer will commoditize, or where real points of control lie — so companies are hedging by expanding into adjacent layers rather than committing to one position.
- Nvidia is cited as the clearest example: historically an infrastructure/GPU company, it now spans chips, networking, platform software, and cloud services, and has moved further up the stack by acquiring Hugging Face (a developer platform for discovering/sharing/deploying models) and building its own model family (Nemotron).
- This blurring means traditional company categorization ("chip company," "software company," "model maker," "developer platform") is becoming less meaningful — companies increasingly occupy multiple roles simultaneously.
- The piece frames this as a structural land-grab dynamic across the industry, not limited to any single firm — an implicit signal that value/control in AI is still up for grabs at every layer.
