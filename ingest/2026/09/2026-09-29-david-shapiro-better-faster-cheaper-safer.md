# Better, Faster, Cheaper, Safer

## Insights

- Shapiro proposes "better, faster, cheaper, safer" (BFCS) as a litmus test for labor substitution: historically, price/speed/quality alone haven't been enough to displace human labor, but when a technology surpasses humans on all four dimensions simultaneously, substitution becomes economically inevitable.
- He reframes the classic "good, fast, cheap — pick two" business tradeoff, arguing AI breaks the tradeoff because models/agents can run in parallel around the clock, delivering higher quality and volume without the usual time/cost sacrifice.
- Gives a concrete example: an AI-animated video cost him ~$2 in tokens versus a quoted $10,000–$40,000 for human animators — a claimed ~5,000x cost reduction with comparable or higher quality, completed in an afternoon.
- Introduces "cognitive hyper-abundance" — the idea that expertise (doctors, lawyers, engineers, developers, researchers), historically scarce and expensive, is becoming cheap and abundant via AI, echoing Sam Altman's "intelligence too cheap to meter."
- Argues "safer" isn't just physical safety — it includes capital risk, PR risk, legal exposure, and financial risk; businesses are fundamentally risk-averse, so machine safety/precision (e.g., automated oversight in rocket launches) is a major adoption driver alongside cost and speed.
- Clarifies the actual bar for substitution is low: machines only need to beat humans, not some abstract ideal — and while some jobs (childcare, live music) may retain an irrational human premium, these niche exceptions won't sustain a broad labor market.

## Quote

> Every person, business, and government will make the locally economically rational choice to go for the better, faster, cheaper, safer option.
