# Uber’s About-Face

## Insights

- Uber, facing lagging autonomous vehicle progress and losing ridesharing/delivery share to Waymo, has reversed decades of anti-labor positioning to partner with taxi unions, backing bills (e.g., in New Jersey, Washington DC) mandating "hybrid networks" of human drivers alongside AVs.
- The company is simultaneously promoting slower AV rollout to protect driver wages in the US while touting its robotaxi launch in London as beating Waymo — a contradiction the piece frames as strategic timing/self-interest rather than genuine concern for workers.
- An ethics professor (Alison Taylor, NYU Stern) is quoted arguing this reversal creates reputational risk given public distaste for corporate flip-flopping and Uber's long history of anti-union mistrust, which complicates any real cooperation with labor.
- Same week as the union-friendly pivot, Uber laid off 10% of its workforce and mandated near-universal return-to-office, with shares rising on the layoff news — illustrating that shareholder value, not worker welfare, is described as the underlying driver of all these moves.
- The piece situates Uber's current comms strategy as managed by exec Jill Hazelbaker, framing the company's "maturation" narrative since Kalanick's departure as a PR veneer rather than a genuine culture shift, citing a related NYT report on aggressive/victim-blaming legal tactics in sexual assault litigation as evidence of unchanged underlying ethos.
- Notes that Uber's former public policy head now works for Waymo, illustrating fluid alliances between AV companies, ridesharing platforms, and organized labor as AI-driven automation reshapes competitive and political battle lines in the industry.
