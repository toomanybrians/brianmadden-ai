# The AI Industry and Its Allies Aren’t Serious About Slowing Down

## Insights

- Investigative reporting (The Intercept) on Pentagon contracts with Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and xAI suggests these labs' personnel are more deeply integrated into DoD AI development than previously disclosed, raising doubts about contractual limits labs claim to place on military use of their systems.
- The American Prospect reported Anthropic has built an extensive monitoring system to track activists opposed to rapid AI development — a safety-focused lab simultaneously surveilling its critics.
- A researcher (Coxon) who briefly worked at both OpenAI and Anthropic quit publicly, warning both companies are "racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives"; an Anthropic colleague publicly agreed, estimating >10% chance AI kills all humans within a decade, while admitting no alignment plan for superintelligence exists yet.
- Despite viral warnings (155M+ impressions), no coordinated policy response emerged — political reactions split along existing lines (a Sanders superintelligence ban bill with no passage chance vs. vague "someone must act" statements), while lab leaders (Altman, Amodei) stayed silent and, per the author, are expected to avoid any real cross-lab or US-China cooperation.
- "China is racing ahead" is used by figures like Sen. Ted Cruz to justify no US regulation/slowdown, while critics argue that framing is never defined in concrete terms and forecloses any US-China dialogue on AI safety.
- Author frames a recurring pattern: frontier labs and their leaders discuss AI risk/opportunity in abstract terms (probability estimates, GDP growth scenarios) rather than concrete terms, which conveniently insulates them from accountability when real harms or whistleblower claims surface.

## Quote

> They are racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives.
