# I distilled myself, and you should too

## Insights

- The author "distilled" himself into a portable skill/agent (published on GitHub) with an automated pipeline that continuously updates the distillation based on his public writing, talks, and work, so others can query his judgment, workflows, and stories.
- His stated motivation: his AI agents were bottlenecked on his personal judgment calls, constantly escalating decisions or making choices he'd have to correct — distillation was meant to encode his judgment so agents could act autonomously without him in the loop.
- He argues a distillation only captures the "repeatable"/documentable subset of a person (what can be written into markdown); his actual competitive advantage ("moat") lies in undocumentable things — how he acquires knowledge, derives new insight, and communicates, plus the trust others place in him.
- Framing: offloading the repeatable/distillable parts of oneself to AI is not a threat but a way to free up time to strengthen the non-distillable, harder-to-replicate parts of one's value.
- As a demo, he built a general-purpose "distillation" bot (via a one-click-importable Grok Bot) that can distill any public figure from their interviews/writings, then ran a simulated multi-agent debate among distilled versions of Sam Altman, Elon Musk, Dario Amodei, and Mark Zuckerberg on AI safety/access, which converged on a negotiated consensus statement about ordinary access, safety bars, and not releasing the most dangerous capabilities unrestricted — illustrating how distilled personas can be used for synthetic multi-perspective simulation/debate.

## Quote

> My moat is in the way I go about acquiring more and more knowledge about the world... My moat is in the people who trust me.
