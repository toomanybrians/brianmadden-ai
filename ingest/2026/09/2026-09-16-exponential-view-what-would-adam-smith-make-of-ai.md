# 🔮 What would Adam Smith make of AI?

## Insights

- Azhar constructs "R Adam Smith," an AI persona trained on Smith's writings and current AI debates, to interview about AI — an experiment in using AI to simulate historical thinkers' perspectives on AI itself.
- The simulated Smith argues AI differs from his pin-factory model of specialization: rather than narrowing a worker's task, AI broadens one person's access to many specialties at once (design, accounts, correspondence, sales), potentially letting more people become producers.
- The simulated Smith's proposed test for whether AI is a genuine improvement: does it make goods cheaper, open trades to newcomers, and let ordinary wages buy more — abundance for common households as the measure of value, not efficiency alone.
- Azhar predicts (before running the simulation) that Smith would embrace AI as a productivity tool, distrust industry collaboration/coordination among AI companies, and worry about extending moral agency to machines.
- On AI labs (e.g., Anthropic's Dario Amodei) lobbying government to slow AI development, the simulated Smith suggests such appeals may be sincere but are not free of self-interest.

## Quote

> Sincerity does not abolish self-interest.
