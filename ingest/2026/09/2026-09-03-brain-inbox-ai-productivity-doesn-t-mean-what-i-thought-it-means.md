# AI Productivity Doesn't Mean What I Thought It Means

## Insights

- The author expected AI-assisted writing to reduce human effort toward zero, but found instead that AI raises the ceiling of output quality for the same amount of work — it doesn't shrink effort, it redirects it.
- His writing workflow is an iterative human-AI loop: the model drafts, he issues line-by-line editing instructions rather than touching raw text directly, and the system updates a personalized style guide nightly based on his edits.
- Despite this workflow, editing volume stayed flat — a median of ~136 line-level sentence edits per piece — showing AI didn't reduce editing work, only changed its nature.
- To test whether quality improved, he built an AI scoring rubric and had an AI panel rate 15 random published posts per year (2021–2026) on a 1–5 scale.
- Quality scores rose across all percentiles over the five years, but the bottom (10th percentile) rose fastest — from 2.59 to 3.81 — nearly double the gain at the 90th percentile, suggesting AI's main effect is raising the floor by preventing weak drafts from being published.
- Conclusion drawn: AI-driven efficiency in knowledge work doesn't create more free time — it concentrates effort into higher craft/quality per unit of work.

## Quote

> Efficiency in knowledge work does not increase our hours. It concentrates our craft.
