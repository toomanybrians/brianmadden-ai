# The Biggest AI Risk Isn’t Falling Behind, It’s Thinking Too Small - Lessons from Barcelona

## Insights

- Argues most organizations use AI purely for optimization (automating tasks, cutting costs, speeding existing processes) rather than reinventing what's possible — framing this as the central leadership risk, not a technology gap.
- Claims AI "magnifies intent": if leadership's ambition is incremental or cost-focused, AI will scale that same incrementalism rather than transform it.
- Cites a claim that more ambitious/imaginative AI users outperform other AI-enabled peers by 7x, framing high performers as using AI to sharpen judgment (interrogating assumptions, pressure-testing ideas) rather than outsource it.
- Distinguishes "competing against AI" (defensive, focused on automation/replacement, preserving relevance) from "competing with AI" (augmentation-focused, creating new relevance) — argues the former is the wrong strategic posture.
- Proposes five leadership questions, including: what assumptions about work/value/growth no longer deserve to survive, and whether AI is led as an IT initiative vs. a reinvention agenda embedded in leadership.
- Suggests the strongest organizations won't be those removing the most labor, but those freeing people for more strategic, creative, relational, high-impact work.

## Quote

> If your ambition is incremental, AI will help you scale incrementalism.
