# Self-regulation 'not enough' for AI safety, Gary Marcus says on @PBS @NewsHour

## Insights

- Gary Marcus rejects "extinction"-level AI doom scenarios as unrealistic (comparing them to fiction), but argues serious "catastrophe" risk is real and underappreciated.
- He cites a near-war incident caused by an AI reporting false information, plus escalating AI-driven cyber hacking, as evidence of real-world harm already occurring (analogized to the WannaCry grid/hospital disruption scenario).
- He argues industry self-regulation is insufficient and points to a NYT report that OpenAI reportedly knew about problems linked to a "Hugging Face incident" months in advance but rushed products to market anyway.
- Marcus calls for a dedicated federal AI agency modeled on drug approval: independent pre-release evaluation requiring makers to prove benefits outweigh risks, plus post-release auditing and mandatory incident disclosure.
- He criticizes the current White House review process as opaque and inconsistent — noting Anthropic's "Mythos" system was delayed while a comparable OpenAI system (which he says had more problems) was not, undermining public trust in US AI.
- He downplays the "race against China" framing, arguing China already regulates AI, that regulation and competitiveness aren't mutually exclusive, and predicts the outcome resembles a "Coke and Pepsi" tie via separate sovereign AI ecosystems rather than a winner-take-all race.
