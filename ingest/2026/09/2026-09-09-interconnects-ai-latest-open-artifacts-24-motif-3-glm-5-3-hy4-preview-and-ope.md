# Latest open artifacts (#24): Motif-3, GLM-5.3, Hy4-preview and open model licenses

## Insights

- Open-model licensing is diverging by geography: Western labs (Google, Meta) are moving toward permissive Apache 2.0 licenses, while frontier Chinese labs (Kimi, MiniMax, Zhipu) are moving toward more restrictive custom licenses with commercial-use thresholds.
- Zhipu's GLM-5.3 switched from MIT to a custom license requiring a Z.AI security review for any "Model as a Service" provider (and affiliates) exceeding $10B in aggregate revenue over 12 months before commercial use is permitted.
- The GLM-5.3 license's undefined term "affiliates" (with a Chinese-law-defined equivalent term in the Chinese-language version) is flagged as a source of legal uncertainty that could deter enterprise adoption despite the high revenue threshold.
- A recurring "Chinese model playbook" pattern is described: releasing a strong model anonymously as a "stealth model" (e.g., GLM-5.3-Flash as "Ox-Alpha" on OpenRouter) to generate speculation and hype about its origin/size before revealing the creator, which also blunts benchmark-gaming accusations.
- Smaller/resource-constrained labs (Motif-Technologies, dots-studio/RedNote) are producing competitive results — Motif-3 under MIT license with strong scores for its size, and dots3 reportedly achieving a perfect score at IMO 2026 using an internal harness.
- Tencent's Hy4-preview shows a growing, more capable open-model effort but currently suffers from "overthinking"; the author notes its predecessor's trajectory (Hy3-preview to Hy3) suggests the final version could compete at the frontier of open models.

## Quote

> While the 10 billion US dollar threshold is very high compared to other licenses of this kind, "affiliates" is not defined in the license.
