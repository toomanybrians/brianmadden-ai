# TG-AI-F: AI is now everyone’s political problem

## Insights

- Political attitudes toward AI infrastructure have reversed sharply within about a year: officials who once aggressively courted data-center investment (e.g., NJ's 2023 AI Hub push, PA Gov. Shapiro's 2025 "competitive as hell" stance) are now imposing restrictions after community backlash.
- Pennsylvania Gov. Shapiro signed an August executive order requiring data center developers to meet strict compliance requirements and blocking "speculative, irresponsible" projects — a direct response to local community protests, despite his own administration having touted a $20B Amazon data-center investment months earlier.
- A viral incident is credited with pushing AI existential-risk debate into mainstream politics: ex-OpenAI/Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon publicly resigned, alleging frontier AI labs privately believe their work could "kill us all by the end of the decade" even while downplaying this publicly.
- A current Anthropic researcher (Evan Hubinger) amplified the claim, publicly estimating >10% chance AI causes human extinction within a decade — a rare instance of an active frontier-lab employee stating such odds openly.
- The Trump administration pushed back on "doomer" narratives: Trump called AI existential fears a hoax, the Defense Department publicly distanced itself from effective altruism ("Americanism, not effective altruism"), and adviser David Sacks framed EA-linked groups as a "Doomer Industrial Complex" that could hurt U.S. AI competitiveness.
- The episode illustrates AI policy splitting into two entangled but distinct fights: local/economic backlash over data-center build-outs (land, energy, community impact) versus a national ideological fight over catastrophic AI risk and regulation.
