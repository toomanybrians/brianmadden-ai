# In 1997 nobody could tell you why you'd want a gigabit connection. AI is in 1997.

## Insights

- The article challenges the "good enough AI" argument (associated with Ben Thompson's defense of Apple) by drawing an analogy to 1997-era gigabit bandwidth: nobody could justify the cost because the applications that would need it hadn't been invented yet — demand for intelligence isn't a fixed list capability catches up to, it's generative.
- Distinguishes "finished work" (finding a confirmation number) from "where demand actually starts" (rebooking the canceled flight) — solving a task well can reveal a bigger, adjacent job rather than closing off future demand.
- Argues cheap AI models create their own demand by making small software cheap enough to build, and that software becomes how people discover new uses for the models — a compounding loop rather than a saturation point.
- Apple's device ownership gives it "the moment a need arises" — real leverage, but the piece frames this as an opportunity, not a settled advantage, since usefulness created by cheap AI capacity doesn't determine who captures the payment for it.
- Notes Apple has already signaled (in launch-day fine print) that server-side model features will have usage limits with paid tiers coming later, undermining the "good enough, no need to pay more" framing.
- Frames accumulated memory/context as a moat that can erode: a better model can reconstruct context from data you already hold, but can't reconstruct a reason for doing something that was never recorded — a subtler kind of lock-in than raw model quality.

## Quote

> In 1997 nobody could explain why you would want a gigabit connection.
