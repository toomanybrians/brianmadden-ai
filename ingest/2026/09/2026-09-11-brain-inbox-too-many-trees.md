# Too many trees

## Insights

- Author describes a personal shift from treating AI safety/alignment/x-risk concerns as background expert chatter to feeling them "viscerally" dangerous, triggered by a viral post from an Anthropic researcher, a "Hugging Face incident," and independent METR reports.
- Cites Evan Hubinger's stated risk estimate of AI causing catastrophic harm as "greater than 10%," contrasting it with far smaller risks (bike-helmet deaths, listeria in soft cheese, plastic exposure) that parents routinely take extreme precautions against.
- Argues an informal poll of non-AI-focused friends/family shows the AI safety conversation hasn't reached the mainstream — people find terms like "superintelligence" and "alignment" alienating, some compare it to Y2K hype, and one friend noted people prioritize jobs, pay, and utilities like data centers provide.
- Notes the paperclip-maximizer analogy resonated more with lay audiences than technical alignment language, suggesting a communication gap between AI-safety insiders and the general public.
- Claims the public conversation (X, news coverage, politicians) expanded rapidly in a single day, comparing it to an "Overton window" shift, though she says this hasn't yet reached policy or "kitchen table" conversations.
- States there is currently no proactive AI safety coordination or regulation, and describes hearing "only crickets" from policymakers in Washington, DC.

## Quote

> This AI safety conversation has *not* entered the mainstream.
