# ⚙️ IFA showed robots are nearing the mainstream

## Insights

- IFA 2026 featured humanoid/robotic products (boxing, dancing, stair-climbing, soccer-playing robots) that are now actually purchasable rather than pure demo spectacle, though prices remain steep (Xiaomi CyberOne: $99,999; Unitree G1: $13,500).
- IFA Berlin's CEO frames robotics' two biggest remaining obstacles as data scarcity for training and consumer trust around data use — both of which AI-driven simulation tools (Nvidia Omniverse/Cosmos, Fei-Fei Li's World Labs) are helping close.
- The author argues consumers are already growing comfortable sharing more personal data (e.g., via smart glasses) in exchange for smarter AI features, which could accelerate at-home robot adoption despite privacy concerns.
- Prediction: household humanoid robots are unlikely to become common within 5–10 years, but cost curves may follow prior hardware categories (computers, cars) toward financed, mainstream affordability.
- Separately, OpenAI reports an internal model beyond GPT-6 Astra used ~10,000 agents to make progress on the Navier-Stokes equations, with OpenAI's chief scientist saying model capabilities are "advancing faster than our understanding of them."
- Meta launched Muse, a personal AI agent for everyday tasks (scheduling, purchases, document creation) requiring users to connect calendars, email, and payment accounts, prompting privacy experts to flag inadequate reliability/privacy assurances given Meta's trust history.

## Quote

> "We're developing these systems, and their capabilities are advancing faster than our understanding of them in some sense." — Jakub Pachocki, OpenAI chief scientist
