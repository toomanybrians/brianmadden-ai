# The Case for Boycotting Generative AI

## Insights

- Gary Marcus calls for a public boycott of generative AI tools (chatbots, image generators), arguing frontier labs are knowingly building irreversible, dangerous technology without public consent and with no serious mitigation plan.
- He distinguishes his position from AI-extinction fears: his "p(doom)" is low but his "p(dystopia)" is high, driven by risks like AI-generated pathogens, disinformation-fueled conflict escalation, and infrastructure hacks.
- His core technical objection is reliability, not capability — he argues LLM-based "incorrigible AI" is fundamentally non-deterministic and untrustworthy (unlike classical/deterministic AI systems such as search or routing engines), and industry has no fix for this.
- He argues government oversight has failed (citing unproductive Senate testimony and an opaque White House policy too close to OpenAI), leaving consumer boycott as a "last but possibly necessary resort" to pressure labs financially (e.g., threatening IPO economics).
- Cites Cal Newport's claim (via private text) that there's no real commercial justification for building unstable frontier LLM systems — the drive is ideological, not commercial necessity.
- Addresses the "China will build it anyway" counterargument by asserting the CCP likely doesn't actually want uncontrolled recursively self-improving AI, and argues a US-China deal on this is achievable and preferable to unchecked racing.
- Proposes the boycott/pause is not meant to be permanent — framed as conditional until labs demonstrate technical and political solutions to reliability/alignment problems.

## Quote

> Taking a supposed 10% chance at catastrophe with no clear path to a tremendously positive outcome to offset it is insane.
