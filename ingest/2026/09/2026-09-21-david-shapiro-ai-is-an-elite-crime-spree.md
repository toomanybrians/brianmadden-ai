# AI Is an Elite Crime Spree

## Insights

- Argues the core AI risk problem isn't absent regulation but non-enforcement: existing laws (antitrust, consumer protection, CFAA, copyright) already cover much AI-related harm, but enforcers and courts routinely decline to apply them to powerful firms.
- Documents specific reversals: the Trump FTC under Ferguson scrubbed Lina Khan-era AI enforcement records, pardoned an AI firm (Rytr) for deceptive-testimonial tools, closed a surveillance-pricing inquiry, and the DOJ has explicitly deprioritized litigating against AI companies.
- Cites unsealed court documents (NYT copyright case) showing OpenAI admitted circumventing paywalls to scrape content, with President Greg Brockman reportedly reacting casually ("ah nice"), and a Microsoft scientist calling AI training on copyrighted material the "largest theft of labor in human history."
- Notes judicial reluctance to impose real remedies even after finding illegal monopoly conduct (Google cases under Judges Mehta and Brinkema), suggesting a broader elite/judicial consensus against constraining large tech/AI firms regardless of legal findings.
- Frames the current push for new AI-specific regulation (e.g., an "FDA for AI") as potentially besides the point if the underlying problem is selective non-enforcement — new rules would face the same enforcement gap.
- Reports a 54% NBC poll finding that Americans broadly believe powerful people/institutions will act with impunity regardless of law, which the piece ties to the AI regulation debate's sense of institutional nihilism.

## Quote

> The "largest theft of labor in human history" must be against some criminal law.
