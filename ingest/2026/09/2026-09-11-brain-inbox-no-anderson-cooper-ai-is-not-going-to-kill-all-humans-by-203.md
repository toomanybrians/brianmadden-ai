# No, Anderson Cooper, AI is not going to kill all humans by 2030

## Insights

- Marcus distinguishes existential risk (literal human extinction) from catastrophic risk (mass casualties or civilizational damage), arguing AI extinction risk within five years is "all but indistinguishable from zero" while catastrophic risk from AI is real and underappreciated.
- He argues AI safety "Doomer" rhetoric from ex-frontier-lab employees (like Jacob Coxon) has functioned to enrich and empower the very AI companies it warns about, since alarm drives more VC funding without producing implemented safety solutions.
- He faults extinction scenarios (e.g., Yudkowsky/Soares's "If Anyone Builds It, Everybody Dies") for failing to explain, with any rigor, how AI motive plus superintelligence would translate into total human annihilation — and for underestimating human resistance, geographic/genetic diversity, and resourcefulness.
- He cites AI researchers (Sutton, LeCun, Sutskever, Fei-Fei Li) as skeptical that superintelligence arrives by 2030, suggesting more fundamental breakthroughs are still needed.
- He lists concrete catastrophic-risk vectors AI plausibly elevates: bioweapon design assistance, disinformation/deepfakes undermining democracy, cyberattacks on infrastructure (banking, power grids), authoritarian manipulation of training data/LLM outputs, mass surveillance, and erosion of critical thinking in education.
- A virologist source (Dr. Angela Rasmussen) is quoted pushing back on AI-engineered pandemic-extinction scenarios, noting even historically deadly pathogens (rabies, Ebola, plague) fall well short of near-total mortality needed for human extinction.

## Quote

> In my view, the chance that AI will eliminate humans in the next five years is all but indistinguishable from zero.
