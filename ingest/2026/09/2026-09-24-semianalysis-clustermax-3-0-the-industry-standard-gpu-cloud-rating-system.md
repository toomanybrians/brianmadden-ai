# ClusterMAX 3.0: The Industry Standard GPU Cloud Rating System Returns

## Insights

- Frontier labs (OpenAI, Anthropic) are increasingly bringing GPU infrastructure in-house rather than renting from neoclouds — they "own the scheduler," scale the Kubernetes control plane themselves, and build core cluster services, because off-the-shelf managed offerings can't handle their scale.
- This produces a bifurcated market: a "Matthew principle" where the largest, most profitable labs get better financing, pricing, and compute access, while smaller labs get stuck with worse prepay terms — even as the smaller-lab neocloud market grows in absolute terms.
- Combined salary ranges for OpenAI/Anthropic job postings mentioning "Kubernetes" total roughly $27.7M–$42.7M, illustrating how much organizational investment now goes into owning infrastructure talent rather than outsourcing it.
- Agentic coding tools (Codex, Claude) are already reshaping how technical teams operate: they helped fill documentation gaps and unblock work, but also made confident, plausible-but-wrong infrastructure decisions (e.g., misconfiguring networking, blaming providers) when domain expertise was thin — described as amplifying existing skill rather than substituting for it.
- Agentic coding is also seen as increasing customer willingness to accept bare-metal or lightly-managed clusters instead of paying for fully managed services, since AI tools reduce the operational burden customers previously outsourced.
- The piece flags significant, under-discussed security and reliability gaps at GPU cloud providers, framed as both a technical risk (compromised nodes as attack surface) and a structural/reputational risk for the AI industry amid regulatory scrutiny.
