# Can agents buy from your product? Three questions that tell you. Plus: Nate's Library MCP.

## Insights

- Stripe's Cursor account team found that Radar, Stripe's fraud-detection product, was well-tuned for payment fraud but useless against agentic abuse: users creating accounts, burning free credits, running trials, or racking up usage they'd never pay for — abuse that never involves an actual payment.
- By the time a conventional payment-fraud system has a transaction to evaluate, the underlying compute has already been consumed, meaning fraud detection built around "payment events" is structurally too late for usage-based/agentic products.
- The piece frames fraud/abuse detection as effectively a distribution decision: a company's ability to tell good users from abusive ones determines which customers (and which self-serve access models) it can actually afford to keep open.
- The article previews a distinction between three separate judgments needed to let an agent transact — budget, task quality, and permission to share information — arguing a spending limit or wallet only settles one of these, not all three.
- It also previews Stripe's acquisition of OpenRouter as connecting "what a task costs" to "what it earns," suggesting that accurately metering tokens/usage still doesn't answer whether an agent spent well or what price should be charged.
