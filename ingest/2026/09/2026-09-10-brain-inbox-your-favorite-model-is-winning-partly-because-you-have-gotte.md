# Your favorite model is winning partly because you have gotten good at talking to it. That is worth knowing before …

## Insights

- Comparing two AI coding assistants (Anthropic's "Fable 5.1" and OpenAI's "Astra") on the same app-building brief revealed that perceived quality differences may partly reflect user practice/familiarity rather than pure model capability — "your favorite model gets more work, more context, and more chances to fix an answer."
- Speed of first output mattered more than final quality alone: because Astra returned a working version faster, the author could iterate with corrections sooner, and the kept app was the one that had absorbed more rounds of feedback — not necessarily the better initial design.
- A model that didn't "win" the comparison still shaped the outcome: seeing Fable's version surfaced design questions (like clipboard sizing, image-inspection detail) the author hadn't thought to specify, informing decisions even though that app wasn't kept.
- The author reports a shifting personal workflow: increasingly preferring Astra for drafting tasks, while still turning to Claude/Fable 5.1 specifically for thinking through unsettled arguments — suggesting different models may suit different cognitive tasks within the same project.
- The piece frames a broader methodological caution: cross-model comparisons should account for accumulated user skill in prompting a favored tool, since that skill itself is a hidden variable in perceived performance differences.
- The full piece (paywalled) reportedly covers task-specific guidance across writing, research, spreadsheets, email, code, and 3D work, plus criteria for when cheaper/faster models suffice versus when tasks need more judgment-intensive handling.

## Quote

> Your favorite model gets more work, more context, and more chances to fix an answer.
