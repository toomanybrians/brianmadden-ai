# 🧠 OpenAI's AI Researchers Need Human Brakes

## Insights

- OpenAI reports reaching an internal "automated research-intern" milestone: agents now complete well-defined research tasks under human direction, some equivalent to several days of a skilled researcher's work.
- By mid-August, OpenAI researchers were using roughly 3.1 "agent-workdays" for every 8-hour human workday — but this is a time-spent ratio, not a 3.1x productivity multiplier; more code and experiments don't automatically translate into more discoveries.
- Human oversight remains heavy: more than half of successful agent tasks estimated at 4-8 hours of human-equivalent work still required at least one human intervention.
- OpenAI's internal goal is a fully automated AI researcher by March 2028, but the same disclosure describes deliberately restricting model use to prioritize safety work over speed.
- Chief scientist Jakub Pachocki argues researchers lack a reliable theory for predicting whether a model will keep following human intent as its capabilities grow, and that monitoring AI's written reasoning will become less trustworthy as systems do more processing outside human-readable language.
- Pachocki calls for cross-lab coordination, including voluntary slowdowns, framing the core tension as deciding when a useful research assistant becomes too capable to supervise at the speed it works.

## Quote

> Each useful result creates an incentive to delegate another task, but the human researcher reviewing it still has to understand how the work was done.
