# Korea Just Made AI a Public Utility, Free for Everyone

## Insights

- South Korea's science ministry selected SK Telecom, KT, and Kakao to build free, unlimited AI agent services for every citizen and resident, with beta tests in September 2026 and nationwide rollout by December; the government frames this as an "AI-basic society," with the unstated goal of independence from US/Chinese AI providers.
- The state is subsidizing this directly: 512 Nvidia B200 GPUs this year, running-cost subsidies starting 2027, a ~10 trillion won ($7.2B) 2026 AI budget (triple last year's), and a mandate that at least half of every service run on domestic models (LG's K-EXAONE, SKT's A.X).
- The services are agentic, not just chat: booking hospital appointments, navigating taxes/housing/public services, embedded in KakaoTalk, and even placing phone calls to offices lacking digital interfaces.
- Scale is a real constraint — 512 GPUs split three ways is small next to frontier clusters, so "unlimited" will lean on smaller domestic models; the ministry claims essential functions stay free even if demand exceeds funded capacity, untested until December.
- Frames a broader split emerging globally: AI as a paid subscription (US model, ~$20/month) vs. AI as state-provided utility (Korean model) — positioning governments as deciders of both funding and whose models power national AI infrastructure.
- Context from the same newsletter: MIT concluded generative AI can now credibly complete most undergraduate assignments and is urging shifts to oral exams and portfolios; Nvidia posted a $96.2B quarter (92% from data centers, guidance assumes zero China sales); a class-action lawsuit alleges Anthropic's $200 Max plan delivers only 1.1–1.7x the usage of the $100 plan despite "20x" marketing.
