# You keep reopening the same decision at midnight because checking it costs a weekend you don't have. GPT-6 Astra i…

## Insights

- Frames AI agents' value not as speeding up existing tasks but as making previously "too expensive to investigate" decisions worth pursuing — options that exist in theory but were practically out of reach due to time/effort cost.
- Uses Tim Urban's life-paths diagram (branching futures from a single present point) as a model for how cheaper investigation widens the set of futures someone can seriously consider.
- Cites a specific benchmark: Astra (GPT-6) more than doubles its predecessor's score on business-workflow tasks, but the resulting figure (41.4%) still falls short of dependable task completion.
- Distinguishes between giving an agent a decision already made ("help us move") versus an open question ("help us understand whether moving would improve our lives") — the latter can legitimately conclude "don't do it."
- Notes that agents narrow the field of options they show you, so a key safeguard is instructing the agent to actively investigate what would prove its own recommendation wrong.
- Observes that customer retention often rests on switching being "another job" — a friction that AI agents can now reduce from either side (helping customers leave more easily, or helping companies retain them).

## Quote

> A possibility you cannot afford to investigate is barely a choice at all.
