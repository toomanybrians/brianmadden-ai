# Debating RSI, the US-China Gap, and Jaggedness with JS Denain of Epoch AI

## Insights

- OpenAI and Anthropic's published RSI (recursive self-improvement) evidence — e.g., ~2x/month growth in researcher Codex spending — is suggestive but not strong evidence of imminent self-sustaining acceleration or full automation of AI research; gains cluster in engineering/troubleshooting tasks, not high-level strategic research decisions.
- Both speakers frame AI capability disagreement as largely a "capabilities theory of everything" question — most skepticism about extreme scenarios (industrial explosion, robotics-driven takeoff) traces back to lower expectations of how large and fast capabilities will actually grow, not just alignment optimism.
- Individual researcher productivity gains (e.g., a hypothetical 10x for tasks like the idea-to-experiment loop) don't necessarily translate to 10x organizational output, due to bottlenecks like compute allocation and strategic decision-making, which show little AI uplift so far.
- Robotics/industrial-explosion scenarios face two distinct doubts: (1) robot capability trends lag LLM trends and are less mature (multimodal/vision-action models seen as more rudimentary than text models), and (2) even with capable robots, physical build-out (factories, supply chains) takes real time — though one speaker argues elastic supply and financial incentives could overcome this faster than assumed.
- Estimated capability gap between top Chinese labs and OpenAI/Anthropic is roughly 4–9 months (via release-date benchmarking like ECI), narrower than the multi-year gap in compute/capital, prompting discussion of what explains the discrepancy.
- Proposed explanations for the narrow US-China capability gap despite compute disparity include distillation from US models, "Claude router" usage data revealing realistic prompt distributions, secondhand RL environments/data purchased after being pioneered by US labs, leakage of architectural ideas via personnel turnover, and possibly more efficient/grind-focused organization of research labor in Chinese labs.
- Analysis of Chinese AI labs' job postings reveals differing strategies: Zhipu (Z.ai) shows heavier B2B sales hiring, MiniMax shows more international expansion hiring, and evidence suggests increasing in-housing of data operations and infrastructure (e.g., data center staffing) rather than reliance on external compute/data providers.
