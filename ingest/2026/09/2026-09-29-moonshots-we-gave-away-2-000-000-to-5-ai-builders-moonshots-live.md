# We Gave Away $2,000,000 To 5 AI Builders | MOONSHOTS Live

## Insights

- The event frames the traditional career path ("school → degree → job") as obsolete, proposing a replacement social contract: identify a personally meaningful, solvable problem and use AI plus entrepreneurship to build a company around it, in as little as 90 days from a "clean sheet of paper."
- The XPRIZE's stated purpose isn't the specific winning companies but proving — and publicizing — that ordinary individuals can go from idea to revenue-generating business quickly using AI, as an inspirational template for others.
- Kathy Wood argues that AI-driven declines in entry-level hiring aren't a crisis but a "blessing in disguise": job-seekers should start solo, AI-native companies while job hunting, since demonstrated initiative and AI fluency will make them more hireable (or let the startup succeed instead).
- Wood also predicts AI will lower startup failure rates by making processes like drug discovery cheaper and faster (citing a hypothetical drop from $2.4B/13 years to $600–700M/8 years), implying AI could shift the economics of entrepreneurial risk broadly.
- The five funded startups (repair/gig marketplace, tax-credit automation, 3D asset generation, exam prep, business formation tooling) were all solo/small-team ventures built rapidly with AI tooling, presented as proof-of-concept for the "one person + AI = company" model.
- Judges' informal remarks reveal an underlying "post-economic," techno-optimist worldview among this influential circle — treating AI-driven abundance and even radical personal-life interventions (e.g., pharmacological "fixes" for relationships) as reasonable extensions of the same accelerationist logic.
