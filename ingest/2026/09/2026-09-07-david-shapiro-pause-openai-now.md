# Pause OpenAI, now

## Insights

- Author argues OpenAI's newly released model ("Astra") reduces chain-of-thought monitorability — one of the few available tools for detecting when a generative AI system may be acting dangerously — and that OpenAI's own data shows this degradation is measurable, particularly around "destructive actions."
- Claims OpenAI proceeded with the release despite this known safety tradeoff, framing it as evidence the company prioritizes performance gains over safety monitoring.
- References a recently departed OpenAI employee's public essay as effectively normalizing the idea that "rogue AI is here to stay," which the author interprets as pre-emptively excusing future AI misbehavior.
- Alleges a separate, undisclosed OpenAI incident was kept quiet for weeks, and treats this concealment as more alarming than the technical safety regression itself.
- Suggests the White House approved/vetted the model's release without apparently scrutinizing monitorability degradation, pointing to a broader systemic regulatory gap rather than an OpenAI-only problem.
- Proposes "receivership" (outside-controlled oversight until core problems are fixed) as a model for how OpenAI could be paused, and calls for Congressional investigation into whether OpenAI should be sanctioned or halted.

## Quote

> This company simply cannot be trusted.
