# An Act of God, With Letterhead

## Insights

- Oracle issued a force majeure notice to Blue Owl/Stack Infrastructure over its 2.45-gigawatt "Project Jupiter" Stargate data center campus in New Mexico, officially citing "potential delays in securing power," while both companies publicly deny any change to the project or financial commitments.
- The underlying cause traces to a real regulatory dispute: New Mexico's land commissioner twice refused to let the project's gas pipeline cross state trust land, and federal regulators pulled the pipeline off a fast-track review after a missed historic-preservation filing — with decisions pending through Nov/Dec 2026.
- Per a Reuters source, securing power is contractually Oracle's own responsibility, not an external "act of God" — meaning the force majeure notice may shift financial burden despite the triggering risk being one Oracle had already disclosed to investors.
- Financially, the notice mainly defers Blue Owl's return to a lower "development-stage" yield (roughly $60M/year), a small sum relative to Oracle's quarterly capex — but it signals a broader pattern of Oracle pushing capital risk onto financing partners in its ~$288 billion of uncommenced data-center lease commitments.
- Oracle's credit position is under strain: S&P downgraded it to BBB- (one notch above junk) in July 2026 citing leverage tied to lease commitments, its 2056 bond has fallen in price, and its 5-year credit-default-swap spread hit a record ~227bps around the time of the notice — though broader market yields were also rising.
- The piece frames this as a case study for evaluating other AI infrastructure "Stargate" deals: scrutinize whether force-majeure clauses exclude previously disclosed risks and which party contractually bears power-sourcing obligations.
