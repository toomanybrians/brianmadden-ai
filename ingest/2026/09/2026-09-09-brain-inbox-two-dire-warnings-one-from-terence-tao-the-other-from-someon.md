# Two dire warnings, one from Terence Tao, the other from someone who just quit Anthropic

## Insights

- Terence Tao's public stance on AI has shifted sharply: from cautious optimism about human-AI coexistence in math (per a 2024 Atlantic interview) to recent posts expressing "deep concerns" about AI's consequences for the field.
- Tao distinguishes solving puzzles from generating genuine new mathematical insight, and frames his concerns partly around "good intellectual taste" — a quality he suggests AI lacks or threatens to erode in practitioners.
- Tao is pushing for transparency from AI labs, in the context of the OpenAI/NYU/Alpöge-Buckmaster Navier-Stokes dispute, where OpenAI reportedly paid $22.5 million and gave only evasive language about whether it used the researchers' data to improve its models.
- Marcus argues that erosion of trust in AI companies' handling of researchers' IP directly threatens science-facing promises like AI "curing cancer," since researchers won't want to share work with labs suspected of appropriating it.
- Jacob Coxon, who recently resigned from Anthropic, published a warning (deemed less compelling by Marcus, more focused on future than current harms) about what Marcus calls "self-indulgent and dangerous thought processes" at leading frontier labs, including a comment from Anthropic's Alignment Science lead that Marcus found unreassuring.
- Marcus states he doesn't view extinction as likely but sees catastrophic harm as plausible, believes no lab has a serious plan for it, and argues neither major lab is seriously exploring alternatives to the LLM architecture, which he calls "utterly incorrigible."
