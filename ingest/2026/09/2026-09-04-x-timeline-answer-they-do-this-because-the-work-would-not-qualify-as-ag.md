# answer: they do this because the work would not qualify as AGI under traditional

## Insights

- Gary Marcus argues AI companies avoid calling systems (e.g., "Astra") AGI because the work wouldn't qualify under traditional/established definitions of AGI.
- Marcus references his own long-running bet with Miles Brundage, with criteria for AGI dating back to 2014, as a benchmark the system likely won't pass.
- Cites the Hendrycks et al. "Definition of AGI" paper, which operationalizes AGI as matching a well-educated adult's cognitive versatility, using Cattell-Horn-Carroll psychometric theory across ten weighted cognitive domains.
- That framework found current models have a "jagged" cognitive profile — strong on acquired knowledge but critically deficient in areas like long-term memory storage.
- The paper quantifies progress numerically: GPT-4 scores 27% and GPT-5 scores 57% on their AGI measure, still leaving a substantial gap to full AGI.
- A quoted post separately notes OpenAI's charter defines AGI as "highly autonomous systems that outperform humans at most economically valuable work," implying a discrepancy between that definition and how AI executives talk about new releases.
