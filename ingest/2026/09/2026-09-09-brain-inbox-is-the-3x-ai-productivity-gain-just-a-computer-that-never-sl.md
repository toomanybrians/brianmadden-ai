# Is the 3x AI Productivity Gain just a Computer that Never Sleeps?

## Insights

- OpenAI's internal data shows researchers logging 3.14 "agent-workdays" per 8-hour human shift by running roughly four AI agents in parallel — the celebrated "3x productivity gain" may largely reflect machines working around the clock rather than smarter output per hour.
- The compute cost behind this is steep and rising fast: median daily inference spend per researcher jumped from $14 to over $600 in five months (a 40x increase), with 90th-percentile users burning $7,000+/day (~$2.5M/year annualized).
- Unlike factory automation (capex, depreciating machinery, justifies night shifts), AI inference is pure variable opex — meaning firms can run agents overnight without labor-cost tradeoffs, incentivizing constant usage regardless of output quality.
- Over half of AI-completed tasks (4-8 hour range) still require human correction, so much of the "extra" work involves engineers cleaning up automated errors rather than doing higher-value creative work — effectively shifting job content from architecture to supervision/debugging.
- A footnote calculation suggests the real "yield" is closer to 2x effective delivered output, not the marketed 3x, once the ~50% defect/intervention rate on overnight machine runs is factored in.
- Social/competitive dynamics (fear of falling behind peers running agents 24/7) may be driving adoption of always-on AI workflows independent of whether the economics or quality justify it.

## Quote

> The 3.14 workday ratio is not three times smarter thinking. It is one engineer supervising three shifts of machine runtime.
