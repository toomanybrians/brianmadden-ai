# An Ode to Counter-Positioning

## Insights

- Counter-positioning (from Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers framework) is defined as building a business model that gives incumbents conflicting incentives, making it painful for them to copy even when a challenger is clearly better — it's a "take-off phase" power, not a durable long-term moat.
- Classic examples cited: Ramp vs. corporate card incumbents (spend-less vs. rewards-for-more-spend), Dell vs. Compaq (direct sales vs. dealer channel), Android vs. Nokia (subsidized by search revenue vs. needing to monetize the stack directly), Amazon vs. Barnes & Noble (fulfillment infrastructure vs. sunk store investment), Microsoft/DOS vs. IBM (commoditizing hardware to become the integration layer), and early Facebook vs. MySpace (small/exclusive network vs. maximizing growth).
- Key mechanism: incumbents are "stuck" either because copying would cannibalize their existing profit pool, or because their infrastructure/CapEx/debt load makes pivoting prohibitively costly — sometimes a challenger actively benefits from destroying the incumbent's profit pool (Microsoft/IBM).
- Applied to current AI assistants: Instinct (raised $350M Series B at $2.5B valuation) is described as a better product than Meta's newly launched "Muse," but being better is explicitly said not to constitute counter-positioning or a moat — Meta can freely copy it because doing so costs them nothing and complements their existing assets (user data, WhatsApp, Marketplace, Ray-Bans, data centers, cash).
- Meta reportedly offered ~10 figures to acquire Instinct; when Instinct declined, Meta built a competing product itself, illustrating that "acquisition hope is not a strategy" for unmoated AI startups.
- Instinct's recent "Trusted Person" network (letting assistants talk to significant others/family/friends) is framed as an attempt to build genuine network effects before Meta's Muse can out-scale it — the piece frames this as a race against time rather than a settled outcome.

## Quote

> Being better is not counter positioning.
