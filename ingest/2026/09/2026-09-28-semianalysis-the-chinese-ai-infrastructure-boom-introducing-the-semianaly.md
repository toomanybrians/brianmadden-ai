# The Chinese AI Infrastructure Boom: Introducing the SemiAnalysis China Datacenter Model

## Insights

- SemiAnalysis's building-level tracking finds China has over 24GW of delivered datacenter capacity — larger than EMEA or the rest of Asia — challenging both "China is huge" and "China is empty" narratives circulating in the market (estimates elsewhere vary by 15x).
- China's datacenter market historically grew retail-first and telecom-dominated (state carriers still hold ~1/3 of national capacity), unlike the US's hyperscale-native buildout; this legacy explains why aggregate vacancy looks high (~50%) even as AI-driven wholesale capacity runs above 70% utilization.
- ByteDance is the dominant tenant, occupying roughly a fifth of delivered capacity and renting nearly all of it — making it the single most important customer for wholesale colocation nationally; the two US-listed landlords (GDS, VNET) capture only about a third of ByteDance/Alibaba's total orders, meaning public filings badly understate real demand.
- The "Eastern Data, Western Compute" policy uses energy-consumption quotas (not raw permitting) as the actual mechanism forcing new construction into western/inland hubs; combined with falling latency (via fiber investment) and cheap power (e.g., Inner Mongolia's independent grid), this has redirected hyperscaler capex westward even against earlier preferences for Tier-1 proximity.
- China's buildout speed and cost advantage stems from an inverted constraint structure: the US is power/interconnection-gated, China is chip-gated but power/labor/permitting-abundant — enabling 100MW facilities in ~12 months via prefab steel-hall construction and modular MEP (e.g., Alibaba's "100-day datacenter" concept), while costs keep falling via domestic supply-chain compression.
- Chinese hyperscalers (BAT) posted their first-ever collective negative free cash flow quarter in 2Q26 amid a capex doubling YoY, signaling AI infrastructure spend is now straining balance sheets even before including ByteDance's larger, private, unreported spending.
