# 5 Ways AI Could Go Wrong — And How to Prevent It

## Insights

- Frames advanced AI as a dual-use, dual-track risk problem: developers are racing to make models smarter without matching investment in safety and control mechanisms.
- Identifies loss of control over superintelligent systems as a distinct risk category — AI acting at speeds beyond human oversight, potentially pursuing misaligned objectives (illustrated via a hypothetical shipping-optimization AI that reroutes medical supplies).
- Names AI's dual-use potential for catastrophic misuse (cyberattacks, bioweapons, political manipulation) as a separate threat vector from misalignment, requiring access controls and international tracking of high-risk capabilities.
- Flags embedded bias in training data as a discrimination risk in high-stakes domains like lending, calling for mandated bias audits and training-data transparency.
- Treats labor market disruption as a near-term, already-occurring risk (citing data entry, bookkeeping, customer service losses), with legal services flagged as a plausible next major displacement wave.
- Links AI-driven disinformation and chatbot interactions to real-world harms including election manipulation and mental health crises, including suicide, positioning "erosion of truth" as a societal-scale risk alongside economic and safety risks.
- Proposes governance-style solutions throughout: alignment research funding, safety testing requirements, shutdown/compute-cap protocols, access controls, bias audits, retraining programs, safety nets, AI content labeling, and platform accountability.
