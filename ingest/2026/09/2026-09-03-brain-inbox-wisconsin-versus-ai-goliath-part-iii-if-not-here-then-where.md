# Wisconsin versus AI Goliath Part III: If Not Here, Then Where?

## Insights

- Sam Altman, in a TIME interview, reframed community opposition to data centers by proposing they be built on land "people don't have much use for" — sparsely populated areas where compute results can be moved elsewhere digitally.
- Reporting from two Wisconsin towns (Wisconsin Rapids and Port Washington) finds broad local opposition to data centers, arising not from reflexive NIMBYism but from unanswered questions about utility costs, noise, and environmental impact that companies allegedly avoided disclosing (e.g., by skipping environmental impact reports).
- Companies building data centers (naming Vantage, Oracle, OpenAI in Port Washington) are reportedly not hiring locally even for construction jobs, contradicting economic-revitalization pitches made to these communities.
- "Closed-loop" water cooling systems, marketed as efficient and low-impact, are characterized as more PR framing than proven technology; a cited example (Meta's Cheyenne, Wyoming data center) allegedly contaminated the local water supply with bacteria after using such a system.
- The piece notes a broader pattern of local, state, and federal governments removing regulatory hurdles to speed data center construction (citing the Stargate Project signed by Trump with Altman present), often preventing environmental/labor/safety questions from being answered before building starts.
- Argues data center resource consumption (energy, water) is being described as unprecedented in scale and not comparable to prior industrialization, with unknown longer-term consequences.
