# The Morning Download: Companies Maintain AI Deployments With a Focus on Governance

## Insights

- Despite public alarm from AI developer leaders about existential risk (cybersecurity breaches, resignations calling for limits), enterprise deployment of AI is not slowing — companies distinguish between frontier research risk and internal deployment of "well understood and tested" models.
- At a WSJ Technology Council Summit town hall, about half of attendees supported slowing frontier AI development and prioritizing safety guardrails, while only a few feared AI could cause human extinction within a decade — showing a split between abstract risk concern and practical deployment behavior.
- AI budgeting remains highly unreliable; KKR's CIO noted most organizations likely didn't budget accurately for AI in 2026, and described the complexity of allocating "tokens and budgets" and attributing costs to specific agent actions ("agent economics").
- A former Microsoft chief AI officer argued that deploying AI without modernizing underlying legacy systems is risky, since AI amplifies both strengths and weaknesses of existing systems; she suggested treating AI agents as "personas" with distinct strengths/weaknesses for management purposes.
- Executive search and board involvement in AI strategy has intensified sharply — one search consultant described five board members involved in a single Fortune 100 executive search, calling this level of board engagement unprecedented.
- A CVS executive criticized creating technology-specific C-suite titles (e.g., Chief AI Officer), comparing it to the earlier "Chief Cloud Officer" trend, arguing that tying a role/bonus to a specific technology distorts its application as a "solution for everything"; he advocated for outcome-based executive roles instead.
- FedEx's CEO stated he personally drives AI strategy because "the gap between business and technology is fast going away," with the CIO explicitly deferring to that arrangement while noting frequent internal debate over decisions.

## Quote

> "The problem with associating a person with a technology is that that technology suddenly becomes a solution for everything." — Tilak Mandadi, CVS
