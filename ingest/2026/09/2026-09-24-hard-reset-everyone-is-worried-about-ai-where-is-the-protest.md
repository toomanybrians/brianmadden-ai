# Everyone Is Worried About AI. Where Is The Protest?

## Insights

- Politico polling shows a majority of Americans, across party lines, worry about AI safety, and nearly half favor pausing AI development — yet this consensus hasn't translated into visible mass protest.
- Anthropic publicly validated a whistleblower's claim that AI has a one-in-ten chance of causing mass human extinction within ten years, an admission from an AI developer itself rather than an outside critic.
- NVIDIA's Jensen Huang argued against any new AI regulation or slowdown, framing speed and safety as compatible — a stance the piece contrasts with majority public opinion.
- A "Stop The AI Race" protest during Dreamforce drew fewer than 50 people despite favorable conditions (SF location, major tech event, active news cycle); the group's largest-ever US anti-AI protest in July drew only ~400.
- The piece argues AI safety is fragmented into many separate issues (job displacement/power consolidation, surveillance, misinformation, environmental costs of data centers), which diffuses potential collective action into disconnected fights.
- Local, place-specific resistance (e.g., Wisconsin communities protesting data centers at town councils, and candidates centering AI/data center policy in state and federal races) is described as more active and tangibly effective than centralized SF protests.
- National AI discourse is framed by developers/politicians as an inevitable "race" (echoing Space Race rhetoric) and recast as a US-vs-China security issue, which the author suggests discourages regulation and normalizes acceptance.
