# Millennium Pastimes

## Insights

- AI models have reportedly begun solving Millennium Prize problems (an OpenAI model solved forced Navier-Stokes; OpenAI claimed progress on another; rumors of Anthropic targeting a third), compressing what mathematicians expected to take a millennium into days.
- Terence Tao's argument (as characterized here): the value of a proof lies largely in the struggle to produce it, not just the result — when AI removes the struggle, humanity gains solutions but loses the accumulated understanding, character, and insight that come from failed attempts.
- "Proof abundance" is framed as a new problem: if AI generates enormous volumes of true statements/proofs, truth becomes a commodity but interpretation (which proofs matter, which are correct, which open new paths) becomes the scarce bottleneck — mathematics risks becoming a "Library of Babel" rather than a cathedral.
- Argues that no matter how much AI automates the production and even interpretation of knowledge, a human "bridge" must remain at the end of the chain to decide what to build, teach, fund, or believe — the chain of epistemic delegation can't fully terminate without humans.
- Describes a "problem scarcity" risk: as AI solves career-defining problems faster than humans can, fewer people will enter fields that are being "bombarded" by AI, eliminating the multi-decade "ground projects" (per Bernard Williams) that give some people's lives structure and purpose — and argues this dynamic isn't unique to math.
- Draws analogy to industrialization/agriculture (leaving fields fallow) to argue that constant AI-driven "harvesting" of intellectual problems may exhaust the field's fertility, and to chess/speedrunning as a precedent where humans preserved a human-only competitive category after computers surpassed them — floats the idea of math splitting into "research" (AI-dominated) vs. "human practice" (preserved as an amateur pursuit).
- Notes a shift in who can compete at the frontier: from pencil-paper-and-outlier-brain to needing massive compute, full internet-scale data, and agent swarms — raising concern that intellectual production is becoming another means of production convertible into capital.

## Quote

> The means of intellectual production are means of production, period; they have managed to translate skill into capital.
