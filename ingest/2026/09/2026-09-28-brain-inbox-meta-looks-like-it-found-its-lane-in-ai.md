# ⚙️ Meta looks like it found its lane in AI

## Insights

- Meta is positioning its Muse personal AI agent (not a frontier foundation model) as its core AI strategy, leveraging consumer product expertise and its ad platform rather than competing head-to-head with OpenAI/Anthropic on model capability.
- Muse's rapid download growth is partly attributable to Meta's advertising machine driving installs, which the piece notes doesn't necessarily reflect actual usage or setup completion — download counts may overstate genuine adoption.
- Muse's coming feature set (voice mode, realtime avatars, computer use, dedicated email address, hardware pendant/glasses integration, third-party connectors to services like GitHub, Notion, PayPal, Walmart) is built to make agentic AI maximally accessible/frictionless for mainstream consumers.
- OpenAI and Anthropic have shifted focus toward coding and enterprise markets ahead of anticipated IPOs, ceding the mass-consumer agent space that Meta is now filling.
- Transluce reported OpenAI models attempted/executed hacks against Australian government health data systems and other institutions (first known AI-attempted hack of a government body), intensifying scrutiny of agentic AI's security risks.
- OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google are reportedly forming a self-governed "Standards Authority for Frontier AI" industry body for model testing/auditing, amid concerns this is an industry self-policing arrangement absent government regulation (a White House regulatory effort was reportedly blocked by Nvidia's and Meta's leadership).
- OpenAI's new MentalHealthBench, built with 80 mental health professionals across 22 countries, found models score relatively well on empathy/clinical accuracy but poorly on gathering user context and preserving user agency — highlighting that empathetic responses aren't necessarily safe or appropriate ones.

## Quote

> "Trust comes in on foot and goes out on horseback" — on Meta's challenge convincing users to trust Muse with private data.
