# Open-Source AI & Open Models Reading List

## Insights

- The open-closed model performance gap has narrowed to roughly 4-6 months, and since ~2024 the leading open-weight models have consistently come from Chinese labs rather than American ones.
- Multiple sources argue open models are on the "Pareto cost frontier" (best performance-per-cost) even when not at the absolute performance frontier, making them attractive for enterprise adoption.
- Real-world enterprise behavior is shifting toward Chinese open models for cost reasons (e.g., Perplexity adopting DeepSeek R1, Thomson Reuters moving off Claude to Qwen), prompting lawmaker scrutiny of companies like DoorDash, Airbnb, Cursor, and Apple for using Chinese models.
- Distillation (training on another model's outputs) is described as the most contentious debate in open models in 2026, including documented cases of Chinese labs extracting reasoning traces from proprietary APIs and circumventing Anthropic's terms of service at scale.
- Framing tension exists between viewing open models as economic/innovation complements to closed models versus as a proliferation risk requiring nonproliferation-style control — several cited authors argue the latter approach is misguided since bad actors will access capable models regardless.
- Political/regulatory anxiety is building around vague federal oversight potentially leading to restrictions or bans on frontier open models, alongside concern that a "distillation panic" overstates how much Chinese progress depends on copying closed-model outputs.
