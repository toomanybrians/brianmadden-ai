# Open-Source AI & Open Models Reading List

## Insights

- The open-vs-closed model gap has narrowed to roughly 4-6 months, and since ~2024 the leading open models have consistently come from Chinese labs (DeepSeek, Kimi, GLM, Qwen) rather than US labs.
- Open models are framed as occupying the Pareto cost-frontier rather than the absolute performance frontier — cheaper/comparable value rather than best-in-class capability, positioning them as complements to closed frontier models for enterprise agentic workflows.
- Multiple real-world cases show Western companies (Perplexity, Thomson Reuters, DoorDash, Airbnb, Cursor) adopting Chinese open models to cut costs, which has drawn lawmaker scrutiny and probes.
- Distillation (training on another model's outputs, including reasoning traces) is described as the single most contentious open-model debate of 2026; research shows techniques exist to systematically extract reasoning traces from proprietary APIs, and Anthropic confirmed Chinese labs used such methods, though the degree to which distillation alone explains China's competitiveness is disputed.
- Regulatory risk is flagged directly: vague "vibe regulation" and federal oversight trends are described as potentially leading to a clash with or ban on frontier open-weight models in the near term.
- Case is made that safety risk from open models is often overstated relative to already-occurring harms from bypassed safety guardrails on closed models, and that nonproliferation-style access control is seen as an unworkable strategy given open weights can't be un-released.
