# AGI, ASI and the Singularity: What Are We Actually Talking About?

## Insights

- Jensen Huang (NVIDIA CEO) claimed in March 2026 that AGI has already been "achieved," specifically in the context of AI's ability to build a billion-dollar tech company — though he distinguished this from AI being able to build something like NVIDIA itself, citing a hypothetical viral, short-lived AI-created service as an example of what's possible now.
- Google co-founder Sergey Brin has reportedly been directing resources toward recursive self-improvement in AI (per Reuters, citing a source close to him), and Anthropic ran experiments in August 2026 showing AI agents conducting research and training models.
- The piece frames "AGI" as contested not on whether broad-task AI is the goal, but on how well a system must perform unfamiliar tasks, apply knowledge to new situations, and recover from mistakes before the label applies.
- Traces "AGI" terminology to Mark Gubrud (1997) and Shane Legg's independent 1997/2002 suggestion to Ben Goertzel as a book title, which helped popularize the term via subsequent conferences.
- Ray Kurzweil's timeline: 2029 for passing the Turing test, 2045 for the Singularity — and in a 2024 interview he tied the Singularity to humans merging with AI to expand human intelligence, not just machines surpassing humans.
- The article treats recursive self-improvement (better AI systems building still-better successors) as one candidate mechanism — not a settled fact — for how AGI could progress toward ASI and the Singularity.
