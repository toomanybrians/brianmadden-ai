# College Is Not One Thing

## Insights

- Miessler argues the "is AI ending college?" debate is malformed because college bundles many distinct components (content delivery, lectures, social life, credentialing, etc.) that must be evaluated separately.
- Textbook-based content delivery was already broken pre-AI and is well-suited to replacement by AI systems that can pull current research and apply rigor.
- Lectures are highly variable in value: for most professors (low/middle/upper-middle tier), AI-generated custom video or dynamic interactive visualizations could outperform them; only truly exceptional "genius" teachers offer a Feynman-style curiosity-driven experience AI likely can't replicate soon.
- Social and campus-life dimensions — leaving home, exposure to different people/cultures/politics, competition via grades, extracurriculars, romantic relationships, and decades-long friendships — are framed as largely non-substitutable by AI.
- The college credential's value is described as declining sharply, except for degrees from highly reputable schools that function mainly as an admissions-based filter.
- Proposes reframing the education debate as a decomposition exercise: identify which components are AI-vulnerable, which are still valuable, and which can be replaced by workforce entry, internships, or entrepreneurship instead of a redesigned college experience.

## Quote

> College is not one thing.
