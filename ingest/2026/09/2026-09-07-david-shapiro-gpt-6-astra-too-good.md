# GPT-6 Astra: Too Good

## Insights

- Author argues benchmark saturation has made "how good is it?" the wrong question for frontier models like GPT-6 Astra — capability comparisons are becoming meaningless once models beat every existing test, much like a chess engine beating a human is no longer newsworthy.
- Proposes that real-world impact is the only remaining valid measure of AI progress: does it move GDP, cure disease, create new careers — not benchmark scores or launch-post engagement metrics.
- Distinguishes superintelligence from omnipotence: even a vastly superhuman AI is still bottlenecked by physical laws and, critically, by "human inertia" — the slowness of people and institutions to adopt and act on what AI makes possible.
- Coins the framing "the world moves at the speed of meat" — even with perfect personal context, an individual's life only changes as fast as they can absorb and act on AI's output; humans remain the rate-limiting factor.
- Notes that hype dynamics (record-liked launch posts, viral reactions, competitive benchmark wins against Anthropic) are decoupled from actual diffusion of AI into everyday life and infrastructure.
- Predicts that in the near term, despite AI's theoretical power, daily lived reality (jobs, buildings, environment) will remain largely unchanged — adoption and societal transformation lag far behind model capability.

## Quote

> AI capabilities have become so extreme on paper that the only benchmark left is reality.
