# RT @clairlemon: An Open Letter to Scott Alexander

From @sapinker 
https://t.co/

## Insights

- Pinker declines Scott Alexander's public debate challenge, arguing formal debates are "spectator sport, not an epistemic tool" that reward rhetoric over truth-seeking, and proposes verifiable predictions/bets instead.
- Reiterates his core 2018 argument (from *Enlightenment Now*) against AI-extinction scenarios: intelligence is not an unlimited scalar power; motivation/self-preservation is a product of evolution, not inherent to engineered systems; genuine intelligence involves trading off competing goals rather than monomaniacal pursuit of one; and doomer scenarios assume humans would grant AI unchecked, irreversible control.
- Acknowledges LLMs are more generally capable than his 2018 assumptions predicted, and that AI development has been released faster and more recklessly than the incremental, safety-tested engineering culture he expected.
- Cites a 2025 RAND analysis that, even granting AI intended to wipe out humanity, found "no describable scenario" making it conclusively an extinction threat.
- Argues doomsday rhetoric has historically backfired (nuclear fears fueling arms races and the Iraq invasion) and can damage young people's mental health, confidence, and willingness to invest in the future.
- Distinguishes between real, near-term AI dangers he supports guarding against (bioterrorism, cyber-sabotage, unconstrained agents) via concrete safeguards (oversight, liability, kill switches) versus what he calls unfounded "extinction" prophecy.
- Notes a sociological critique (via Cal Newport) that frontier AI labs are influenced by an apocalyptic/utopian ideology treating superintelligence as inevitable, which may reduce their concern for mundane safety risks along the way.

## Quote

> Trading off multiple competing goals is the essence of intelligence, so no genuine AI would wreak the ridiculous havoc imagined in the doomer scenarios.
