# ⚖️ OpenAI Gives Astra a Legal Toolkit

## Insights

- OpenAI launched Astra for Law (Sept 17), pairing GPT-6 Astra with a specialist search index of 230M+ URLs and legal-specific instructions, aimed at connecting research answers to firm-specific expertise and review workflows.
- On a 200-question US legal research benchmark, Astra for Law hit 54.0% "all-pass" correctness vs. 38.7% for standard Astra with web search at max reasoning effort — a real gain but still leaving significant error rates.
- Cost data suggests task-specific tooling beats raw compute spend: Astra for Law's cheapest setting reached 46.8% accuracy at $1.31/answer, beating baseline Astra's 38.7% at $4.86 — implying better-configured research setups outperform just buying more reasoning.
- Early adopters (Cooley, Sullivan & Cromwell, Ropes & Gray) are embedding the tool into specific tasks — IPO filings, contract redlines, diligence source-tracing — rather than treating it as a general-purpose answer engine, which lets lawyers check outputs against firm-specific standards.
- Access is initially restricted to selected firms via "Trusted Access" in ChatGPT and Codex, with API access and confidentiality controls to follow later.
- Broader newsletter theme: multiple AI labs (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic) are now building surrounding infrastructure/environments — legal toolkits, shared household agents, parallel coding coordination, physical lab robotics — to link model answers to follow-on work, not just improve raw model output.

## Quote

> Better research results still leave lawyers responsible for checking the work.
