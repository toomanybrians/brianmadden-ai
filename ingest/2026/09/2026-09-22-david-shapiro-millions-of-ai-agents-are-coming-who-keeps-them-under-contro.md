# Millions of AI agents are coming. Who keeps them under control?

## Insights

- VAST Data, founded in 2016 as a data storage/management company, has grown from a $9.1B valuation in late 2023 to $30B, driven largely by AI's changing infrastructure demands.
- The company frames itself as building an "AI operating system" — arguing that AI infrastructure must now manage not just data, but compute, models, agents, permissions, and security together, not as separate layers.
- AI's data needs have moved past traditional "big data" (structured databases) into massive unstructured stores — images, video, audio, genomes — requiring different infrastructure design.
- As organizations adopt AI, their own proprietary data and the models trained on it are becoming valuable IP, raising the stakes on how that data is secured and exposed to external AI providers.
- A future with millions of AI agents and models creates a fundamental control problem: tracking what each agent knows, what it can access, and what it's permitted to do — a challenge compounded further when agents extend into physical robots.
- An unspecified "OpenAI/Hugging Face incident" is cited as an example revealing agent security risks, reinforcing the view that safety and policy enforcement need to be built into the infrastructure layer rather than left to the models or agents themselves.
- Large enterprises are described as adopting AI more slowly than AI-native companies, often because legacy tech stacks weren't designed for real AI production workloads and break under those demands.
