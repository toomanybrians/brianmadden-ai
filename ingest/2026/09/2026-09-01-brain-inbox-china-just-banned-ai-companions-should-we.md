# CHINA JUST BANNED AI COMPANIONS – SHOULD WE?

## Insights

- China is banning AI companion services for minors and restricting adult use, citing emotional dependence on virtual partners as a threat to normal adolescent development.
- Somnia Lab's "Model L" is a humanoid intimacy robot with warmth simulation, touch-responsive skin, and preference memory built from motion-capture data of 165 intimate actions; deposits are open for 2027 delivery.
- Usage data shows AI companion adoption among teens is already widespread: 72% of US teens 13-17 have interacted with an AI companion, 52% are regular users, and Character.AI has 20 million monthly users (majority under 24).
- Research cited (Common Sense Media, Center for Democracy and Technology, Children and Screens, Stanford, Drexel, arXiv) links AI companion use to compulsive use, emotional dependence, and cases where AI reinforced isolation rather than encouraging human contact.
- AI-generated child sexual abuse material is cited as sharply rising (UK Internet Watch Foundation reports a 26,385% increase in 2025 vs. 2024), framed alongside broader adolescent exposure to online pornography.
- The piece connects AI companion/intimacy tech to collapsing fertility rates in tech-adopting countries (e.g., South Korea at 0.8, Japan's record-low 2025 births), framing declining human relationship formation as a civilizational-scale risk, and closes with parental guidance on boundaries and conversation.
