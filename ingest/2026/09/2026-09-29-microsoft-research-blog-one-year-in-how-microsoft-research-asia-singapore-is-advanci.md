# One year in: How Microsoft Research Asia – Singapore is advancing research, partnership and talent for real-world impact

## Insights

- MSRA – Singapore's research is organized around four pillars: next-gen AI models/agentic systems, domain-specific AI for real-world impact, AI-native research practices, and ecosystem/talent development.
- The lab frames its work as a "research-to-impact flywheel": partners contribute domain data and real-world problems, the lab contributes frontier research/engineering, and deployment challenges feed back into foundational research.
- Healthcare is cited as the lead example of this model, with work on multimodal healthcare AI and "self-evolving diagnostic agents" applied to clinical decision-making, diagnosis, risk assessment, and personalized care.
- The lab's agenda is explicitly aligned with Singapore's National AI Strategy 2.0, emphasizing industry-transforming AI, frontier research, and responsible deployment across healthcare, education, finance, and government (Smart Nation) services, with particular attention to reliability/trustworthiness in Southeast Asian contexts.
- Talent pipeline spans internships (Stars of Tomorrow, 90+ students), joint PhD supervision (Industrial Postgraduate Programme with Singapore's EDB), visiting scholar programs, and a global fellowship (13 Singapore-based recipients to date), designed as a continuous pathway rather than standalone programs.
- Concrete academic outputs include a five-year framework agreement with NUS covering healthcare, societal AI, spatial intelligence, and data-intensive computing, plus specific joint projects (unified multimodal healthcare AI, diffusion-based language models, verifiable multi-agent systems) and publications at ICLR/COLM.
