# This dismissal of AI skepticism is egregiously wrong, or at least deeply selecti

## Insights

- Gary Marcus pushes back on Jeff Clune's claim that AI skeptics have "consistently been wrong," calling it selective or inaccurate.
- Marcus reasserts his own prior predictions: that pure LLMs would remain vulnerable to hallucinations, and that they would need to be supplemented by (neuro)symbolic methods to overcome fundamental limitations.
- The exchange reflects an ongoing debate in the AI field over whether skepticism about LLM capabilities (hallucination-proneness, need for hybrid architectures) has been vindicated or refuted by subsequent progress.
- Clune's original claim (quoted) argues that AI skeptics are broadly wrong on substance, erring mainly on timing, and that this pattern holds for tech progress generally — a framing Marcus explicitly contests.
