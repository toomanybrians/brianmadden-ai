# 🔮 AI politics & the future of growth ++ #602

## Insights

- Public sentiment toward AI in the US has soured sharply: Edelman Trust Barometer data cited shows only 34% of Americans saw AI positively in 2020 (23% negative), falling to just 19% positive vs 50% negative by 2024 — despite roughly a quarter of American adults now using chatbots daily.
- Over one in six Americans reportedly believe AI will "almost certainly" destroy humanity, reflecting a gap between existential fear and everyday adoption.
- Despite public hostility, business adoption continues unabated — cited examples include Anthropic nearing $100bn annualized revenue, a four-chair local barbershop installing an AI receptionist, and IT executives at a Las Vegas event unanimously continuing AI implementations.
- A tension is identified between AI lab leadership's own stated risk beliefs (Anthropic's leadership reportedly worried they "might kill all their customers") and their continued aggressive commercial expansion.
- Commentary referenced from other thinkers: one figure (credited with coining "AGI") pushes back on labs calling for centralized slowdown, favoring a decentralized "prosocial" approach instead; Jaron Lanier argues that describing AI as a powerful "being" cedes rhetorical ground; Mustafa Suleyman states AI systems don't have rights, feelings, or consciousness and shouldn't be trained to act as if they do.
- The piece frames a structural problem: if businesses keep adopting AI while voters reject it, the conflict will ultimately be resolved in the political arena rather than the market.

## Quote

> "AI's do not have rights, feelings or consciousness. We must not train them to act as though they do." — Mustafa Suleyman
