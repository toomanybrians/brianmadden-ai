# OpenAI proposed cutting Cursor off from its models on November 12. The 5-prompt audit I use tells me what I'd lose…

## Insights

- OpenAI is set to cut Cursor off from its models on November 12, triggered by SpaceX's acquisition of Cursor and OpenAI's resulting lack of confidence in the new ownership — an example of AI access being revoked for reasons entirely outside the end user's control.
- The piece frames three companies' current strategies as answers to a "rent vs. own" question about AI infrastructure: OpenAI is trying to own more of the full AI system/stack, NVIDIA is selling the underlying hardware every competitor needs, and Anthropic is spreading investments across multiple competing companies rather than betting on one.
- Central claim: the model itself is the replaceable component, and access to it can disappear overnight; what's actually hard to replace is the accumulated "working context" — months of preferences, files, and project history — that a given AI has built up about a user's work.
- Argues that end users make the same rent-vs-own bet daily, in small ways: every file uploaded, preference saved, or account connected to a single AI provider increases lock-in and risk if that access is cut off.
- The author spends over $600/month across three AI plans as a deliberate strategy, and proposes a practical "provider exit test" — a 30-minute check on a real project to determine whether the work could be picked up by another AI provider or is effectively trapped.

## Quote

> The harder thing to replace is everything the model has learned about your work.
