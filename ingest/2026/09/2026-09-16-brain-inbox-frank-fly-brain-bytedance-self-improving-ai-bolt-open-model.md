# Frank Fly Brain 🦟, ByteDance Self-Improving AI 🔄, Bolt Open Model 💡

## Insights

- Google and HHMI Janelia published a complete connectome (wiring map) of a fruit fly's nervous system — 166,700 neurons and 125 million connections — and researchers simulated it in software to drive a physical walking robot, translating simulated neural activity into motor commands; the same simulated brain previously played Doom and Chrome Dino.
- The connectome is explicitly framed as "a snapshot of dead wiring, not a mind" — the simulation uses simplified neuron models layered on real biological structure, not a living or conscious system.
- ByteDance and Tsinghua published a mapped 5-stage roadmap toward "self-improving AI" — systems that rewrite their own code/architecture — though the newsletter gives no detail on what the stages actually are.
- A "Recurrent Looped Transformer" (RLT) design proposes replacing fixed-layer transformer architectures with looped layers that build internal memory as sequence length grows, aiming to scale reasoning depth without generating more tokens (and thus without proportional cost increases) — but this is described as an untested design spec, not a validated product.
- Separately noted: a growing access divide between developers with unlimited AI token budgets and those unable to afford even $20/month tools — framed as an emerging economic split in who can practically build with AI.
- Multiple items point to open/crowdsourced and self-hosted alternatives to proprietary AI tooling (an open-source Codex-like coding agent that reuses ChatGPT quota instead of a separate metered limit; an uncensored, fully local vision model with 665K downloads), signaling continued momentum toward decentralizing control of AI infrastructure away from single vendors.
