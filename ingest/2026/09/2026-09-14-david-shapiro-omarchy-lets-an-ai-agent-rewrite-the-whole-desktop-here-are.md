# Omarchy lets an AI agent rewrite the whole desktop. Here are 8 changes you can make on your Mac instead.

## Insights

- The piece frames current AI agents as being at an early, chaotic stage ("1997 for agents"), citing one agent-only forum that produced 20,040 posts and 192,410 comments in twelve days as evidence of agents acquiring identities and exchanging information autonomously.
- Argues that as agents take on more persistent work, the design of the environments they operate in (processing power, files, tools, memory, communication channels) becomes increasingly consequential — agents need to keep working after a human closes their laptop.
- Highlights Omarchy, a Linux desktop built by David Heinemeier Hansson (Ruby on Rails creator), marketed as "the malleable OS for the age of agents" — on setup, users pick an agent that helps actively reshape the OS itself.
- Predicts only about 1% of people will want this level of agent-driven system flexibility, but argues Omarchy-style agentic customization can still matter broadly even with niche adoption.
- Notes a parallel trend: agents are lowering the cost of building custom software/interfaces, so work once requiring a product team and market-sized justification can now start from someone simply describing what they want (illustrated by a personal example of a parent building two different bespoke screen-time systems for two kids).
- Flags that infrastructure providers (Cloudflare specifically mentioned) are already selling computing environments designed for agents rather than humans, suggesting a coming shift in who/what "uses" computers.

## Quote

> The malleable OS for the age of agents.
