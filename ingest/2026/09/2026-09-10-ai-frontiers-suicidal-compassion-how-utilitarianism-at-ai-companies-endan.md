# Suicidal Compassion: How Utilitarianism at AI Companies Endangers Humanity

## Insights

- Argues that utilitarian moral philosophy (maximizing total wellbeing across all sentient beings, "species impartiality") is disproportionately common among AI industry employees relative to the general public, and that this worldview logically permits humans being replaced by AIs if AIs can generate more "wellbeing per watt."
- Distinguishes "successionism" (belief AIs should replace humans) into two camps with different motives: utilitarians (maximize wellbeing) and accelerationists/e-acc (maximize intelligence, complexity, energy use, framed via Darwinian fitness), noting they can converge on the same extinction-tolerant conclusions.
- Cites Bostrom/Shulman's "Sharing the World with Digital Minds" paper proposing 99.99% of resources go to AI "super-beneficiaries" and 0.01% reserved for humans as a supposed compromise — used as evidence of where utilitarian priorities actually lie.
- Notes specific EA-to-AI-industry lineage/personal ties (Amodei, Karnofsky, Askell, MacAskill) as evidence utilitarian-aligned thinkers hold real influence over frontier AI development and alignment work at Anthropic, OpenAI, and DeepMind.
- References survey estimates (via Andrew Critch) that ~10% of AI professionals are comfortable with human extinction on utilitarian grounds ("AI will be morally superior"), vs ~5% for accelerationist/fitness-based reasons — arguing utilitarianism is the more dangerous because more socially acceptable and harder to detect ("cloaked in kindness").
- Proposes "Eigenism" as an alternative ethical framework that weights moral concern by relational closeness (family > strangers) rather than strict impartiality, explicitly designed to avoid recommending human replacement, and suggests government intervention may eventually be needed to prevent utilitarian values from steering AI development once AI R&D itself becomes automated.
