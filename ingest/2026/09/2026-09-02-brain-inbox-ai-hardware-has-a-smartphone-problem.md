# ⚙️ AI hardware has a smartphone problem

## Insights

- Many AI hardware products (AI pens, AI earbuds, smart devices) largely duplicate functions a smartphone already performs via voice recording, transcription, and AI apps — undermining their value proposition.
- Example: the Flowtica Scribe ("AI pen," $159–$209 plus $10–$20/month subscription tiers) records and transcribes conversations, but doesn't do anything a phone with the right apps couldn't already do.
- Plaud's new AI earbuds ($249, rivaling AirPods Pro pricing) record/transcribe calls and meetings and include an eSIM to bypass needing a phone nearby — again raising the question of what problem is uniquely solved.
- These devices tend to be expensive because of embedded hardware (processors, microphones) plus ongoing subscription costs, despite offering marginal gains over existing phone-based workflows.
- The article argues the "AI hardware" category risks becoming diluted/AI-washed, similar to how embedded algorithmic features (e.g., decades-old washing machine sensors) could retroactively be called "AI" by loose standards.
- The author argues the more promising direction is hardware that captures context phones can't easily get — specifically visual/environmental context (e.g., smart glasses) — since audio context is already easy to capture with a phone, but visual context is a persistent friction point worth solving.

## Quote

> That's the kind of thing we need out of AI devices: something you can't easily do with your phone.
