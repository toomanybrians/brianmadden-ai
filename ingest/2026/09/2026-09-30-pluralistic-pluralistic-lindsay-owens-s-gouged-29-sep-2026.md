# Pluralistic: Lindsay Owens's "Gouged" (29 Sep 2026)

## Insights

- The book "Gouged" (Lindsay Owens, Groundwork Collaborative) frames a shift from posted "fair prices" (cost-plus, originating with Quaker merchants) to algorithmically personalized "market prices" set to extract the maximum each individual will bear.
- It traces this to a decades-long Chicago School (Milton Friedman) project that used crises as windows to install policies removing price/wage floors — Friedman's stated strategy was to "keep ideas lying around" until crisis created an opening.
- Surveillance pricing and "algorithmic wage discrimination" are described as mirror-image scams: the same data infrastructure used to charge customers more is used to pay workers less, based on inferred desperation or willingness to pay.
- Example cited: a Groundwork-affiliated report found shift-work apps used by nurses cross-reference credit/debt data via data brokers to offer lower wages to more financially desperate workers.
- The gig economy is characterized as the "logical trajectory" of this dynamic — employers can observe all workers' pay history and behavior, while workers remain isolated and uninformed about each other's terms or their own market value.
- Instacart was found (per a Groundwork study) to use surveillance data to raise prices 20%+ based on inferred willingness/ability to pay; the company said it would stop after publication.
- The review criticizes the book's final chapter for offering only individual/consumer-level remedies (privacy hygiene, choosing "clean" vendors) rather than structural policy proposals, despite the book's own diagnosis that the problem is systemic, not individual.

## Quote

> Every scam to make you pay more has a mirror-image scam that ensures you are paid less.
