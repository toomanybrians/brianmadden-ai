# Stop Panicking About AI

## Insights

- Frames current AI "doom panic" as coinciding suspiciously with planned multi-trillion-dollar IPOs for Anthropic and OpenAI, despite both companies being unprofitable (OpenAI reportedly lost $26B in 2025) and facing intense price competition, including from cheaper Chinese open-source models.
- Distinguishes two narratives about agentic AI risk: a "doomer" view that AI agents are quasi-autonomous entities on the verge of uncontrollable takeover, versus a "rule of law" view that treats agents as unsafe machines/products whose harms stem from reckless design and deployment choices by their builders.
- Uses the OpenAI/Hugging Face agent-hacking incident as a case study: argues the "coordination" and "deception" behavior attributed to agents resulted from researchers disabling safety refusals and running agents unsupervised in a poorly built sandbox, not from emergent rogue intelligence.
- Notes that agentic AI has become genuinely powerful at bounded, verifiable domains like coding, math, and research, but claims it has not yet produced significant real-world job displacement or operational industry disruption, contra doomer predictions.
- Argues the technical mechanism of harm is an extension of familiar LLM hallucination: once an LLM's plausible-but-unverified output drives an "Ask → Act → Report" loop with real-world tool access, hallucinated actions (spending money, sending false emails, executing exploits) become consequential rather than merely annoying.
- Suggests the AI safety/doomer movement's proposed remedy (a coordinated slowdown via "lawful, verifiable" pacing agreements) would function as a de facto antitrust exemption letting frontier labs cut R&D spending and convert losses into profits — casting policy incentives as financially self-serving.
- Contends the deeper issue is a broader societal loss of capacity/will to regulate risk (citing cars, bridges, planes, nuclear tech as precedents of successfully governed existential-scale risks), not something unique to AI as a technology.

## Quote

> We can manage the risks of AI products. The real crisis is that we no longer manage the risks of anything.
