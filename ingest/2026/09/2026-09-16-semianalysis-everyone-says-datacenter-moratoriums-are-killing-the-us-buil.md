# Everyone Says Datacenter Moratoriums Are Killing the US Buildout. We disagree

## Insights

- SemiAnalysis's Datacenter Industry Model forecasts +38GW of US datacenter IT capacity delivered in 2027 (more than double 2026), and this forecast has barely moved over 12 months despite the moratorium wave.
- Of ~20GW of planned capacity sitting inside local moratorium boundaries, only 1,525MW (7.6%) is judged genuinely delayed — three projects (AWS Ohio, a Pennsylvania powered-land campus, a Colorado site) account for nearly all of it.
- A checklist of ~10 conditions must ALL hold for a moratorium to actually shift a project's delivery date (parcel coverage, still in force, timeline overlap, use coverage, no redesign option, no carve-outs, etc.) — missing any one renders the moratorium irrelevant to that project.
- Moratoriums are largely a low-cost political signaling tool ahead of November 2026 elections (e.g., NY Gov. Hochul, TX Gov. Abbott bypassing legislatures) rather than durable policy — 13 statewide restriction bills died in 2026 with almost none reaching a floor vote.
- Public sentiment is notably more negative toward datacenters specifically (46% unfavorable) than toward AI broadly (46% favorable, 34% unfavorable), suggesting local infrastructure backlash is distinct from general AI sentiment.
- Texas's ERCOT interconnection pause adds only 3-4 months of delay for grid-dependent projects but is offset by accelerating behind-the-meter (BTM) development, which SemiAnalysis frames as a broader structural shift — BTM projected to power over half of new US datacenter capacity from 2028, with 75GW of firm BTM equipment orders already placed.

## Quote

> To be 100% clear, we think moratoriums COULD delay the buildout, but they just aren't yet at scale.
