# The Overhang

## Insights

- Mollick argues most public debate focuses on future AI risk/capability while ignoring that *current* models (GPT-6 Astra, Fable 5.1) already reliably perform weeks' worth of human work when properly guided — the "capability overhang" is the gap between what models can do and what almost anyone actually does with them.
- Demonstrated via examples: an AI converting the text-only game Zork into a 3D action-adventure (inventing visuals for a grue, a white house, combat sequences) and reconstructing Umberto Eco's 30,000-book library in 3D from incomplete video/photo evidence, marking each book as certain/guess/unknown.
- In an unprompted book-trailer task, the AI independently chose to use Blender, wrote a script with jokes, generated voice/music/sound, and produced a film in 45 minutes — showing autonomous tool use and creative judgment, using only a fraction of a paid ChatGPT token budget.
- Proposes four human advantages still differentiating value in AI collaboration: deep knowledge (expert intuition enabling both diagnosis of AI errors and a shift from doing work to managing it), wide knowledge (cross-domain literacy needed to prompt AI toward non-default approaches, e.g. avoiding generic design "eyebrows"), taste (the scarce resource now that generation is cheap — selecting/rejecting AI outputs), and agency (proactively testing unmapped AI capabilities rather than waiting to be told what's possible).
- Cites unspecified Anthropic research indicating that user expertise affects not just quality but quantity of useful output an AI produces.
- Frames the core societal task as developing models of AI-human work that enhance rather than only replace labor, since capability-driven change is "inevitable" even if labs paused training — but the *character* of that change remains an open, shapeable choice.

## Quote

> The capability overhang, the gap between what these models can do and what almost anyone is doing with them, is an opportunity.
