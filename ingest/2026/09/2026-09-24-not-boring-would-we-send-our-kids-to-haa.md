# Would We Send Our Kids to HAA?

## Insights

- The Horowitz Andreessen Academy (HAA), co-founded and led by Gagan Biyani, is framed as a residential, SF-based alternative to college for ambitious young people, oriented around building real things with peers rather than coursework.
- The authors argue AI is eliminating the "safe career path" logic that traditionally justified specific educational choices: AI is already tackling hard open math problems, threatens to automate document-drafting/precedent-finding work in law, and leaves medicine as one of the few fields protected mainly by licensing rather than by durable skill scarcity.
- Their conclusion from this uncertainty is that no set of schools or degrees can guarantee a safe career into the 2040s–2050s, so the practical parenting/educational strategy shifts from planning a "safe" path to exposing kids broadly and letting them go deep on whatever genuinely excites them.
- They frame real, hands-on building experience (not abstract coursework) as the differentiator young people will need, citing examples of 19-20 year olds handling adult-level responsibility on a16z's team and young founders who spent formative time (e.g., during COVID) self-teaching via unstructured exploration (YouTube, obsessive practice).
- The piece treats HAA as a bet on an emerging alternative model of talent development — a "Nashville for Nerds" — that pairs obsessive young builders with peers, mentors, and stakes earlier than traditional college offers, though the authors don't claim it will replace universities.
- They explicitly note this isn't a neutral trend-spotting exercise but a live personal question for parents, given how AI has scrambled which skills/careers will still be valuable by the time today's young children enter the workforce.

## Quote

> There is no safe career path today, let alone one that will remain safe for twenty years.
