# The Ads Model for Prompts Vertically Integrates AI

## Insights

- Meta launched a two-tier API pricing structure for its new open model (Muse Spark): a "Standard" tier with zero data retention at full price, and a "Contributor" tier offering ~92-95% discounts in exchange for the right to train future models on customer prompts/completions.
- This makes explicit an economic trade that was previously implicit — consumer AI subscriptions already granted training rights by default, effectively subsidizing compute costs with data; Meta is now formalizing this per-token as a priced "data dividend."
- At enterprise scale (e.g., 1B tokens/day), the price gap between private and data-sharing tiers amounts to roughly $454,000/year — framed as the effective market value Meta places on customer data access.
- The strategic logic: public web data is exhausted, so frontier model gains now depend on post-training data, RLHF, and real usage/reasoning traces; buying this directly via subsidized inference bypasses the ~$10B/year third-party data-labeling/human-labeling market.
- Analogized to how search/social platforms vertically integrated ad supply chains by capturing behavioral data directly from users — Meta is claimed to be doing the same for AI training data via its inference network, calling it a "self-funding data flywheel."
- Argument that compute pricing has shifted from a pure infrastructure cost to a currency directly tradable for training data/tokens needed for frontier model development.

## Quote

> Compute is no longer sold simply as an infrastructure utility. It has become a currency traded directly for the training tokens needed to build the next frontier model.
