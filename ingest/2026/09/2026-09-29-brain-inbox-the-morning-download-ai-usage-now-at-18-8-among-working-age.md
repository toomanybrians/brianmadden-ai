# The Morning Download: AI Usage Now at 18.8% Among Working-Age People

## Insights

- Microsoft's Q2 data puts global AI usage among working-age people at 18.8%, up ~1 point from Q1; usage is nearly twice as high in the global North (28.8%) as the global South (16.2%).
- National AI usage doesn't track national AI development: UAE (73.3%) and Singapore (64.3%) lead, while the US ranks 21st (33%) and China 67th (17.5%) despite driving AI development.
- Microsoft's Ferres attributes high UAE/Singapore usage to top-down national narratives framing AI as progress, rooted in those countries' historical experience of tech-driven productivity gains decades ago.
- US usage surprised researchers on the low side; Ferres links it partly to growing negative public perception of AI over time, plus the sheer scale/complexity of the US and China as diffusion factors.
- Within the US, AI diffusion is fastest in small coastal university towns (e.g., Williamsburg, VA) and in Texas — not in California/Washington tech hubs.
- Global North usage skews toward consumer uses (shopping, search, feedback); global South usage, with younger populations, skews toward learning.
- Framed as analogous to past infrastructure buildouts (telecom, electrification): historical zones of economic power tracked tech diffusion rates, suggesting AI diffusion patterns could reshape future global economic standing.
