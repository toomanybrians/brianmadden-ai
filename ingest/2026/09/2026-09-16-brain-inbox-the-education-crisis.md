# The Education Crisis

## Insights

- OECD's PISA 2025 report (nearly 800,000 students, 91 countries) shows science, math, and reading scores at record lows since 2015, with reading down 31 points — roughly 1.5 years of schooling; declines predate COVID, with reading peaking in 2012 and math/science in 2009.
- The OECD attributes the decline to increased screen time and less reading for pleasure, with a rising share of "hasty readers" who skim text and answer comprehension questions incorrectly; skills in evaluating information and critical thinking are falling fastest.
- This was the first PISA cycle to survey AI/chatbot use: nearly half of students use chatbots regularly for schoolwork, and daily AI users scored 28 points lower in science than non-users (equivalent to ~1.5 years of learning).
- A Wharton experiment with ~1,000 high schoolers found students using ChatGPT did 48% better on practice problems, but scored 17% worse than non-users once the tool was removed for the actual exam — suggesting AI use masks skill loss rather than building it.
- U.S. students perform relatively better internationally but show a widening internal achievement gap between top and bottom performers (science gap now 290+ points), with the White House's classroom-AI executive order flagged as likely to worsen outcomes based on the report's findings.
- Cites Stanford economist Eric Hanushek's estimate that a one-standard-deviation skills gain is worth ~2 points of annual GDP growth, and that the post-2013 performance decline could cost the U.S. $90 trillion in future GDP and cut average lifetime income by 8%.
