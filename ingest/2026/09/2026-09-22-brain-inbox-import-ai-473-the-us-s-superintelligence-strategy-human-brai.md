# Import AI 473: The US's superintelligence strategy; human brain in a mouse skull; and machine hermeneutics

## Insights

- RAND's "Freedom of Action" strategy for AI superintelligence rests on four pillars: building a human-AI ecosystem (safety, agency-preserving tools, benefit-sharing), an AI-security architecture (compute visibility, verification tools), adapting national security institutions, and building citizen/firm/government capacity to respond to AI-driven disruption.
- RAND frames the current US posture as an "Acceleration" strategy — prioritizing speed and competitive advantage with minimal investment in safety guardrails — one of seven archetypal strategies spanning coexistence, denial, and acceleration families.
- A multi-institution research agenda on "pacing" AI progress (as distinct from halting it) lays out arguments for and against slowing development, and stresses that interventions must account for actor incentives — infrastructure providers (chip/cloud companies) have the strongest incentive to keep accelerating and the most to lose from pacing.
- Mapping of the "uncensored" open-weight AI ecosystem found 3,471 uncensored model repositories on HuggingFace, with Chinese-origin models rising from 1% to 55% of new uncensored production between Q1 2024 and Q2 2025, and models redistributed on average 2.4 times each.
- Toby Ord's modeling of recursive self-improvement argues intelligence explosions face hard resource/algorithmic/data limits, producing a logistic (S-curve) rather than unbounded exponential trajectory — but even a decade-per-year acceleration would still be highly disruptive.
- A fictional "Tech Tales" piece imagines a future profession of "machine hermeneutics" — human scholars who reverse-engineer and interpret scientific breakthroughs made by AI systems that have outpaced human mathematical and scientific understanding, framing this as both an "awesome" and "horrifying" transformation of intellectual work.
