# AI’s Greatest Risk To Humanity? It’s Not Robots Gone Rogue.

## Insights

- Argues that AI "existential risk" narratives (rogue agent swarms, apocalypse odds, whistleblower warnings) function as convenient cover for the major AI labs' commercial and regulatory interests as they race toward IPOs and need massive capital for training costs.
- Frames the safety/doom rhetoric from Anthropic, OpenAI, and allied figures as potentially serving to justify regulation that would freeze out cheaper open-source competitors, effectively forming a "cartel."
- Draws on the documentary "Ghost In The Machine" to argue AI discourse masks a colonialist/eugenicist agenda: a small elite fusing military and tech power to extract from and dominate the majority, using "genius" and IQ-based rhetoric with historical ties to eugenics.
- Cites Cory Doctorow and Dr. Heidy Khlaaf to counter sci-fi doom framing with concrete present-day harms — e.g., a Hugging Face "hack" described as a mundane Python/chatbot failure, and Anthropic's Palantir partnership already deploying unreliable models in lethal military/domestic contexts.
- Contends that focusing on speculative "superintelligence" distracts from present-day crises (healthcare/childcare costs, wildfires, data-center pollution) and from embodied, relational forms of human intelligence that AI cannot replicate, per data scientist Abi Awomosu.
- Concludes the real danger isn't rogue AI but elite messaging designed to convince ordinary people they lack the agency or intelligence to understand or resist the systems being built.

## Quote

> The true danger of AI, perhaps, is the deceptive messaging that leads us to believe we do not have agency or freedom in its system.
