# Could rogue agent swarms take over the entire internet in the next six months?

## Insights

- Authors dispute Dario Amodei's essay (as reported by Axios) suggesting rogue AI agent swarms could take over "the entire internet" within six months, calling the claim vague and implausible.
- They argue "taking over the internet" isn't coherent given decentralization; even outsized players like Cloudflare, Google, and AWS going down simultaneously wouldn't take down the whole internet, and those firms have stronger security than average sites.
- They cite a UK AISI report on "Mythos" finding it could only autonomously compromise small, weakly defended systems — not evidence of internet-scale takeover capability.
- They question motive and economics: attackers have goals and budgets, and destroying the internet would also destroy their own targets and revenue potential; coordination logistics for such an attack aren't explained.
- They note real but narrower risks: many companies still have weak cybersecurity (low-hanging fruit for attacks on individual sites), and as models get cheaper/more efficient and local hardware improves, large-scale attacks not requiring big data centers become more feasible over time.
- Conclusion: current claim isn't credible as stated, especially the six-month timeframe, but it strengthens the case for restricting AI systems that can't be closely monitored and questioning why autonomous agents are being released onto the internet at all.
