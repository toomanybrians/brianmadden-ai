# Weekly Dose of Optimism #209

## Insights

- Runway's Solaris is described as an "Interface World Model" that generates application interfaces frame-by-frame in real time with no underlying code — the rendered image *is* the application, responding directly to clicks/drags rather than executing programmed logic.
- World Labs' Atlas is an "omni world model" trained jointly on text, images, video, camera poses, depth maps, and 3D; it can generate a minute of 1440p video with precise camera control and invent coherent 3D space between unrelated input photos.
- Runway's GWM World 2 generates continuous, interactive real-time video simulations (720p/24fps with audio) that respond to user input, positioned as a step toward general-purpose environment simulation rather than just interface or scene generation.
- OpenAI's newly released model (Astra 6) is reported by early testers to enable coordinated multi-agent behavior inside simulated environments, prompting some observers to invoke "AGI," though the author frames this as models handling tasks humans "shouldn't be doing" rather than a specific claim about labor markets.
- Physical Superintelligence (PSI), an AI-for-physics startup, reportedly redesigned a proposed interstellar mission (Fermi Explorer) using ~10 billion tokens in a week — versus three months/one billion tokens by the original team — producing a novel "perihelion-pump" maneuver later validated by NASA engineers, illustrating AI-driven acceleration of specialized scientific/engineering work.
- Broader theme across the roundup: compute-hungry AI systems (world models, simulation, physics modeling) are driving concrete infrastructure deals, e.g. Google's 396 MW enhanced-geothermal power purchase agreement with Fervo, framed explicitly as power demand tied to AI/data-center growth.
