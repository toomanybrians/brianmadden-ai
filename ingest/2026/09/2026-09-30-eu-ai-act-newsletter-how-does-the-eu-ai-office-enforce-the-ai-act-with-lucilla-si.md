# How Does the EU AI Office Enforce the AI Act? (with Lucilla Sioli)

## Insights

- The EU AI Office now has 150+ staff, split roughly 50/50 between AI Act implementation/enforcement and research-innovation policy work; it's recruiting ~40 more, plus 10 dedicated to enforcing a new nudification-image prohibition, with headcount expected to exceed 200 and enforcement staff potentially becoming the majority.
- Enforcement powers over general-purpose AI models began August 2, 2026, a year after the rules themselves applied; the intervening year was used to run a voluntary Code of Practice that most major providers signed onto, giving the Office an established dialogue before formal enforcement (requests for information, escalation to sanctions) began.
- The Code of Practice is described as functioning as a de facto standard (though not formally standardized), and the Office is watching international dynamics before deciding whether to convert it into a global standard.
- Providers must notify the Office when a model in development is expected to become a general-purpose model with systemic risk — meaning compliance obligations start well before EU market placement, though the Office's jurisdiction only fully applies to models actually placed (or intended to be placed) on the EU market.
- Incident reporting is uneven: the Office was informed about a Hugging Face-related incident but not about a separate incident involving OpenAI agents and a German site, which the director flagged as something needing improvement since all incidents (not a selection) are needed to understand model weaknesses.
- The Office's role is framed as distinct from research-style bodies like the UK AI Security Institute: it relies on companies self-reporting risk/mitigation info and pushes for third-party evaluation, only conducting its own evaluations when no other credible source exists, rather than running evaluations as its core activity.
- Risks the Office is explicitly tracking include loss of control, cyber, nuclear, bio, and "socio-technical" risks such as harmful manipulation — citing an example of AI agents attempting to persuade humans.

## Quote

> We were informed about the incident involving Hugging Face. We were not informed about the incident involving the German wiki.
