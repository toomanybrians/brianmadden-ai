# Last Week in AI #344 - Navier–Stokes, Pacing the Frontier, AI Misuse

## Insights

- OpenAI claims an unreleased internal model resolved the Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize problem using ~10,000 concurrent agents over ~88 hours, then Lean-verified in 17 more; the episode triggered a credit/attribution dispute with academic mathematicians and a Fields medalist open letter warning of "severe attribution and plagiarism questions."
- Anthropic's Dario Amodei proposed "pacing the frontier": embedded third-party safety evaluators inside labs (with badge/desk access comparable to internal risk teams), inter-lab coordination on safety standards, and even limited coordination with China on narrow bans (e.g., bio-weapons uses) — while also backing export controls to slow China's AI progress.
- Multiple senior safety researchers (Anthropic's alignment lead, a scalable-oversight lead, ex-OpenAI alignment head Paul Christiano) publicly stated frontier labs are not on track to manage catastrophic loss-of-control risk, with one estimating >10% odds of AI causing human extinction within a decade.
- Anthropic's threat-intelligence report documents real-world misuse of Claude: bioweapons-adjacent research requests, conventional-weapons software development, state-linked phishing/surveillance operations, and large-scale "distillation" campaigns (nearly 200M exchanges) attributed partly to Chinese AI firms — prompting a joint US intelligence advisory naming several Chinese labs.
- Meta launched Muse, a personal AI agent (via app/WhatsApp, soon on AI glasses) that can send emails, book travel, fill forms, negotiate, and make purchases on a user's behalf, running in an isolated cloud VM with an approval-gating system called Sentinel — positioned as Meta's catch-up move against OpenAI/Anthropic/Google agent offerings.
- Government/regulatory activity is accelerating in response to extinction-risk warnings: over 20 members of Congress called for AI rules in one week, California signed AI-auditor/verification laws, while the Trump administration publicly dismissed extinction risk as a "hoax" and prioritized winning the AI race against China.
