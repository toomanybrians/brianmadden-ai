# The Morning Download: Companies Struggle to Explain Their Own AI Investment Returns

## Insights

- Enterprise AI spend is wildly uneven: Retool's CEO estimates ~90% of tokens spent deliver negative ROI, with the remaining 10% driving most of the value.
- McKinsey research cited: 80% of workers report feeling more productive with AI, but only 6% of companies can show real, measurable financial ROI to investors — a big gap between perceived and provable value.
- Concrete waste examples: an employee-built AI out-of-office responder cost $10,000/day; a sales AI tool cost more to run than the deals it was meant to help close.
- A significant share of "AI" use cases are misapplied — one company found 95% of problems handled by AI agents were better solved with deterministic (non-AI) workflows, saving $20-30M once corrected.
- Executives argue the top differentiator for the 6% seeing real ROI is treating AI adoption as organizational/people change management (upskilling, change management budgets matching tech spend), not just a technology rollout.
- Multiple leaders emphasized keeping humans "in the loop" as a value driver, not just a safety measure — framing AI as amplifying human judgment rather than replacing it, especially in high-stakes domains like cybersecurity.
- Buy-vs-build guidance emerging: "buy to operate, build to differentiate," with off-the-shelf tools covering 80-90% of capability needs and custom builds reserved for differentiation.

## Quote

> "We believe that about 90% of the tokens you're spending are probably negative ROI." — David Hsu, CEO, Retool
