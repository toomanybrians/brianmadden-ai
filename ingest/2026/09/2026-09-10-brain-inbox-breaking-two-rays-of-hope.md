# BREAKING: Two rays of hope

## Insights

- Senator Blumenthal sent a letter to OpenAI demanding details on its agents' role in various hacks and when the company became aware of them, echoing Watergate-style "what did they know" framing.
- Protect Democracy filed a lawsuit seeking to compel the Trump administration to disclose the criteria used to evaluate AI systems before approval.
- The author argues opacity in U.S. AI regulation harms American businesses by creating unpredictability, risks pushing customers toward other vendors, and creates the appearance of favoritism or corruption.
- Regulatory opacity also prevents the scientific community from assessing whether current evaluation criteria are adequate, and can function as a tool enabling authoritarian control.
- Specific concern raised: evaluation criteria may underweight "monitorability" — the ability to observe what AI agents are doing — and GPT-6 Astra reportedly has reduced monitorability yet was approved anyway.
- Frames both the Senate letter and the lawsuit as positive steps toward accountability, given risks raised by current and former Anthropic and OpenAI staff.

## Quote

> The current opacity keeps the scientific community from weighing in on the adequacy... of the current criteria.
