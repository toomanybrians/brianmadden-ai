# Max Nadeau on why ambitious people should start AI safety nonprofits

## Insights

- Coefficient Giving (formerly Open Philanthropy) is launching "Project Tailwind," offering staged funding for new AI safety nonprofits: preseed grants ($200K–$2M), seed grants ($2M–$20M), and grants up to $200M for organizations with an established track record — with even larger grants anticipated within a year or two.
- The primary bottleneck for AI safety progress is identified as talent, not funding — existing organizations reportedly have long lists of high-value projects (including engagements with AI companies and governments) they lack staff to pursue.
- The funder is explicitly borrowing venture-capital tactics — "hits-based giving," rapid-decision "pitch days," and scouts to identify founders — while trying to avoid grantmaking's typical failure mode of over-scrutinizing budgets at the expense of funding upside.
- A described organizational pivot (Redwood Research's leaders downsizing to two people for a year to rethink strategy before rescaling around AI control research) is framed as a model of impact-driven willingness to disrupt sunk-cost momentum, despite backlash and employee costs.
- Nonprofits are argued to have an advantage over for-profit AI safety startups because they can target large, speculative, not-yet-monetizable risks (e.g., AI-enabled pandemics, loss of human control) rather than narrower problems with identifiable paying customers that attract VC funding.
- Work done outside AI companies is argued to be least redundant when it involves independent evaluation/analysis of AI risk (citing METR's misalignment risk assessment vs. company system cards) rather than prosaic safety techniques, which AI companies are better resourced to develop themselves.

## Quote

> I think the most important bottleneck, maybe the only bottleneck, is talent. — Max Nadeau
