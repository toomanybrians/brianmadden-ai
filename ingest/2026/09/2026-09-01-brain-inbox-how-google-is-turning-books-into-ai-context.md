# ⚙️ How Google is turning books into AI context

## Insights

- Google's Gemini Notebook (formerly NotebookLM) added "Expert Intelligence," letting users load ebooks alongside existing sources like PDFs, slides, and notes, then query, summarize, or generate flashcards/audio overviews from that content.
- The feature is currently limited to ebooks purchased through the Google Play Store, excluding the much larger base of readers who buy on Kindle or other platforms — a significant adoption bottleneck.
- Google is offsetting that limitation by giving away one free eligible book per US user while supplies last, apparently to seed usage of the new feature.
- The piece frames this as consistent with NotebookLM's original value proposition: grounding AI answers in user-supplied sources rather than open web scraping, which reduces hallucination risk and builds trust.
- The author expects future expansion beyond Google's own store (e.g., Kindle, Apple Books, academic repositories like MIT Press Open Access) based on user demand, following a common pattern of companies launching features on owned platforms first.
- Broader context in the newsletter: source transparency and grounding are described as increasingly important for AI credibility amid rising public distrust of AI-generated content ("AI slop").
