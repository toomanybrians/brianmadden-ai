# The New R&D Frontier

## Insights

- Frames AI as this generation's "R&D frontier" — comparable to aerospace, nuclear energy, pharmaceuticals, and semiconductors — but notes the funding model has inverted: government funded ~two-thirds of R&D in the Apollo era, while businesses now fund ~three-quarters, with major tech companies' AI-focused R&D spending exceeding NASA's entire budget.
- Argues this shift moves scientific investment from government labs to private companies, but claims this makes AI *more* of a public-interest concern, not less — comparing it simultaneously to medical research (expensive, uncertain, high-impact), defense (national security, global competition), and banking (foundational infrastructure for markets and governments).
- Lists concrete practical policy questions: access to energy, chips, talent, and research infrastructure; predictable rules; protection from misuse, cyberattacks, foreign interference, and supply-chain disruption; ensuring democratic nations lead frontier AI development over non-democratic competitors.
- States a local-benefits principle: communities hosting data centers should get jobs, new energy generation, workforce training, and grid investment, and that families shouldn't face higher electricity bills due to nearby AI infrastructure — positioned as a burden companies/governments must actively address, not just assert.
- Chris Lehane (OpenAI) argues that as model capabilities rise (citing GPT-6 Astra and early signs AI can accelerate AI research itself), the safety bar must rise correspondingly, in a "narrowing window" for establishing safeguards.
- OpenAI is lobbying Congress for mandatory, capability-based national safety requirements, and in the interim is supporting four California bills (independent safety assessments, AI-auditor standards, youth protections, biological-threat safeguards) — including some it previously opposed, now reversed based on recent capability gains.
- OpenAI also states intent to pursue industry-led safety standards with other frontier labs and to advocate for internationally compatible approaches to measuring AI capabilities and determining when development should slow or stop.

## Quote

> As capabilities grow, confidence in safety must increasingly set the pace of AI progress.
