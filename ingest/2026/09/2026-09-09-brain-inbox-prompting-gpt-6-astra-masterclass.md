# Prompting GPT-6 Astra: Masterclass

## Insights

- GPT-6 Astra reportedly performs worse when users carry forward prompting habits built for older, less capable models — accumulated instruction lists, large AGENTS.md files, and stacked Skills can slow it down or cause it to stop early instead of continuing.
- The piece frames Astra as needing fewer explicit instructions than prior models, suggesting a shift where over-specifying prompts becomes a liability rather than a help as models get more capable.
- Astra's technical spec includes a 1.05 million token context window, five selectable reasoning levels, and the ability to change reasoning level mid-conversation rather than fixing it at the start.
- The model integrates computer use, web search, file search, tools, subagents, Skills, plugins, and long-running workflows — positioning it as an agent/automation platform rather than a single-turn chat interface.
- A specific counterintuitive claim raised (not elaborated in this excerpt): a "Low" reasoning setting can outperform "Max" in some cases, implying reasoning-level selection is task-dependent rather than always-maximize.
- The source frames the shift as moving beyond "prompt engineering" toward workflow/settings design — deciding which features, settings, and context configurations to use rather than crafting clever wording.

## Quote

> Some instructions that helped older models can now slow Astra down, waste context, make it test too much or cause it to stop when you expected it to continue.
