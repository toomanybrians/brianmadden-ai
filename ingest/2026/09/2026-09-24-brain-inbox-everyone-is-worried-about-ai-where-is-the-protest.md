# Everyone Is Worried About AI. Where Is The Protest?

## Insights

- A Politico poll found a majority of Americans worried about AI safety, with no meaningful partisan split, and nearly half favoring a pause on AI development — yet Anthropic publicly validated a whistleblower's claim of a 10% chance of AI causing mass human extinction within ten years.
- Despite broad public concern, turnout for anti-AI protest is minimal: a "Stop The AI Race" protest at Dreamforce drew fewer than 50 people, and the group's largest-ever US anti-AI protest (this past July) drew only 400.
- The author argues AI safety isn't one issue but several bundled together — job displacement/economic power consolidation, mass surveillance and military use, misinformation, and environmental costs from data centers — and this breadth may be diffusing organized resistance rather than galvanizing it.
- Rhetorical framing is identified as a deliberate tactic: language modeled on the "Space Race" presents AI advancement as inevitable and a national duty, and calls for regulation are reframed as US-vs-China national security issues, which the author says makes resignation feel like acceptance.
- Local, place-specific organizing (e.g., against data centers in Wisconsin) is described as more effective and higher-turnout than symbolic protests in San Francisco, suggesting decentralized, localized action may be a more viable resistance model than one unified movement.
- NVIDIA's CEO is cited opposing any new AI laws or slowdowns, arguing speed and safety aren't mutually exclusive — positioned in the piece as directly at odds with majority public sentiment in the poll.
- The piece notes AI and data center policy is becoming a live electoral issue, with candidates for state and federal office centering it in campaign platforms this fall.
