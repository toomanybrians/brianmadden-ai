# Attacker vs. Defender AI Advantage

## Insights

- Argues attackers will hold a durable AI-driven advantage over defenders because defenders are embedded in organizational bureaucracy — a structural/political/process problem, not a technology problem.
- Notes that friction inside effective companies (e.g., change control) isn't inherently bad, but it inevitably slows response time compared to attackers who can move fast and sloppy.
- Frames the dynamic through Theory of Constraints: the full chain from "learning something new" to "implementing it in the system" is where defenders lose time.
- Attackers can operationalize new capabilities in minutes or hours; most businesses take days, weeks, months, or even years to do the same.
- Concludes the fight is fundamentally unfair, with bureaucratic friction as the root cause — AI/tech merely exposes and sharpens an existing structural asymmetry.

## Quote

> This is not a fair fight in the slightest, and the root cause is beaurocratic friction, not technology.
