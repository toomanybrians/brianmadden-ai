# ⚙️ How judgment wins in the age of AI

## Insights

- Rokt's CTO Sam Dozor argues the "experimentation" phase of AI adoption is over for engineering orgs; engineers must now orchestrate teams of AI agents rather than execute tasks directly, or risk falling behind the pace of development.
- The value of being a fast, flawless manual executor (writing/debugging code quickly) has dropped; the scarce, non-automatable skills are judgment, strategy, and client/customer empathy.
- Because AI absorbs tedious execution work, Rokt can skip junior "grind" training and move people directly into systems design and product strategy training.
- Dozor suggests AI could compress organizational career ladders into fewer rungs, letting people differentiate by interest/aspiration rather than accumulated technical skill — Rokt has already merged engineer/PM/designer roles into a single "builder" title.
- Adoption's main obstacle was cultural/psychological, not technical: overcoming employee fear and skepticism required deliberately building excitement rather than anxiety around AI.
- Rokt observed a "J-curve of productivity" — a temporary productivity dip while employees learned to work with AI, followed by a sharp output increase once trained.

## Quote

> Some people fell in love with the manual mechanics of building and testing software. It's those with high-level product and commercial aspirations... that ultimately will be the winners.
