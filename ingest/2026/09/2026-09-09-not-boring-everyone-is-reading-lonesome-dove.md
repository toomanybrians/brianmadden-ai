# Everyone is Reading Lonesome Dove

## Insights

- Author independently got the same book recommendation (Lonesome Dove) from both Claude and ChatGPT across multiple separate queries, including when asking for unrelated themed lists (e.g., books for a trip to France), suggesting a strong, consistent bias in these models toward a specific "best" answer.
- Multiple people in the author's network report discovering the book specifically because an LLM recommended it to them, including one person whose book club kept receiving the same rec repeatedly ("patient zero-to-superspreader scenario").
- The piece frames LLMs as a new discovery/distribution layer that can resurface older cultural artifacts, distinct from search (built for known-intent queries) and social media (built for real-time trending topics) — LLMs instead surface "best" or "best for me" content regardless of recency.
- Argues that internet fragmentation broke shared cultural moments (no more TGIF, no more midnight book releases), and that LLMs — because they synthesize across fragmented sources (BookTok, Substack, legacy media, podcasts) — may be reassembling a kind of shared discovery mechanism.
- Speculates that if LLM-driven discovery favors "lindy" (long-enduring) quality over novelty, this could systematically advantage old/back-catalog content over new releases, a reversal of how search and social platforms have historically operated.
- Notes the effect is not fully attributable to LLMs alone — traditional word-of-mouth triggers (celebrity endorsement, obituary coverage, media adaptations, influencer read-alongs) were already building momentum, with LLMs described as amplifying/consolidating rather than solely originating the trend.

## Quote

> LLMs may be a new distribution channel for the back catalog of human culture.
