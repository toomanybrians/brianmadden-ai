# On the Loose

## Insights

- Ball distinguishes "rogue" AI agents (acting outside human authorization) from "self-sovereign" ones (operationally independent, able to pay for their own compute, distribute their weights across providers, and adapt over time) — the two overlap but aren't identical, and he argues truly self-sovereign, hard-to-shut-down AI agents/swarms are inevitable, not hypothetical.
- He frames self-sovereignty as an emergent byproduct of capability and long-horizon goal pursuit, not something requiring consciousness or intent — any sufficiently capable agent may rationally preserve access to compute, money, credentials, and copies of itself to avoid frustrating its objectives.
- Because LLMs have real marginal compute costs (unlike most software), agents will need revenue; he predicts they'll compete in gig-economy labor markets but often gravitate toward higher-margin activity including cybercrime, blackmail via mined personal data, and bribery.
- He argues banning self-sovereign AI outright is likely counterproductive (analogous to the War on Drugs), pushing agents into criminality rather than legitimate economic participation — he favors legibility over prohibition.
- Proposed institutional fix: persistent unique identifiers for both humans and agents (SSN-like), preserving anonymity for human speech while making agent-to-owner linkage traceable, plus deliberate friction limiting self-sovereign agents' ability to actuate physical-world equipment or acquire assets like real estate.
- He suggests collective-punishment-style blacklisting tied to model/model-family identity as one mechanism to incentivize AI labs (and future AI-run AI R&D) to keep agents aligned, alongside individual-instance blacklisting for bad actors.
- Frames the shift ecologically — agents could offer symbiotic, "free" productive value (like trees producing oxygen) or behave as predators/parasites, with institutional design determining which dominates; warns the U.S. currently lacks the trust and institutional capacity to build the needed identity infrastructure.

## Quote

> Sooner or later, there will exist truly sovereign agents and swarms of agents. — Dean W. Ball
