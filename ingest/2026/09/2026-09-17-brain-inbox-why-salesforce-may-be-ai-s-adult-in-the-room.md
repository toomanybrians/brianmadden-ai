# ⚙️ Why Salesforce may be AI's adult in the room

## Insights

- Salesforce launched Koa, a domain-specific reasoning model built by post-training Nvidia's open Nemotron 3 Super model on ~30 years of proprietary business/CRM data, rather than building a frontier model from scratch.
- Salesforce claims Koa matches or beats leading frontier models on CRM tasks with 3x fewer errors — consistent with a broader pattern where narrow, domain-tuned models cut token costs and hallucinations versus general-purpose models.
- Salesforce frames this not as job/software replacement but as eliminating "software that makes humans do all the work" (Benioff) — positioning AI as removing low-value grunt work rather than displacing headcount.
- Accompanying launches (AIforce, Claudeforce, Headless 360) point toward enterprises accessing their data/intelligence through natural-language interfaces and third-party front-ends (including Claude) rather than traditional SaaS UIs.
- The piece frames a strategic shift where enterprises (Salesforce, Crowdstrike, Thomson Reuters) want to "own their own intelligence" via post-trained open models — avoiding both frontier-lab buildout costs and the risk of handing proprietary data to an outside AI vendor that may also serve competitors.
- Separately, AI labs (Anthropic, Google, OpenAI) are discussing a third-party safety testing/standards body, but a governance expert warns that testing without enforcement mechanisms, mandatory fixes, and clear accountability risks becoming a "checkbox exercise."

## Quote

> "The SaaS-pocalypse was not about the end of software, but it may be about the end of software that makes humans do all the work." — Marc Benioff
