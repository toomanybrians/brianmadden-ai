# 📈 Monday data: Damming the slop floods

## Insights

- AI-generated content has become normalized across formats: roughly one in three new webpages has some AI-attributable content, and press releases hit nearly 50% likelihood of containing AI-generated text as of June (doubling within a year).
- There's a mismatch between AI content production and consumption: over half of new music submitted to Deezer at peak was fully AI-generated, yet AI tracks capture only 1-3% of streams — unclear if this reflects taste, discovery friction, or something else.
- Pocket FM (AI-powered audio drama app) shows a different pattern of audience acceptance: 93% AI-produced content, catalog grew from 100,000 to 2.5 million hours/year in two years, with revenue improving once listener data was fed back into production — suggesting production method may matter less than output quality in some content categories.
- Distribution and discovery infrastructure is becoming a chokepoint: search, aggregators, AI, and social platforms now account for 37% of UK news consumption vs. just 8 minutes/day direct-to-publisher; Google's AI Overviews cost even large publishers 22% of referral traffic.
- Fraud and authenticity are emerging as a serious verification problem: Deezer found up to 85% of streams on AI-generated tracks were fraudulent, and UMG is suing distributor DistroKid over allegedly passing off mass-generated AI content as legitimate music.
- Investment is heavily skewed toward AI policy/regulation ($240M+ across four funds) versus AI auditing/certification infrastructure, which the piece frames as the underfunded lever that could most shape how AI content floods affect the internet experience.
