# OpenAI cuts image latency 50% while Anthropic maps your job's AI risk

## Insights

- Anthropic released an interactive economic model projecting three 2030 scenarios based on how much AI absorbs task-level work: "modest" (GDP +1.6%, stable wages), "substantial" (AI handles ~half of knowledge work, GDP +8.3%, knowledge-worker wages flatten, forced career switching), and "extreme" (15% annual growth, GDP doubling every 4.5 years, knowledge-worker unemployment at 17.9%, labor's share of GDP falling from 60% to 45%).
- The model's central point: aggregate economic growth from AI does not automatically translate into higher personal earnings — in the extreme scenario, capital owners capture most gains rather than workers.
- In a survey of 10,000+ Americans using the same tool, most respondents' expectations clustered around the "substantial" scenario, with only about 10% anticipating the "extreme" outcome.
- OpenAI released two new image-editing models (GPT-Image-2.5 Flare and Sunburst) whose key advance is localized editing — changing only the targeted element (e.g., swapping a jacket while preserving pose, or background while preserving product) rather than regenerating the whole image; Flare offers ~50% lower latency, Sunburst offers higher precision for critical edits.
- Amazon has built a tool that simulates on the order of 1,000 synthetic users to predict A/B test winners with 75–90% accuracy before real users are exposed to the test.
- Separately, researchers found reasoning models exhibit fractal-like sensitivity to starting conditions when solving hard problems, causing large (up to 10x) variance in reasoning length/token cost between similar prompts — framed as an inherent tradeoff of deep problem-solving rather than a fixable inefficiency.
