# Steve Ballmer Proves a Tech Oligarch’s Ruthlessness Shouldn’t Be Mistaken for Smarts

## Insights

- Uber cut 10% of staff (~3,300 people) and Xbox previously made mass layoffs, both citing the need to "remove layers" of management — a recurring corporate rationale for restructuring that avoids specifics about what work is actually being eliminated or automated.
- Sam Altman told Time he wants "a personal robot" to handle tasks he doesn't want to do, while separately criticizing Meta's AI glasses as uncomfortable due to their camera/light — signaling how AI leaders frame ambient AI/robotics as personal-convenience tools even as they critique competitors' implementations.
- Anthropic is cultivating a friendly relationship with the Trump administration: Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick has been accommodating toward the company, and co-founder Tom Brown publicly praised Trump's comments framing AI data centers as a source of prosperity.
- A federal judge ruled Google is running an illegal ad-tech monopoly (the second such ruling in a year, following a separate search-monopoly finding) but declined to force a breakup — Google's regulatory VP called the outcome a win, suggesting minimal near-term consequences for dominant tech platforms.
- The piece's central argument — that a tech billionaire's ruthlessness (via alleged NBA salary-cap circumvention) was widely mistaken by media and peers for shrewdness or intelligence — offers a case study in how wealth and aggression get conflated with competence among tech-adjacent leadership circles.
