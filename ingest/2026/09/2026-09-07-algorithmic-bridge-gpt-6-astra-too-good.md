# GPT-6 Astra: Too Good

## Insights

- OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra launch reportedly beat Anthropic's Fable 5.1 across benchmarks, saturating tests like FrontierMath and ARC-AGI 3, and generated OpenAI's most-liked launch post ever, with OpenAI president Greg Brockman calling it the entry to a "new era of artificial general intelligence."
- Author argues capability benchmarks have become meaningless once frontier models reliably saturate them — the analogy given is judging chess engines by whether they can beat Magnus Carlsen, when all modern engines already do.
- The claim is that "reality" is now the only valid benchmark left — real-world impact (GDP growth, job transformation, solving poverty/disease/war, novel scientific output) rather than paper capability.
- Central argument: superintelligence is not omnipotence — models remain bound by "human inertia," meaning adoption is capped by how fast slow, bottlenecked humans can absorb and act on AI capability, regardless of how capable the model is.
- Coins the phrase "the world moves at the speed of meat" — even a maximally capable AI given full personal context on a user still can't change that person's life faster than the person can process and act on its guidance.
- Predicts that near-term, visible real-world transformation from GPT-6 Astra (or successors) will be minimal — expects the physical/social landscape to look largely unchanged for years despite capability leaps.

## Quote

> A superintelligence is still subordinated to the laws of physics, which include but are not limited to, human inertia.
