# Speech Transcript: From digital workspace to AI-powered work

## Insights

- Frames enterprise AI adoption as already underway rather than something to plan for: AI enters organizations through official pilots, personal chatbot accounts, embedded SaaS copilots, and unmonitored proofs of concept simultaneously, making "strategy" the wrong starting question — visibility into existing use is.
- Proposes three concurrent "waves" rather than sequential phases: (1) governing AI already present via existing access/identity/governance/control tooling, reconfigured rather than replaced; (2) building a managed "canonical knowledge base" layer between raw inputs and desired outputs, because feeding raw files to models produces unreliable results; (3) AI becoming portable — onto local devices and into an organization's own region via open-weight models, independent of any central vendor datacenter.
- Argues visible knowledge-work artifacts (documents, email, transcripts) represent only ~20% of actual knowledge work; the remaining ~80% is unwritten judgment, reasoning, and informal process that AI must be pointed at deliberately, since it's invisible in existing data.
- Identifies agent identity/permissions as the one genuinely new governance problem: AI acting under a human's inherited credentials is unsafe by default, distinct from prior EUC/security playbooks.
- Describes "forward-deployed engineers" (a term used by AI labs/hyperscalers this year) as a rebrand of 1990s business-transformation consulting, and claims this function can be staffed internally rather than requiring outside hires.
- Positions the vendor's own role as unchanged for decades: delivering, governing, and securing whatever "existing work" runs on, now applied to AI rather than prior computing shifts (DOS, client-server, web, cloud).
