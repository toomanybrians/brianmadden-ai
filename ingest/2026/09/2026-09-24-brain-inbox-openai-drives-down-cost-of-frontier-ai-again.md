# ⚙️ OpenAI drives down cost of frontier AI again

## Insights

- OpenAI released GPT-6 Sol and Luna, priced roughly 50% cheaper per million tokens than prior versions (Sol: $2/$10 per million input/output tokens vs. $4/$20 previously; Luna: $0.10/$0.50 vs. $0.20/$1.20), while claiming improved factuality, coding, computer use, and alignment.
- GPT-6 Sol reportedly beats OpenAI's own prior models and Anthropic's Opus 5 on benchmarks for business-workflow automation, agentic tasks, and real-codebase software engineering, even though OpenAI still calls its pricier "Astra" model its best overall.
- Anthropic's Opus 5.5 launched days after Anthropic publicly called for slowing frontier development; it's priced 40% cheaper on typical workloads, over 30% faster, and includes safety routing that reroutes risky cyber/bio requests to older, more restricted model versions unless the requester is specially vetted.
- Cache-read pricing for Opus 5.5 dropped 60% to $0.20/million tokens, notable because Anthropic says cache reads dominate agentic and coding workloads — a signal of where enterprise AI cost pressure is concentrated.
- The newsletter frames both releases as labs "reading the tea leaves" on cost: enterprises increasingly resist paying premium prices for frontier capability, especially as agentic deployments consume far more tokens than chat use.
- Separately, Qualcomm's new Snapdragon chips can run 30B-parameter MoE models on-device (up from ~4B previously), aimed at enabling on-phone agentic orchestration without cloud reliance — positioned as infrastructure for ambient, agentic computing.
