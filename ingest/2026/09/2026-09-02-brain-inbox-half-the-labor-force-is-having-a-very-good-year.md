# Half the Labor Force Is Having a Very Good Year

## Insights

- Using a percentile-vs-own-history method (ranking each group against its own 2003–present record rather than comparing absolute unemployment levels across groups), the author finds non-college workers (22+) are near multi-decade employment strength while young college graduates have deteriorated sharply since 2024 — a divergence that only appeared recently after two decades of the groups moving together.
- The split is not really about education per se but about task type: sorting by occupation instead of degree shows knowledge work/desk jobs at the top and physical/in-person work (production, construction, transportation, healthcare support, protective services) at the bottom — the sharpest divide is desk vs. non-desk, not degree-holder vs. non-degree-holder.
- Within non-graduates, task type still matters: sales/office non-grads are the weakest subgroup (40th percentile), while management/skilled professional and blue-collar non-grads are doing much better (9th and 22nd percentiles respectively) — meaning "some college, office job" is now a uniquely weak position.
- The weakness is also geographic/density-linked: non-metro America and much of the industrial Midwest/Southeast are near best-in-decades employment levels, while large metros (where office/knowledge work concentrates) are recovering more slowly — the weak spot is specifically "educated, metropolitan, desk-based" labor.
- Some of the non-graduate strength may be supply-driven (immigration enforcement shrinking the less-educated labor pool, plus a construction/factory/data-center building boom absorbing manual labor) — but this supply explanation doesn't account for rising unemployment among graduates, which the author flags as a separate, unexplained phenomenon.
- Young graduates are a relatively small slice of the labor force (~20 million, about 1 in 8 workers), meaning the widely-discussed "bad job market for grads" narrative may be overgeneralized relative to the broader labor market's condition.

## Quote

> Put the cuts together and the weak spot in this labor market has a precise profile: educated, metropolitan, desk-based.
