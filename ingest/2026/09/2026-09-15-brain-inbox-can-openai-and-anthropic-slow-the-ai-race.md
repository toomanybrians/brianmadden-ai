# ⚙️ Can OpenAI and Anthropic slow the AI race?

## Insights

- Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published "We Must Pace the Frontier," proposing a three-part framework: embedded third-party evaluators inside frontier labs, a democratic coalition of labs coordinating safety standards, and global coordination (including attempts to engage authoritarian governments) to verify compliance.
- Amodei is explicit this isn't a pause — training and technical progress continue, but with added time for alignment/safety verification before release; he calls this a shift from "racing to the bottom" on capabilities to "racing to the top" on safety.
- This stance reportedly contradicts Anthropic's own earlier position that a unilateral pause by one lab wouldn't matter much, since it would just change who's in front rather than create broader industry-wide deliberation.
- Sam Altman told OpenAI staff in an all-hands that OpenAI would consider pacing frontier development, potentially in coordination with other labs — following an August move where OpenAI already paused some internal safety runs on cyber capabilities.
- Two OpenAI figures — new board member Paul Christiano and chief scientist Jakub Pachocki — separately published essays arguing no major lab is currently on track to reduce AI risk to an "acceptable level" and calling for voluntary slowdowns to become normal until shared safety bars exist.
- Other major labs (Google DeepMind, Meta, SpaceXAI) did not respond to requests for comment, leaving open whether the OpenAI/Anthropic move will become industry-wide or remain isolated — with added uncertainty about how Chinese labs would respond to any Western slowdown.

## Quote

> "Progress will still seem fast," but pacing means giving safety and security measures time to catch up. — Dario Amodei (paraphrased framing from essay)
