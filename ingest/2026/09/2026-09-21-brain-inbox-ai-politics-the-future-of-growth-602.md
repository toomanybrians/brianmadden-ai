# 🔮 AI politics & the future of growth ++ #602

## Insights

- Public sentiment toward AI in the US has sharply declined even as adoption grows: Edelman Trust Barometer data cited shows positive sentiment fell from 34% (2020) to 19% (2024), while objection rose to 50%.
- A gap is emerging between elite/business AI enthusiasm and public opinion — over one in six Americans reportedly believe AI will almost certainly destroy humanity, even as adoption continues (a quarter of US adults reportedly use chatbots daily).
- Despite public distrust, businesses keep buying: cited examples include a four-chair barbershop installing an AI receptionist and unanimous continued enterprise AI implementation among IT executives surveyed at a Las Vegas event.
- Economic data cited shows a disconnect between infrastructure spending and measured consumer benefit — near $1 trillion invested in AI infrastructure versus an estimated $172 billion in aggregate US consumer surplus from AI (per Erik Brynjolfsson's research).
- The author frames this sentiment/adoption gap as likely to become a political issue, since business buying continues while voter sentiment sours.
- Cited debate on framing AI: Jaron Lanier argues the language used to describe AI ("being"-like framing) itself cedes ideological ground; Mustafa Suleyman argues AI should not be trained to simulate rights, feelings, or consciousness since it possesses none.

## Quote

> "AI's do not have rights, feelings or consciousness. We must not train them to act as though they do." — Mustafa Suleyman
