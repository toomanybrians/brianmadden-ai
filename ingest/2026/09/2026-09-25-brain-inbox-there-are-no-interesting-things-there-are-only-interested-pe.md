# There Are No Interesting Things; There Are Only Interested People

## Insights

- Argues that "remaining economically valuable" is the wrong question to optimize for in an AI future — in both extreme outcomes (worthless money or redistributed abundance), individual economic value ceases to be the relevant variable.
- Explicitly rejects the "get good at what AI can't do yet" strategy as untenable, since the boundary of automatable skills keeps shifting every few months (cites coding, math, writing).
- Central claim: meaning/interestingness isn't a property of tasks or objects but of the person's orientation — reframes the "find meaning" advice as a category error.
- Identifies relationship-based work — jobs where the value lies in the specific human relationship, not the task performed — as the only category intrinsically resistant to AI substitution.
- Recommends developing "manual competence" (cooking, basic home repair, noticing the physical world) not as literal AI-proofing but as reducing the number of abstracted layers between action and consequence.
- Advises disengaging from AI-accelerationist and doomer commentators alike, framing both as anxiety-driven and practically useless for personal decision-making regardless of whether their predictions are correct.

## Quote

> There are no interesting things; there are only interested people.
