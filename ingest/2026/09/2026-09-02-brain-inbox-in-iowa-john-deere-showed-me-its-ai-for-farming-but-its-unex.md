# In Iowa, John Deere showed me its AI for farming. But its unexpected AI-era boom? Moving dirt for data centers

## Insights

- John Deere's construction/earth-moving business is now benefiting more from the AI boom than its precision-agriculture AI products: Construction & Forestry sales jumped 18% to $3.6B, driven by data center, energy, and infrastructure projects, while Production & Precision Ag sales fell 6%.
- Deere's new generative AI product, "JD," is a chatbot layered onto its existing Operations Center platform, letting farmers query years of accumulated machine/field data (fuel use, operator performance, harvest timing) in natural language rather than through dashboards.
- Most of Deere's actual functional AI (autonomous driving, weed/crop discrimination) is "old-school" machine learning/computer vision, largely stemming from a 2017 acquisition (Blue River Technologies), predating the generative AI wave — generative AI has added a conversational data-query layer, not new core capability.
- Deere's CTO frames the current data center buildout as historically unprecedented compute expansion ("never brought on the compute capacity... in the history of humanity") that will be disruptive/dislocating broadly, not just for Deere.
- Local communities (e.g., Salix and Ames, Iowa) are contesting data center development over farmland loss, electricity rates, water use, and unclear local benefit — even as the industry claims minimal national impact on farmland acreage.
- Deere's next-gen product AI ambition is "end-to-end robotics" — shifting from hand-coded perception+response logic to neural networks that learn the full decision process — but this requires more onboard compute (e.g., Nvidia's Thor chip) than current durable, dust/chemical-exposed machinery can passively cool, creating a hardware engineering bottleneck.

## Quote

> "We've never brought on the compute capacity that we are bringing on in the next two to three years ever in the history of humanity." — Jahmy Hindman, John Deere CTO
