# 🧠 I do not want your brains to rot

## Insights

- Azhar distinguishes "cognitive offloading" (a low-cost, strategic delegation of mental effort) from "cognitive surrender" — an uncritical abdication of reasoning itself — arguing AI's power makes the latter more likely and more widespread.
- He cites a non-peer-reviewed paper claiming a "cognitive divergence": measurable declines in attention span and reading behavior predate AI, and advanced AI accelerates this by encouraging delegation of progressively simpler tasks.
- The mechanism of concern: repeatedly offloading small cognitive tasks to AI weakens the underlying mental practices/muscles needed to sustain cognitive capacity over time.
- Frames the core tension as a possible tradeoff between productivity gains and a loss of selfhood or cognitive identity ("less ourselves").
- Notes the self-aware irony of using an AI-generated visual to illustrate concerns about AI-driven cognitive decline, treating that choice as deliberate and meaningful rather than incidental.

## Quote

> Cognitive offloading is a strategic delegation that costs nothing. Cognitive surrender is something different.
