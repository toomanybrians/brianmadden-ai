# Oppenheimer, Inc.

## Insights

- Galloway frames AI lab leaders (Altman, Amodei, Musk) as an "Algorithmic Oligarchy" capturing outsized economic upside while pushing risks/harms onto the public — comparing their belated risk-warnings to "Dr. Frankenstein begging us to rein in his monster."
- Cites Geoffrey Hinton's framing of two AI risk tiers: near-term misuse risks (job disruption, wealth inequality, deepfakes, cybercrime, embedded bias, bioweapons proliferation) versus longer-term existential risk from systems more intelligent than humans.
- Notably dismisses the "job apocalypse" narrative as "bullshit," distinguishing it from other more credible near-term harms.
- Presents concrete AI-for-good evidence: an AI sepsis-detection model at Cleveland Clinic cut mortality 41% (potentially 140,000+ US lives/year if scaled), plus AI accelerating antibiotic design, pancreatic cancer detection, wildfire detection, and hurricane forecasting.
- Documents AI's role in escalating cybercrime: 1 in 4 breaches this year were AI-enabled (up 56% YoY), ransomware recovery costs up 11% YoY to $1.7M average, global cybercrime costing $10.5T annually — described as "democratizing" attack capability down to nation-state level for small actors.
- Argues regulation is a market/economic stabilizer, not just a safety measure, given AI's outsized weight in the S&P 500 (Mag 7 = one-third of index value) and its contribution to 32% of this year's S&P earnings growth — proposes an international AI regulator modeled on the IAEA, with bilateral US-China authority.

## Quote

> "This is another example of a small number of players capturing staggering economic upside while outsourcing the downside risks/harms to the broader public."
