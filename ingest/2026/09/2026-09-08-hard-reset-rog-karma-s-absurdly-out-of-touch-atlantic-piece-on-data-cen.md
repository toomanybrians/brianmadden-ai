# Rogé Karma’s Absurdly Out-of-Touch Atlantic Piece on Data Centers

## Insights

- Argues that AI companies' public statements urging regulators to "slow us down" should not be taken at face value, framing them instead as a fundraising tactic that leverages public anxiety to attract investor money.
- Challenges the framing of AI risks (e.g., "chatbot-generated bioweapons," rogue agents, autonomous hacking) as inherent to the technology, arguing that these harms trace back to human actors directing or failing to safeguard the systems, not autonomous AI decision-making.
- Cites specific incidents (e.g., an AI Security Institute case, an OpenAI/Hugging Face "hack") as examples where poor human oversight and weak safeguards — not emergent AI agency — caused the reported failures, suggesting some companies may benefit from the resulting media attention.
- Uses Gallup polling data showing 61% of Americans oppose local data center construction, with top reasons being resource strain (50%), quality of life (22%), costs (20%), and pollution (16%) — versus only 4% citing fear of AI "taking over" — to argue grassroots opposition is about tangible local harms, not AI risk per se.
- Critiques the proposal that local data-center opposition should be "channeled" into a global AI non-proliferation movement, arguing this framing dismisses legitimate community concerns as a "distraction" from existential AI risk.
- Draws a historical contrast with anti-nuclear movements, noting they originated from concern about nuclear weapons/war rather than "local environmental concerns," disputing an analogy made in the critiqued piece.

## Quote

> All of the harms he mentioned are not really risks associated with AI, but with human actors.
