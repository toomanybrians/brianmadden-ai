# Evaluators need visibility into how models are trained, because alignment interv

## Insights

- Alignment interventions can suppress the visible symptoms of a problem without addressing its underlying cause, making the model appear safer than it is.
- An evaluator who only examines a finished model has no way to distinguish a genuinely fixed problem from one that has merely been hidden.
- Meaningful evaluation therefore requires visibility into the training process itself, not just output-level testing of the final model.
- The suggested industry goal is "employee-equivalent access" for embedded evaluators — treating them like insiders rather than external auditors.
- Key evaluation questions are procedural: who reviews a flagged issue, and who has authority to halt a training run.
- Answering those process questions requires access to the people involved, not just to model artifacts or documentation.

## Quote

> An evaluator who sees only the final model cannot tell the difference.
