# 🧮 AI's Million-Dollar Math Claim

## Insights

- Mathematician Tristan Buckmaster says OpenAI privately claimed an internal model produced a proof for the Navier-Stokes Millennium Problem ($1M Clay Institute prize), but he has not seen the proof itself — the claim remains unverified.
- Buckmaster and Levent Alpöge did publish AI-assisted results (using Claude and OpenAI models) on Euler and related fluid equations, extending prior human research; Terence Tao called this published work a "remarkable achievement" but that praise doesn't confirm the separate Navier-Stokes claim.
- A dispute has surfaced over the published Euler paper's authorship: Buckmaster alleges pressure to exclude collaborator Alpöge due to his Anthropic affiliation, which OpenAI's Sébastien Bubeck denies, framing it as following academic norms — a concrete case of AI-lab affiliation creating friction in research credit/attribution.
- Stripe's internal AI agent "Kai" is reportedly used weekly by more than 10,000 employees, described in a video interview covering how the company built context, permissions, and monitoring/evaluation infrastructure for it post-prototype — an example of enterprise-scale internal AI agent deployment.
- ChatGPT is testing a feature (limited, unconfirmed broad rollout) that connects to a user's apps (messages, documents, email) to mimic their writing style, raising the standard caveat that matching phrasing doesn't guarantee draft accuracy.
- The piece frames the stakes: a verified AI-assisted solution to a Millennium Prize problem would be evidence of AI producing genuinely original, high-level mathematics, potentially encouraging researchers to tackle other long-stalled problems with AI assistance — though one result wouldn't itself resolve practical applications like turbulence modeling.
