# What Counts as a Moat in the Age of AI?

## Insights

- Classic business moats (patents, proprietary tech, network effects, switching costs, brand) all assumed copying was slow and expensive; AI collapses that cost/time, undermining each one — patents especially, since a frontier model could design around any filed patent in hours.
- Nvidia's CUDA — held up as the strongest software moat in existence — is described by AI researchers on the source's podcast as already breakable: frontier models can now recompile AI code to run efficiently on non-Nvidia chips (AMD), work that previously required specialist engineering teams. CUDA's inference-time moat is characterized as already dead; its training-time moat as having limited lifespan, and most AI workload is shifting to inference.
- Nvidia's countermove (buying Mellanox in 2019 for interconnect) is framed as an example of the only sustainable strategy left: continuously relocating your moat rather than defending a fixed one — "the moat is not a wall you build once, it's a wall you iterate on."
- SaaS/software moats are called especially fragile — any interface-based product can reportedly be cloned by a frontier model in a weekend; switching costs (a 40-year enterprise software moat) evaporate when an agent can do the work itself in minutes.
- Referencing an MIT/Harvard "Coasean Singularity" paper, the piece argues that as AI agents handle search/negotiation/transaction at near-zero cost, customer relationships shift away from whoever owns the storefront toward whoever the agent trusts — a threat to distribution as a moat.
- The two moats claimed to still hold: (1) "speed of reinvention" / organizational metabolic rate — the ability to keep pivoting faster than competitors (cited examples: Nvidia's chip-to-interconnect shift, Tesla's move to robotaxis, SpaceX's move to Starlink/orbital compute); and (2) "capital velocity" — hyperscalers' ability to fund massive compute purchases immediately from existing cash flow and fill it with existing customers, likened to owning oil reserves in a liquid oil market. Brand is offered as a third, narrower moat, holding mainly at the luxury end as a wealth signal, not for commodity goods.
- Draws a historical caution from the 1999 dot-com era: it was largely "second owners" rather than pioneers who captured lasting value, suggesting the same may apply to who profits from the current AI-driven margin compression across industries.

## Quote

> Your moat is only good until the next moat. Jensen is moving Nvidia's moat very intelligently.
