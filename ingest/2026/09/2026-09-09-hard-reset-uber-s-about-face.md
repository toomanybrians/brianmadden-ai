# Uber’s About-Face

## Insights

- Uber is reversing its historic anti-taxi, anti-regulation stance, now partnering with labor unions to push "hybrid network" legislation (e.g., in New Jersey, Washington DC) mandating minimum numbers of human drivers alongside autonomous vehicles.
- The shift is attributed not to genuine concern for driver wages but to Uber's autonomous vehicle program lagging behind Waymo, which is taking ridesharing and delivery market share.
- Uber is simultaneously promoting driver-wage-protection narratives while publicizing its robotaxi launch in London beating Waymo — a contradiction a corporate ethics professor (Alison Taylor) flags as reputationally risky "corporate hypocrisy and flip-flopping."
- Even as it courts unions rhetorically, Uber laid off 10% of its workforce and mandated near-total return-to-office, framed as making operations "simpler and faster" — shares rose after the layoffs.
- The piece frames Uber's underlying motivations as consistent across eras (early taxi-industry hatred, now tactical labor alliances): self-interest, shareholder value maximization, and executive compensation, with personnel continuity (e.g., a former Uber policy head now leads policy at Waymo) illustrating how the same individuals move across now-competing camps.
- A cited NYT report on Uber's legal defense tactics in sexual assault cases is used as evidence of a harsher corporate ethos persisting beneath a recent PR "maturation" effort.
