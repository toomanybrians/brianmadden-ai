# Why Diffusion Will Win AI Inference with Inception Co-Founder and CEO Stefano Ermon

## Insights

- Inception argues diffusion models can outperform autoregressive LLMs on inference by generating tokens in parallel rather than sequentially, improving speed and hardware utilization on standard GPUs.
- The company has extended diffusion architecture — originally developed for images and video — into discrete text and code generation, a nontrivial adaptation given the continuous-vs-discrete modality gap.
- Ermon frames the current AI bottleneck as hardware and latency, not just model capability, positioning diffusion as a structural fix for inference-time constraints rather than a training-time one.
- Inception's Mercury models are already deployed in real-world use cases, including voice agent applications, suggesting diffusion-based inference has practical traction beyond research demos.
- Serving diffusion-based language models at scale reportedly requires a distinct software stack from what's used for autoregressive LLMs, implying infrastructure — not just model architecture — is a differentiator.
- Ermon predicts the next phase of AI competition will center on efficiency (compute/hardware utilization) rather than raw capability gains, with academia continuing to play a role at the frontier of these innovations.
