# The Socket Decides: Who Gets to Build Europe's AI?

## Insights

- The EU is preparing public support for large-scale AI data centre buildout as explicit industrial policy, tying computing capacity directly to Europe's competitiveness against the US and China.
- Grid connection queues, not construction timelines, are becoming the binding constraint on AI data centre siting — local network congestion persists even when national generation capacity is adequate.
- Real-world examples show multi-year gaps: Dutch grid operators warned of no meaningful relief until 2031 and full network expansion not before 2036 in parts of Noord-Holland.
- JLL estimates ~7-year connection waits for 50MW data centres in Frankfurt, Paris, and Northern Virginia, versus 2-3 years in Dallas and Phoenix — a structural timing disadvantage for European (and some US) hubs.
- Connection-queue allocation rules function as de facto selection criteria, deciding which sectors/projects (hospitals, factories, data centres) get prioritized access to constrained electricity capacity.
- Even securing a grid connection doesn't guarantee viability: geopolitical instability (e.g., renewed Iran-related tensions) adds uncertainty to future electricity costs for developers.

## Quote

> A decision about access to the grid becomes a decision about which businesses get to expand.
