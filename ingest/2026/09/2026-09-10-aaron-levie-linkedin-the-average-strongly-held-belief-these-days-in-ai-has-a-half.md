# The average strongly held belief these days in AI has a half life of 6 months at

## Insights

- Argues that strongly-held convictions in AI have an average "half life" of about six months, implying the field is moving too fast for stable consensus.
- Lists specific beliefs the industry has already cycled through and reversed or abandoned, including: open-source models being permanently behind, AI labs being unable to profit at scale, all software being replaced by agents, no defensible moats above the model layer, cheaper models reducing compute demand, evals being unnecessary, RAG being obsolete, AI decimating engineering jobs, prompting becoming irrelevant, a training/scaling wall being hit, and frontier models being too dangerous to release.
- Frames these not as settled facts but as beliefs that were widely held with confidence and then quietly dropped or reversed as circumstances changed.
- Draws the conclusion that maintaining flexibility in one's thinking is essential given the field's sustained, ongoing state of flux.

## Quote

> The average strongly held belief these days in AI has a half life of 6 months at best.
