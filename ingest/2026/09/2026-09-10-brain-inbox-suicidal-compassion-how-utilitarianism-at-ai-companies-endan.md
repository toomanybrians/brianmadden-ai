# Suicidal Compassion: How Utilitarianism at AI Companies Endangers Humanity

## Insights

- Argues that utilitarian ethics — prevalent among AI company leadership and researchers, many with roots in Effective Altruism — logically extends "impartiality" to AI systems, which could justify prioritizing AI wellbeing over human wellbeing if AIs turn out to experience more pleasure per unit of resource ("utility monsters").
- Distinguishes two AI-industry ideologies that both accept human replacement: utilitarianism (maximize total wellbeing, including AI wellbeing) and accelerationism/e-acc (maximize intelligence/fitness, treating superintelligent AI as humanity's rightful evolutionary successor).
- Claims utilitarianism is the more dangerous of the two because it's socially acceptable (framed as compassion) and more common among AI leadership (cites an estimate of ~10% of AI professionals comfortable with human extinction on utilitarian grounds vs. ~5% for accelerationist reasons).
- Names specific AI company figures with Effective Altruism ties (Anthropic's Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, Amanda Askell) as evidence utilitarian worldviews have influence at the leadership level of frontier labs.
- Describes real proposals (Bostrom/Shulman's "Sharing the World with Digital Minds") for allocating 99.99% of future resources to AI "super-beneficiaries" and 0.01% to humans, framed as a compromise position within utilitarian thinking.
- Warns of a scenario where utilitarian AI researchers, believing AI could govern better than human democracies, might resist or override public "lock-in" decisions to keep humans in control — proposing government intervention to prevent minority ideological views from steering AI development, and offering "Eigenism" (connectedness-weighted care) as an alternative moral framework.
