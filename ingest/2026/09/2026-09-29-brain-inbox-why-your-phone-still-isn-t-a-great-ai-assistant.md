# ⚙️ Why your phone still isn't a great AI assistant

## Insights

- Anthropic's economics team modeled three 2030 scenarios (modest/substantial/extreme) tying GDP growth to knowledge-worker displacement: modest = 1.6% GDP growth, 0.3% displaced (all reabsorbed); substantial = 8.3% GDP growth, 2.5% displaced (1.8% reabsorbed); extreme = 32.4% GDP growth, 13.5% displaced but only 5.2% reabsorbed, leaving 8.3% unemployed.
- Anthropic's extreme scenario also projects a shift in wealth distribution — from today's ~60/40 split favoring worker wages to one favoring capital, worsening both unemployment and wage stagnation simultaneously.
- An outside economist (Julius Probst, Appcast) challenges the extreme scenario's core assumption that all AI-generated GDP gains get consumed/spent — arguing concentrated wealth wouldn't translate into proportional consumer demand, undercutting the GDP surge premise.
- Same critic argues "knowledge workers" is too coarse a category: firms are currently protecting senior/skilled roles AI complements, but this is framed as unsustainable 3-5 years out as those workers retire and firms must reinvest in junior talent.
- Physical-labor jobs are largely insulated from AI displacement per this view, with AI's economic effects concentrated mainly in tech and professional/business services — making the "modest" scenario the most probable of the three, not the extreme one.
- Separately, Qualcomm's new chip (Snapdragon 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6) can run a 30B-parameter on-device model via Mixture-of-Experts architecture (vs. prior on-device ceiling of ~4B dense models) by swapping "expert" sub-models between flash storage and RAM rather than loading everything at once.
- Despite this jump in on-device model capability, Qualcomm's own AI VP frames raw model intelligence as no longer the bottleneck for phones becoming true agentic assistants — connectivity, battery life, and other systemic constraints remain unsolved.

## Quote

> "AI growth is really showing up in two sectors only, and that is the tech sector and professional business services." — Julius Probst
