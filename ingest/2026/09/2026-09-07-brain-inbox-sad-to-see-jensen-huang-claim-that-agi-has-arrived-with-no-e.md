# Sad to see Jensen Huang claim that AGI has arrived, with no evidence and no definitions

## Insights

- Jensen Huang publicly declared "the race to AGI is over" (referring to Nvidia's Astra) without offering evidence or a working definition of AGI, which the author frames as an attempt to settle a scientific question by corporate declaration rather than argument.
- The author points to agidefinition.AI (a definitional effort involving Hendrycks, Bengio, and others) as the kind of rigorous framework such claims should be measured against, and argues Astra falls short by conventional definitions.
- References a personal 10-item bet with Brundage as a benchmark for AGI capability; predicts Astra may succeed at autoformalization and possibly reliable coding, but likely fails on the other eight criteria.
- Argues that if Astra were truly AGI it should show a dramatic capability leap over competitors, but real-world comparisons place it roughly on par with "Fable 5.1," and at least one respected benchmark suggests only an on-trend improvement, not a discontinuity.
- Concludes that genuine AGI arrival won't require squinting or corporate endorsement — the results will be self-evidently obvious.

## Quote

> Declaring victory without a definition simply muddies the waters.
