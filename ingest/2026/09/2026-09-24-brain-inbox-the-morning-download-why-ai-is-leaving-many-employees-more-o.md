# The Morning Download: Why AI is Leaving Many Employees More Overworked and Less Productive

## Insights

- Korn Ferry's third annual global workforce survey (16,000+ professionals across 11 markets) found that AI tools are currently adding as much burden as efficiency for many employees rather than net productivity gains.
- 62% of respondents said their workloads had already increased significantly in recent years regardless of AI; 52% said AI tools specifically increased the number of tasks expected in their roles.
- Korn Ferry's Bryan Ackermann frames this as an expected "J-curve" — an initial productivity dip that's a necessary precursor to eventual gains once employees become comfortable with new tools.
- A key failure mode: organizations respond to the burden with more training/upskilling, which often adds further burden rather than relieving it.
- Ackermann's proposed fix is structural, not additive: carve out real time in employees' existing workloads (e.g., project deadline extensions) so they have bandwidth to learn, and make training personalized/role-specific rather than generic — framed explicitly as a leadership responsibility, not a tooling problem.

## Quote

> When you combine already overworked employees now having to learn to use a tool, you get more overworked employees, not more productive employees. — Bryan Ackermann, Korn Ferry
