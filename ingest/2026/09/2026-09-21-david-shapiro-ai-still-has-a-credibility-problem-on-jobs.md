# ⚙️ AI still has a credibility problem on jobs

## Insights

- Pew Research surveyed 42,000+ people across 36 countries and found 46% believe AI will lead to fewer jobs, versus only 9% who think it will create more; 25% were unsure.
- Confidence gaps are steep by country: ~76% of Australian and South Korean respondents, and 71% of US respondents, expect AI to reduce jobs — among the least optimistic populations surveyed.
- A large share of US respondents (46%) tie AI to a shrinking middle class and widening rich-poor gap; younger Americans (56%) are notably more likely than those 50+ (38%) to hold this view.
- This isn't a new trend — a March Quinnipiac poll found 55% of Americans think AI will do more harm than good and 70% expect it to reduce job opportunities.
- Bay Area tech employers filed layoff notices for 14,500+ employees between June 2025–June 2026, while total regional tech employment (~375,000) is down about 7% since 2024, suggesting job losses are landing in Silicon Valley first.
- The piece frames a structural tension: AI labs depend on enterprise revenue as their "cash cow" but still need public trust for broader adoption/scaling — and argues the industry is currently losing that public narrative, partly because companies have used AI as a stated reason for cutting labor costs.

## Quote

> Companies can, will, and have used AI as a scapegoat to cut labor costs.
