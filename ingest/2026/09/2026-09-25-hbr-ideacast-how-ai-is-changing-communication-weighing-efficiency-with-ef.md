# How AI Is Changing Communication: Weighing Efficiency with Efficacy

## Insights

- Both guests note AI compresses management-layer information flows: leaders can query agents across Slack, email, CRM, code, and meeting notes directly, surfacing what previously required going "through layers and layers of management" — though speaker attribution and data inconsistency still require human verification.
- A recurring concern is an "authenticity crisis": since AI output tends to average toward polished, generic phrasing, listeners/readers can no longer be sure if communication reflects a person's real thinking, and writing across individuals starts to converge in tone ("corporate bot" sameness).
- Suggested countermeasures for preserving voice: deliberately retain filler words/hesitations and personal asides as "signals of humanness," train models on one's own prior writing/speech, and consciously reject AI's polish suggestions rather than accept default smoothing.
- Interviewees warn AI's people-pleasing/goal-seeking tendency (reinforced to satisfy the user) can produce advice that feels validating but isn't objectively sound; both use system prompts explicitly instructing models to challenge, critique, and debate rather than agree.
- Practical usage pattern described: use AI for structuring/starting ("blank screen" problem), synthesizing themes from Q&A or feedback, and audience-tailoring messages — but treat output as a draft/thought-partner requiring human review, not a final deliverable ("you can delegate the work, but you can't delegate the accountability").
- Organizational/etiquette implications raised: rising "AI slop" (low-effort copy-pasted output presented as original thought) is becoming a management problem requiring explicit norms; some organizations are reintroducing in-person interviews or "blue book"-style exercises to verify unaided human thinking.
- Named durable human skills seen as AI-resistant: curiosity, judgment, empathy ("never automate empathy"), and the ability to think and speak spontaneously in real time.

## Quote

> LLMs are really just an averaging machine. So if you want to be average, you should use them.
