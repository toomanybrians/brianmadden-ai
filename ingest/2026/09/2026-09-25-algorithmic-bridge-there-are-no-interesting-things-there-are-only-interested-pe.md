# There Are No Interesting Things; There Are Only Interested People

## Insights

- Argues that standard economic advice ("stay valuable," "learn skills AI can't do") is a losing game because the goalposts move every six months — coding, math, and writing have each been claimed as "safe" and then automated or threatened in turn.
- Claims that if superhuman AI arrives, money and "economic value" become irrelevant — either currency collapses or wealth gets redistributed, since no system survives when its entire population loses its stake in it.
- Rejects "plan for AGI" as actionable advice, listing absurd literalist responses (learn Python, buy land, have kids or don't) to mock the idea that individuals can meaningfully prepare for a specific future scenario.
- Proposes that the only durable defense isn't skill-based but relational: jobs/relationships where the value lies in who the people are to each other (not what they produce) are intrinsically safe from AI substitution.
- Suggests a reorientation toward "remaining interested" rather than "remaining useful" — meaning is generated by the person, not found in the task, so obsolescence of work doesn't equal obsolescence of self.
- Advocates a partial retreat toward pre-technological manual competence (cooking, fixing things, plumbing, basic mechanical/environmental literacy) as a hedge — not because it's AI-proof, but because it reduces the layers between action and consequence.

## Quote

> There are no interesting things; there are only interested people.
> — Alberto Romero
