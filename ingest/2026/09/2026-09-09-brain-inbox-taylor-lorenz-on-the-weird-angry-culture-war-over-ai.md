# Taylor Lorenz on the weird, angry culture war over AI

## Insights

- Lorenz identifies a disconnect between consumer enthusiasm for AI products and hostility toward the AI industry, attributing it to youth economic anxiety, general distrust of tech companies, and events like "Luddite Week."
- She describes organized networks of AI-generated "slop" accounts producing anti-data-center content purely to farm engagement and views, framing this as a continuation of an outrage-driven online economy she's tracked for over a decade rather than a genuine grassroots movement.
- Lorenz argues the real driver of anti-AI misinformation spreading on social platforms is mundane platform incentives ("bad incentives online"), not coordinated foreign influence campaigns.
- She faced significant backlash after disclosing she uses common AI tools (captions, background removal, video editing) to produce her podcast, illustrating how even mundane AI use is treated as controversial.
- On the data center debate, she holds a dual position: communities affected by data centers need more bargaining power, but the U.S. also genuinely needs more compute capacity and a modernized energy grid.
- The conversation surfaces how AI discourse fractures into distinct "tribes" with different vocabularies — e.g., the term "AI researcher" carries different meaning depending on the online subculture, and heavy AI/coding-assistant users are developing their own emerging dialect.
