# A Philosopher’s Guide to AI Welfare

## Insights

- Consciousness, sentience, and agency are typically fused in humans and animals but may occur separately or in fragmented form in AI systems (e.g., agency in one part of a system, sentience in another), so each must be studied independently rather than assumed to co-occur.
- Three evidence types for AI consciousness—behavioral, internal (architecture/interpretability), developmental (training data/process)—each carry a distinct failure mode: the mismatch/gaming problem (similar behavior, different causes), the specificity problem (biological theories don't map cleanly onto artificial mechanisms), and the solution space problem (AI's lack of biological/evolutionary constraints makes analogies to animal consciousness unreliable).
- Anthropic's "J-space" research on Claude is cited as a serious but premature attempt to identify a global-workspace-like structure — suggestive of consciousness-relevant architecture, not proof of it.
- Sentience is proposed to plausibly emerge from consciousness plus RL-derived evaluative signals persisting into inference, but the report stresses this is just one candidate pathway (evolution shows non-RL routes to evaluative signals also exist) and interaction between components matters, not just co-presence.
- Agency is defined at three escalating levels — minimal (goal pursuit via environmental interaction), intentional (beliefs/desires/means-end reasoning), rational (reflective normative assessment of one's own beliefs/desires) — and is easier to detect functionally than consciousness/sentience but harder to justify morally (echoing the "zombie" problem: goal-directed behavior without subjective experience).
- The "correct entity" to evaluate for moral status is unresolved: candidates include the full model (weights), a model-persona, an instance (single conversation), an instance-persona, or a single forward pass — analogized to species/individual/momentary-experience distinctions in biology; the author argues for pluralism across these levels rather than picking one.
- Frames the field as shifting urgency: AI welfare risk is moving from "negligible but nonzero, prepare for the future" to "must respond to present systems now," given the pace of AI development.

## Quote

> With sentience, we might feel confident that it matters by itself, but not that AI systems have it.
