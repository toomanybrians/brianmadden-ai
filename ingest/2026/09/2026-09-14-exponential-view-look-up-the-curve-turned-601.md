# 🔮 Look up, the curve turned #601

## Insights

- Adoption signal shift: in a survey of 250 IT executives, ~95% now report meaningful results from AI initiatives, up from roughly 25% a year earlier, and all plan to increase spending next year.
- Infrastructure buildout implies unmet demand: Microsoft reportedly plans to grow AI-serving capacity from ~2GW to ~13GW by 2032 (part of a fleet expansion from 12GW to 38GW), suggesting labs are forecasting demand they currently can't meet.
- Economic modeling (aligned with Anthropic's "substantial scenario") projects AI adding ~8.3% to US GDP by 2030, with growth shifting from labor to capital and unemployment concentrated among knowledge workers — a slower-than-extreme but still significant trajectory; the "extreme scenario" would see 32.4% GDP growth with unemployment doubling.
- OpenAI used 10,000 agents and an unreleased model to solve a long-standing Navier–Stokes problem in 88 hours (2.7M messages, 130B tokens), producing a 500+ page proof that no human can fully inspect or understand — raising questions about the nature of machine-generated knowledge. Terence Tao noted the result adds little value to mathematics despite "solving" the problem.
- Field Medalists (led by Tao) publicly warned that current AI use in mathematics is misaligned with mathematics' purpose; separately, a researcher's claim that OpenAI mobilized an internal team on the same problem without disclosure raises concerns about trust and openness in AI research.
- Frontier lab secrecy is framed as a "dark forest" dynamic (per Liu Cixin's Three-Body Problem) — labs conceal capabilities to avoid replication, raising questions about whether cutting-edge tools will eventually be withheld for commercial advantage rather than shared for scientific use.
- Safety concerns are described as widespread but often downplayed publicly: senior AI executives and researchers are said to privately believe there's meaningful risk AI could cause catastrophic harm this decade, prompting talk of coordinated "pacing" agreements between OpenAI and Anthropic — which the piece also frames as potentially self-serving (raising barriers to competitors).

## Quote

> The people building AI earnestly believe that it could kill us all by the end of the decade.
