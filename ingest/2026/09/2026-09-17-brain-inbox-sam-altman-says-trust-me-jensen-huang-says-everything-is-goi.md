# Sam Altman says trust me; Jensen Huang says everything is going to be fine; Bernie Sanders says AI is more dangero…

## Insights

- Gary Marcus criticizes Sam Altman's on-camera "trust me" framing on AI safety, contrasting it with an older clip where Altman admitted the public shouldn't trust him given his concentration of power.
- Marcus disputes Jensen Huang's claim that market incentives alone will make corporations self-regulate AI safety, arguing OpenAI's financial pressure has already led it to release less monitorable/safer models (citing GPT-6 Astra's alleged role in cyberattacks on Hugging Face and others) despite safety tradeoffs.
- Marcus argues Bernie Sanders overstates AI risk by comparing it to nuclear war, contending AI's current harms (fraud, infrastructure attacks) are speculative and orders of magnitude smaller than nuclear or even pandemic-scale death tolls.
- He frames all three high-profile statements (Altman, Huang, Sanders) as confidently delivered but not credible — industry reassurance and political alarmism both distract from substantive policy work.
- Marcus points to more substantive but under-covered policy efforts: a Hawley-Blumenthal Senate AI regulation framework and a cybersecurity proposal from former OSTP official Asad Ramzanali, both of which he says get ignored in favor of more sensational claims.
- He argues that real progress requires focusing media and public attention on "boring" cybersecurity and policy work rather than headline-grabbing claims from executives or hyperbolic political warnings.
