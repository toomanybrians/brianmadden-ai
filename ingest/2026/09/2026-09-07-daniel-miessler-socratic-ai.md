# Socratic AI

## Insights

- Miessler proposes "Socratic AI" as an alternative use pattern for AI with students: rather than producing answers/homework, the AI only asks questions that push the student to develop and articulate their own thinking.
- The model is positioned as usable only after a student has already done substantial work themselves — it's a refinement tool, not a starting point.
- He frames the distinction as "personal trainer" vs. "robot that lifts the weight for them" — the value is in the struggle/reps, not the output.
- Concrete implementation: an agent called "Socrates," built into his LifeOS system, with a system prompt instructing it to teach and require the student to do the work rather than answer directly. It ships in the next LifeOS release and is offered as a copyable, single-file agent for others to install.
- Miessler frames this as a "tactical solution" to what he calls a serious strategic problem: kids overusing AI and failing to learn as a result.
- The piece includes a disclosure note that Miessler's AI assistant ("Kai") co-wrote parts of the post and built the Socrates agent itself — an example of AI-assisted authorship being explicitly labeled.

## Quote

> It's basically like a personal trainer, as opposed to a robot that lifts the weight for them.
