# Are Phone-Free Schools Working?

## Insights

- International PISA 2025 results show the lowest-ever OECD average scores in reading, math, and science, with declines starting in the mid-2010s — predating COVID and not reversed or even slowed by school reopening or ed-tech adoption.
- The PISA report authors themselves speculate that smartphones and other attention-maximizing digital devices may be incompatible with sustained academic attention, and note declining reading-for-enjoyment alongside a shift to skimming-style digital reading.
- A widely-covered NYU study (Allcott et al.) on school phone bans found "close to zero" overall test-score impact, but this pooled result masked a more encouraging pattern: high schools (where phone use and distraction were higher) saw modest positive effects on math, while only middle schools saw small negative effects.
- Other 2025-2026 natural experiments (Norway, Rio de Janeiro) found phone bans improved grades, reduced bullying and psychological-symptom healthcare visits (especially for lower-income girls), and boosted test scores — but only when bans were enforced school-wide rather than classroom-only, and only when phone use in school actually measurably declined.
- Disciplinary incidents and well-being often worsened in year one of a phone ban before improving by year two or three, suggesting an adjustment period as new norms are established — a caution against judging these policies on short time horizons.
- The piece argues research has been too narrowly focused on test scores, and calls for measuring broader outcomes (social interaction, mental health, discipline, technology-use substitution) to actually assess whether removing phones changes the intended in-school experience.
- Generative AI use for completing assignments is flagged as a newer, less-studied factor layered on top of smartphones and one-to-one laptop/tablet policies as a possible contributor to the achievement decline.

## Quote

> Devices such as smartphones – designed to maximise a user's attention – are [not] compatible with sustained academic attention.
