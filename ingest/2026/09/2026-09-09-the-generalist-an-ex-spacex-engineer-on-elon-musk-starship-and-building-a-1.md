# An Ex-SpaceX Engineer on Elon Musk, Starship, and Building a $1B-Valued Startup (Scott Morton, Founder & CEO of Revel)

## Insights

- Revel builds a software platform for testing and controlling complex hardware systems (rockets, nuclear reactors, supersonic aircraft), founded in 2024 by ex-SpaceX control-software engineer Scott Morton.
- The core premise: physical/industrial systems have grown far more sophisticated, but the software used to test and control them is still fragmented and often dates to the 1980s–90s.
- Revel built its own programming language, RevelCode, aimed at combining performance and accessibility with "runtime safety" for high-stakes physical systems — a deliberate rejection of ad hoc or looser AI-generated code for safety-critical infrastructure (framed explicitly as "why no one will vibe-code a nuclear reactor").
- One episode theme is how AI can speed up high-consequence software teams without displacing rigorous testing and review — treating AI as an accelerant within, not a replacement for, safety-critical engineering discipline.
- Company has raised $180M (including a $150M Series B), reached a ~$1B valuation, and counts Impulse Space and Radiant Nuclear as customers; its platform scales from small benchtop tests to industrial systems with hundreds of thousands of telemetry channels.
- Morton cites a "no ego" decision-making style learned from Elon Musk — holding strong convictions but abandoning them quickly when given new information — as central to sound engineering leadership, alongside concerns about preserving engineering culture while scaling toward 500 employees.

## Quote

> No ego is extremely important to make the best decisions possible. – Scott Morton
