# Where I stand on RSI

## Insights

- The author distinguishes true recursive self-improvement (RSI) from "lossy self-improvement," arguing automatable research is too narrow to produce massive net acceleration given scaling laws' exponential costs, diminishing returns from parallel agents, and resource/political bottlenecks in building strong LLMs.
- Argues frontier labs' internal culture (OpenAI, Anthropic) amplifies AI-risk anxiety, and that jumping from that anxiety to extinction-level fears is more cultural/quasi-religious than evidence-based; cites Richard Ngo's view that safety-community timelines will be "directionally correct but factually wrong."
- Summarizes expert timeline forecasts (Dwarkesh podcast guests O'Neill, Millidge, Schulman) on thresholds like a drop-in remote knowledge worker (~1-3 years), 10x AI-researcher productivity uplift (~2-10 years), and ASI surpassing top human experts on all computer-based work (~3-10 years) — with wide disagreement and heavy caveats about jaggedness of AI capability.
- Claims RSI is most plausible today as efficiency gains (cheaper inference, better multi-agent orchestration) rather than as expansion of peak intelligence, since LLM serving has clear measurable metrics to optimize while raising "peak intelligence" does not.
- Notes that post-training remains uniquely resistant to automation because it requires human judgment about how models "should behave" in many domains, per quoted Schulman commentary, and errors there often don't show up in benchmarks.
- Cites Anthropic's Claude system card language stating internal AI usage has helped maintain current progress rates but shows no clear sign of "dramatic acceleration" beyond that — used as evidence current takeoff is incremental (software engineering, log monitoring, experiment management), not an intelligence explosion.

## Quote

> We do not yet see clear signs of dramatic acceleration beyond that rate.
