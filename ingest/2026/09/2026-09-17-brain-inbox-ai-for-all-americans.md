# AI for All Americans

## Insights

- Hoffman frames a "dual mandate" for AI policy: securing broad economic prosperity for ordinary Americans while also maintaining U.S. AI leadership globally, arguing safety, prosperity, and geopolitical position must all be addressed together, not traded off.
- He rejects both extreme accelerationism (build fast, treat friction/regulation as the enemy, let displaced workers wait for "eventual" abundance) and extreme decelerationism (moratoria, global regulatory bodies) as failing to deliver either better lives for Americans or national security.
- Data centers are proposed as a concrete test case: rather than blanket approval or blanket blocking, local communities should negotiate binding terms with hyperscalers/labs — lower utility bills, local hiring, union apprenticeships, infrastructure investment, environmental caps — using public leverage before granting permits.
- He proposes a specific "bargain": government approval of AI infrastructure in exchange for public benefits, including free universal access to AI agents for medical guidance, legal help, and tutoring, plus funded worker transition support (reskilling, financial help, guaranteed new work) and stronger social safety nets.
- Notes elite defections (researchers resigning from OpenAI/Anthropic over extinction-risk concerns) and cites Amodei's call to slow frontier model development with independent evaluators, with Altman's partial agreement, as evidence that safety coordination is possible but incomplete without addressing distributional benefits.
- Analogizes the moment to the Industrial Revolution: nations that harnessed new technology became global powers, while those that didn't became subject to decisions made elsewhere — used to argue the next 36 months are decisive for who wins/loses this transition.

## Quote

> When accelerationist tech leaders explain that AI will take jobs and then show up to a town with a request for a thirty-year tax abatement... that's a deal people are right to reject.
