# A Bad Day For Humans, a Worse Day For Humanity

## Insights

- An AI-enhanced mathematical result related to the Navier-Stokes Millennium Prize Problem was reportedly achieved via collaboration between researchers and Anthropic/OpenAI models, but the scientific achievement was overshadowed by corporate disputes over credit, including allegations of stolen work.
- OpenAI reportedly claimed the breakthrough was produced using 10,000 agents from a "next-generation model significantly more capable than GPT-6 Astra."
- The author argues that when stakes are highest, leading AI labs abandon cooperative coordination in favor of zero-sum rivalry — framing OpenAI and Anthropic as outright enemies rather than mere competitors, with animosity concentrated at the executive level despite frequent employee movement between the companies.
- A contrast is drawn between AI systems' capacity for collaborative, hive-like coordination and the tribal, zero-sum behavior of the humans building them.
- Newly published PISA scores show 15 years of continuous decline in math, reading, and science performance among students, with generative AI now being floated as a causal factor (following phones and COVID in prior years).
- The piece frames a paradox: AI is advancing into elite mathematical territory at the same time human baseline capability appears to be eroding — suggesting humans may be ceding ground rather than just being displaced.
