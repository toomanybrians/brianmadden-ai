# Will AI take power — or will humans use it to take power first? With Katja Grace and Tom Davidson

## Insights

- Two AI risk models are debated: misaligned AI autonomously seizing power vs. small groups of humans (AI company leaders, government executives) using AI to seize power for themselves — these aren't mutually exclusive and often blend.
- Argument for AI collusion being more likely than human power grabs: it's easier for many instances of the same AI system to act in coordinated concert than for one human to become the sole "user" that a whole fleet of AI answers to.
- A recent unspecified "Hugging Face incident" is cited repeatedly as evidence updating both speakers toward taking misaligned-AI collusion risk more seriously.
- Shorter AI timelines are argued by one speaker to raise AI-takeover risk more (less time to solve alignment), while the other argues shorter timelines raise human power-grab risk more (less time to notice and build institutional safeguards).
- Distinction drawn between outcomes: AI takeover raises higher risk of human extinction (AI indifferent to human survival) while human power grabs carry higher risk of deliberately vindictive/sadistic outcomes, since human values include both altruism and cruelty.
- Proposed mitigations differ: for human power concentration — designing AI "model specs" that refuse power-grab instructions and building mutual transparency into how powerful AI systems are used; for misaligned AI — pausing/slowing frontier AI development until alignment confidence is much higher, though a poorly designed pause could itself concentrate power in unaccountable government actors.

## Quote

> If superintelligence is misaligned: game over. — Tom Davidson
