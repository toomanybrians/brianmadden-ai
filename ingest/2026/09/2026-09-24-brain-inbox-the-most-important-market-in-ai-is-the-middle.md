# The Most Important Market in AI is the Middle

## Insights

- Anthropic and OpenAI cut prices within 90 minutes of each other, illustrating intense head-to-head competition in the "messy middle" of the AI model market — capable-enough models at affordable prices for multi-step business workflows.
- Frontier pricing has been dropping in leapfrog fashion: Anthropic's $10/$50 per million tokens was matched by OpenAI at $5/$30 for equivalent capability, and Anthropic's previously static Opus pricing ($5/$25) saw its first-ever cut.
- Low-end models are falling even faster — one model (Luna) was cut 80% then another 50% within months — and open-weight models on published gateways run at an 86% discount to closed-model blended pricing.
- Large customers are fine-tuning open-weight models for further savings: Cursor's Composer 2 (fine-tuned on Kimi K2.5) cut costs 86% vs. its prior in-house model; Harvey cut cost-per-cell 55% vs. Sonnet 5 while improving quality over Fable 5.
- The most expensive/capable frontier models are capturing a shrinking, thinner share of usage than expected — Fable 5.1 got only 3.7% of gateway spend in its first 12 days, and frontier models' share of large-account token consumption fell from 53% to 45% in a month.
- The article frames enterprise AI demand not as a pyramid with a small premium-paying peak, but as a normal distribution with a "fat middle" that optimizes for intelligence-per-dollar against relatively fixed task requirements, pushing spend toward commodity-tier pricing as capability costs fall.

## Quote

> Demand for intelligence is not a pyramid with a small, wealthy peak paying for everything beneath it. It is a normal distribution with a fat middle.
