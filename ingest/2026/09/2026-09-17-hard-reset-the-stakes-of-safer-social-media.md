# The Stakes of Safer Social Media

## Insights

- Lush (the cosmetics brand) left major social media platforms in 2021 after the Facebook Files showed Meta knew about its products' harms, and has since promoted public "digital divestment" guidance for users, framing attention capture as a threat to mental health, relationships, and time.
- Panelists distinguished their own generation — who grew up alongside social media as it was being built — from current teens, who now face a fully mature social media landscape plus AI's normalization, compounded by what one speaker called generalized "nihilism and despair."
- Larz May (founder of #HalfTheStory) described being served self-harm instructional content by an algorithm as a college student over a decade ago, and argues the stakes have escalated from image-perfection culture to AI "changing the future of humanity's course."
- May raised a specific fear about identity: young people spending decades of life behind screens risk having the things they built their identity around (post-college) "suddenly get replaced by AI."
- Nikki Iyer (Design It For Us) linked platform business models directly to youth harm: more time online means more data and more effective targeted advertising, which disproportionately affects people who haven't yet formed a stable sense of self.
- Proposed interventions discussed: tech education in schools (compared to sex ed), giving young people a seat at tech policy-making tables, and relearning tolerance for boredom/discomfort instead of algorithmic escape.
- The author connects this generational anxiety to adult experience too, describing lost sleep after the Anthropic whistleblower news cycle as evidence of an ongoing "doom loop of unchecked AI development."

## Quote

> AI is quite literally changing the future of humanity's course. — Larz May
