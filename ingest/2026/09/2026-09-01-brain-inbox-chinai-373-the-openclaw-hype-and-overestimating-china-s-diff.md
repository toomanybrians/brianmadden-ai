# ChinAI #373: The OpenClaw Hype and Overestimating China’s Diffusion Advantage

## Insights

- OpenClaw (open-source AI agent, nicknamed "lobster" in China) drove a short-lived viral frenzy from roughly Nov 2025–March 2026, spawning Mac Mini shortages in Shenzhen, paid deployment services, and offline setup booths from Tencent and other tech giants — then rapidly faded from mention.
- Widely cited claims that China's OpenClaw usage was "almost double" the U.S. (via SecurityScorecard data, picked up by NBC News and cited by Council on Foreign Relations researchers as evidence of a Chinese "diffusion advantage") appear to be misinterpreted or outdated; a direct check of the same data source on Aug 29, 2026 showed 18.7k U.S. instances vs. 17.0k China instances.
- OpenClaw was a poor proxy for real AI diffusion regardless: it required high technical effort (manual model selection, data/terminal integration, skill packages), was token-expensive, and had persistent security issues — friction that "ready-made" competitors like OpenAI's Codex and Anthropic's Claude Code quickly addressed.
- OpenClaw didn't die but evolved into more accessible, cloud-hosted derivative products (30+ from firms like Zhipu AI, Tencent, ByteDance), shifting AI diffusion in China toward cloud-delivered agents rather than self-hosted open-source tools.
- Because China's cloud computing adoption significantly lags the U.S. (per the author's separate "China's AI Implementation Gap" analysis), and diffusion increasingly runs through the cloud, China may face an actual AI diffusion deficit rather than an advantage — the opposite of the popular narrative.
- JPMorganChase analysts frame OpenClaw-derived agent enthusiasm as only a short-term stock boost for Chinese tech, while their own revenue growth forecasts for major Chinese cloud providers show deceleration from 2025 into 2027.

## Quote

> This frenzy was always a misleading indicator of AI diffusion.
