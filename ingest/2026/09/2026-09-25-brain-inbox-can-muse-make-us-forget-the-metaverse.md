# Can Muse make us forget the metaverse?

## Insights

- Meta is repositioning Muse (a consumer AI agent, less than a month old) as "the centerpiece" of its strategy, ahead of the metaverse — a pivot Zuckerberg framed as AI proving bigger than VR, without acknowledging the scale of the prior metaverse bet.
- Actual adoption numbers are muddled: Zuckerberg cited "millions" of Muse users, but a day earlier The Information reported just over 500,000 week-one users — far below Threads' 100 million in five days — suggesting the messaging may outpace real traction.
- The Muse pitch coincides with Meta's plan to spend up to $145 billion on infrastructure/capex this year (exceeding cumulative Reality Labs losses of over $85 billion since 2021), raising the possibility that promoting Muse is partly about sustaining investor confidence.
- The author's hands-on critique of long-term agent use: agents like Muse/OpenClaw require constant task assignment, approval of actions, and review of output — functioning more like "interns" than "coworkers," which reads as enterprise-software behavior rather than a natural mass consumer product.
- Meta's new VR Glasses are described as a genuine hardware leap — one-sixth the weight and about one-third the price of Apple's Vision Pro, with no overheating, extendable battery via an attached "puck," and a functional "surface keyboard" for typing on any surface.
- Historical parallel drawn: Meta's 2016 Messenger bot platform generated major hype and developer buildout but collapsed within two years, as did its subsequent cryptocurrency effort — framing Muse as the latest in a pattern of Meta rallying around new platform bets after previous ones falter.
