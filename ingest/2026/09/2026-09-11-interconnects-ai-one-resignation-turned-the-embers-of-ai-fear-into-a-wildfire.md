# One resignation turned the embers of AI fear into a wildfire

## Insights

- A single AI researcher's (Jacob Coxon) public resignation citing safety risk went viral far beyond what anyone anticipated, in part because prior events (an OpenAI-HuggingFace incident, an OpenAI Navier-Stokes breakthrough) had already raised public anxiety, priming the ground for the story to "catch."
- The author distinguishes sharply between vague "existential risk"/extinction talk (which he considers not worth debating, given how low he estimates the probability) and concrete, worth-debating AI risks like cyberattacks on infrastructure and bio-risk.
- He argues frontier lab employees, especially at Anthropic, operate in an insular culture ("religious energy") that distorts their forecasts and views of current AI progress, independent of individual intent.
- The resignation appears to have been a coordinated media moment (a WSJ exclusive, advance notice to safety advocacy groups, same-day Daniel Kokotajlo Rogan appearance) rather than a spontaneous event or an organized political conspiracy — no one involved anticipated the scale of virality.
- The author rejects the recursive self-improvement (RSI) "fast takeoff" thesis, proposing instead "lossy self-improvement": AI is superhuman in narrow domains (math, coding) but remains jagged, limited by human bottlenecks in building models and allocating resources, and not on a path to across-the-board superhuman intellect.
- He argues the actual near-term risk is inadequate safety/security practices at labs (slow incident response, under-hardened infrastructure) rather than existential risk, and worries the episode will push open-source AI development toward severe restriction if a similar incident is later shown to be intentional.

## Quote

> It feels like a very narrow path to believe in AI risks, but to not worry about extinction from the technology.
