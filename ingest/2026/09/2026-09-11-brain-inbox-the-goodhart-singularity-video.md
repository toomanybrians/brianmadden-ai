# The Goodhart Singularity (video)

## Insights

- Challenges the "default story" of AI progress: automating AI R&D alone (recursive self-improvement in coding/research) is widely assumed sufficient to rapidly produce domain-general superintelligence, taken largely for granted by figures like Sam Altman, Jack Clark, and the AI 2027 forecast.
- Argues this assumption is wrong because getting good at any domain requires practice against real problems, and AI companies lack the data needed to practice most non-coding, non-research tasks.
- Claims this data gap can't be closed via sample efficiency (the data often doesn't exist) or synthetic data/simulation, partly because researchers can't evaluate success on tasks not yet understood, and partly because real-world market actors have preferences that are fundamentally unsimulable.
- Concludes that superintelligent capability outside coding/research will depend on slow, costly, real-world deployment and data collection — not on isolated data-center self-improvement.
- Introduces the "Goodhart Singularity" concept: an isolated AI optimizing against benchmarks only appears to approach superintelligence while actually just overfitting to evals that fail to generalize.
- Frames this as good news: it implies AI capability growth will be paced by real-world diffusion/deployment rather than occurring as a sudden, isolated takeoff that "takes the rest of the world by surprise."

## Quote

> I think it's impossible to get good at solving problems without access to a source of those problems.
