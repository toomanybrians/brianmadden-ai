# 📈 Monday data: More AI numbers, more clarity?

## Insights

- Only 15% of S&P 500 companies made a quantified claim about AI's business impact this quarter (up from 9% a year prior) — adoption of AI is real but reporting of measurable results is still rare and slow-growing.
- Claims tied to cost/productivity gains (avg. 47% improvement, 24% of calls) currently exceed revenue/demand-side claims (avg. 40%, 18% of calls) in both prevalence and magnitude, though both are rising at similar rates.
- Reported ranges are wide and overlapping (20-60% for cost/productivity; 20-83% for revenue/demand), suggesting these self-reported figures carry significant uncertainty and shouldn't be treated as precise benchmarks.
- Corporate language has shifted from "pilot" (never exceeding 4% of calls) toward "deployment" (up from 7% to 12% since 2025) and especially "agentic" (up from 9% to 24%), while "generative AI" mentions fell from 13% to 5%.
- The near-absence of "pilot" language despite ongoing experimentation suggests companies undercommunicate early-stage AI work, making it harder to track how initiatives evolve from testing to proven results.
- Specific disclosed examples include triage time cut ~75% (FIS agentic servicing), demand-signal processing cut ~90% across 190 parts (GE), and document review configuration time down 60% (WTW).

## Quote

> 15% this quarter have made a quantified claim of AI's impact on the business, up from 9% at the start of last year.
