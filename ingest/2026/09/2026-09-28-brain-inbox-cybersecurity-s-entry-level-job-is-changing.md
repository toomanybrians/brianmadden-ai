# ⚙️ Cybersecurity’s entry-level job is changing

## Insights

- CrowdStrike's CPO argues AI attackers aren't just faster than humans — they reason differently, assembling known tactics/techniques/procedures (TTPs) into novel attack chains that legacy security tools (built on assumptions about human reasoning) weren't designed to detect.
- He frames entry-level SOC work as being fully automatable at the tier-one level (alert triage) and largely automatable at tier-two (evidence gathering, hypothesis generation), pushing the effective "entry-level" job definition up to tier-two/tier-three work that requires more judgment.
- His productivity argument: rather than headcount reduction, AI lets the same number of security staff handle exploding data volumes and attack complexity — framed as "do more work with the same resources" rather than replacement.
- He predicts AI tooling will surface previously unknown vulnerabilities and close them, but expects new categories of weakness to emerge in turn — an ongoing offense/defense arms-race pattern rather than a final resolution.
- Proposed solution to AI's non-deterministic outputs: ensembles of models "voting" on each other's conclusions plus structured harnesses to control inputs, analogous to older multi-engine malware detection approaches.
- His core reassurance to enterprises: they aren't "outmatched" by AI-enabled adversaries as long as they adopt AI defensively and partner with vendors focused on adversary tactics, treating this as another historical technology inflection point.

## Quote

> The whole idea of entry-level is just going to move up the stack, like a tier two or tier three.
