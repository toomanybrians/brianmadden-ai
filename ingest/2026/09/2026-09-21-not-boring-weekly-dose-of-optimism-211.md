# Weekly Dose of Optimism #211

## Insights

- TypeSafe (founded by ChatGPT/RLHF co-inventor Diogo Almeida) launched Jev, a model built specifically for structured business decisions (routing tickets, fraud flags, churn prediction) rather than open-ended text generation, returning probabilities/decisions in parallel instead of token-by-token output.
- Company-reported figures claim Jev is 20–200x faster and 40–400x cheaper than frontier LLMs for the narrow decision-type tasks it targets, though these are unverified vendor benchmarks pending third-party validation.
- The framing suggests a shift toward matching AI architecture to task type — using lightweight, fast, cheap models for high-volume operational decisions rather than defaulting to general-purpose generative models for everything.
- The piece explicitly invokes Jevons paradox (hence the product name "Jev") — the idea that making intelligence cheaper and faster will increase, not decrease, total usage as companies embed AI decisioning into many small workflow points previously handled by rules engines or human review.
- Separately, a Lowercarbon partner's essay ("Natural General Intelligence") argues current data-gathering and modeling tools are advanced enough to comprehensively model the planet the way biology has modeled complex biological systems — an example of AI/ML applied to previously intractable, high-volume real-world data domains.
