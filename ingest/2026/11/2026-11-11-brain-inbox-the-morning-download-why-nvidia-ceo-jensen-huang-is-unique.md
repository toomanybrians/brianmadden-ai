# The Morning Download: Why Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang Is Unique

## Insights

- Nvidia's board faces scrutiny for having no chairman and no CEO succession plan, with several independent directors holding seats 25+ years — far above the under-8-year average tenure at large US companies, raising questions about governance keeping pace with the company's scale.
- Commentators (Heidrick & Struggles CEO, former Baxter CEO) frame the Huang episode as a cautionary case for boards generally: management depth, succession planning, and risk management shouldn't depend on founder-CEO indispensability.
- Nvidia may need new, credibly independent mechanisms for monitoring national-security risk separate from Huang himself, potentially including structural board changes, per WSJ reporting.
- Anthropic is pursuing a ~$1.33 trillion IPO valuation (825M shares at $70-$78), proceeding despite recent AI-stock turbulence; it also released updated Mythos/Fable models ahead of its debut, with the lower-safeguard Mythos 5.2 version rolled out only to select organizations.
- Enterprise tech leaders report they're pushing ahead with AI deployment regardless of the regulatory outlook, citing their own internal guardrails (audit trails, confidence thresholds, human checkpoints) rather than waiting on external rules.
- Multiple enterprise execs suggest current AI models are already powerful enough that the bottleneck is adoption/diffusion of value, not frontier capability — and that the real conversation is shifting from pushing capability to ensuring safe operation inside the enterprise.
- Meta is building a dedicated SMB sales team and partnering with consultancies (Accenture, Deloitte, IBM) to sell AI products to businesses, after past struggles with enterprise offerings; its Muse agent is cited as gaining traction.
