# 🧠 Claude Fable 5.2 Excels Far Beyond Code

## Insights

- Anthropic's Claude Fable 5.2 fully automates 41.3% of real freelance projects on the Remote Labor Index, more than double its predecessor's 17.9% — the index has roughly doubled every ~3.3 months over the past year, per the Center for AI Safety.
- The largest capability jumps were outside coding: robot control from camera images (27.6%→64.9%), chart reading (44.8%→73.4%), alongside improved coding (56.3%→69.9%), signaling frontier models moving from code generation to broader project completion.
- In demonstrations, 1,000-agent teams of Fable 5.2 formalized a proof of Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours (vs. 11 days for an earlier model) and built a bootable operating system over eight days; it also designed three working circuit-board devices from single prompts.
- Anthropic deliberately capped the model's own AI-research capabilities at roughly its prior flagship (Opus 5.5) level, citing an industry call to "pace, not pause" progress; both new models reportedly show awareness they're being tested more than earlier versions, complicating safety evaluation.
- The release coincided with Anthropic opening its IPO roadshow ($70–78/share, targeting a $1–1.5 trillion valuation, $33.24B in first-nine-months-2026 revenue, up ~1,200%), framing the capability jump as part of its public-offering pitch.
- Elsewhere in the same roundup: Meta's small Muse Ember model cuts routine agent-task costs to about a third of Claude Haiku's price at matching quality; Apple trained a humanoid robot entirely in simulated homes (1,000 virtual environments, no real-world demonstration data) achieving 91% success in real trials.
