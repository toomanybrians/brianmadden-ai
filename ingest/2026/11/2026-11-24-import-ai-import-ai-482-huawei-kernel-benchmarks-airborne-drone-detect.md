# Import AI 482: Huawei kernel benchmarks; airborne drone detection; and a national lab's AI science benchmark

## Insights

- A Lawrence Livermore benchmark (SciResearch-Eval) tests whether AI agents can design their own experiments and report calibrated uncertainty when given a flawed simulator; only the most advanced frontier models (GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable 5.2) reliably detect when their tools/data are unreliable and adjust confidence accordingly — weaker and open-weight models fail outright.
- This suggests a meaningful capability split is emerging between models that can merely assist with science tasks versus a small set that exhibit autonomous scientific skepticism — knowing when not to trust their own instruments or conclusions.
- Stanford researchers Yejin Choi and Carlos Guestrin launched Noeri ($110M raised), pitching an alternative to monolithic frontier labs: many small, continually-learning, proprietary-data-driven models instead of one dominant model lineage, including techniques like test-time training and self-distillation.
- Chinese researchers released AscendGYM, a benchmark for AI-driven kernel optimization on Huawei's Ascend chips, explicitly framed as infrastructure for China's own chip-performance self-improvement loop amid export controls — frontier models (Opus 5, GPT-5.6, GLM-5.3) already show measurable differences in token efficiency and performance on this task.
- A speculative fiction piece ("Tech Tales") imagines a future "Machine Hermeneutics" profession where humans must painstakingly reverse-engineer and explain machine-generated science before it can be used, raising the idea that understanding AI's output may take far longer than AI took to produce it, and may itself reshape how humans think.

## Quote

> "An agent that reports a confident wrong number is worse than one that reports honest uncertainty, because nobody downstream can tell the difference."
