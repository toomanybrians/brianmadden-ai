# The AI Model You Use Every Day Is Getting Better at Controlling Robots. Claude Fable 5.2 More Than Doubled Its Predecessor's Score on a Simulated Robot

## Insights

- A robotics lab (Most Robotic, founders Lucy Cai and Claire Mao) gave OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra control of a kitchen arm ("Podrick") to prepare dinner; the model learned to locate a rice-cooker start button by repeatedly halving the search area — effectively discovering binary search on its own rather than being programmed with it.
- The piece frames robotics progress through Rich Sutton's "Bitter Lesson": historically, hand-engineered, expert-taught solutions get overtaken by general methods that simply search and learn from more data/compute as scale increases.
- A new version of Claude reportedly more than doubled its predecessor's score on a simulated robot-control benchmark, suggesting gains in software/language capability are translating into physical/robotic control skill.
- The author argues robot demos should be read for which parts were human-programmed versus which parts the model figured out independently, using examples like salad-making and rice-cooker operation.
- Historically, factories made robots economical by fixing/constraining the environment ("bolting the world in place"); teaching robots new tasks via demonstration in unstructured settings (like a home kitchen) is comparatively expensive.
- The article flags 2026 developments as a turning point: general models being handed real robot control (e.g., a cited Stanford result of 24/25 successful trials, an agent writing its own air-fryer routine), while specialized/open-source robot controllers are also accelerating (a humanoid controller test reportedly sped up to 3.4 minutes on a desktop GPU).
