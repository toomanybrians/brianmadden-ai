# Salesforce Just Helped Microsoft. You're Picking The Winner Of A Trillion-Dollar Fight.

## Insights

- A speaker reports the nine biggest cloud companies are on track to spend $957 billion on capacity this year and over $1.5 trillion next year, framing the stakes of the "agentic control plane" fight.
- On October 27th, Salesforce reportedly shipped a bridge making Microsoft's Copilot Studio agents easier to use within Salesforce workflows, without requiring customers to rebuild those agents.
- On the same day, Oracle announced an expanded partnership with OpenAI to build a workspace bringing Oracle business applications and data into ChatGPT, still in development.
- The core claim: AI agents are redrawing competitive boundaries because closing a task (e.g., month-end close, selling to a customer) now crosses software categories that used to be separately owned (Microsoft/Salesforce/Oracle), forcing previously complementary vendors into contest over "who mediates the agent layer."
- "Agentic control plane" is defined narrowly: the software that gets an employee's agent the right information, the right tools, applies company rules, and returns the work for review—likened explicitly to Windows' dominance of the 1990s workplace experience, with no current equivalent winner established.
- Salesforce's Agentic Context Engine (in preview, per the speaker) is described as aiming to supply shared business context to agents across multiple platforms, including agents built by other vendors—positioned against Microsoft's "Site Queue" products as a related but not equivalent approach.
- Mechanism for why context/canon matters more than raw data access: a spreadsheet can have the correct revenue figure but an agent can apply the wrong definition of "revenue," and a customer record can be accurate while missing a promise made in an email the day before—connecting systems grants access but not the judgment needed to interpret it.
- Owning underlying data doesn't settle the competition: Microsoft Fabric is cited as letting customers reference external data in place without migration, meaning a rival (e.g., OpenAI via Oracle) can become central to a workflow without owning or moving the underlying systems of record.
- A recruiter example (anonymized) illustrates switching-cost mechanics: an agent that learned her screening logic, hiring-manager preferences, and what led to actual hires created lock-in beyond the raw candidate data—her manager reportedly didn't grasp how much tacit tuning was embedded before a provider-change discussion came up.
- Gartner is cited forecasting about $1.46 trillion in worldwide software spending this year, used to frame the scale of business this agentic-control-plane competition could eventually touch.
- The speaker frames this as deliberately avoiding winner-take-all: companies have commercial incentive to preserve interoperability (the Microsoft-Salesforce bridge, the OpenAI-Oracle partnership) because forcing customers to choose one vendor risks losing the relationship entirely—"frenemy competition" as the healthier outcome for buyers.
- OpenAI's "Frontier" offering (described as launched in February, connecting business systems, context, agent work, and permissions) is cited as an earlier instance of OpenAI's enterprise-integration push, of which the Oracle deal is presented as a continuation.
- Practical guidance offered to viewers: when evaluating or buying agent platforms, prioritize interoperability, ask where value concentrates (data vs. context vs. workflow), and avoid defaulting to whatever tool is already in daily use without deliberate evaluation.
- The speaker predicts the question of a dominant control-plane winner (or confirmed multi-winner landscape) will likely resolve within 12 to 24 months.
