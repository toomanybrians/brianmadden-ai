# AI Just Cracked the Hardest Problem in Mathematics. It Will Change Everything

## Insights

- An Anthropic employee (Levent Alpöge), working with Claude, published a claimed proof of the Riemann Hypothesis: 17 million lines of Lean code (machine-verified) but no human-readable explanation — his attempt at one ran to 250,000+ pages he called "unreadable...slop."
- The same week, a related team used Claude to solve a harder form of the Navier-Stokes problem, reinforcing a pattern of AI cracking historically "unsolvable" math problems in rapid succession.
- Mathematicians are split: some call the release of an unverified, unintelligible proof irresponsible ("frankly completely outrageous" — Brian Conrad); others (Tao, Maynard, Bridson) see it as a potentially historic result, even while unable to fully verify or understand it yet.
- A recurring worry: the proof may not require genuinely new mathematics, just exhaustive application of existing techniques at inhuman scale — undermining the traditional view that RH-level problems require inventing new mathematical frameworks (contra Tao's prior prediction that RH needed a new kind of math).
- The piece frames this as creating a "silicracy" rather than democratization: only an Anthropic employee with access to private frontier models (not public Claude, which even Tao uses) could produce this result — meaning research breakthroughs now depend on proprietary model access and even a CEO's permission to publish, not raw intelligence or effort.
- Speculates mathematicians may become "cuckulators" — prompting AI and cleaning up its outputs rather than doing original work — with a "centaur" phase (human+AI collaboration) framed as temporary, likely to end once humans become pure friction in human-AI collaboration, as already seen in chess (Stockfish vs. Stockfish).
- Raises the risk of permanent illegibility: if AI-generated proofs exceed human capacity to verify or understand even with explanation, human and machine knowledge production could structurally diverge going forward.
