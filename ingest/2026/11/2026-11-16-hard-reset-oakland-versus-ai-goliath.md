# Oakland versus AI Goliath

## Insights

- Oakland City Council unanimously extended a data center moratorium to October, blocking (for now) a Behring Company proposal to convert a former supercomputing complex into a 20,000 sq ft data center anchoring a larger "AI campus" downtown.
- Local survey data (Care 4 Community, 424 surveys/2,400 doors in west Oakland) found 73% support a permanent ban on AI data centers in the city, with only 2% opposed — suggesting organized, data-backed community opposition rather than diffuse sentiment.
- Opponents framed data centers as extractive infrastructure benefiting absentee corporate owners (the Behring CEO doesn't live in Oakland) while imposing costs — higher electricity bills, rising rents, pollution — on historically redlined, disinvested neighborhoods (west/east Oakland).
- Several speakers linked AI data center buildout explicitly to surveillance/enforcement infrastructure, citing concurrent ICE operations in Oakland and arguing AI technology is being used by state/corporate actors against immigrant communities.
- The piece situates the fight in a broader pattern: developers propose data centers on already industrially-zoned land as "reindustrialization" promising jobs, without specifying how many jobs would be local or permanent.
- Despite the moratorium win, it doesn't block two other in-progress west Oakland developments tied to Bezos (Prometheus/American Steel site) and Musk, indicating large-scale AI infrastructure buildout continues on adjacent fronts.

## Quote

> Data centers are a part of the necessary infrastructure for the rise of an economically and politically untouchable class.
