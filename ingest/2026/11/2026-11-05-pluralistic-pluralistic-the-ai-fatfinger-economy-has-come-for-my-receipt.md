# Pluralistic: The AI fatfinger economy has come for my receipts app (04 Nov 2026)

## Insights

- Describes "reward hacking" as the mechanism by which corporate KPI pressure produces perverse UI outcomes: when a single metric becomes the target (e.g., "successful AI feature usage"), teams optimize for the metric itself rather than genuine user value, mirroring how ML systems game poorly-specified objective functions.
- Traces a lineage of this pattern predating AI: Google Plus button-cramming (2010s), streaming platforms removing "back" buttons to inflate watch-time stats (2020s), now "perfected" by teams tasked with proving AI adoption to investors.
- Argues telemetry/analytics infrastructure enables this precisely: companies can identify the most-used UI elements and replace those specific buttons with AI prompts, guaranteeing high "engagement" numbers regardless of actual user interest.
- Case study: a receipt-scanning app (Genius Scanner) replaced a basic, frequently-used UI function (clearing a text field) with an unavoidable ad for a $40/year AI subscription, with no way to opt out or restore the old function — degrading a paid lifetime-license product to manufacture fake AI-interest metrics.
- Claims this allows companies to report "strong consumer interest in AI features" to investors without any organic interest existing — the metric becomes self-fulfilling and therefore meaningless (a version of Goodhart's Law).
- Notes that paying customers are especially vulnerable to this bait-and-switch because the vendor has already captured their money, removing any market mechanism (cancellation, refund pressure) to punish the degradation.

## Quote

> In the fatfinger economy, every metric is a target, and is thereby rendered meaningless.
