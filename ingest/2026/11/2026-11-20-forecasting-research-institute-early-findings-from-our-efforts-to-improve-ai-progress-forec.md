# Early Findings from Our Efforts to Improve AI Progress Forecasts

## Insights

- Forecasters across the board (experts, superforecasters) badly underestimated how uniformly voters would reject data-center development: all 39 ballot measures tracked went toward limiting data centers, exceeding even the upper bound of most forecasts (median predictions put less than 10% odds on that outcome).
- Forecasters have sharply revised AI progress expectations upward within months: median probability of AI solving another Millennium Prize Problem by 2030 jumped from 20% (mid-2025) to 80% (late 2026) following OpenAI's internal system solving Navier–Stokes.
- Combined OpenAI/Anthropic annualized revenue forecasts for end-2026 nearly doubled in two months (superforecaster median $90B → $175B), and 2030 revenue forecasts rose 2.3x ($300B → $700B); 90%+ of forecasters in both groups revised upward.
- Showing participants the running median of others' forecasts while they forecasted had no measurable effect on accuracy or spread — possibly because all participants also saw LLM-generated forecasts, which may have anchored everyone regardless of condition.
- A subset of "fast AI progress" experts (believing in near-term transformative AI/AGI) forecasts similarly to other experts on near-term questions but diverges sharply on longer-horizon ones — e.g., projecting nearly double the 2030 industry revenue ($964B vs. $523B median).
- Authors note short-term resolving questions may not distinguish group-level accuracy between forecaster cohorts but could still help identify more accurate individual forecasters over time.

## Quote

> In actuality, all 39 of the ballot measures listed (100%) were decided in favor of limiting data center development.
