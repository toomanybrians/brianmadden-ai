# Claim 5 is about past deployments. Evaluators re-judge flagged trajectories that

## Insights

- Describes an evaluation framework with numbered "claims" used to audit AI safety/deployment processes, of which at least two (4 and 5) are detailed here.
- Claim 5 focuses on auditing past deployments: evaluators re-examine trajectories previously flagged and dismissed as benign, and independently re-sweep logged production traffic to catch missed "RID" (rogue/internal deployment-type) attempts.
- Claim 4 focuses on disclosure and response capability: evaluators trace both real and deliberately planted incidents through the full triage, escalation, and reporting chain.
- Claim 4 also tests organizational readiness to act — specifically verifying who has actual authority and ability to halt a model, including during off-hours.
- Underlying premise: detection of problematic AI behavior is insufficient on its own — it only matters if paired with timely disclosure to people who can respond, and if initial review processes are themselves periodically re-audited for false negatives.

## Quote

> Detection only matters if the right people learn about it in time to respond.
