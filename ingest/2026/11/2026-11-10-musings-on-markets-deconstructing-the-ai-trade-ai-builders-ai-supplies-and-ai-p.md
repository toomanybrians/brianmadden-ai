# Deconstructing the AI Trade: AI Builders, AI Supplies and AI Products!

## Insights

- Damodaran segments the "AI trade" into four groups with diverging economics: AI Suppliers (chips, electrical equipment, power, real estate — first to show revenue/profit), AI Builders (hyperscalers like Alphabet/Amazon/Meta/Microsoft plus Oracle/Coreweave — capex hits earnings/cash flow immediately, payoff comes later via leasing), AI Products (LLMs like OpenAI/Anthropic, Palantir — last to profitability, funded by capex spending elsewhere), and AI Customers (the real determinant of long-term value, currently generating only ~$250B in aggregate ARR against far larger invested capital).
- The same capex dollar is accounted for differently across groups: builders record it as a capital expense (amortized, dragging cash flow), suppliers record it as current-year revenue (boosting earnings), and product companies later record their lease/compute payments as either operating or financial expenses — creating divergent earnings optics from one transaction.
- Financial cross-holdings (Microsoft/Nvidia in OpenAI, Alphabet/Amazon in Anthropic, Nvidia backstopping data center financing) create circular financing and accounting opacity, often used to prop up weaker players or sustain investment momentum.
- Argues the "easy AI trade" (buy anything AI-labeled) is over as interests diverge: overbuilding by builders hurts builders but benefits suppliers; long-term leases benefit builders but raise default risk for product companies locked into compute commitments.
- Frames AI as a textbook "Big Market Delusion" — large market potential plus overconfidence among founders/VCs/CEOs leads to systematic overinvestment, overcommitment to compute, and market overpricing, followed by an eventual (unpredictable) correction.
- Post-election market drop (Nov 5, ~$1.8T in AI market cap lost in one day) hit chip/electrical suppliers hardest while hyperscalers' aggregate market cap actually rose that week, read as evidence of a maturing market now discriminating between AI groups rather than treating "AI" as monolithic.
- Election results signaling voter aversion to data centers (Texas holds ~a third of near-term US data center buildout) is flagged as a fundamentals-level risk to compute growth and a factor pressuring Anthropic's IPO pricing downward from earlier $1.5–2T estimates.

## Quote

> As AI moves from the hype phase to business building, I hope that we stop talking about "the AI trade."
