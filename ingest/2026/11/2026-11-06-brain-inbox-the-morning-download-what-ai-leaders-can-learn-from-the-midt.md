# The Morning Download: What AI Leaders Can Learn from the Midterms

## Insights

- Post-midterm political backlash is creating concrete regulatory friction for AI infrastructure: governors-elect in Ohio and Texas have called for conditional freezes on new data center construction, and Meta's president acknowledged future builds will be "harder."
- Grassroots opposition to data centers is broad and bipartisan: more than a dozen Ohio towns voted to halt/restrict development, with similar bans passing in Kansas, Florida, and California; a Ballotpedia/CNBC review found voters rejected data centers in 40 of 41 local ballot measures across 10 states.
- AI venture funding may be cooling independent of politics: PitchBook reported global AI venture funding fell 43.4% quarter-over-quarter to $94.3 billion in Q3, the second consecutive quarterly drop.
- Paradoxically, a regulatory-driven slowdown in new construction could benefit incumbents with existing data centers/power already secured — scarcity value rises and capex/credit pressures ease for companies already running infrastructure (cited: Amazon and Microsoft both rallied 6%+ post-election).
- Mercedes-Benz is deploying edge-based conversational AI (Liquid AI foundation models) directly in vehicles for navigation/music, with broader queries handed off to cloud models — framed as a possible template for other on-device products like wearables and smartglasses.
- OpenAI's Greg Brockman said the company had a "pretty rough" first half of the year losing enterprise ground to Anthropic, but rebounded via new coding tools, with enterprise now OpenAI's largest revenue segment (including a new JPMorgan Chase partnership for AI/cybersecurity services).
- Cited economic stakes: Goldman Sachs projects AI investment will account for 1.9% of US GDP this year; LinkedIn estimates 750,000 new US jobs (2023–Sept) tied to AI.
