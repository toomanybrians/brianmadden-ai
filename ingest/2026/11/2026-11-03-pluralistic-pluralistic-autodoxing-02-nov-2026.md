# Pluralistic: Autodoxing (02 Nov 2026)

## Insights

- A new malware strain ("Volta") uses an LLM prompted to "act like an intelligence analyst" to produce a structured, citation-backed dossier on hacking victims, merging stolen data with prior breach troves (Ticketmaster, Disney, AT&T, etc.) to prioritize victims by exploitation value.
- The malware augments its victim profile by combing through email and app data (Gmail, Doordash, Uber Eats), then uses this merged dossier to make social-engineering attacks (calls, emails, chatbot-driven cons) far more convincing.
- The piece frames AI's role here as amplification, not origination: AI's new "potency" in infostealer attacks is attributed to the vast pools of breach data created by decades of lax corporate/government data retention policy — AI is "supercharged by breach data" rather than inventing the underlying vulnerability.
- Malware (Volta) is noted as disproportionately targeting younger users' sessions (Discord, Telegram, Steam, Roblox), raising concern that mandatory "age verification" data collection will hand attackers even richer troves to target minors with AI-enhanced precision.
- General argument: policy decisions to permit/require mass data collection (by private firms or states) create long-fused, cumulative risk — AI-driven attacks are presented as the current payload of that accumulated risk rather than a standalone phenomenon.

## Quote

> AI capabilities have made infostealers much worse, but the reason for that new potency lies in how AI capabilities are supercharged by breach data.
