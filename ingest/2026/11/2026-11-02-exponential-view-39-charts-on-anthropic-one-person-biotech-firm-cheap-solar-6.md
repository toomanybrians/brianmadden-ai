# 🔮 39 charts on Anthropic, one-person biotech firm & cheap solar #608

## Insights

- Applies Ronald Coase's 1937 theory of the firm (firms exist where internal coordination costs beat market transaction costs) to ask how AI agents shift that boundary.
- Argues AI agents differ from human employees in two structural ways: they can share identical reasoning patterns (model weights) and the same working state, and handoffs between agents lose no information — unlike human-to-human handoffs.
- Introduces the term "supercooperative firms" — organizations where these AI properties make scale increasingly advantageous at every size, potentially producing very large, AI-run firms in big, deep markets.
- Notes this concentration trend predates AI: the largest 1% of American firms already held 97% of corporate assets in 2018, suggesting AI could accelerate rather than originate the pattern.
- Cites Anthropic's simultaneous presence in coding, bio, legal, and finance as an example of an AI-capable firm scaling horizontally across markets past traditional Coasian limits.
- Suggests this could necessitate new regulatory or governance frameworks for firms of unprecedented scale enabled by AI.
