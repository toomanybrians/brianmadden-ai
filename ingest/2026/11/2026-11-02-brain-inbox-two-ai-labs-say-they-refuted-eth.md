# Two AI Labs Say They Refuted ETH

## Insights

- OpenAI and Anthropic both published papers (six hours apart, Oct 26) claiming to refute the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH), a decades-old conjecture underlying complexity theory, with both results formally verified by the Lean proof checker.
- Anthropic says over 800 published papers rely on ETH (or a stronger variant) for at least one result; those results don't become false if ETH falls, but they lose the theoretical premise supporting their hardness claims.
- The two labs give different, mutually unverifiable accounts of discovery: OpenAI names no model or compute figure, while Anthropic says an internal Claude research model found the core algorithm using 14 million output tokens with "no human input," before researchers handled presentation.
- The two independently-derived proofs reach the same asymptotic result and share some underlying machinery, which the piece treats as corroborating evidence but also as a sign the result was "within reach" once enough machine search was applied.
- This follows OpenAI's September claim on a Millennium Prize fluid-flow problem and over 700 machine-written manuscripts in early October, raising the piece's central framing question: who controls verification and disclosure when the discovering systems are internal and non-public.
- OpenAI also claims, in the same batch, a harmonic analysis proof settling the Kakeya conjecture in all dimensions and an existence proof in particle physics — claims not independently detailed in the excerpt.

## Quote

> Have the machines started producing genuinely new science, and if so, who controls it: the researchers who have to check it, or the two companies whose models produced it?
