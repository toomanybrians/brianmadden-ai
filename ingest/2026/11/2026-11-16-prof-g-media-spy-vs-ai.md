# Spy vs. AI

## Insights

- Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned after disclosure of an affair with a woman U.S. intelligence officials suspected of spying for China, plus affairs with subordinates in violation of company policy; the board had reportedly been looking for reasons to keep him before he stepped down.
- Nvidia's board is characterized as structurally weak for a company of its scale and national-security importance: half the directors predate the AI pivot, average tenure is ~15 years (nearly double the S&P 500 norm), and none have significant national security experience or current/former S&P 500 CEO credentials.
- The piece frames Nvidia as a single point of failure for U.S. AI infrastructure — its chips reportedly run Pentagon classified AI, intelligence agency models, and nuclear-arsenal-supporting supercomputers — making governance and leadership security at one company a national security issue.
- Huang's public position on China chip exports reportedly reversed within about two years, from accepting export restrictions to lobbying for and winning approval to sell advanced chips (H200) to China, a shift the piece says looks "compromised" in hindsight though no public evidence of deliberate wrongdoing is cited.
- Prominent VCs and founders (Sequoia's Shaun Maguire, Anduril's Palmer Luckey, a16z's Martin Casado) publicly defended Huang after the scandal broke, which the piece interprets as evidence of "founder idolatry" overriding governance/security concerns.
- No successor was in place when Huang departed despite the company's scale, with CFO Colette Kress named interim CEO — framed as a failure of succession planning rather than proof Huang was irreplaceable.

## Quote

> Our most critical node isn't a factory. It's a handful of men who are protected by boards vs. governed by them.
