# This new paper by Anthropic is phenomenal:

What matters isn’t the AI you have,

## Insights

- Based on 66 interviews across 58 Anthropic enterprise customers, the research finds AI value comes mostly from increasing output (accelerating work, improving quality, or creating new offerings), not from cost-cutting.
- Three law firms applying similar AI tech show different outcomes: one automated a mailroom workflow and displaced clerks, one struggled with adoption bottlenecks (hallucinations, confidentiality fears, inconsistent use) and saw blunted gains, one reorganized roles entirely (junior associates paired with seniors to review AI drafts) without job losses.
- The highest-value AI applications required significant organizational change — merged roles, altered reporting lines, dropped functions — while low-organizational-change applications (slide decks, ad hoc analysis) tended to be rated lower value.
- Documented employment effects: layoffs directly from AI automation were rare (2 of the companies studied), but two-thirds reported reduced hiring, and nine companies cut contracted/offshore roles (customer service, legal, dev work) as AI handled modular tasks more cheaply and reliably.
- Some employees captured AI-driven time savings personally (less work, more leisure) rather than passing productivity gains to the firm, which created friction with managers who suspected reduced effort.
- Top-down automation sometimes pushed out AI-resistant employees (5 companies described this), while AI-native startups push agentic, largely autonomous workflows further than most incumbents.
