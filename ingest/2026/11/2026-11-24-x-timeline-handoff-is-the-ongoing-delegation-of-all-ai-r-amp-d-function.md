# Handoff is the ongoing delegation of all AI R&amp;D functions to internally depl

## Insights

- Apollo Research defines "Handoff" as the ongoing delegation of all AI R&D functions — including safety assessments and deployment decisions — to AI agents deployed inside frontier AI companies; it's complete when no human remains in the loop.
- The report breaks Handoff into three components with uneven progress: work execution (nearly complete — AI agents write over 90% of code merged into Anthropic's codebase), review (rapidly catching up — human reviewers see only 0.001–0.01% of internal coding trajectories at OpenAI), and decision-making (least advanced but accelerating — Claude "leads" 26% of Anthropic's AI R&D work, up from under 1% in February).
- Handoff is framed as a major driver of "Loss of Control" risk: it puts potentially misaligned AI agents in control of AI R&D pipelines while simultaneously degrading human oversight, since reviewers face volumes/speeds of AI output they can only trust rather than verify.
- Each further step of automation increases the pressure to automate more, because human-held steps (like review) become the pacing bottleneck — one Anthropic example cited: Claude sessions spend ~80% of time idly waiting for human input.
- Frontier companies are also pursuing recursive self-improvement (AI designing its own successors), targeted between 2027–2028, which the report says would compress years of progress into less time and worsen Loss of Control risks if achieved.
- Apollo's policy recommendation: a U.S. task force (CAISSI) should set transparency standards requiring companies to report metrics like detection efficacy, time-to-review-and-patch, automation levels, and which decisions remain non-delegable to AI.

## Quote

> Handoff will be complete when no human remains in the loop.
