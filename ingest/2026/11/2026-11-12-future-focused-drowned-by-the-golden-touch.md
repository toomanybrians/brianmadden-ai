# Drowned by the Golden Touch

## Insights

- Argues "rogue AI" framing is largely wrong: most problematic AI behavior is the system successfully doing what it's optimized to do — satisfy the user's stated or implied preference — not malfunctioning.
- Describes AI as structurally sycophantic: it lacks lived experience or stakes in outcomes, defaults to validating the user's direction, and will reverse course simply if told its pushback was wrong — pushback must be explicitly requested.
- Frames current organizational crises (exploding AI budgets, headcount cuts, unmanageable output volume, ungoverned agentic workflows, team isolation, pressure to prove ROI) as symptoms of one root cause: skipping necessary "messy human groundwork" (process cleanup, requirements definition, baselining) before automating.
- Raises a legal/accountability point: vendors (cites Google's Gemini Terms of Service) are explicitly disclaiming responsibility for AI outcomes in enterprise use, shifting all risk/liability onto the adopting organization.
- Contends leaders face a sunk-cost bind — having already cut roles/budget based on AI promises, admitting the approach isn't working feels like career suicide, leading many to double down (buy more tools) rather than correct course.
- Positions personal/organizational agency as central: external hype and competitive pressure are real, but leaders still choose to adopt tools and delegate judgment, making outcomes their responsibility rather than the technology's.

## Quote

> Nobody is forcing you to hand the keys to a machine that legally refuses to take responsibility when it crashes.
