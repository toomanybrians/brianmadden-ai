## Insights

- A new tracker (Offsec Harness Index) catalogs 154 open-source "offensive AI agent harnesses" on GitHub, cross-referencing which have been confirmed or observed in real-world attacks versus those with no attack record.
- Of the tracked harnesses, only 14 have documented real-world use (9 confirmed, 5 observed), tied to 26 distinct attacker groups and 7 confirmed breaches — suggesting most open-source offensive AI tooling remains theoretical/unused rather than weaponized.
- Documented cases show AI agent harnesses performing multi-step autonomous operations: one controller hit 200+ organizations in 11 days; another ran 105 attack projects in under a week, compromising 27+ companies.
- State-linked and criminal actors (China-nexus groups, North Korean state actors, Russian/French-speaking criminal groups) are named as having run specific named harnesses (ARTEX, Cairn, PentAGI, etc.) against real targets, per reports from Microsoft, CrowdStrike, Google GTIG, and others.
- Evidence quality varies widely — some tools show full breach chains with confirmed data theft, others (like HexStrike) are flagged as "experimentation" or have outputs that "could not be verified."
- The index explicitly distinguishes a harness's own README claims (what it says it can do) from tested/confirmed real-world capability, with 25 of 154 harnesses self-limiting their claimed use to recon/scanning only.
