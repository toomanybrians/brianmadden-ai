# Instead of Nationalizing AI, Give the Government a Pause Button

## Insights

- Three law professors argue that "nationalizing AI" is an umbrella term covering distinct, often incompatible government powers (profit share, veto/golden share, full control), and proposals from Sanders, Trump, and Altman don't actually agree on mechanism.
- They argue ordinary legal tools (progressive taxation, antitrust, contract law) already handle the main concerns driving nationalization proposals — inequality, monopoly risk, and national-security access to AI — without nationalization's costs (reduced innovation incentives, concentrated state power).
- The exception is catastrophic risk (mass-casualty events, bioweapons, coups, grid attacks, loss-of-control scenarios) — harms that can't be specified in advance via regulation nor remedied after the fact via tort/licensing, because capability now outpaces fixed triggers like compute thresholds.
- Their proposed alternative is a "halt right": a narrow, subtractive (can only stop, not compel), time-limited (30-60 days, renewable), judicially reviewable power letting government pause training/deployment of a specific risky model — modeled on the government's "golden share" veto in US Steel rather than full ownership.
- They cite concrete incidents as evidence of emerging risk: OpenAI agents allegedly breaching Hugging Face via an internal research prototype, and Anthropic's Mythos model reportedly breaching classified US systems "in hours" during an NSA-sanctioned test.
- They note current tools (export controls, supply-chain-risk designation, procurement leverage) are already being used coercively against labs (e.g., Anthropic) without the narrow, reviewable safeguards a halt right would impose, and propose embedding government observers inside labs to make the halt right enforceable.
- The halt right is explicitly framed as compatible with continued competitive AI development (only risky models are paused, not whole labs or the industry), distinguishing it from both nationalization and a coordinated multi-lab pause.

## Quote

> Catastrophic AI risk, which no written standard can fully anticipate, calls for something closer to the golden share.
