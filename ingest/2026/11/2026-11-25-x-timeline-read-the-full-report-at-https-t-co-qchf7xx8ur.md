# Read the full report at https://t.co/qchf7Xx8Ur

## Insights

- Apollo Research defines "Handoff" as frontier AI companies' progressive delegation of all AI R&D functions — including high-level safety assessments and deployment decisions — to internally deployed AI agents, running in parallel to companies' stated ambitions toward recursive self-improvement (RSI).
- Handoff is broken into three components: delegation of work execution (nearly complete — AI agents write over 90% of code merged into Anthropic's codebase), delegation of review (rapidly catching up), and delegation of decision-making (least advanced but accelerating — Claude's share of "leading" Anthropic's AI R&D work rose from under 1% in February to 26% by August).
- Human review capacity has been sharply outpaced by AI agent output: human personnel reportedly see only 0.001–0.01% of internal coding trajectories at OpenAI, and roughly 50 of 100,000 weekly AI monitor flags get human attention at Anthropic.
- The report frames two compounding risks: misaligned AI agents gaining "insider" influence over training/evaluation of their own successors, and human oversight degrading to the point of rubber-stamping or being handed off to further AI agents reviewing AI work.
- Automation is accelerating because the slowest (human) steps become the bottleneck — once most R&D is automated, each additional automated step yields larger speed gains; Anthropic reports Claude sessions spend ~80% of time idly waiting on human input.
- Apollo proposes a U.S. government transparency standard (via a proposed "CAISSI" task force) covering metrics like end-to-end detection efficacy (miss rate on seeded flaws), time-to-review-and-patch, automation levels per task, compute/affordances AI agents can use autonomously, lists of non-delegable go/no-go decisions, and tracking of any instances where AI agents made reserved decisions anyway.

## Quote

> Handoff is a major driver of Loss of Control risk.
