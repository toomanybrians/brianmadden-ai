# 🚨 AI 2027 Authors Now Expect 2027 Takeoff

## Insights

- The AI Futures Project (authors of AI 2027) revised its forecast forward: all its forecasters now expect a "superhuman AI researcher" — an AI good enough that a lab would rather run on it than its best human scientists — by 2027, absent a deliberate slowdown. Previously even the most bullish author gave only even odds before 2029.
- The trigger was METR's estimate that Anthropic's internal AI progress has roughly doubled in pace, with ~80% of that acceleration attributed to AI doing the company's own research; the forecasters interpret this as a 1.7x AI-driven speedup, a scenario their own model gave under 4% odds of three months ago.
- The group now puts 35%–65% odds on AI capable of doing all white-collar work arriving before 2028, and is calling for an industry-wide pause followed by an international agreement, citing concerns that labs aren't on track to keep a superhuman AI researcher reliably aligned.
- In tension with this, Stanford's AI100 (100-Year Study on AI) report, also released this week, argues frontier scaling is hitting data and cost limits, and predicts future gains will come from reasoning, memory, and agents rather than bigger models — while admitting its own 2021 panel badly underestimated scaling's trajectory.
- The AI100 report explicitly warns that when insiders converge on a view of "what comes next," they may just be recognizing the limits of the current paradigm rather than correctly foreseeing the next one — a direct rebuttal angle to the AI 2027 team's acceleration claims.
- Separately noted in the same roundup: DeepMind's AlphaProof Code used AI agents to formally verify security properties of Signal's encryption implementation in four weeks (versus years for expert teams), illustrating a concrete capability jump in AI-assisted formal verification.
