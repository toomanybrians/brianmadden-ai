# ⚙️ Why the IMF is worried about AI circular financing

## Insights

- A near five-hour FAA systems outage (first "ground stop" since Jan 2023) triggered immediate speculation about AI involvement, but officials attributed it to contractor error/faulty equipment during a copper-to-fiber networking upgrade, not the FAA's new $875M AI-powered SMART system.
- SMART (Strategic Management of Airspace, Routes and Trajectories) centralizes 200 data streams into one platform and had just been expanded nationwide three days before the outage; despite being cleared of blame, it had already drawn Congressional requests for an audit over hallucination risks in mission-critical infrastructure.
- IMF director Kristalina Georgieva warns that "circular financing" in AI — where investors fund companies that funnel money back via purchases/investments — has reached roughly $1 trillion, two-thirds debt-funded, and could become a broader financial stability risk if the AI sector wobbles.
- Nvidia's equity investments ballooned from ~$2.2B two years ago to $99B as of July, with ~$50B going to frontier AI labs; leadership turmoil (CEO ouster) raises questions about whether the next leadership will pull back from these financing arrangements, which could disproportionately hurt smaller "neocloud" providers dependent on Nvidia capital.
- OpenAI has embedded four independent auditors (Apollo Research, METR, Transluce, Goodfire) with researcher-level access to checkpoints, code, data, and monitoring systems, focused initially on alignment and safeguards — following a similar Anthropic move and a White House "morally binding" self-regulation pledge.
- OpenAI retains rights to redact confidential findings and request disclosure delays, raising questions (reinforced by a prior Reuters report on labs limiting auditor publications) about how much of what embedded auditors uncover the public will actually see.

## Quote

> "We have a world economy that is standing on one leg on the AI leg. If this leg wobbles, that can lead to financial stability troubles." — Kristalina Georgieva
