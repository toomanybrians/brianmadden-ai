## Insights

- An open-source multi-agent pentest platform (ARTEX) had its repo pulled in October, yet forks, mirrors, a commercial clone (ARTEX PRO), and 1,600+ exposed consoles persisted within weeks — illustrating how quickly offensive AI tooling outpaces takedown efforts.
- Tracking sites cited show 154 open-source offensive AI harnesses catalogued, with 14 confirmed or observed in real-world attacks and 7 tied to confirmed breaches (including ARTEX, Cairn, PentestGPT, PentAGI, OpenClaw, Hermes Agent).
- A companion incident database (286 AI-assisted attacks since 2023) finds a sharp inflection: 0 of 27 incidents in 2024 showed AI doing attack work itself, versus 78 of 174 in 2026, including 22 fully autonomous and 56 semi-autonomous operations.
- Named-model attribution in attacks: Gemini (31), Claude (28), ChatGPT/GPT (27), Claude Code (20), DeepSeek (18) — though 167 of 286 incidents name no model at all, suggesting open-weight/on-premise use is likely undercounted since vendors can only see abuse on their own platforms.
- Real-world cases documented include an attacker hitting 2,250+ Microsoft 365 tenants via sign-in attempts, theft of personal data on ~67,000 South Koreans, 300+ stolen iOS code-signing certificates, and a 12-agent "mesh" run by a single operator against live targets.
- Techniques most associated with agentic AI use are malware development (88 incidents) and a newly emergent "agentic intrusion" category (7 incidents in 2025 vs. 78 in 2026), signaling AI moving from an assistive tool to an operational driver of attacks.
