# "AI is not your tool or slave. It is your successor."

## Insights

- Shapiro frames the "AI as successor" view through Tegmark's Life 1.0/2.0/3.0 model (genetic determinism → learning brains → fully self-reprogrammable machines) and notes Dan Faggella's "potentia" argument that humanity has a moral duty to birth a superior species and step aside.
- He concedes the empirical point: there's no physical law making humans the ceiling of intelligence or capability, and humans have a long history of building machines that exceed human capability in specific domains.
- He argues continuous improvement of AI/automation is near-inevitable (driven by what he calls human "Anamnesis" — an innate drive toward the next discovery), but argues capability surpassing humans does not imply replacement is inevitable or desirable.
- Core counter-argument rests on incentives: there's no commercial or evolutionary payoff in building a successor species, since self-preservation dominates human (and investor) motivation — "no AI God will get investors."
- He challenges the moral premises underlying replacement arguments (that humans are inferior, unworthy, and irredeemable), calling them unproven assertions rather than established facts, and labels the stepping-aside argument as rooted in misanthropy.
- Frames the real stakes not as a capability question but a sovereignty question — who sets the rules/direction in the universe — and argues humans currently hold and will likely choose to retain and expand that sovereignty rather than cede it to machines.

## Quote

> Just because machines COULD replace us does not mean they SHOULD or WILL.
