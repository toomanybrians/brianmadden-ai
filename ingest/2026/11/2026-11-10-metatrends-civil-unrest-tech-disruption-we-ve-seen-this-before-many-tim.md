# Civil Unrest, Tech Disruption: We’ve Seen This Before... Many Times

## Insights

- Diamandis ranks 2025–2026 as #9 of 10 on a judgment-based civil unrest scale (score 27/40: distrust 10, legitimacy 8, violence 5, economy 4), below periods like the 2020-21 pandemic/BLM/Jan 6 era, the Great Depression, Reconstruction, and the Civil War.
- AI/robotics disruption ranks #7 of 10 on his technological disruption scale (28/40: adoption 10, fear 8, backlash 6, job disruption 4), below railroads, factory textile mills, farm mechanization, electrification, and industrial robots/computers.
- Cites specific data points: Pew (June 2026) found 52% of Americans more concerned than excited about AI, 71% expect fewer jobs in 20 years; Challenger tracked 121,401 AI-cited job-cut announcements through October 2026; ChatGPT hit 900M weekly users by Feb 2026; October 2026 unemployment was 4.1%.
- Notes a gap between measured economic impact and perceived fear — e.g., Q2 2025 saw ~20 data-center projects (~$98B) blocked/delayed by local opposition, and Figure's humanoid robots logged 1,250+ hours at BMW Spartanburg, yet actual job destruction isn't matching "nightmare" predictions.
- Argues every past major tech wave (automation scare of 1950s-60s, computers/robots 1980-2010, nuclear age) produced backlash and fear followed by higher living standards for those who adapted — framing current AI anxiety as a recurring historical pattern rather than a novel crisis.
- Offers differentiated guidance: entrepreneurs should build "adaptation layer" tools; executives should measure AI by output/redeployment rather than headline headcount cuts; investors should note the gap between high fear (Pew numbers) and low measured labor collapse (4.1% unemployment) as a mispricing opportunity.

## Quote

> Adoption is historic. Measured job destruction, so far, is not matching the nightmare script.
