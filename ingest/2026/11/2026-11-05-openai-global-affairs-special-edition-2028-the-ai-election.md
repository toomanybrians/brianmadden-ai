# Special Edition: 2028 – The AI Election

## Insights

- Frames AI as the defining 2028 US election issue, comparing the moment to 1932's industrialization-driven social contract crisis and FDR's New Deal response.
- Cites polling: 65% of voters say AI matters to their vote; 81% say AI couldn't have a soul; only 41% expect to personally benefit financially from AI vs. 84% who expect the richest 1% to benefit.
- Notes a shift from 2026 (one in five Democratic ads attacked data centers, little room to be "pro-AI infrastructure") to an anticipated 2028 electorate with more firsthand AI usage and experience rather than fear-based opinions.
- Argues AI-driven productivity gains require rethinking tax policy (ownership, profit-sharing, access to capital) since existing tax systems assume older labor/capital relationships.
- Frames jobs argument around AI infrastructure buildout itself as a source of union/trade employment (e.g., one compute campus needing 25,000 tradespeople), distinct from AI's effects on existing professional roles.
- Positions US-China AI competition as a question of past investment choices (energy, compute, talent) rather than future potential, warning that blocking infrastructure could cede ground to China.

## Quote

> The ultimate measure of whether America wins on AI isn't just whether we have the world's most powerful AI. It's whether Americans are more powerful because of it.
