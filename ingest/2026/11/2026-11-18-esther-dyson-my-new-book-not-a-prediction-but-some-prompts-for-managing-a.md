# My new book: Not a prediction, but some prompts for managing AI

## Insights

- Dyson's forthcoming book ("In Defense of Limits: Human Time and AI Scale," MIT Press, March 2027) argues for focusing on AI policy rather than politics, as a response to AI's growing economic power and unpredictability.
- Central distinction: AI offers intelligence, but humans possess wisdom — the capacity to make moral trade-offs between unquantifiable alternatives, which AI cannot do but humans can "feel."
- She warns that AI can supercharge both good and bad outcomes equally, so society needs new ways of measuring and defining what's actually valuable, not just more efficient.
- Argues against a maximizing mindset — money, power, even happiness — proposing that limits in all directions, not "more," are what let people find meaning in finite life.
- Frames the goal as empowering individuals and collectively controlling AI's power, envisioning society as "a complex, emergent system striving for collective well-being" rather than a machine to optimize.
- Draws on her personal history (tech industry since 1982, ICANN founding chair, NASA, community-building work, time observing Russia) as the basis for her perspective on institutional drift and loss of mission over time.

## Quote

> Human life should not be about "more," but about trade-offs between things that can be valued but not priced.
