# 📈 AI-exposed workers wait a month longer for a job

## Insights

- Workers laid off from AI-exposed roles in 2025 took roughly one month longer than peers in other roles to find new employment — a concrete labor-market signal of AI's disruption effect on re-employment speed, not just job counts.
- Anthropic purchased a supplier's existing two-year-old order for 1.7GW of gas generators for $3.4 billion, described as the largest known power-equipment purchase by an AI lab — signals AI labs now competing directly for energy infrastructure, including secondhand claims on existing orders.
- 2026 US VC funding data, when Anthropic and SpaceX (the "two giants") are excluded, looks similar in scale to 2025 — suggesting much of the apparent AI funding surge narrative is concentrated in a small number of outsized deals rather than broad-based growth.
- Apple grew its memory-chip inventory 2.5x year-on-year, read as a hedge against anticipated memory cost increases — an enterprise supply-chain response to AI-driven demand pressure on hardware components.

## Quote

> Workers let go of jobs in AI-exposed roles in 2025 took about a month longer than peers in other roles to get a new job.
