# The Value of Human Frailty: Nvidia’s Jensen Huang Test!

## Insights

- Damodaran frames CEO value analytically: a CEO's worth equals the difference between a company's value with them versus without them, and this value can be positive, neutral, or even negative depending on company lifecycle stage.
- He identifies five CEO roles (storyteller, business manager, people leader, public face, succession planner) whose relative importance shifts as a company moves through its lifecycle — visionary/storytelling early, defense/succession-planning at maturity.
- Management matters more in companies driven by firm-specific decisions (vs. macro forces), those with contestable competitive advantages, and those at transition points (IPOs, restructurings) — Nvidia is characterized as a "unicorn" that looks like a young growth company at massive scale.
- Applied to Nvidia: Jensen Huang's departure as CEO (amid an affair scandal with alleged China-spy connections and relationships with reporting employees) could cost 5-10% of equity value if effects are short-term, or far more if long-term growth/margin assumptions are revised downward.
- Key uncertainties flagged as determining the real value impact: who replaces Huang, whether he stays informally involved, whether Nvidia's flat management layer can function without his "intuition and vision," and whether the China angle has legal/regulatory legs.
- Broader governance point: even highly capable founder-CEOs need structural restraints against overreach and entrenchment ("Imperial CEO" drift), and the strongest companies are those built to outlast their founders (cited: Microsoft after Gates, Amazon despite Bezos's public persona).

## Quote

> The essence of corporate governance is that even the very best managers need restraints to prevent overreach.
