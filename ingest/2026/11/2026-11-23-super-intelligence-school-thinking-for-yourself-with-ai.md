# Thinking for Yourself With AI

## Insights

- Reported performance averages for AI tools can improve while actual service degrades, because tickets that never get a reply (or get no reply within the measurement window) are excluded from the average entirely — they're treated as if they don't count.
- In the illustrative example, an AI reply tool handled only half of inbound tickets within the hour, but the resulting "10 minute average reply time" (down from 20) makes the tool look like a clear improvement.
- Ironically, if a previously-unanswered ticket is addressed late (e.g., at 55 minutes), the average *worsens* even though one more customer was actually helped — meaning the metric incentivizes leaving slow cases out rather than resolving them.
- The piece frames this as a critique of trusting AI-tool rollout metrics (and persuasive write-ups built on them) at face value, urging readers to define their own evaluation question *before* encountering a vendor's or team's "convincing recommendation."
- The core risk flagged: once a compelling narrative/metric is presented, it becomes hard to recall what you originally wanted to verify — a framing/anchoring problem in evaluating AI tool performance claims.

## Quote

> Once you've read a convincing recommendation, it gets surprisingly hard to remember what you wanted to know in the first place.
