# 🔮 The people vs tech; the Europoor; forgotten Creole++ #609

## Insights

- Azhar argues AI's productivity payoff hasn't yet shown up systematically in aggregate statistics, and shouldn't be expected to yet — comparing it to the PC era, where it took fourteen years for computers to register in headline productivity numbers, though individual firms reported gains years earlier.
- He expects AI to follow a similar diffusion pattern to computing, but potentially faster.
- A recent US quarter showing 3.6% productivity growth is cited by some as evidence of a productivity "turning point," but Azhar is skeptical, noting productivity data is noisy and not yet confidently attributable to AI.
- Concrete firm-level example: law firm A&O Sherman is committing 3% of billable time to AI, roughly $1.1bn over five years, with the stated ambition of "redesigning the service completely."
- Azhar reports hearing a consistent pattern in private conversations with executives: small AI successes now, paired with growing confidence about achieving large-scale results within a few years.
- Separately, a political comment is noted (Gina Hinojosa, Texas Governor-Elect) framing current politics as ordinary people versus a small number of powerful tech billionaires controlling government — cited alongside pieces questioning why AI provokes hostility in America, with one view attributing it to American individualism.
