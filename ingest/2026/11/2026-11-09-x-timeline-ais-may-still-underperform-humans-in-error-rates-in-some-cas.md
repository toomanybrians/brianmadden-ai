## Insights

- Argues that assumptions about AI underperforming humans on error rates need updating, implying AI error rates have improved substantially and may now match or beat human error rates in many cases.
- Acknowledges AI may still underperform humans in error rates in some specific cases, but frames this as increasingly the exception rather than the rule.
- Distinguishes between checking AI output for factual errors versus checking for differences in judgment, perspective, opinions, or approach — suggesting the latter remains a valid reason for human review even if error-checking isn't.
- Claims the common assumption that humans' role is to check AI work for mistakes is "likely not true today" — i.e., this framing of human-AI collaboration is outdated.
