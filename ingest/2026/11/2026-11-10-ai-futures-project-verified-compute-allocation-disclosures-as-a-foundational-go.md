# Verified Compute Allocation Disclosures as a Foundational Governance Tool

## Insights

- Proposes "pacing" — deliberately delaying the point at which AI systems reach a given capability level — and argues the first concrete step should be Verified Compute Allocation Disclosures (VCAD): companies regularly report and third-party auditors verify how compute is split across AI R&D, safety R&D, and external inference.
- Argues VCAD is a near-universal prerequisite for enforcing any pacing scheme short of simply shutting off GPUs, because almost any restriction (training-run approval, internal deployment lags, safeguard mandates) can be circumvented via unmonitored "hidden" compute.
- Distinguishes delaying training runs from slowing software/algorithmic progress; delaying only training runs risks a "software overhang" — a backlog of unused algorithmic gains that could burst out suddenly once runs are approved, or be stolen/diffused to other actors (e.g., China).
- Lays out a framework for pacing proposals along four axes: conditional vs. unconditional, input restrictions vs. safeguards, and what must be verified to enforce each.
- Suggests VCAD could drive pacing voluntarily (via public disclosure creating trust and reputational pressure among competing labs) or via future government mandate, and that building this infrastructure now is urgent given the risk of misaligned AI reaching recursive self-improvement.

## Quote

> We think that frontier AI companies should begin pacing as soon as possible.
