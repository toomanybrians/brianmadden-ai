# Sara Hooker has a warning for enterprises: Your AI provider could become your competitor

## Insights

- Sara Hooker (ex-Cohere VP of Research, now founder of Adaption) argues enterprises often fail to recognize their own proprietary data as their most valuable AI asset.
- She claims frontier labs like OpenAI and Anthropic are aggressively seeking real-world enterprise data and customer interactions to improve their models — meaning enterprises giving up this data may be fueling competitors who could later encroach on their own markets.
- This dynamic is prompting enterprises in regulated/data-sensitive industries (healthcare, finance, legal) to seek ways to reduce dependence on frontier AI labs and instead build or customize their own models using their own data.
- Hooker cites rising and unpredictable costs of frontier AI models as another driver pushing enterprises toward more independent, self-built AI systems that can continuously learn and improve.
- She argues no single AI model can serve all needs, and suggests current chatbot-style interfaces may not be the right long-term interaction model — more human-centered interfaces are needed.
- Hooker criticizes AI industry messaging around existential risk, arguing that stated extinction probabilities are presented as objective/scientific but are actually subjective, and that doom narratives conveniently consolidate power among a small number of frontier labs.

## Quote

> Extinction probabilities are often presented as scientific when they are deeply subjective.
