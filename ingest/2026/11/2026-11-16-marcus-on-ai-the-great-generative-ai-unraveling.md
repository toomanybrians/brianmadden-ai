# The great Generative AI unraveling

## Insights

- Marcus reiterates a long-standing thesis (since Aug 2023): generative AI labs share similar techniques/datasets, so no durable technical moat exists, driving price wars that erode profits despite massive training costs (he cites Claude 5.5 at a reported $10B to develop).
- He points to accumulating evidence of poor ROI and reliability problems (instruction-following failures, a hypothesized Microsoft outage from AI-written code, and the "Klarna effect" of companies publicly rehiring humans after automation claims) as signs the technology hasn't lived up to its billing.
- He flags a concrete market turn: Nvidia, CoreWeave, and Oracle stocks all down sharply (29%, 54%, 35% over six months) and Anthropic's expected IPO valuation cut from $2T to ~$1.5T — read by Marcus as a bubble deflating in real time, not just rhetoric.
- He argues the real driver of AI economics isn't the underlying math (which he says "never made sense") but market psychology — the delayed collective realization that the economics don't work, which he believes is now happening.
- Notes a leadership/structural risk: a change in Nvidia's interim leadership may mean less willingness to keep subsidizing/financing neocloud partners (e.g., Nscale) the way Jensen Huang reportedly did, which could cascade into funding trouble for companies dependent on that backing.
- Underlying claim: core AI companies (OpenAI, Anthropic, Nscale, CoreWeave) have never been demonstrably profitable on their own, aside from one subsidized quarter for Anthropic, raising doubts about their ability to stand independently if external financing dries up.
