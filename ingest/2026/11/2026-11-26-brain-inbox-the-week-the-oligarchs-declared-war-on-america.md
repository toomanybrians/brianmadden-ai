# The Week the Oligarchs Declared War on America

## Insights

- Nvidia faces three concurrent problems described in the piece: monopolistic conduct toward customers, its CEO's central role in circular financing arrangements fueling data center/AI capital expenditure, and alleged chip-smuggling to Chinese firms in violation of export controls.
- Trump's reinstatement of Jensen Huang as Nvidia CEO after a resignation tied to a national-security/espionage scandal is framed by the author as an explicit prioritization of stock market stability over law enforcement or national security concerns, with Wells Fargo likened the move to 2008-style bailout logic.
- The market reaction was immediate and large — Nvidia alone reportedly gained a trillion dollars in market value following the reinstatement, read by some (e.g., Cantor Fitzgerald) as restoring confidence that underpins the broader AI investment bubble.
- Incoming Senator Abdul El-Sayed's critique frames Nvidia as effectively "too big to fail," raising concerns about systemic economic exposure to a single company/individual's fate within the AI buildout.
- The piece treats this episode as evidence that the AI boom's financing is entangled with concentrated personal/corporate power (circular financing, data center expansion) and that this concentration generates political risk independent of whether the AI bubble itself eventually bursts.
- Author's broader framing: the episode shows Silicon Valley/Wall Street elites acting as if election outcomes and public governance constraints on big tech (data centers, monopoly, corruption) are irrelevant to them, regardless of electoral results.
