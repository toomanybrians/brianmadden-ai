# Not counting the labs part (compute/scaling/etc) this will be what improves A in

## Insights

- Proposes a single recurring loop — Idea → Capture (articulation) → Build → Evaluate (Evals) → Iterate — as the core mechanism driving AI-assisted work of any kind, from software to business, fitness, or language learning.
- Argues that AI execution is no longer the bottleneck; the real failure point is humans failing to fully or coherently articulate what they want, since AI can only act on the captured artifact, not the idea in someone's head.
- Frames "Evals" (verification checks) as nearly as important as articulation itself — turning fuzzy goals into measurable, checkable criteria is what makes iterative improvement ("hill-climbing") possible across any domain, not just engineering.
- Describes a five-question method for converting vague goals into checkable criteria: what would completion look like, how would a stranger verify it, what number by when, what would cause rejection, and what's the cheapest check for failure.
- Notes that passing all current evals but still feeling dissatisfied is itself a signal — it reveals a missing criterion, prompting the eval set to evolve alongside the goal.
- Positions this hill-climbing loop as a general-purpose problem-solving framework applicable to personal growth, enterprise work, and AI system design alike, not an AI-specific technique.
