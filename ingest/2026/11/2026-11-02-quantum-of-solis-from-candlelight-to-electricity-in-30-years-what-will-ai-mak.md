# From Candlelight To Electricity In 30 Years. What Will AI Make Possible By 2056?

## Insights

- Draws a historical analogy: the 1870–1900 shift (candlelight to electricity, wood to steel, horses to railroads/automobiles) as a model for thinking about AI's trajectory over the next 30 years — the value didn't come from improving the old technology but from redesigning systems around the new one.
- Cites Blackstone data as evidence AI ROI is real: portfolio-wide annualized Anthropic spend grew from ~$25M to ~$525M in a year (21x); Phoenix Tower turned a $4M AI investment into ~$4.5M annual value via 5x faster lease processing; Enverus reportedly sees 18x return on model spend for software engineering.
- Frames a core risk ("optimization trap"): most organizations are using AI to make existing workflows more efficient (automation, cost reduction) rather than asking what they'd build differently if intelligence were abundant rather than scarce — equates this to "making a better candle."
- Argues most companies are structurally "finite" — designed for an era of scarce expertise, slow information flow, and hierarchical coordination — and that an "Infinite Company" instead assumes intelligence (human, model, agent) can be distributed throughout the organization, continually redesigning work and decisions rather than just automating tasks.
- Predicts a shift in what customers pay for: as AI agents handle more knowledge work, demand moves away from paying for hours/seats/complexity toward paying for outcomes — with implications for pricing, org structure, workflows, and talent models.
- Notes a countervailing constraint: intelligence may become abundant, but physical infrastructure (chips, power, data centers, permitting, capital) remains scarce and slow to scale — turbines ordered now may not arrive until 2030-31 — and inside companies, the limiting factor is often organizational capacity to change, not the technology itself.

## Quote

> You can't cut your way to growth. You can't automate your way to innovation.
