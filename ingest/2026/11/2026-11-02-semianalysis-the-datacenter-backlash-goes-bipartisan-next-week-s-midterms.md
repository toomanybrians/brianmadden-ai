# The Datacenter Backlash Goes Bipartisan: Next Week's Midterms and the 2028 Buildout

## Insights

- SemiAnalysis models 62.2 GW of US datacenter net additions for 2028, most of it not yet under construction and concentrated in states holding gubernatorial elections on Nov. 3 — making this the real inflection point, not 2027 (already ~75% built).
- Anti-datacenter backlash has become genuinely bipartisan: of 20 midterm nominees tracked, 9 campaign on outright construction pauses and 11 on conditions, split roughly evenly by party; common planks include making datacenters pay grid costs (15 candidates), ending tax breaks (12), and limiting/reporting water use (11).
- Texas is the single largest risk to the national forecast — it's ~30% of 2028 US GW, and its TCEQ permit freeze (plus an ERCOT interconnection-queue audit) could last anywhere from ~8 months to ~1 year depending on whether Gov. Abbott reopens permitting after the Dec. 10 ERCOT report or his challenger's proposed moratorium-until-legislation path wins out.
- If every pro-moratorium candidate won, ~30 GW of planned 2028 capacity would sit in states with pauses; if only conditions-only candidates win, exposure drops to 5.7 GW — conditions (water studies, infrastructure cost-sharing, dropping NDAs) are seen as survivable since they change project economics rather than blocking approval outright.
- Hyperscaler insiders (ex-Amazon, Microsoft, Google) interviewed say big tech can absorb higher costs and self-fund power, but money doesn't guarantee timely permits, transmission access, or construction schedules — and relocating to another state doesn't dodge the same bottlenecks.
- Power to actually change rules is distributed unevenly: governors can freeze agency permits quickly (as in Texas and Oregon) but broader restrictions like tax-break repeals require legislatures; utility commissioners (elected in GA, AZ, OK) and PJM-region grid governance remain largely insulated from a single election's outcome.
