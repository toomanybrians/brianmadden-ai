# Anthropic just raised a record $107 billion at a $1.5 trillion valuation 🤯📈; Visa tested 22 AI Shopping Agents and 23% bought the wrong thing 🤖🛍️

## Insights

- Visa's Agentic Commerce Benchmark tested 22 AI shopping agents (from Google, Meta, OpenAI, Anthropic, and others) across 15 scenarios; overall pass rate was 57.2%, with only 34.2% of model-scenario pairs passing all three repeated attempts — showing failures cluster in specific scenarios rather than occurring randomly.
- Agents largely fail not by misreading information they have, but by failing to ask the questions needed to surface hidden requirements, or by treating a shopper's silence as confirmation no constraint exists.
- More expensive/capable models only modestly outperform cheaper ones (67% vs. 50% pass rates), suggesting better models don't fully solve the underlying failure mode.
- Liability for agent errors is unresolved and being handled inconsistently: Amex has pledged to cover charges from registered-agent errors, while JPMorgan and Wells Fargo have rewritten terms to push risk onto customers; OpenAI's ChatGPT Wallet approval-screen design similarly shifts risk to merchants/issuers even when the agent misunderstood the task.
- Existing authorization/dispute frameworks assume a human knowingly clicked "buy," which breaks down when an agent completes a technically "authorized" purchase based on a flawed understanding of the user's actual needs.
- Expect card networks/issuers to respond by restricting agent purchasing autonomy by product category — routine purchases automated first, higher-consideration purchases (e.g., TVs) kept behind human approval — and by building dispute rules requiring logs of what requirements an agent established before a sale.

## Quote

> Capable models rarely misread a fact once they have it. Yet, they fail by not asking the question that would bring it out.
