# We really are in a (temporary?) place where a combination of expertise and being

## Insights

- Current AI capability represents a temporary window where expertise combined with AI use enables breakthroughs that neither humans nor AI alone could achieve.
- Evidence cited: people who have made progress on Millennium Prize Problems (unsolved, high-difficulty math problems) using AI have themselves been accomplished mathematicians, not AI novices.
- Claim that frontier AI models cannot yet solve such problems independently — human mathematical expertise remains a necessary complement.
- A specific example referenced: a highly credentialed individual (Harvard valedictorian, Princeton PhD) who had worked on the Riemann problem for years and later moved into AI work achieved a solution — framed as a case of AI as a "complement" rather than a replacement.
- The quoted post attributes success to human judgment about what is worthwhile, verifiable, and possible — faculties framed as distinct from and necessary alongside AI capability.
- Implicit framing: this expertise-plus-AI advantage may not be permanent, suggesting an expectation that AI capability could eventually close this gap.

## Quote

> It is no coincidence that people proving Millennium Problems have all been great mathematicians, the models can't (yet) do it on their own
