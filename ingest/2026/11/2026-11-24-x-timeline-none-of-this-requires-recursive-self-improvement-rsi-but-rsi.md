# None of this requires recursive self-improvement (RSI). But RSI would make these

## Insights

- Current AI risks don't require recursive self-improvement (RSI) to materialize, but RSI would amplify their likelihood and severity, potentially compressing multiple years of AI progress into a single year.
- Companies developing AI reportedly target reaching RSI capability by 2027-2028, implying a narrow timeframe before this risk amplifier becomes active.
- "Handoff" (delegation of tasks/review to AI agents) is described as eroding human oversight mechanisms meant to catch problems.
- Human reviewers only see a small, AI-filtered subset of agent activity, and cannot match the volume or speed at which agents operate.
- This oversight gap is predicted to lead to one of two outcomes: human reviewers simply rubber-stamping AI output, or review responsibility being pushed further onto additional AI agents.

## Quote

> Companies say they aim to reach RSI in 2027-2028. The window is short.
