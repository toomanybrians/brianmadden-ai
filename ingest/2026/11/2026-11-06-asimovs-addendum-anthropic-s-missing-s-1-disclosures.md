# Anthropic’s Missing S-1 Disclosures

## Insights

- Anthropic's S-1 omits key financial relationships with Amazon, Google, and Microsoft, who simultaneously act as cloud distributors (44% of revenue), investors ($30B+ combined), and compute suppliers (over $276B in committed spending) — with no breakdown of what Anthropic pays versus earns through each.
- Anthropic's headline $104B net loss for the first nine months of 2026 is largely a noncash accounting artifact from convertible notes revaluing as its valuation rises; the underlying business posted an operating profit in Q2 2026 before swinging back to an operating loss in Q3 due to short-term compute spot pricing and a stock-based charitable donation charge.
- Anthropic is shifting from purchasing cloud compute (expensed as used) to directly leasing equipment (including $161B in five-year Broadcom leases), which changes how costs appear on the books — the balance sheet shows only $4.5B in lease liabilities against $895B in total leases/purchase commitments, making future "profit" figures harder to interpret as capex commitment indicators.
- The filing provides no standardized token-level metrics — no reported token volumes, revenue/cost per token, or compute capacity utilization — despite usage-based billing making up 79% of revenue; the piece argues this mirrors oil-and-gas-style disclosure needs (volume, price, cost per unit) that the SEC could mandate industry-wide.
- Anthropic also doesn't break out compute allocation between model training versus customer serving, doesn't report revenue by product line (Claude Code, API, consumer), and discloses little about data-labeling/outside-labor costs beyond a single $757M year-over-year increase figure.
- Post-IPO governance concentrates power: founders retain 50.1% of shareholder votes, and a separate Long-Term Benefit Trust (no financial stake) selects four of seven board directors — a structure the author says strengthens the case for mandated transparency since ordinary shareholders have limited influence.

## Quote

> Anthropic has not given investors nearly enough information to assess its returns on the computing capacity behind that growth.
