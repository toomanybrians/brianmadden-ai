# You've never lost $19 billion, and you're still playing it safer than Zuckerberg. Pick the project you shelved and give it the bigger attempt it deserves.

## Insights

- Frames Meta's pivot from Reality Labs (reported $19.2B loss in 2025) to Muse, an AI agent that reached 10 million US/Canada setups in about six weeks per Zuckerberg's October 21 earnings call, as a case study in changing approach after failure rather than abandoning ambition.
- Notes Muse's small build team (fewer than 200 people, per Alexandr Wang) as evidence that AI-era products can be built by small, high-autonomy groups rather than large organizations.
- Argues that AI collapses the time/cost components (learning, waiting for help, searching for answers) that previously made multi-year skill or project estimates prohibitive, citing fast historical builds (iPod: ~10 months from approval to launch; Xerox Alto: prototype in months) as precedent for compressed timelines when deep experience meets decisive execution.
- Draws on Justin Skycak's "skill tree" concept — people abandon learning when they hit an unseen prerequisite gap rather than recognizing a specific missing piece they could backfill with AI tutoring.
- Claims AI lowers the barrier for niche, non-commercial projects (single-school resources, community histories, tools for small unmet needs) since individuals with domain knowledge no longer need an organization's buy-in to build them.
- Identifies embarrassment/fear of failure as the one cost AI hasn't reduced, distinguishing between genuine technical limitations (shrinking) and avoidance disguised as "I'm not technical" (unchanged); also warns that easy AI-assisted "starting" can become a way to avoid finishing.

## Quote

> With AI, starting is so easy that you can spend all your time starting.
