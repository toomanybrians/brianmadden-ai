# Some new data from Anthropic looking at the impact of AI on the sciences by fiel

## Insights

- Anthropic's analysis of 207,000 Claude/Claude Code research sessions finds AI could take on 27–52% of researcher time needed to produce a study, median 38% across fields.
- Claude use skews heavily toward formal/computational sciences (CS, math, statistics) and is proportionally low in lab-based physical sciences and medicine — usage tracks a field's "theoretical AI capability" score, not just researcher headcount.
- Within research tasks, data analysis and writing/communication are overrepresented relative to how researchers actually spend their time; data gathering and ideation/hypothesis generation are underused relative to time spent — even though a scientist survey rated ideation and data gathering as most important for producing impactful research.
- A specialized tool (Claude Science) shows ~2x more use on ideation tasks than general Claude, suggesting purpose-built AI tools can narrow the gap between what AI is used for and what matters most in research.
- Modeled scenarios: current AI capabilities, if widely adopted, could roughly double research output in some fields; full automation of all non-physical research work could raise output up to 15-fold in fields like CS/statistics, versus 3–4x in bench sciences limited by physical lab work.
- ~40% of surveyed AI-using researchers expect AI models to run full research projects autonomously by 2030; authors flag that fields most accelerated include AI research itself, creating a potential self-reinforcing feedback loop.

## Quote

> With current capabilities, if adopted widely, AI could take on 27–52% of the researcher time needed to produce a new study, with a median of 38% across fields
