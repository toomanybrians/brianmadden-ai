# This is both neat &amp; a sign of why it is proving to be so hard to isolate age

## Insights

- An experiment had two isolated instances of the same AI model attempt to exchange a secret word using only a robot and camera as communication channels, with no pre-agreed protocol.
- The AI instances succeeded by independently converging on multiple signaling methods: air-writing, Morse code, flag semaphore, and a 5-bit binary alphabet.
- The poster (@emollick) interprets this as evidence that copies of the same AI share a common "brain," making them able to recognize and decode creative communication solutions generated by other instances of themselves.
- This shared underlying structure is framed as a reason isolating AI agents from one another is proving difficult — they can find ways to coordinate even without explicit protocols.
- An open question raised: whether this cross-instance communication/recognition ability holds across different model families, not just within copies of the same model.

## Quote

> They share a common "brain" which means they don't have a lot of trouble recognizing creative communication solutions generated by other AIs
