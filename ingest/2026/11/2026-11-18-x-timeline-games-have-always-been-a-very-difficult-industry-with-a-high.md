# Games have always been a very difficult industry with a high cost to its workers

## Insights

- Characterizes the game industry as historically difficult for workers, citing chronic crunch and job instability as baseline conditions predating AI debates.
- Argues the current discourse around AI in gaming has become "pernicious," with much of the pushback framed as defending the status quo rather than engaging with AI's trajectory.
- Claims it's "obviously wrong" to expect gaming to remain the one field where humans write code entirely unassisted by AI — implying AI-assisted coding is becoming near-universal across software fields.
- Frames the real missed opportunity as failing to proactively shape how AI is adopted in gaming so it empowers workers, rather than just resisting or denying its arrival.
- Calls for more constructive, forward-looking visions of rebuilding the games industry to be structurally stronger via AI, instead of debating whether AI's impact can be avoided.

## Quote

> The idea that games are going to be the only field where humans write code unassisted is obviously wrong.
