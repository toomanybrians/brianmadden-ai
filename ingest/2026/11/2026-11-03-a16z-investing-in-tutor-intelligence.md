# Investing in Tutor Intelligence

## Insights

- Tutor Intelligence's deployment model inverts the typical robotics approach: rather than building a general model first and finding use cases later, it identifies a specific job, deploys hardware immediately using human teleoperators ("tutors"), and builds the model from real production data afterward.
- Human tutors remotely operate the robots in live customer environments and are skilled enough that customers reportedly can't distinguish their output from human employee work — this lets customers get value before full autonomy exists.
- Each deployment generates training data that feeds back into improving the fleet's model, creating a flywheel where "every robot put to work makes the next robot smarter, cheaper, and easier to deploy."
- Business model shifts robotics from capex to opex via hourly pricing, which the piece argues opens the market to SMB and mid-market manufacturers, not just large enterprises that could previously afford million-dollar upfront systems.
- Two robot products (Cassie, single-arm for case picking/palletizing; Sonny, dual-arm for higher-dexterity e-commerce fulfillment) share the same underlying model, reflecting vertical integration across brain, hardware, and data collection.
- Traction cited: deployment across major manufacturing/logistics networks, plans for 1,000+ robots under contract by end of 2027, ~1/3 of customers in the past 12 months were expansions from existing accounts, and implementation times as low as 8 hours.
- The author frames the core thesis as: in robotics, product experience and reliable task completion for customers matter more than model/data/hardware specifics — "the main thing is getting the robot to complete human tasks."

## Quote

> The main thing in robotics is getting the robot to complete human tasks – not the training data, not the specifics of the model, not the type of hardware used.
