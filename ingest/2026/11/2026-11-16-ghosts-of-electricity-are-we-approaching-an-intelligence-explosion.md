# Are we approaching an intelligence explosion?

## Insights

- Using Anthropic's internal METR data, the piece finds that while AI capability growth (AECI index) roughly doubled in pace after October 2025, R&D spending rose ~6.2x and technical headcount ~3.1x over the same comparison — meaning capability gained per dollar fell ~69% and per employee-year fell ~38%.
- The author distinguishes scaling laws (exponential input growth needed for exponential capability growth) from a true "intelligence explosion" (increasing returns, where progress gets easier as capability grows) — and argues current data shows the former, not the latter.
- Modeling research as a power-law production function (following Jones and Aghion-Jones-Jones), the estimated "returns to knowledge" parameter (ϕ) comes in below 1 in nearly all specifications (~0.84 central estimate) — consistent with "ideas getting harder to find," as in Bloom et al.'s findings for semiconductors, agriculture, and drug development.
- A statistical test suggests the October 2025 acceleration is best explained by a one-time jump in research effectiveness (estimated 1.4–2.3x), plausibly from coding agents, combined with higher spending — not a sustained change in the underlying diminishing-returns relationship.
- The more advanced model checks for recursive self-improvement (AI supplying its own research input) and finds the relevant feedback parameter (ψ) near zero (1-3%), well below the ~26-46% threshold needed to produce a self-sustaining explosion — i.e., no evidence yet that AI is doing its own research at a meaningful scale.
- The author frames diminishing research productivity as potentially a normal equilibrium rather than irrational spending, citing an organizational-economics model (Garicano & Rossi-Hansberg) where new technology requires organizations to build specialized expertise with diminishing marginal returns, while flagging that revenue/value per capability point may still be rising sharply (e.g., Claude economic value estimates) even as raw capability gains per dollar fall.

## Quote

> Progress does not stop in this case, but slows down to the pace that these constraints allow. Only an intelligence explosion escapes this arithmetic.
