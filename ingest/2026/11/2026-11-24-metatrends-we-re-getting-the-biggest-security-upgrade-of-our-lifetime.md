# We're Getting the Biggest Security Upgrade of Our Lifetime

## Insights

- Diamandis predicts AI will be able to patch almost every known software vulnerability within roughly two years, driven by a shift in engineering economics: companies can now have AI do a first-pass review of decades-old legacy code (much of it in C/C++) rather than pulling engineers off product work to do it manually.
- He simultaneously predicts a possible "cyber apocalypse," arguing the same AI capability that finds dangerous flaws can be used offensively; which outcome dominates depends on who (defenders vs. attackers) gets access and acts first.
- Argues frontier AI labs currently hold a 4-5 month capability lead over openly available models, and that this gap can be used defensively by giving trusted security/maintainer teams early access to find and fix flaws before equivalent tools are widely available.
- Cites a real example: a frontier model (via OpenAI) and an independent research team found a critical PHP flaw; the PHP Foundation maintaining this globally-depended-on language had only one full-time staff member and 14 mostly part-time contractors — illustrating a mismatch between software dependency and maintenance capacity.
- Notes a structural lag even after fixes exist: Verizon data shows median time for companies to fully remediate actively-exploited known vulnerabilities rose from 32 to 43 days year-over-year, meaning better AI detection doesn't guarantee faster organizational patching without authority, budget, and time allocated by leadership.
- Frames recovery/continuity planning (e.g., who can authorize manual workarounds when a system goes down) as equally important as prevention, citing that ~40% of hospitals in an AHA survey reported patient care delays after the 2024 Change Healthcare attack.
