# I worry that we will retreat in the face of AI ability gains, rather than pushin

## Insights

- Raises concern that as AI capabilities improve, organizations may passively cede high-value tasks to AI rather than actively redesigning workflows and UX to keep humans in meaningful roles.
- Argues this requires deliberate effort: rethinking tasks and interfaces while AI remains "jagged" (uneven across capabilities) rather than assuming automation will naturally preserve good human work.
- Uses a concrete worry case: a future where the highest-value remaining human job tasks are menial (e.g., greeting customers, tidying carts) because AI has absorbed the more substantive work.
- Frames this as a design/policy choice, not an inevitability — the retreat into diminished human roles is a risk to actively resist, not a foregone conclusion.
- Response is prompted by a referenced list of "top 20 tasks with highest bottleneck pressure" (source not detailed in this item).
