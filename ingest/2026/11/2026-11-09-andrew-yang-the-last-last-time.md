# The Last Last Time

## Insights

- Draws a direct parallel between the 2008 financial crash and the current AI market: both involved "illusory" growth built on bad underlying economics that eventually came due.
- Argues most current AI infrastructure spending (cited as ~$3 trillion buildout) won't be repaid, because demand for frontier-model compute doesn't match the revenue needed to justify it — cheaper open-weight models already handle an estimated 85% of use cases at a fraction of the cost.
- Points to DeepSeek's January 2025 announcement (comparable results to ChatGPT at far lower cost) as an early signal the infrastructure buildout was overbuilt — a warning the market didn't fully absorb, triggering a $590B tech selloff and bans of DeepSeek from some U.S. agencies over privacy concerns.
- Claims Anthropic and OpenAI's growth rates are already decelerating, undercutting the valuations built on continued exponential growth.
- States plainly that "most work is lower-order work" — implying the bulk of knowledge work is replaceable by cheap, commoditized AI rather than requiring premium frontier models.
- Predicts AI will "shred millions of jobs" while simultaneously describing it as a genuinely transformative technology — holding both the hype-bubble critique and the real-disruption claim at once.
- Notes a historical pattern: counter-cyclical consumer businesses (cost-saving, practical services) tend to do well in downturns, based on his own experience in both the 2000 dot-com bust and 2008 crash.

## Quote

> AI is in the process of changing the way we think, work and love – for better or worse – and will shred millions of jobs.
