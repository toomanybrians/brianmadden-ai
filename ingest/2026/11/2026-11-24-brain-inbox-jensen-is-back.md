# Jensen is back

## Insights

- An Anthropic mathematician published a Claude-assisted proof of the Riemann hypothesis, a problem considered among math's hardest; proof-checking organizations (Lean FRO, Mathlib Initiative) confirmed its formal correctness after days of verification.
- The achievement is notable because leading mathematicians (e.g., MIT's Andrew Sutherland) had previously treated this problem as a benchmark AI likely wouldn't reach in their lifetimes — a marker of genuine frontier-pushing capability, not just pattern-matching.
- No single human currently understands the full proof, even though it has been independently verified as sound by multiple mathematicians examining the code and formal statement — raising questions about human comprehension keeping pace with AI-generated results.
- Meta's chief AI officer framed the proof as evidence of a "phase change" in AI capability, arguing nearly every field could now be accelerated "beyond what humans can understand or monitor," and called for urgent AI safety work ahead of more capable systems.
- Separately, commentary on Nvidia's governance crisis (Jensen Huang's reinstatement as CEO) notes the company's org chart was "a mirror" of total centralization around one leader, with no board chair, independence, or succession plan — framed as a cautionary case of founder/key-man risk at a company central to AI infrastructure.
