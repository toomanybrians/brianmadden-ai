# Pluralistic: Formalities (31 Oct 2026)

## Insights

- The US Copyright Office holds that copyright protects only human creative authorship, not "sweat of the brow" labor — a position Doctorow argues is legally correct and now cuts against AI-generated content.
- AI outputs themselves are not copyrightable (following the same logic as the monkey-selfie case), but creative prompts, human edits to AI output, and curated selection from AI outputs can each carry narrow, separately defensible copyright claims.
- This creates a documentation problem: companies using AI in hybrid workflows must track precisely which elements (words, pixels, sounds) were AI-generated versus human-created/edited, or risk losing enforceable rights over their products.
- Doctorow predicts this will effectively recreate copyright "formalities" (deposit/registration/record-keeping) — abolished in US law since 1976 — but now enforced informally by corporate legal/administrative practice rather than government mandate.
- The practical incentive this creates: firms seeking full, defensible copyright protection may increasingly favor all-human production pipelines with documented "no-AI" policies and monitoring, rather than AI-assisted workflows with ambiguous IP status.
- Businesses using AI will need new internal administrative/record-keeping systems to preserve commercial rights — effectively a compliance and labor-tracking burden introduced by AI adoption.

## Quote

> AI slop isn't copyrightable, but there can be copyrightable elements in an AI-generated image.
