# Is Private Equity Cooked?

## Insights

- Venture and buyout performance diverge sharply across technology cycles: top-quartile VC has outperformed buyout during the internet build-out and the mobile/cloud wave, but trailed during the dot-com bust aftermath (1998–2005); the author argues we're at the start of another major tech wave now.
- Private equity's 2010s "golden era" was driven largely by falling interest rates and rising valuation multiples (Bain: >50% of buyout returns), not operational improvement — a dynamic the piece argues can't be recreated by modest rate cuts.
- SaaS businesses followed a venture-to-buyout pipeline: VC funded them unlevered while the model was unproven in the mid-2000s, then PE bought them once mature and predictable enough to leverage in the 2010s — but PE now needs 10–12% EBITDA growth to hit returns once requiring only ~5%, a bar portfolio companies (e.g., Golub Capital's software borrowers, down to <3% revenue growth) are struggling to clear.
- 2021–2022-vintage buyout deals are described as the stress point: loan-to-value for some has jumped from 44% to 75% in a year, 70% of 2025–26 foreclosed debt originated in 2021–2022 deals, and PE backed 54% of major 2025 US corporate bankruptcies.
- The piece frames capital currently chasing new technology rather than legacy buyout assets — citing Nvidia's acquisition of Hugging Face ($13B) and SpaceX's acquisition of Cursor ($60B) — and notes Anthropic's $65B funding round exceeded the largest buyout on record (EA take-private, $56.6B), as evidence capital is rotating toward the AI wave.
- Valuation opacity is flagged as a structural issue: PE marks its own holdings privately (and can sustain inflated marks via continuation funds and NAV-based loans), while venture marks are set by new-round pricing from outside investors, making venture markdowns more immediate and PE distress less visible until debt matures (~$140B in 2028, ~$160B in 2029).
