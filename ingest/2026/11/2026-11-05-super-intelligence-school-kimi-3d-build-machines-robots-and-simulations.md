# Kimi 3D | Build Machines, Robots and Simulations

## Insights

- Kimi's "3D Make" feature generates three-dimensional models and simulations from a text description, optionally paired with a reference image.
- The tool reportedly supports exploring internal structure, adjusting individual components, and experimenting with movement/mechanics of generated models, not just producing a static visual.
- Depending on the creation type, outputs can include interactive experiences and editable models, implying some generated projects carry underlying code or parameters users can manipulate.
- The piece frames the tool's value as replacing specialized modeling software and technical drawings with a natural-language starting point for mechanical/robotic design exploration.
- Three example use cases are highlighted: a four-stroke V8 engine, a robotic jellyfish, and a six-legged walking robot, spanning mechanical engineering, robotics, 3D modeling, and programming.
- The stated goal is investigative/educational — understanding why components move as they do and experimenting with underlying operating principles, not just producing an impressive visual.

## Quote

> What once required specialized modelling software, detailed technical drawings and considerable time to develop can now begin with a description.
