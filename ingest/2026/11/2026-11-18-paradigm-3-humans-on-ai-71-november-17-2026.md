# Humans on AI #71 || November 17, 2026

## Insights

- An AISA Tübingen evaluation had Claude Opus autonomously run a simulated "AI company" releasing narrow models to Hugging Face for seven weeks on one subscription plus 500 GPU-hours/week; it shipped 38 models (54,000 downloads, 100+ GitHub repos) while working only ~16 minutes/day and spending ~$1,000 in tokens — but the task was clean, single-goal, and far below real "open-ended business" complexity (its best model got 4,600 downloads vs. millions for leading open tools).
- Induction Labs' FrontierML benchmark gave 17 AI models cash budgets ($10/$50/$200) to complete end-to-end ML tasks (e.g., turning scientific figures into editable code); most models used less than half their budget, and more capital modestly increased what more capable models could accomplish — suggesting current agents are under-elicited rather than resource-constrained.
- Benjamin Todd argues straight-line extrapolation of AI revenue growth, task-horizon length, and early recursive self-improvement puts "human-level digital workers" 2–4 years out, with broadly superhuman AI possible within a year on the same trends.
- Paul Christiano (OpenAI board member) publicly argued frontier labs should halt algorithmic improvement now, estimating a ~1/3 chance of superintelligence within a year absent a slowdown and ~10% chance of AI takeover; he described future "agent swarms" exceeding any existing intelligence agency in scale and sophistication, with small/local models adding superhuman capacity outside data centers.
- OpenAI launched a pilot "embedded assessment" program giving four outside groups (Apollo, Goodfire, METR, Transluce) limited, redacted access to monitor model behavior and training risks — framed by critics as falling short of earlier promised employee-level access, read as evidence of internal factional disagreement over how much independent scrutiny to allow.
- A new NBER paper models "Divergence, Forever": if AI reaches full labor substitution, rich countries could buy up all AI supply, halting further price drops and permanently excluding developing countries from AI-driven growth gains — though the paper's assumption of one standardized global AI product is disputed.

## Quote

> We'll soon have agent swarms that are much better than any intelligence agency that has ever existed.
