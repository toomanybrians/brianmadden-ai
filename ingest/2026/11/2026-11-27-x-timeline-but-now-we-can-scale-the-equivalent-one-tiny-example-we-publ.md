# But now we can scale the equivalent! One tiny example, we published our open sou

## Insights

- Wharton Generative AI Labs released Primer, an open-source system that uses AI agents to let educators build adaptive educational simulations without coding, addressing a historically expensive and labor-intensive process.
- The core design principle: the educator remains the source of pedagogical judgment. A structured interview elicits the lesson, student context, and assessment criteria, producing a design document the educator must approve before anything is built.
- The system uses a pipeline of specialized agents (Intake, Design, Casting Director, Image Generation, Experience Builder) for authoring, and separate runtime agents (mentor, practice, feedback) that guide students through preparation, roleplay/teachback practice, and coached feedback.
- Explicit rationale given: generative models are "capable but not pedagogically reliable on their own" — left unstructured they help too quickly, drift from objectives, or produce unstructured roleplay — so learning-science principles (scaffolding, retrieval, feedback) are hard-coded into agent instructions rather than left to model defaults.
- Everything (prompts, templates, orchestration code) is released open source/LLM-agnostic, explicitly to allow deployment under varied privacy/governance constraints and to let others inspect, modify, or extend the pedagogy and architecture.
- Framed by the author as an example of a broader shift: AI now lets you "scale" what previously required direct, unscalable human expert attention (e.g., one-on-one tutoring/financial literacy work).
- Explicitly flagged as an experimental prototype, not vetted for classroom use or reviewed for compliance with student-privacy regulations (FERPA, COPPA, etc.).
