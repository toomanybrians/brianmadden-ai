# Jensen Huang and the end of the ‘lol nothing matters’ era of American politics

## Insights

- Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia CEO following reports of multiple extramarital affairs, one with a subordinate and one involving a suspected Chinese spy; CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO with no prepared successor in place.
- The piece frames this as exposing Silicon Valley's "cult of the founder" — a VC-driven ideology (explicit at firms like Founders Fund and Andreessen Horowitz) that founders shouldn't be subject to normal governance, which enabled long-term bets but also let founders accumulate outsized, unchecked power.
- Huang is presented as a case study in founder-driven dismissal of AI risk: he reportedly called loss-of-control concerns nonexistent ("I know how it works, so there's nothing there"), likened AI to a microwave, and argued safety is purely an engineering/testing problem solvable by moving faster.
- Huang treated chip export controls to China as a matter of patriotic necessity rather than a genuine policy tradeoff, reportedly fearing Nvidia's GPUs would be commoditized and resisting any framing that acknowledged competing national-security risks.
- Despite running a company valued in the hundreds of billions with 60+ direct reports, Huang had no succession plan and reportedly intended to lead Nvidia until age 120 — described as a textbook case of "key-man risk."
- The scandal is also tied to a chip-smuggling investigation (~240,000 GPUs allegedly smuggled into China) implicating a federal agency, with reports that Trump was briefed and pressured Huang to stay on despite the national-security exposure.

> he thought only he could run the company he founded, and now the rest of us have to live with the consequences.
