# I have a new concept I call The Bitter Wall.

- The Bitter Lesson tells us to st

## Insights

- Coins "The Bitter Wall": a counterpart/limit to the Bitter Lesson, which holds that scaling compute/intelligence beats hand-coded human instructions.
- Argues intelligence is not the bottleneck for AI progress much longer — the bottleneck is whether the user can articulate what they actually want.
- Uses an "alien architect" analogy: a vastly superhuman builder is useless if the person requesting the house can't specify beyond "a nice house."
- Claims future gains in AI output quality will come primarily from better articulation/specification of intent, not from smarter models.
- Frames this as the gap behind "models are the how, harnesses are the what" — harnesses matter because they supply context models can't infer on their own.
- Connects this idea to a broader shift he's described elsewhere: from "prompt engineering" to "intent engineering."

## Quote

> It doesn't matter how smart a person or an AI is if it doesn't know what you want.
