# The Anthropic Feedback Loop: Cost of Capital Diversions and Delusions...

## Insights

- Damodaran's Anthropic valuation ($1.45T equity) hinges most heavily on a projected $1 trillion in revenue within ten years — a scale comparable to Amazon or Walmart — built on the premise that Anthropic becomes the dominant, pricing-powerful player in the "premium" AI tier.
- He frames AI competitive moats not as traditional patents/brand/cost advantages but as "stickiness" — Claude agents combined with client/customer/employee data creating switching costs even against technically superior or cheaper alternatives.
- Anthropic's governance (public benefit corporation + founder-controlling voting shares) is flagged as a red flag: concentrated founder control in an "echo chamber" may impede the fast, outsider-informed pivots needed when AI business economics shift unexpectedly.
- Continuing compute investment is treated as a major ongoing requirement, not a one-time cost — sustaining trillion-dollar revenue requires continually replenishing and expanding compute even as data-center buildout faces rising local/political pushback.
- Regulatory risk is identified as the input Damodaran is least confident about, since it's driven by politics rather than economics — a single election could shift from modest profit-margin costs to major revenue/profit impacts.
- Broader argument: inflated cost-of-capital/hurdle-rate assumptions (common among VCs, PE, CFOs) are largely fictional relative to realized returns, and using unrealistically high rates to judge AI valuations (like Anthropic's) is a distraction from the real driver — revenue/growth assumptions.

## Quote

> If I were forced to pick one variable around which my valuation revolves the most, it is my estimate of the target revenues for Anthropic.
