# Wisdom from Steve Jurvetson (VC & SpaceX Board Member)

## Insights

- Jurvetson's investing heuristic—"things unlike anything I've seen before, yet adjacent to where I've been"—is framed as a deliberate alternative to pattern-matching; the piece contrasts this with most of Silicon Valley currently doing "AI for X" pattern recognition that allegedly can't find step-function companies.
- A named growth-rate comparison: AI productivity is claimed to be compounding at ~4x/year for 13 years versus Moore's Law's ~2x/year, yielding a claimed 67-million-fold gain vs. 8,000-fold — framed as the source of "perpetual future shock."
- The "bottleneck internalization" thesis (from Salim Ismail, affirmed by Jurvetson): when an external dependency becomes strategically limiting, an exponential organization should vertically integrate it — examples given are Tesla's Gigafactory (batteries) and SpaceX's engine/PCB manufacturing, with electricity, chips, and compute named as today's limiting bottlenecks.
- Mission-driven business models are argued to resolve the "short-term vs. long-term" conflict inside companies — a profit-maximization goal allegedly creates internal fights over time horizon, while mission focus aligns everyone; quote: "you can't suck and lead."
- A recurring "dead company" test is offered for evaluating legacy/incumbent strategies: ask whether a product, if it succeeds at all its objectives, would still be a failure (applied here to legacy space-launch programs with structurally high cost-per-kilogram).
- Several participants float the idea of giving AI systems board observer seats or using them to aggregate constituent input, floated as a step toward both better governance and a "purer form of representative democracy."

## Quote

> "I try to invest in things that are unlike anything I've seen before, yet adjacent to where I've been."
