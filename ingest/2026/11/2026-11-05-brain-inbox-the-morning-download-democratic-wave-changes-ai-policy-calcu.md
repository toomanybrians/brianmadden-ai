# The Morning Download: Democratic Wave Changes AI Policy Calculus

## Insights

- Democratic gains in House, Senate, and several governorships (Texas, Ohio, Georgia, South Carolina) introduce new political uncertainty into AI infrastructure build-out, after a prior assumption that Congress would stay gridlocked on tech policy.
- Analyst Dan Ives (Yorkville Ives) predicts the midterm results will slow the planned 800–1,000 U.S. data center buildout, with Texas especially exposed since it's expected to supply 25-30% of national data center gigawatt capacity; new Texas governor Gina Hinojosa has called for an immediate construction moratorium.
- Incoming Democratic governors in Ohio (Amy Acton) and Georgia (Keisha Lance Bottoms) have also campaigned on data center moratoriums or conditions, potentially putting roughly half of the nation's proposed data centers under stricter oversight.
- Slower AI infrastructure expansion could keep enterprise AI compute prices elevated for longer, potentially slowing AI diffusion across the economy and giving competitors like China a relative advantage in the global AI race.
- Democrats are expected to increase scrutiny of AI itself — summoning executives to testify about ties to the White House — citing recent AI-model hacks and researcher warnings about longer-term catastrophic risk.
- Separately noted: Google will now disable AI tools (Search, Gemini) by default on K-12 accounts following findings that classroom AI use led to student emotional/academic dependency; U.K. cybersecurity body warns state actors (Russia, China) will routinely use AI in cyberattacks, with an AI-powered ransomware attack on critical infrastructure expected by 2028.
