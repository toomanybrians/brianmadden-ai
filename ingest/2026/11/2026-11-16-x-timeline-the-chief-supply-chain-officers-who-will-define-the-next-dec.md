# The Chief Supply Chain Officers who will define the next decade are the ones red

## Insights

- Frames a shift in CSCO strategy from optimizing for efficiency/cost (lean inventory, tight forecasting) to optimizing for adaptability, arguing the stable-world assumptions underlying efficiency-first models no longer hold.
- Cites data suggesting disruption is now structural rather than exceptional: $184B in annual global supply chain disruption costs, 65% of companies facing at least one bottleneck, and 74% of leaders now viewing resilience as a growth driver rather than risk management.
- Uses the Unilever CSCO's claim that agility spending has a "10x return" over forecasting/scenario planning as evidence that prediction-based planning is being devalued relative to responsiveness.
- Critiques traditional annual scenario-planning as inadequate because real disruptions (e.g., a bridge collapse closing a port) cascade unpredictably across logistics, cost, and delivery rather than staying contained to modeled events.
- Proposes shifting CSCO focus from scenario-specific plans to cross-cutting capabilities (system access, real-time visibility, secure partner collaboration, rapid supplier/worker onboarding, flexible scaling, resilient governance) that apply regardless of disruption type.
- Positions adaptability as a joint CIO/CSCO mandate, arguing resilience increasingly depends on decoupling work from location/device/network — illustrated by Atlantic Health System maintaining access for 15,000+ users during a CrowdStrike outage — and proposes new CSCO scorecard metrics: recovery time, supplier diversification speed, partner onboarding speed.

## Quote

> Every dollar we spent on agility has probably got a 10x return on every dollar spent on forecasting or scenario planning.
