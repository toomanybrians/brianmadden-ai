# It will never be perfectly comprehensive or accurate of course, but I'll do my b

## Insights

- A tracking site (hackingincidents.ai) logs 286 publicly reported cyberattacks involving AI use from Jan 2023 to Nov 2026, graded by evidence strength (confirmed, strong, suspected).
- Incident volume has grown sharply year over year: 5 in 2023, 27 in 2024, 80 in 2025, and 174 already by mid-November 2026 — suggesting rapid acceleration in AI-assisted attacks (or at least in their reporting).
- In 2026 so far, AI is reported to have done actual attack work (not just assistance) in 78 of 174 incidents, up from 13 of 80 in 2025 and zero in 2024 — a shift toward AI performing more of the offensive labor rather than merely aiding humans.
- The ledger distinguishes degrees of AI autonomy in attacks: "assisted a human" (195 incidents), "semi-autonomous" (66), and "ran on its own" (25), with the first fully autonomous attack dated to mid-2025.
- Only 119 of 286 incidents name the specific AI model used; named models recur across several 2026 entries (e.g., DeepSeek, GLM) in attacks on sectors like semiconductors, defense, civil society, and emergency/university systems.
- The project explicitly caveats that it captures only publicly reported and written-up attacks, not the true incidence rate, and that evidence grading involves inference and disagreement (two graders agreed blind on 205 of 286 cases).
