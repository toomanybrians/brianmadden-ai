# We should spend more effort thinking about how to preserve craft and augment it,

## Insights

- Argues that automation is "seductive but not an inevitable path" — framing it as a choice rather than a predetermined trajectory for how intellectual work evolves.
- Advocates for actively preserving and augmenting craft in knowledge work, as an alternative to defaulting toward automation.
- Uses the powerloom analogy: recognizing a transformative technology doesn't require either romanticizing the old craft or rejecting the new tool outright.
- Claims many intellectual tasks previously considered "pure craft" — and thus assumed immune to industrialization — may now become subject to industrial-style logic (standardization, scale, mechanization).
- Characterizes this shift as significant and difficult ("not an easy change"), not a neutral or purely positive development.

## Quote

> You don't have to love powerlooms or hate hand weaving to recognize a powerloom when you see one.
