# The Morning Download: Nvidia CEO Ouster Reverberates Globally

## Insights

- Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's resignation is tied to allegations that the company built elaborate workarounds — via Southeast Asian data centers and partners like Megaspeed — to route high-end chips to Chinese customers despite U.S. export controls, with one estimate citing nearly $12 billion in shipments between March 2024 and June 2025.
- Analysts dispute how much of Nvidia's revenue actually depends on China: official figures put it at ~7% of total revenue, but short-seller Culper Research estimates over 20% when workaround channels are included.
- Huang had already begun pulling back Nvidia's role as an "open checkbook" for AI startups before the resignation, telling cloud partners last month they'd need upfront payments for GPU access; UBS estimates a deeper retreat under interim CEO Colette Kress could remove $50 billion or more from the startup funding market.
- Pending Nvidia-backed deals now in question include a licensing/hiring arrangement with Reflection AI and a planned investment valuing Thinking Machines Lab at $40 billion — both startups described as burning heavy capital on compute costs.
- Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has called for an outsider successor and questioned whether Nvidia knowingly violated export rules, while a "Stand With Jensen" petition has passed 175,000 signatures (though some signers say they never consented) and Huang has reportedly discussed a possible return.
- The U.S. government is simultaneously naming Nvidia and two dozen other firms as AI "national champions" for faster export approvals and overseas backing, even as the export-control controversy unfolds — alongside notes that Alibaba released a new non-open-weight flagship model (Qwen4-Max) claiming to beat Anthropic and Moonshot on coding benchmarks, and Anthropic is pricing an IPO expected to value it at $1.33 trillion or more.
