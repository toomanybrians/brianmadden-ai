# Of Miracles and Moats

## Insights

- Argues that a "miracle technology" (AI) does not guarantee good businesses for its producers: huge genuine usefulness attracts massive capital and competition, which tends to crash prices and destroy producer margins even as consumers and the broader economy benefit enormously — citing airlines, oil, railroads, and fiber/dot-com as prior cycles.
- Lays out a repeatable boom-bust pattern (miracle tech arrives → early scarcity profits → capital and competitors flood in → overbuilding → price collapse → consolidation by a low-cost survivor → demand eventually catches up and exceeds expectations) and argues AI's current "it's not a bubble" arguments are exactly the early steps of this pattern, not evidence against it.
- Notes that AI labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Meta) are making enormous forward compute commitments (Anthropic ~$900.9B through 2045) based on logic that underinvesting is riskier than overinvesting — echoing historical "self-defence" overbuild logic seen in British railway mania and the dot-com fiber buildout.
- Cites Nordhaus's research showing producers historically capture only ~2.2% of the social surplus from innovation while consumers capture ~97-98%, suggesting AI's economic value will mostly flow to users/society rather than to the AI companies themselves.
- Identifies "durable competitive advantage" (moats) — not technological quality itself — as the real determinant of which companies profit from a miracle technology; cites Rockefeller, Ford, and Vanderbilt as examples of winners who built scale/cost advantages rather than just correctly predicting demand.
- Points out that AI may itself accelerate this dynamic by making imitation easier (e.g., Anthropic using Claude models to erode NVIDIA's CUDA moat), intensifying competitive pressure across AI and other tech-driven industries (robotics, power, manufacturing, biotech).
- Suggests regulated/"cornered resource" categories (pre-1978 airline routes, Bell Telephone patents, Alcoa's process) are the rare exceptions that escape this commoditization cycle — AI itself has no such protection since capital can be thrown at the problem freely.

## Quote

> The more miraculous your technology, the more crucial your moats.
