# 🔮 Certified human, twin primes, Anthropic++ #611

## Insights

- Grand Theft Auto VI was built entirely without generative AI (5,000 staff, 30,000 hand-made characters) and made $3.79B in first-day sales, framed as evidence that "proof of humanity" carries market value.
- Research cited shows people rate identical AI-generated paintings more favorably when labeled human-made, suggesting authenticity labeling itself has economic/psychological weight.
- The piece revisits the William Morris/Arts and Crafts parallel: handcraft resistance to mechanization only became viable as a luxury good for the wealthy, not a mass-market alternative — raising the question of whether digital "certified human" content can scale affordably where physical crafts couldn't.
- Anthropic had a major financial week ("Wall Street got a piece of Anthropic"); separate analysis frames possible future business models (device-level licensing vs. becoming an "AI utility") and notes an internal culture described as having quasi-religious conviction about needing to "race fast" to build safely.
- Two competing Riemann hypothesis proofs emerged same-day: one by an Anthropic mathematician working with Claude (17.4M lines of Lean formal proof code), another entirely human (Stanford's Ravi Vakil/Alessandro Masullo) — notably the human team is now also formalizing their proof in Lean for faster verification, and no Lean-verified AI proof has reportedly ever been retracted.
- US power grid has 2,000+ gigawatts of new generation (mostly solar/wind/storage) queued for connection, exceeding current total capacity; falling solar costs (24% per capacity doubling) combined with slow grid interconnection is reportedly pushing AI data centers toward on-site solar+battery power rather than waiting for grid connection.
