# Banks Without Front Doors: AI Agents and the Future of Banking 🏦

## Insights

- In October 2026, major North American banks (JPMorgan first on Oct 13, then Citi, Bank of America, Morgan Stanley, Schwab, Canada's big four) moved to block AI agents from initiating transactions in customer accounts, officially citing security.
- The financial stakes behind the lockout: JPMorgan, Bank of America, and Wells Fargo hold $1.6 trillion in deposits paying no interest; paying even 3% on that would cost ~$47 billion/year, nearly half their combined profits — framed by Apollo's chief economist as risk of an "agentic bank run."
- Wells Fargo took a different approach (effective Nov 24): account holders are held liable for whatever their AI agent does — liability assigned to the user rather than blocking agent access outright.
- Fifth Third Bank illustrates a split strategy: it publicly bars AI agents from initiating transactions in its own customer-facing accounts, while simultaneously serving as the regulated banking partner behind Stripe Treasury, which lets businesses have AI (ChatGPT/Claude) pay invoices and move money.
- The piece frames this as evidence that the "front end" of banking (login, screen, cross-sell, customer inertia — where a lot of bank margin lives) is becoming separable from the "back end" (charter, deposit infrastructure), with distribution control determining who captures the margin.
- Article promises deeper material (not detailed in the excerpt): a liability map for who pays when an AI agent errs (US/EU, Regulation E implications), founder playbooks, operator/investor checklists, and dated predictions on how this plays out.
