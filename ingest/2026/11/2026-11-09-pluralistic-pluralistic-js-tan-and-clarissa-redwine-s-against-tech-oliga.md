# Pluralistic: JS Tan and Clarissa Redwine's "Against Tech Oligarchy" (07 Nov 2026)

## Insights

- Tech workers historically held outsized leverage not through unionization but through scarcity and individual value (each engineer estimated to add ~$1m to company bottom lines), which bosses offset with "peer"/family-style management culture rather than real power-sharing.
- As the tech labor market saturated with workers, employer behavior shifted sharply toward union-busting and retaliation, with companies like Kickstarter and later Google/Microsoft facing organized worker pushback.
- The book argues traditional union organizing (quiet "bonfire" style one-on-one recruiting toward a certification vote) fails in tech workplaces due to high turnover, remote/isolated work, and employees' self-conception as workers rather than entrepreneurs-in-waiting.
- Tech strikes don't have the same disruptive power as industrial strikes because automated systems keep running without workers present — a dynamic cited as weakening traditional strike leverage (illustrated by Musk's Twitter layoffs).
- Tactics described as effective in this environment are public, confrontational actions (occupations, livestreamed disruptions, protest flotillas) rather than withheld labor — exemplified by Microsoft worker campaigns against company contracts tied to Gaza surveillance.
- Employers have developed "constructive dismissal" workarounds (e.g., Mapbox's return-to-office mandates targeting union supporters) to push out organizers without severance liability.
