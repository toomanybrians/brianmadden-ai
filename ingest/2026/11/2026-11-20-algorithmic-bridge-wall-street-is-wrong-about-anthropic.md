# Wall Street Is Wrong About Anthropic

## Insights

- Anthropic's own employees reportedly view the IPO ($1.5T valuation, $83/share) with indifference — described as "parochial" and met with a "shrug" in NYT reporting — because they're focused on AI outcomes, not wealth.
- The piece frames Anthropic's internal worldview as binary: AI either ends catastrophically (extinction-level risk) or leads to "unimaginable abundance" where current economic structures, including money and work, cease to matter — no stable middle scenario where equity value matters long-term.
- Cites Dario Amodei's "Machines of Loving Grace" claim that in a positive outcome, "our current economic setup will no longer make sense" — implying work-for-pay and share value become moot.
- Notes aggressive internal timelines: Jack Clark reportedly moved his prediction for full recursive AI self-improvement from 2028 to 2027; an internal Anthropic survey predicted AI would do 80% of frontier model development work by end of 2027; AI Futures Project gives 35–65% odds of AI automating all white-collar work before 2028.
- Argues retail/Wall Street investors are implicitly betting that Anthropic is wrong about its own stated timelines and stakes — treating AI as a "normal technology" for return purposes while the company's own staff explicitly reject that framing.
- Suggests a structural paradox: Anthropic's business and valuation only make sense if the market doesn't believe the scenarios the company itself says are most likely.

> Anthropic plays a mundane game today but aims at the divine.
