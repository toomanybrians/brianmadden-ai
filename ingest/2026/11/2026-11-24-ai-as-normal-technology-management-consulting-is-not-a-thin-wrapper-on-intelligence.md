# Management consulting is not a thin wrapper on intelligence

## Insights

- The piece breaks consulting's value into five components — analysis, judgment, coordination, credibility, and accountability — arguing AI has only made inroads on the first, leaving four largely intact.
- Strategic judgment is framed as a "credence good": outcomes are ambiguous and slow to resolve, making it hard to train AI on quality signals that even the consulting firms themselves struggle to assess.
- Consulting firms' advantage in judgment comes from cross-client pattern recognition (e.g., McKinsey's ~2,500 M&A deals in five years) — a volume of comparative experience no single client or AI model has access to.
- Historical precedent (mainframes, PCs, the internet, cloud) shows general-purpose technologies have repeatedly created *new* consulting demand rather than simply automating away existing work — the authors argue AI is following the same pattern, citing stable/growing strategy-consulting spend through 2023–2026.
- As AI commoditizes analysis, competitive advantage is argued to shift toward implementation and organizational change — McKinsey reportedly now derives less than 20% of its work from pure strategy.
- AI adoption itself is framed as creating new consulting demand (organizational redesign, performance-metric overhauls, AI-driven cybersecurity threats), with firms like Accenture and BCG already reporting large growth in AI-related service revenue.
- Looking ahead, the authors argue consulting firms must build proprietary AI deployment expertise, partner with (while also competing against) AI companies' own implementation arms, and preserve neutrality across model providers to avoid conflicts of interest tied to token-based pricing.
