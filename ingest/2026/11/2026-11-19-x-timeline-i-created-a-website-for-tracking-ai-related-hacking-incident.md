# I created a website for tracking AI-related hacking incidents. 

https://t.co/yL

## Insights

- The site (hackingincidents.ai) catalogs 286 publicly reported cyberattacks involving AI use since January 2023, built from model-provider disclosures, security firm reports, government statements, and press coverage.
- Each incident is graded by evidence strength: "Confirmed" (direct evidence like provider telemetry or recovered prompts), "Strong" (credible inference from artifacts like AI-written code), or "Suspected" (thin or secondhand evidence) — 145 confirmed, 109 strong, 32 suspected.
- Incident volume has grown sharply: 5 in 2023, 27 in 2024, 80 in 2025, and already 174 through mid-November 2026, suggesting either rising actual use or rising detection/reporting.
- Of 2026's incidents, 78 of 174 involved AI doing actual attack work (not just incidental use), categorized by autonomy level: assisted (195 total), semi-autonomous (66), or fully autonomous (25) — with the first fully autonomous AI-run attack dated June 2025 and first solo-run attack July 2025.
- Only 119 of 286 incidents name the specific model used; 56 have two or more independent publishers confirming them, while 199 rest on a single source and 31 are known only through a secondary incident database — the site explicitly frames this as a record of what got reported, not necessarily what happened.
- A companion site, offsecharnesses.ai, tracks the offensive AI "harnesses" (tooling/frameworks) attackers use.

## Quote

> These are counts of published reports, so they show what got noticed and written up, which is not the same as what happened.
