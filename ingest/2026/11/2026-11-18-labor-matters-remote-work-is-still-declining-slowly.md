# Remote Work Is Still Declining, Slowly

## Insights

- Remote work (defined as mainly working from home) continues to decline but at a decelerating rate: 17.9% (2021 peak) → 13.3% (2024) → 12.9% (2025), with each year's drop smaller than the last.
- About a third of the 2025 national decline is attributable to federal workers alone, despite them being only 3.5% of the workforce — driven by a January 2025 return-to-office order that cut federal remote work from 12.5% to 8.5% in one year.
- Excluding federal workers, the underlying decline is slowing (0.3 points in 2025 vs 0.5 in 2024), suggesting private-sector pullback is also decelerating even as it continues.
- A weak white-collar labor market is floated as a contributing factor — workers who can't easily find another job have less leverage to resist return-to-office mandates, though this can't be proven from the data.
- The decline is concentrated in management/business/science/arts occupations (19.6% → 18.7%), while sales/office occupations ticked up slightly; information and finance sectors, previously steep decliners, were roughly flat in 2025.
- A persistent ~3-point gender gap in remote work (women 14.5% vs men 11.4%) emerged during the pandemic and has held steady, attributed likely to caregiving needs among mothers of young children.
- Author's forecast: no plateau yet, with the share likely settling around 11-12%; still, most of the pandemic-era shift remains intact — today's rate is more than double 2019's 5.7%, retaining ~60% of the 2019-2021 increase.

## Quote

> I don't see a plateau yet.
