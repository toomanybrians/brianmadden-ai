# https://t.co/BUtXyCs3Zb

## Insights

- LifeOS frames itself as an "AI-powered Life Operating System" built on the premise that AI is highly capable at execution but lacks knowledge of what the user actually wants — positioning the product as solving an intent-specification problem rather than a capability problem.
- The core workflow ("The Algorithm") is a four-step loop applied uniformly to any task — coding, writing, health, habits: Articulate (define done as checkable criteria), Climb (take the next step), Verify (require tool-sourced evidence before counting a step as complete), Learn (fold results back into the system).
- The system is built around persistent context: a "TELOS" document captures mission, goals, beliefs, and challenges once, and this is then carried into every subsequent task/interaction with the AI, rather than re-specified each time.
- "ISA" (Ideal State Articulation) documents formalize what "done" looks like as a set of verifiable criteria tied to evidence, used to track progress from current state to ideal state across a project (demonstrated via the project's own public criteria tracker, e.g. "134 of 141 criteria verified").
- Positions itself as a reframe of prompting — moving "from prompt engineering to intent engineering," i.e., productizing the act of conveying overall goals/intent to AI systems rather than crafting individual prompts.
- The project is open-source (GitHub, MIT license) and installed by having a user's AI coding agent (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor) read and execute an install prompt from the site.

## Quote

> Your AI is brilliant at executing. It just doesn't know what you actually want.
