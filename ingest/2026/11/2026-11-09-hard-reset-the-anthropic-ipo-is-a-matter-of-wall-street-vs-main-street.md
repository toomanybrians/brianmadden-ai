# The Anthropic IPO is a matter of Wall Street vs Main Street

## Insights

- The AI industry's financial stability rests on three fragile points: the leading model developers (Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceX), the "neoclouds" that lease chip access, and Oracle — and all three showed strain this fall.
- Anthropic's SEC prospectus revealed nearly $900 billion in off-balance-sheet debt (lease/purchase obligations) — over 25x its annual revenue — while its Q3 revenue growth reportedly slowed sharply.
- Oracle has taken on heavy debt to build AI data centers despite lacking the monopoly revenue bases of larger rivals like Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, raising default-risk concerns after a data center delay.
- Neocloud failures (Firmus's collapsed $30B IPO, CoreWeave's bond selloff with ~60% five-year default risk priced in, Nscale's imperiled IPO after Microsoft rejected a data center deal) signal investor skepticism toward the "selling shovels" layer of the AI build-out.
- The piece frames Anthropic's planned IPO as a pivotal risk-transfer event: going public would shift potential losses from Silicon Valley/VC insiders onto retail investors, workers, and retirees, while doing nothing to fix the underlying gap between AI infrastructure spending (~$1T+/year) and actual AI product revenue.
- The author argues the SEC should block the IPO given Anthropic's financial opacity, framing its mandate as protecting "Main Street" over propping up the AI trade for Wall Street's benefit.

## Quote

> The SEC's job is to protect Main Street, not Wall Street.
