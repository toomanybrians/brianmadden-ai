# The Morning Download: Thomson Reuters Chief Forges Path With AI, Unique Content

## Insights

- Thomson Reuters CEO Steve Hasker began restructuring the company around AI in 2020, pre-dating ChatGPT by over two years, framing it as a long, capital-intensive process now yielding measurable returns despite stock underperformance since August 2025.
- Hasker rejects the narrative that frontier models will sweep aside authoritative-content providers; he argues proprietary, curated content — not chipsets or energy — is the most important component in the AI ecosystem for ensuring "fiduciary-grade" accuracy.
- Thomson Reuters has built an in-house model to harness its proprietary news, data, and insights, positioning itself as a "content-driven technology company" with AI at its core.
- Multiple large-company CEOs (Thomson Reuters' Hasker, ServiceNow's Bill McDermott, Bloomberg's Vlad Kliatchko) are cited as maintaining long-term AI investment commitments despite a volatile investment environment, described as a stabilizing "ballast."
- Separately, robotics startup Genesis AI raised a $500M Series A at a $3.5B valuation (notable backers include Eric Schmidt, Eric Yuan, Jerry Yang), aiming to build a general-purpose robot with full-stack control, signaling continued frontier investment in physical/embodied AI.
- A Harvard-led peer-reviewed study found the 403 largest U.S. data centers used nearly 2% of national electricity (May 2024–April 2025) — more than NYC and LA combined — with 54% of that power from fossil fuels, quantifying AI infrastructure's environmental footprint.
