# Monopoly Round-Up: Did Bernie Sanders Set the Terms for the Midterm Elections?

## Insights

- Public sentiment on AI has sharply soured: negative views are rising across all age groups, with 47% of young people now believing AI does more harm than good (up from 27% in 2024), and only 9% of people overall saying AI does more good than harm.
- AI data centers have become a potent political flashpoint distinct from abstract AI anxiety — framed as concrete threats to utility rates, water allocation, and tied to surveillance/data-processing infrastructure that voters can see and oppose locally.
- Polling on data centers flipped from public support to broad opposition as awareness increased, prompting bipartisan political distancing: a NY statewide moratorium (reversing a prior pro-AI-investment initiative), a federal moratorium bill, and GOP candidates avoiding full-throated AI boosterism in favor of "local control" framing.
- Some Democratic political consultants reportedly advised candidates to avoid discussing AI altogether, fearing opposition spending from AI-industry-aligned PACs (e.g., Ron Conway's "Leading the Future" PAC) — though that PAC's backer later signaled public regret over the pro-AI spending push.
- A Congressional Progressive Caucus resolution opposing a bailout of AI firms is cited as a marker that AI industry policy has become a live partisan battleground, not a fringe issue.
- Broader economic distrust (of Wall Street, Big Tech, crypto, private equity) is bipartisan, with majorities of Republicans in an October poll expressing concern about corporate/tech concentration of power — AI/data centers are framed as one strand within this wider anti-monopoly political mood.

## Quote

> AI was contributing to general, abstract anxiety, [but] data centers were a physical symbol you could point at.
