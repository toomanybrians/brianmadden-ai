# Squeezing Neoclouds Pushes NVIDIA Back Toward Its Rivals

## Insights

- NVIDIA faces a strategic tension between two customer segments: hyperscalers (Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Meta), who buy heavily ($60.1B/quarter) but are simultaneously developing competing custom silicon, and neoclouds/frontier labs, who buy less ($47.5B/quarter) but build no rival chips and depend on NVIDIA's financing to scale.
- Non-hyperscaler share of NVIDIA's Data Center revenue peaked at 45% then dipped to 44% in Q3, coinciding with NVIDIA signaling tighter financing terms for neoclouds.
- NVIDIA carries substantial exposure to this ecosystem: $102B in cloud/data-center lease commitments (including $32B directly with neoclouds) and $390B in total purchase commitments.
- Interim CEO/CFO Colette Kress indicated a shift toward more disciplined, selective support for neocloud operators, requiring them to bring more of their own capital and infrastructure ("powered shells").
- Neoclouds lack the cash-flow strength of hyperscalers, so tighter NVIDIA financing could constrain growth for smaller, capital-limited operators.
- The piece frames a diversified customer base as NVIDIA's main defense against hyperscalers' push into custom silicon — so curbing neocloud reliance may inadvertently increase hyperscaler leverage over NVIDIA.

## Quote

> We have supported the development of this ecosystem, & we will continue to be disciplined & selective in our support.
