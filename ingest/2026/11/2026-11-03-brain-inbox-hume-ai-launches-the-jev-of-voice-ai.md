# ⚙️ Hume AI launches the Jev of voice AI

## Insights

- Anthropic disclosed SEC filing commitments of ~$895B in spending, with $448B due before 2030, alongside a $23.3B Q3 net loss despite revenue growing twelvefold year-over-year to $17B — illustrating that frontier-lab revenue growth is still outpaced by infrastructure costs.
- Frontier labs face price pressure from both sides: rising compute/infrastructure costs on one end, and customers increasingly shifting to cheaper open-source or efficient alternatives on the other, pushing labs toward matching token-price cuts.
- An industry advisor argued profitability requires movement on at least two of three levers — higher effective prices, lower serving cost per task, or longer useful capital life — or labs will need continuous outside funding.
- Hume AI released an "Audio Decision Model" built on the same architecture as Jev — answering many structured questions about voice input in parallel with confidence scores, rather than relying solely on transcripts — enabling detection of intent, emotion, speaker count, tone, and background context.
- Hume reported its model matched a human rating panel's collective judgment (70.7%) more closely than any single human rater (56.5%) and outperformed rivals like Gemini 3.8 Flash and Qwen 3.8-Omni-Flash on combined accuracy/speed benchmarks.
- OpenAI announced a "Grid Protectors Program" to flexibly manage data center power draw during grid strain and is reportedly rebranding facilities as "AI compute campuses" as part of a PR effort, amid polling showing ~70% of Americans oppose nearby data center construction.

## Quote

> "Labs that can't move at least two of those three will need continued outside funding to stay in the game." — Scott Bickley, Infotech
