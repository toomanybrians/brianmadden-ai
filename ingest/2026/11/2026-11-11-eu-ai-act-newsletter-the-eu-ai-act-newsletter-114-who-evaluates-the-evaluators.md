# The EU AI Act Newsletter #114: Who Evaluates the Evaluators?

## Insights

- The AI Office is developing a scoring system to qualify external evaluators of general-purpose AI (GPAI) models with systemic risk, requiring both domain expertise and independence from a provider's managerial, financial, or operational influence (Article 55(1)(b)).
- A new request channel lets national fundamental rights authorities (e.g., non-discrimination bodies) formally request information/documents from the AI Office when it acts as market surveillance authority, under Article 77 — but only for systems under the AI Office's exclusive competence (GPAI-based systems from the same provider, or systems integrated into very large platforms/search engines).
- Independent analysts argue that funding evaluators alone won't build real EU testing capacity, given the high fixed costs of secure testing environments, and that model access remains politically fraught (Anthropic restricted EU access to its "Mythos" cyber model until September).
- One proposal: a dedicated EU evaluation body could act on the AI Office's behalf, since the Act (unlike the UK's AISI) lets the AI Office compel access via independent experts rather than relying on company goodwill.
- Legal scholars flag a gap in incident reporting: Article 55(1)(c) requires frontier model providers to report serious incidents, but the Act's definition of "serious incident" is written for AI systems, not models, and its reference to "property" damage is ambiguous for things like cybersecurity breaches, near-misses, or purely internal/undeployed models.
- Enforcement against GPAI providers has reportedly already begun, with information requests sent to over 30 providers; fines and potential model withdrawals are seen as likely outcomes depending on responses.
