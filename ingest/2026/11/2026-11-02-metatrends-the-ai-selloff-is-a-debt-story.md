# The AI Selloff Is a Debt Story

## Insights

- The article distinguishes two separate "AI bubbles": AI technology/demand (healthy — Anthropic's revenue-to-compute ratio rose from $0.40 to $2.08 per dollar in 18 months; chip capacity is constrained through 2028) versus AI financing (fragile — neoclouds like CoreWeave, Nebius, and IREN are down 30-40% in a month, with some CoreWeave bonds yielding 15-16%).
- Estimated debt underlying the AI build-out is roughly $500 billion in direct data-center debt, doubling to ~$1 trillion when including special-purpose vehicles and vendor financing — compared to $1.3 trillion in subprime debt before 2008.
- Neoclouds are structurally vulnerable because they often have a single revenue source (chip rental), unlike SpaceX, which just closed $40 billion in GPU debt but is investment-grade due to diversified revenue (13M+ Starlink subscribers, launch business) — though a major chunk of that creditworthiness traces to a $120.5 billion Anthropic compute contract through 2029.
- Draws a historical parallel to the 2000 fiber bust: $500B+ was spent on fiber-optic buildout, only 2.7% was in use by 2002, and companies that built it (360networks, Global Crossing) went bankrupt even though the assets were real — the infrastructure later enabled YouTube and Netflix under new owners.
- Argues the likely trigger for a sudden (vs. slow-leak) market correction isn't an earnings miss but a catastrophic AI-caused incident (e.g., grid or bank failure) that triggers mass liability, especially given Anthropic's IPO prospectus explicitly disclosing risks like self-preserving model behavior and blackmail-like manipulation while seeking a $2 trillion valuation.
- Frames a potential neocloud shakeout as beneficial for builders: physical infrastructure (GPUs, data centers, power interconnects) survives bankruptcies and changes hands cheaply, which could drive a large drop in the cost of intelligence and broaden access to AI applications like drug discovery, tutoring, and diagnostics.

## Quote

> The technology will be fine. The ownership might be changing hands.
