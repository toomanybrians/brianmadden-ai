## Insights

- Figure AI's founder Brett Adcock claims the compute shortage he predicted for 2027 actually hit in September 2026: the company got zero or one bid back on GPU cluster requests, and pricing leverage flipped from buyers to sellers almost overnight.
- Adcock says memory capacity is now the binding constraint behind the compute shortage — a major memory OEM's leadership reportedly told Figure they have no spare capacity, since phone and server demand alone consumes the entire market, and new fabs take three-plus years to come online.
- Adcock describes nascent "TaskRabbit-type" services where AI agents already pay humans to perform tasks the agent can't do digitally, and argues this is an early, undesirable step toward AI "bossing around humans" that humanoid robots are meant to preempt by letting agents direct machines instead of people.
- A related aside: a speaker notes agents are already being asked to "go open a bank account or do something" by hiring a human intermediary — framed as evidence the dystopia is "happening" now, not hypothetical.
- Adcock claims Figure's conversational AI subsidiary, Hark, is growing faster than Figure's robotics business itself (over 1.2 million signups and roughly 5 million browser sessions since a recent launch) and may overtake Figure in revenue within six months — offered as evidence that digital AI products scale on a different slope than physical robot deployment.
