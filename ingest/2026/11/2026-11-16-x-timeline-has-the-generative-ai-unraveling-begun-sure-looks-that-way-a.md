# Has the generative AI unraveling begun? 

Sure looks that way.

A recap of why e

## Insights

- Marcus argues generative AI's economics never worked: heavy competition among labs using similar techniques/datasets eroded any technical moat, driving price wars and persistent lack of profitability (e.g., Claude 5.5 reportedly cost $10B to develop).
- Points to reliability failures as a recurring theme — studies showing poor ROI, weak instruction-following, and a suspected link between a major Microsoft outage and AI-generated code.
- Cites the "Klarna effect" (companies publicly rehiring humans after over-claiming AI capability) as evidence that deployed systems underperform their hype.
- Frames recent market moves — Nvidia down 29%, Oracle down 35%, CoreWeave down 54%, Anthropic's IPO valuation cut from $2T to ~$1.5T — as signs of a bubble actively deflating, not just stalling.
- Highlights a leadership/financing risk: a change in command at Nvidia (less willing to make "YOLO" bets backing neocloud/AI firms) could remove the implicit subsidy propping up companies like Nscale, CoreWeave, and Anthropic.
- Notes external signals of credit stress — banks pulling back on AI loans, bond buyers balking, hedge funds exiting — suggesting funding conditions for AI infrastructure are tightening sharply.
- A commenter adds a separate technical critique: LLMs may lack genuine "intrinsic" understanding or reasoning, displaying only surface-level ("extrinsic") intelligence.
