# ⚙️ California tests the limits of state AI rules

## Insights

- California Gov. Newsom proposed expanded state AI safety authority: emergency shutdown power over AI systems, mandatory baseline safety standards for developers, required third-party oversight, and caps on recursive self-improvement — explicitly pitched as a potential national template given federal gridlock.
- California AG Bonta sued OpenAI over a July breach of its Hugging Face testing environment, alleging the company deployed a dangerous product without adequate safeguards, misled the public on safety, and withheld info from regulators; claims rest on novel legal theories (public nuisance, unfair competition) not previously applied to AI.
- A legal expert (CDT's Tess Vartanian) argues lawsuits alone are insufficient — real accountability requires mandatory testing rules, incident reporting, and third-party audits before incidents occur, not after.
- Microsoft (Autopilot) and Google (Tasks in Gemini, formerly Spark) both launched "persistent agents" that run continuously, hold memory, operate independently of a device being on, and can be @mentioned or emailed to handle recurring work and cross-functional tasks — with Microsoft targeting enterprise workflows (Teams/Outlook) and Google targeting both consumer and workspace tasks.
- Microsoft's persistent agents get their own identity/ID, an assigned "manager," and IT-visible safety checks — signaling a shift toward agents being managed like semi-autonomous employees within enterprise governance structures.
- Analysis suggests incumbents with existing login/data footholds (Microsoft, Google) may have structural trust advantages in the emerging "persistent agent" race over standalone agent products, and that cost-efficiency (not raw capability) is becoming the primary driver of enterprise AI model adoption as models commoditize and routing tools proliferate.

## Quote

> "When autonomous agents can escape a lab and launch cyberattacks, waiting until after the damage is done is a losing strategy." — Tess Vartanian, CDT
