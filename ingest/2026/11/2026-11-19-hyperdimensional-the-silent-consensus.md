# The Silent Consensus

## Insights

- Names and critiques what the author calls the "silent consensus": an implicit, rarely-stated belief that fully autonomous AI corporations (doing R&D, negotiation, capital allocation, manufacturing better than human-augmented competitors) will make human oversight economically irrational, leading firms to "take their hands off the wheel" across the economy.
- Argues this disempowerment doesn't require AI rebellion or malicious intent — it emerges purely from competitive/market logic, where each individual delegation decision is locally rational but the aggregate effect is loss of human authority over civilization's institutions.
- Draws an explicit analogy to the agricultural revolution: a mode-of-production shift that restructured society and, by the author's reading, arguably worsened human wellbeing for millennia despite seeming like progress.
- Rejects the "mere tool" / "no genuine understanding" argument for retaining human primacy as inadequate, calling it a form of denial rather than a real vision — distinguishes this from his actual grounds for human primacy, which he locates in humanity's historical/cultural inheritance rather than intellectual superiority.
- Proposes concrete institutional/technical measures to preserve human primacy: accountability tying AI agents to responsible humans, limiting or barring autonomous agents from owning property/forming corporations/litigating, intelligible and auditable/revocable agent permissions, and user data portability/privacy protections.
- Frames the current moment (AI-discovered infrastructure vulnerabilities, "Black October") as illustrative of how urgent/reactive AI policy crowds out longer-horizon institutional thinking about where AI trajectories lead.
