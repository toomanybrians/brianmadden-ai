# AI Is Creating Two Different Futures for Young People

## Insights

- Two contrasting case studies illustrate AI's uneven effect on young job-seekers: a 22-year-old with a biology degree applied to 511 jobs, including being rejected by an AI chatbot interviewer; a 27-year-old Cisco engineer was given a two-person team and "unlimited AI tokens" to rewrite 98% of the company's legacy code in 24 months.
- Entry-level career ladders are fracturing because companies are automating the routine tasks that traditionally justified hiring inexperienced workers, while also using AI recruiting systems that strip human contact from hiring/rejection.
- A "talent shortage" is emerging around people who can direct AI at the frontier — organizations reportedly often have just one person who functions as the indispensable "AI fixer," and many have no one like that.
- The piece frames the real divide not as access to AI itself but as relationship to it: being screened/evaluated by AI versus using AI as a force multiplier to direct one's own agenda.
- Argues education should shift from credential-based preparation toward three capabilities: AI fluency, agency/entrepreneurship, and mission-driven problem-seeking, replacing "what job can I get" with "what problem can I solve."
- Notes experience can create assumptions about what's impossible, and that young/inexperienced people, unconstrained by those assumptions plus equipped with AI tools, may be positioned to attempt things veteran professionals wouldn't consider.

## Quote

> The job is not promised. But the tools are becoming available.
