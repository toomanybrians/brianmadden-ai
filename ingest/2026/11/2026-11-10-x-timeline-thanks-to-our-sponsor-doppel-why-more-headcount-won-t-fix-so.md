# Thanks to our sponsor Doppel: why more headcount won't fix SOC alert fatigue, an

## Insights

- Frames SOC (security operations center) analyst work as a "human spam filter" problem: human headcount scales linearly while AI-driven attack automation scales near-infinitely, making headcount-based scaling mathematically unwinnable.
- Cites structural burnout drivers: typical SOC processes ~3,832 alerts/day, analysts expected to evaluate up to 174 per shift, with 50-80% being false positives — producing 71% analyst burnout and 64% likely to switch jobs within a year (per cited Tines report).
- Describes the "swivel-chair effect" — analysts manually pivoting across 5-7 disconnected security tools (ticketing, SIEM, endpoint detection, threat intel, sandbox, email gateway) turning what should be instant triage into ~15-minute investigations per alert.
- Details how generative AI enables attackers to auto-generate 500+ hyper-personalized phishing variants per campaign using "dynamic semantic perturbation" (constantly altered language) and image/SVG-based payloads to evade text-based filters and keyword detection.
- Argues the only viable response is replacing human-in-the-loop triage with autonomous "agentic AI" that reasons over intent, cross-references threat telemetry in milliseconds, and executes machine-speed takedowns of attacker infrastructure (domains, redirect chains) rather than just flagging alerts.
- Positions this as a broader pattern: "only agents can keep up with agents" — i.e., defending against AI-automated adversaries requires AI-automated (not merely human-augmented) defense systems.

## Quote

> You can't hire your way out of an infinite automation attack.
