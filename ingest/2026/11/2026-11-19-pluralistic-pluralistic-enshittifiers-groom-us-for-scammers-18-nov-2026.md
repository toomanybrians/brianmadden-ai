# Pluralistic: Enshittifiers groom us for scammers (18 Nov 2026)

## Insights

- AI tools (voice cloning, deepfakes, image-generated IDs) combined with AI-powered data summarization let fraudsters mine breach data at scale to run precision-targeted spear-phishing against millions simultaneously.
- Corporate cost-cutting ("efficiency") leads firms to fire the IT staff who maintain critical systems while those systems keep running unattended until they catastrophically fail — described as accumulating "tech debt" until a "margin call" triggers technological bankruptcy.
- Institutions (banks, employers, insurers) have normalized chaotic, inconsistent authentication flows — new apps, odd CAPTCHAs, third-party ID verification — which trains customers to comply with exactly the kind of irregular requests scammers use.
- The piece argues incompetence and fraud become indistinguishable to users: "expected incompetence enables fraud," making technologically sophisticated people equally or more vulnerable since they're desensitized to absurd security demands.
- AI customer-service chatbots are described as giving "hallucinated" nonsensical account-access advice, while long hold queues push users toward self-service tools that don't work, further eroding trust boundaries.
- Argues the fix isn't demanding more vigilance from individuals but demanding institutional investment in coherent, well-maintained systems staffed by reachable human experts, since well-run systems are inherently harder to impersonate.

## Quote

> Expected incompetence enables fraud.
