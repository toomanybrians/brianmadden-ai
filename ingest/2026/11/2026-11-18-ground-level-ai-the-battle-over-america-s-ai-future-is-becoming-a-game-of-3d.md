# The battle over America's AI future is becoming a game of 3D chess. I have questions

## Insights

- Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia CEO has reignited a long-simmering Washington fight over who controls access to advanced American AI chips and compute, and how that intersects with the US-China "AI race."
- Huang stated publicly (via X) he would return as CEO if Nvidia's board judged it best for shareholders, employees, Nvidia, and "America" — explicitly framing his potential return in national-interest terms, which the piece notes is contested.
- The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) issued a new rule targeting a "remote-access loophole": Chinese firms barred from directly buying top-tier Nvidia chips had still been accessing that compute by renting capacity from data centers located outside China.
- Notably, the new BIS rule was written "by and at the direction of" the Treasury Department and the National Security Council — unusual since Commerce normally leads export-control rulemaking, suggesting a shift in which part of the executive branch is driving AI chip policy.
- Reporting cited (via a former colleague now at The Information) indicates the White House had been softening its opposition to tougher export controls, partly because Nvidia's aggressive lobbying had alienated lawmakers on both sides of the aisle.
- Commerce reportedly had been working for months to close this loophole, but efforts repeatedly stalled before yesterday's announcement.
