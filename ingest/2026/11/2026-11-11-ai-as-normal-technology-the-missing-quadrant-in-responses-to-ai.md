# The missing quadrant in responses to AI

## Insights

- Proposes a 2x2 framework for AI discourse: one axis is how seriously you take AI's transformative potential, the other is how seriously you take downstream bottlenecks (regulatory, organizational, societal) that slow its real-world impact; most commentary clusters in the two "extreme" quadrants (pure determinism vs. status-quo defense) rather than the quadrant that takes both seriously.
- Argues economic data so far (stagnant productivity gains despite adoption, unchanged aggregate unemployment) is consistent with the view that transformation is bottlenecked by organizational/human change rather than model capability — but warns that aggregate indicators can mask real qualitative change in how professions operate (e.g., software engineering "dramatically different" even without headcount shifts).
- Distinguishes automation (replacing costly labor with cheaper AI, firm-centric) from augmentation (respecting worker agency/job satisfaction) and argues augmentation is both more economically promising and more likely to produce genuinely new goods/industries rather than just squeezing existing ones.
- Claims current benchmarks for AI research capability (recursive self-improvement) are biased toward easily-verifiable tasks; on open-ended, messy tasks (including AI agents attempting actual AI research) frontier models reportedly produced papers that expert reviewers unambiguously rejected — suggesting a real gap between verifiable-task progress and open-ended capability.
- Argues AI safety has over-indexed on alignment and non-proliferation, and that societal resilience (sector-specific, highly ambitious, worst-case-scenario planning including civilizational collapse response) is the most neglected pillar of defense against AI risk.
- Calls for studying specific professions/sectors in context rather than relying on capability benchmarks or "exposure" metrics to predict job/institutional impact, citing prior work on science, legal services, and software engineering as the model.

## Quote

> Understanding the bottlenecks to AI's impacts is only the starting point of analysis.
