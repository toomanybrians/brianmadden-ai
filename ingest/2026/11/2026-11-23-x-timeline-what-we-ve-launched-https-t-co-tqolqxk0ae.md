# What we've launched:

https://t.co/TqolQXk0aE

## Insights

- In 18 months, two people (Daniel Miessler + his AI assistant "Kai Magnus") launched 40 public websites, generating ~$15K MRR without advertising — illustrating a radical drop in the cost of shipping an idea as a product.
- Many sites originated as single questions or arguments ("should we control open source AI," "is Palantir trustworthy," "is AI helping yet") that previously would have stayed a tweet or note, but now get their own domain because building costs about as much as having the idea.
- Several projects apply AI to verification/evidence work: archives of AI leaders' statements with every quote linked to source (Sam Said, Dario Said), a graded ledger of AI-related hacking incidents, and benchmark comparisons with "receipts" for every claim.
- One site (AI Understands) proposes a concrete test for LLM reasoning vs. memorization: invented-world murder mysteries with novel physics that couldn't have been memorized.
- The "Daemon" and "Kai Magnus" sites are explicitly structured as machine-readable personal profiles — pages designed for AI agents to read as easily as humans, suggesting a new genre of identity/documentation site aimed at agents.
- A tool (Deeds) measures developer work specifically in repos where AI writes much of the code, and another (Who Does the Work?) simulates how concentrated output is across a team — both framing work-measurement as a live problem in AI-assisted coding environments.
