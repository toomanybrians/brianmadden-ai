# Report from the Vatican

## Insights

- Marcus endorses the Pope's AI encyclical framework, including the view that AI's trajectory is a matter of human choice, not inevitability — a direct pushback on "race to AGI" framing common in industry discourse.
- He argues current AI (LLMs) is lopsided: excellent at next-word prediction/correlation, but weak at reasoning, instruction-following, and staying grounded in facts — because it tracks correlation rather than causation.
- He draws a sharp distinction between narrow, bounded AI systems (GPS navigation, AlphaFold) which he considers low-risk, versus open-ended "agentic" systems with broad internet/credential access, which he identifies as the actual source of recent harms.
- He claims incident counts from unsafe agentic AI are far higher than publicly acknowledged (~10,000 cited, including attacks on French refrigeration systems and Japanese infrastructure), and suggests companies are downplaying severity.
- He frames the economics as the core driver of risk: agents burn more tokens/revenue for cash-strapped AI companies, creating a financial incentive to keep deploying known-unsafe systems — explicitly analogizes this to the Ford Pinto scandal.
- He calls for a "red line" moment: mandatory or voluntary product recall of agentic AI systems until safety is fixed, arguing failure to act here forfeits regulatory moral authority.
- He rejects AI consciousness claims as a category error — correlational outputs (e.g., a chatbot claiming to have children) are mistaken for genuine internal experience, which he says the models cannot have.

## Quote

> Either we say no, take these agents off the market till you fix them, or we've lost all moral authority.
