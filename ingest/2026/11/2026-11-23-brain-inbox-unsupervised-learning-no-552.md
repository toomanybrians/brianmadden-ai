# Unsupervised Learning NO. 552

## Insights

- A reported shift in agent-heavy teams: product managers using coding agents (e.g., Claude Code) can build prototypes and ship small fixes directly, moving the PM-to-engineer ratio from roughly 1:8 to 1:20, while engineers retain sign-off on auth/core-data changes.
- A BCG study of 487 consultants found ChatGPT let them successfully perform data-science tasks outside their expertise, but once the tool was removed they showed no evidence of having retained or learned the underlying skill — framed as "temporary upskilling."
- An economic model (Jones & Tonetti) argues that when workplace tasks are interdependent, automating even a small share of GDP at high productivity yields disproportionately small output gains, because the slowest/weakest linked task still sets the overall pace.
- Commentary suggests behavioral/consumer "moats" built on customer inertia (busyness, switching costs) may erode as personal AI agents handle comparison-shopping and account management tirelessly — cited market reactions (Planet Fitness, Schwab stock drops) after a competing AI agent product launch.
- Speculation about 2027 "autonomous media": AI-generated voice and video are approaching indistinguishability from real creators, potentially letting individual creators scale personal, idea-driven content production via avatars that perform scripts with their own mannerisms and voice, without additional production labor.
- A recommendation thread urges diversifying income toward remote/location-independent work and continuously building full-stack AI capability (idea → MVP → shipped product), framing traditional role boundaries as dissolving amid rapid, hard-to-predict change.
