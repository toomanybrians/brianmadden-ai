# ChinAI #380: It’s getting more and more difficult to make money

## Insights

- A widely-circulated Chinese essay (budongjing, 100k+ views via Huxiu) argues China's economy has shifted from an "arranging atoms" paradigm (manufacturing, real estate, scarcity-based wealth creation) to an "arranging bits" paradigm, adapting Emad Mostaque's framework from *The Last Economy*.
- The essay claims the old digital-economy playbook of guarding closed systems and software patents is being replaced by open-source networks that scale exponentially — changing how value and wealth are captured.
- AI is framed as "the ultimate shock," with the essay predicting it will perform nearly all digital tasks by end of 2027, intensifying the sense of economic zero-sum competition.
- The piece argues ordinary people increasingly have only "time to sell" for competition over diminishing returns, while outsized gains go to those who already hold repeatable, monetizable assets and resources.
- A notable (and contested-by-the-newsletter-author) framing circulating among Chinese bloggers: the "hard work succeeds" narrative is being replaced by one where "connections matter more than effort."
- The essay's proposed response to this shift is vague/self-help-oriented — "think like an independent network node" and "build your own platform" — without a concrete structural solution.
- Separately, a linked James Bridle essay describes building a chair with ChatGPT's help, illustrating AI's lack of embodied experience: it can replicate the idea of an object from text but not lived, physical familiarity with it.

## Quote

> "Now, however, these engines have slowed down; we have entered an era of 'zero-sum' competition."
