# The thing is that their products are unreasonably effective &amp; getting better

## Insights

- Labs treat their software products as disposable/short-lived because the underlying LLMs are improving so rapidly that conventional software release cycles and stability norms no longer apply.
- This rapid improvement is framed as a reward structure: labs benefit from frequent, abrupt product changes because the pace of model progress outstrips normal product lifecycle expectations.
- A core belief inside the labs, per the thread, is that current products are not meant to be durable — they're a temporary mechanism to generate revenue and sustain the business for a few years.
- The implicit endpoint of this belief is "true" AGI, which the labs reportedly expect will render all current products obsolete.
- This belief is offered as an explanation for labs' erratic behavior: sudden changes and poor communication around product updates stem from not treating these products as long-term assets.

## Quote

> A core belief is that these products are a temporary thing they have to do to raise money for a couple years until "true" AGI makes all products obsolete.
