# I am sad that so much of the underlying evidence in this report was redacted. I

## Insights

- A referenced (redacted) report reportedly found Anthropic's capabilities progress has doubled, largely attributable to AI itself; one analyst's modeling from public info suggests overall company R&D could be running 4-5x faster, with labor uplift higher still — described as a major update toward shorter AI timelines.
- The author argues the science of "loss-of-control" risk is immature: there are no settled methods to measure whether AI systems might undermine or seize human control, and current alignment benchmarks may just be gamed rather than genuinely indicating safety.
- Current industry discourse about third-party "verification" or "auditing" of safety claims is premature — companies aren't yet making precise, falsifiable claims, and no company publicly addresses the full set of questions relevant to loss-of-control risk.
- The proposed fix is "evidence transparency": third parties and companies should publicly share the empirical evidence behind their conclusions (not just high-level judgments), functioning more like open science than formal verification.
- Three stated benefits of evidence transparency: it creates common knowledge between competing companies (public disclosure is often the only viable channel, since private sharing is legally/socially difficult), it brings outside scientists with different incentives into the conversation, and it lets the public evaluate whether third-party evaluators themselves did rigorous work.
- Author acknowledges full transparency is impossible given IP and capacity constraints (e.g., her own org didn't release prompts/code in a cited report), but treats it as a "north star" goal.
- Frames this as urgent: redacted/opaque reporting undermines the world's ability to "figure this out in time," given recent misalignment incidents and both OpenAI and Anthropic reportedly slowing RL training for safety reasons.
