# A portrait of eight billion people

## Insights

- Simile builds a "foundation model of human behavior" by creating digital twins of real people from short interviews (now ~15-30 minutes, down from an original 2-hour protocol), claiming 85%+ accuracy predicting individual behavior.
- Business model mirrors traditional market-research/panel companies but makes the panel reusable: instead of surveying a niche population once, a simulated version can be queried indefinitely by customers (e.g., CVS Health testing pricing, banks testing fee changes).
- Founder Joon Sung Park frames the technology's trajectory in four stages: static individual simulation (surveys) → dynamic individual agents (testing products) → multi-agent simulation (earnings calls, interviews) → full societal/market simulation — the last being the ambitious end goal (modeling all 8 billion people, tackling "wicked problems" like climate change or signals of democratic collapse).
- Draws a historical lineage from Schelling's 1970s agent-based models (simple rule-based agents producing emergent segregation) to today's generative-AI-powered "generative agent-based models," arguing LLMs finally supply the missing building block: rich, learned human behavioral priors.
- Raises an open strategic question: if simulation becomes ubiquitous and firms can preview outcomes before acting, what remains as competitive advantage? Park names point of view, proprietary data, and compute — two of which tend to favor already well-resourced incumbents.
- Trust-building for an "predicting the unhappened" product (likened to cybersecurity) relies less on raw accuracy claims and more on giving customers a clear mental model of the underlying data (interviews, behavioral data, citations) plus a jointly-trained confidence/accuracy-prediction model.
- Company has raised $300M+ at a $2B valuation; partners include Gallup, giving access to 30-40 million real human subjects for grounding simulations.

## Quote

> "Imagine for a moment the foundation model... will be an important part of our technological landscape. What's going to be the killer application? ...simulation."
