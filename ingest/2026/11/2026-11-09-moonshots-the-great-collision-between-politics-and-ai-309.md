# The Great Collision Between Politics and AI | #309

## Fit with Brian's work

**Fit: partial.** One recurring thread—compute turning scarce as an availability constraint rather than a price problem, political/regulatory risk to data-center buildout, and the small-vs-frontier model efficiency debate—bears directly on Brian's compute-scarcity and token-governance arguments; the rest of the episode (recursive self-improvement, Dyson swarms, Fermi paradox speculation) sits entirely outside his scope.

- **Compute availability as the real constraint, not price.** A speaker describes all major compute (Google, Anthropic, xAI) and TSMC capacity as effectively reserved for years by five to seven companies, with no "right to compute" policy yet — this directly extends Brian's August 28 "control your own destiny" cloud-reserved-capacity analogy and his compute-scarcity/token-governance thread, adding a political dimension (DOJ inaction, comparison to Rockefeller-era concentration) he hasn't tracked before.
- **Concrete anecdote of the scarcity biting in practice.** One speaker says a "pilot client" was told by a cloud provider they weren't "big enough" a customer to get access — a small, specific real-world data point supporting the thesis that availability (not price) is becoming the binding constraint, which complements the AWS/Kimi K3 and SemiAnalysis evidence already in Brian's notes.
- **Political backlash to data centers as a physical/regulatory constraint, with fresh numbers.** Goldman Sachs is cited estimating 45% of planned US data-center buildout could face restrictions; 56% public opposition to big tech/data centers and gubernatorial wins for pause-advocating candidates are cited as midterm election outcomes. This extends Brian's September 25 "physical, not financial" compute-availability note (permitting, grid queues, Pennsylvania backlash) with a national-election-scale data point he didn't have.
- **Market correction tied to AI/regulatory uncertainty.** Nasdaq down 5.5%, S&P down over 4%, Nvidia down 9.3%, Korean semiconductor stocks down 6% are cited as the market's reaction to the midterms and AI-infrastructure uncertainty — relevant corroborating evidence for Brian's "pop is coming" frontier-of-mind question about what a slowdown/crash looks like and what triggers it.
- **Labs redirecting compute toward internal R&D, corroborating Brian's compute-redirection argument.** A speaker cites Anthropic's internal token use for development up "235x" in six months and a METR report showing a growing share of total compute going to internal work rather than external product serving — this reinforces (with new, specific multipliers) the compute-availability note Brian already has sourced to Patel about labs redirecting compute from external inference to their own R&D.
- **A "juice-to-GDP" (economic value per joule) benchmark, new angle on cost-per-unit-of-work.** A cited paper tested 220 professional tasks across 44 occupations: GLM 5.2 generated the highest average value (~$70/task), while a smaller Gemma 31B model delivered 2.5x more economic value per joule. This is a different, more quantified instantiation of Brian's October 11 "cost per unit of work, not token price" argument — worth noting as an external benchmark attempt at exactly the metric he's been reasoning about qualitatively.
- **Small/narrow distilled models vs. frontier models as a live "foot race."** A speaker describes investment interest in cheaper, narrow ~$50B-parameter task-specific models (e.g., SQL querying) built for ~$20M, set against the fact that frontier models keep getting smarter and erasing the advantage — a less formal, industry-insider version of the specialized "decision models" tension Brian flagged in his October 11 cognitive-stack note (Jev/System One, OpenAI Decisions API).
- **Open-weight Chinese models reported ahead on efficiency.** A speaker claims Chinese open-weight models are beating American open-weight models on economic productivity per unit of energy, attributing it to training under a more compute-constrained environment — a data point that corroborates (from a different angle) the Nathan Lambert evidence already in Brian's notes about Chinese open-weight models leading on capability and adoption.
- **Passing confirmation that "tokenmaxxing" cooled.** An aside ("token maxing went out of fashion last month") lightly corroborates Brian's October 11 note that companies tying AI usage to performance reviews are reversing course as costs became visible.
