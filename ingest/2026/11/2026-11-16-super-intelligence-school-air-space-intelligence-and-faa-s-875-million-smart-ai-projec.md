# Air Space Intelligence and FAA’s $875 Million SMART AI Project

## Insights

- Air Space Intelligence's SMART system for the FAA has to predict future airspace congestion, but its own recommendations change the conditions it's predicting — once one crew takes a suggested shortcut, that changes the optimal answer for the next flight behind it.
- The FAA expanded SMART to all 22 regional centers on November 10, citing 180 rerouted flights with average savings of ten minutes and ~80 gallons of fuel per flight.
- Three days later, a nationwide departure halt occurred due to unreliable communications/radar data, traced to telecommunications work; as of the piece's writing, no public finding linked this to SMART.
- The piece raises an unresolved technical question: when a system like SMART has to recover from a disruption (grounded flights, aircraft at wrong airports relative to plan), how does it decide which parts of its stale picture it can still trust?
- The FAA's $875 million, twelve-year agreement with Air Space Intelligence covers two linked systems: FMDS (aggregating traffic/capacity data) and SMART (using that data to recommend route and schedule changes).
