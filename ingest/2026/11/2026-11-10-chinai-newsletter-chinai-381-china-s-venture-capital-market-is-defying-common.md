# ChinAI #381: China’s Venture Capital Market is Defying Common Sense

## Insights

- China's 2026 "embodied AI" venture market is producing inflated seed valuations (hundreds of millions of RMB) based primarily on founder resumes — big tech pedigree or elite PhDs — rather than technological maturity.
- Many founding teams are assembled hastily by financial advisors shortly before term-sheet signing, combining people with no prior working relationship who then split equity and claim to be "running a company together" from day one.
- Startups are skipping normal organizational growth stages: one embodied AI company raised a large Series A and built a 500-person org with high-rise offices, logo walls, and an imported HR hierarchy from inception, with no experience scaling from a small team.
- A large share of VC capital comes from government-backed "guidance funds," which require recipients to accept reinvestment obligations and policy objectives that compete with pure financial return, distorting fund incentives.
- Even mature funds (7-8+ years old) rarely complete a full fundraise-invest-manage-exit cycle; some funds with zero successful exits are absorbed by state-owned enterprises, effectively just shifting capital between government pockets rather than resolving the underlying failure.
- The analyst contrasts this with the mid-2010s internet/e-commerce startup boom, arguing that wave's failures were "clean" and left the ecosystem more fertile, whereas today's wave lacks time and tolerance for failure, making the system structurally fragile.

## Quote

> while that previous wave saw a massive number of casualties, the deaths were 'clean'—leaving the soil, in fact, even more fertile.
