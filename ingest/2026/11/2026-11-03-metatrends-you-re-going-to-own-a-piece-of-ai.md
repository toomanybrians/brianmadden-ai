# You’re Going to Own a Piece of AI

## Insights

- In October 2026, Anthropic announced a Public Benefit Fund seeded with 5% of company shares (subject to stockholder approval), plus up to 2% more per 50% rise in valuation above a baseline; combined with founder pledges and employee matching, roughly 20% of the company is directed toward public benefit, including a $200M commitment to study AI's labor-market impact.
- The same week, Elon Musk pledged one SpaceX share to every American child (~72 million, worth ~$10.5B at the time) via Trump Accounts, calling it "the first of many"; the stock is locked for five years or until the child turns 17.
- Separately, Trump posted that data-center-building companies should pay host communities a "dividend" (no executive order or figure specified yet), following a precedent set by Michael Dell's $6.25B deposit of $250 each into 25 million children's accounts.
- Senator Sanders' proposed American A.I. Sovereign Wealth Fund Act (S. 4825) would take a one-time 50% stake in major AI companies, building an estimated $7 trillion fund with a dividend starting above $1,000/person/year.
- The piece frames these moves as a historical parallel to the Homestead Act (land distribution) and Alaska's Permanent Fund (oil-dividend model), arguing equity ownership "compounds" while cash payments get spent — and predicts equity pledges will become standard "social license" requirements for frontier AI companies by 2030.
- Local case studies show resistance to one-time cash offers for hosting data centers (e.g., Hazle Township, PA rejected $10,000/resident from AWS) versus ongoing stakes/utility credits (De Soto, KS: $1,000/household/year for ten years from Digital Realty), suggesting recurring equity-like stakes build more durable community buy-in than lump payments.
- Pew data cited: 71% of US adults expect AI to mean fewer jobs over the next two years, framing the equity-distribution push as a hedge against backlash if AI gains concentrate among a small group of shareholders.
