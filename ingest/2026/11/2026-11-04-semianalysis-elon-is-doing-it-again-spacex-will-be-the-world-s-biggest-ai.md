# Elon Is Doing It Again: SpaceX Will Be The World’s Biggest AI Cloud

## Insights

- SpaceX revised its 2027 datacenter capacity target upward from an already-aggressive 10GW to ~13GW, which would exceed the total datacenter capacity Google built from 1999-2026, compressed into roughly 1.5 years.
- SpaceX is reportedly the fastest datacenter builder in the world, achieving build times of ~91 days for 210MW (down from a prior 122-day record), driven partly by Tesla manufacturing modular MEP (mechanical/electrical/plumbing) equipment for rapid deployment.
- The company is repurposing non-datacenter assets for compute: Starbase (a rocket launch/build site) is being converted to house two datacenters potentially over 1GW combined, and TeraFab (a planned 10GW chip fab) may also include datacenter capacity for third-party rental.
- Power is sourced opportunistically and heterogeneously — behind-the-meter turbines, massive battery storage (BESS nearing 6GWh, potentially the largest US deployment), grid interconnects, and relocatable mobile turbines — with power sourcing sometimes finalized after construction has already begun.
- A regulatory constraint at the Southaven power plant (nameplate generation capacity legally capped from 2.7GW down to 1.24GW by July 2027) creates a potential ~300MW power shortfall for Colossus 2, which SpaceX may address via BESS/gas orchestration, efficient PUE (~1.10-1.15), and tiered SLA uptime across customer-facing vs. internal compute clusters.
- Elon-affiliated entities (SpaceX, Tesla) are described as having a track record of using top-tier engineering talent and manufacturing capability to build infrastructure far faster than conventional datacenter developers or even Chinese competitors.
