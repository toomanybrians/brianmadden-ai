# Three Lessons I Learned From the XPRIZE Board

## Insights

- Outside perspective can reveal a generalizable mechanism inside a specific success: Larry Page saw that XPRIZE's "define the outcome, let anyone compete" structure was itself a transferable innovation model, not just a space-specific win — leading to the creation of a non-space (automotive efficiency) prize.
- Prize models work by specifying the required result precisely (e.g., "100 MPGe equivalent, meeting safety/performance") while leaving the method entirely open to competing teams, who self-fund and self-organize.
- Problem-solving talent can come from completely unexpected, non-credentialed sources — a winning oil-spill recovery design originated with a tattoo artist and a customer testing a scale model in a Jacuzzi.
- Rapid response to an emergent crisis depended on a dense personal network (board member → organizer → funder) rather than formal search or vetting processes — raising the question of how many capable but invisible people get missed without such connections.
- Large funding commitments can be made quickly when the success metric is unambiguous and measurable (e.g., net tonnes of CO₂ removed), even before knowing which team or approach will actually deliver it.
- A pattern across all three lessons: clearly defined outcomes/metrics enable decentralized, outsider-driven problem solving — the organizer's job is naming the result precisely, not controlling the method.
