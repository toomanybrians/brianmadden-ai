# The uncomfortable economics of America's AI race with China

## Insights

- American businesses are drawn to open-weight AI models for cost control, customization, and data privacy/security reasons, rather than relying on proprietary offerings from OpenAI and Anthropic.
- Many of the top-performing open-weight models are Chinese (DeepSeek, Alibaba), raising concerns in Washington and Silicon Valley about cybersecurity, censorship, and tech dependence — while critics also worry open weights generally enable misuse once released.
- A joint House Homeland Security/Select Committee on China investigative report (seven months, examining Airbnb, DoorDash, Anysphere/Cursor usage) called for banning Chinese open-weight models from federal systems and critical infrastructure, while also admitting American firms use them because they're cheap, customizable, and often outperform US alternatives; the report recommends a national American open-weight AI initiative.
- Databricks' AI Index (10,000+ businesses) shows open-weight models grew from 7.3% to 30.9% of enterprise token traffic between July and November; DeepSeek V4.1 and GLM 5.3 tied for third in its coding benchmark against proprietary models.
- Nvidia (via Bryan Catanzaro/Nemotron) and other US players (Meta, Google, Thinking Machines Lab, Reflection AI, Ricursive Intelligence, Liquid AI) are pushing open-weight development partly for commercial reasons (driving hardware/software demand), but an academic (Jennifer Chayes, UC Berkeley) noted researchers there are building on Chinese models due to lack of a Western open frontier model.
- The piece frames the stakes for enterprises concretely: as AI agents deploy at scale, the ability to choose among competing open models affects inference costs and competitiveness; restricting Chinese open-weight models could leave US businesses more dependent on a small number of proprietary US AI giants.
