# Move Fast And Break Things, Quietly

## Insights

- Following Oakland's data center moratorium and a similar extension in San Francisco, big tech firms appear to be shifting from public announcements to quiet, low-visibility moves into local manufacturing/AI facilities (e.g., Tesla and Bezos's Project Prometheus signing leases in a West Oakland building with no press statements).
- Two days after an election that showed broad bipartisan opposition to data centers, OpenAI released a rebranding effort — renaming "data centers" to "compute campuses" and publishing a document emphasizing community partnership, jobs, and framing AI infrastructure build-out as tied to competition with China.
- The piece frames this as a deliberate strategic shift: rather than changing how data centers/AI facilities actually affect communities (utility costs, health effects, resource strain), companies are adjusting rhetoric and visibility in response to public opinion and the political environment.
- Project Prometheus (Bezos's AI company) is described as notably secretive — minimal public information, a content-free website, and media coverage even noting that basics like its founding date or headquarters location remain unclear.
- A speaker quoted from the Oakland city council meeting argues the "new economy" logic of AI/tech investment differs from older corporate investment: moves into a community are framed as extraction and asset-leveraging rather than durable local job creation or tax revenue.
- The author suggests AI companies will keep adjusting tone and strategy opportunistically based on shifts in federal political power, rather than on constant policy.
- A loosely related note: a former airline executive floated AI system implementation as a possible cause of recent airline ground stops/outages, calling the official explanation (a single router) not credible.

## Quote

> Don't cast your vote today based on the illusion we're operating on the old economy... every corporate move is to extract from our communities.
