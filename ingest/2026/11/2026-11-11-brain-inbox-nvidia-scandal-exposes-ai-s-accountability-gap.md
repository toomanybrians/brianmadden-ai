# ⚙️ Nvidia scandal exposes AI's accountability gap

## Insights

- Anthropic's Fable 5.2/Mythos 5.2 reframe AI competition around "finished work" rather than raw token cost — the models are built to orchestrate swarms of cheaper models and stay on a task until it's verifiably complete, rather than stopping at a "plausible checkpoint."
- Anthropic is building model routing directly into its platform (Advisor and Orchestrator modes), letting expensive frontier models delegate routine work to cheaper ones — framed as good for customers (lower cost/faster tasks) and defensive for Anthropic (avoids being disintermediated by third-party routing tools).
- Enterprise users (Datadog, Uber) describe Fable 5.2 handling ambiguous, unfamiliar systems autonomously and running multi-hour agentic workflows (e.g., a 10–12 hour security audit coordinating ~170 reviewer agents) that end in concrete deliverables — write-ups, PRs, policy proposals.
- Meta's budget-tier Muse Ember undercuts Anthropic's Haiku and OpenAI's Luna on cost-per-task while outperforming both on agentic/business-workflow benchmarks, intensifying a "budget-tier model" price war driven by CFOs pushing back on runaway agentic AI spend.
- The Nvidia/Jensen Huang scandal (affair with a woman US intelligence suspects is a Chinese spy) is raised as a governance/accountability case study: the company's board is self-investigating while Huang remains CEO, and the piece argues this illustrates a gap that could be addressed by independent auditors and mandatory security-risk disclosure for powerful AI companies.
- Broader market signal: AI model costs and capability are commoditizing fast at both the frontier and budget tiers, with vendors competing simultaneously on speed, cost-per-completed-task, and ability to autonomously finish long-running agentic work.

## Quote

> "It works until the job is actually complete instead of stopping at a plausible checkpoint." — Andrew Mager, Uber
