# Working in parallel and meeting every four hours to debate ideas, they used form

## Insights

- Google DeepMind used teams of Gemini 4 agents working in parallel, meeting every four hours in a "virtual conference" to trade, debate, and compare ideas, to optimize quantum algorithms — an explicit multi-agent research workflow, not a single model query.
- Because the resulting quantum circuits are too resource-intensive to test on classical computers or current quantum hardware, correctness was established via formal mathematical proof (the Lean theorem prover) rather than empirical testing — agent output was gated by a verification layer before being trusted.
- Results included an 11.8x reduction in computing cost for an inertial confinement fusion calculation, an 8x cheaper simulation for a light-sensitive molecule, a 2.5x improvement for a drug-metabolizing enzyme (Cytochrome P450), a 2.57x speedup for Grover's algorithm, and a 290x speedup for a common arithmetic routine (arcsin).
- The team frames the human role as shifting to "choosing problems and interpreting results" while the agent-plus-proof-checker pipeline compresses work described as having previously taken "years of manual design."
- DeepMind is open-sourcing the Lean library, problem specifications, and verified solutions, positioning this as a reusable method/infrastructure for agent-driven scientific discovery beyond just quantum computing.
- The paper's author list (23 names) suggests this was still a large human research collaboration layered around the agent system, not a fully autonomous replacement for the team.

## Quote

> Proof-checked AI search compresses work that once took years of manual design, empowering researchers to focus on choosing problems and interpreting results.
