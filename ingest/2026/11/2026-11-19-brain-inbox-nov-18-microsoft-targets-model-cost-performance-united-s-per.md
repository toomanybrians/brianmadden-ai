# Nov. 18 - Microsoft targets model cost, performance | United’s personal AI agent crackdown tests businesses

## Insights

- United Airlines has banned personal AI agents from completing transactions on its platform, a move framed as testing how businesses balance customer data protection, transaction control, and the risk of losing sales to AI-driven intermediary platforms.
- Microsoft expanded its in-house MAI model line and introduced new model-routing capabilities aimed at competing on both cost and performance against other model providers.
- Capgemini is flagging that cybersecurity risks are rising in step with the proliferation of enterprise AI agents, and is urging organizations to implement controls as adoption accelerates heading into 2027.
- A KubeCon 2026 analysis (Omdia) identified governance, isolation, and cost as the three dominant requirements enterprises face when running AI agents in production.
- Separate commentary argues that enterprise processes were designed for a "pre-AI world" and that leading enterprises must re-engineer tasks, roles, and operations wholesale rather than bolt AI onto existing workflows.
- Other noted threads: AWS is positioning its non-relational database portfolio as core infrastructure underpinning AI agents, and a pointed open question is circulating about who within the enterprise should actually hold AI leadership authority.
