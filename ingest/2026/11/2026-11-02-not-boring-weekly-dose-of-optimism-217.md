# Weekly Dose of Optimism #217

## Insights

- OpenAI reports its models produced candidate proofs/results across three fields (a generalized local smoothing conjecture in math, a disproof of the Exponential Time Hypothesis in complexity theory, and existence proofs for interacting quantum field theories in physics), a shift from pure problem-solving toward tackling long-standing open conjectures.
- Rather than publishing unverified claims (as happened with earlier GPT-5 claims about Erdős problems that turned out to rehash existing literature), OpenAI is routing outputs through unpaid expert "shepherds" who verify, contextualize, and control publication timing — an emerging human-in-the-loop model for validating AI-generated research.
- Lean (formal proof-checking software) is becoming critical infrastructure for verifying machine-scale mathematical output; a separate effort (Axiom) completed a full Lean formalization of a 15,000-page proof no human has ever checked in its entirety, suggesting verification tooling is becoming a bottleneck/enabler for AI-generated science.
- Anthropic's Claude is being embedded across the full drug-development pipeline (not just molecule discovery) — generating ~35,000 antibody designs, redesigning therapy components, and critically, automating back-office knowledge work: reviewing 1,061 pages of manufacturing records (catching issues human reviewers missed) and cutting review time by 89%, plus drafting a regulatory document in 4 hours versus a typical 3 months.
- The Anthropic drug-development piece explicitly frames the opportunity as needing to accelerate *every* stage of a multi-step process simultaneously, since a single unimproved bottleneck stage limits the whole pipeline — a systemic/process view of where AI delivers value in complex workflows.
- Separately, Neuralink's BCI reported one ALS patient reaching 75 words/minute speech output (vs. 62 wpm in a 2023 benchmark), restoring a form of expressive capability lost to disease, with the device also reconstructing the patient's pre-illness voice.
