# 🔮 What does it cost to make a worker?

## Insights

- Proposes a framework for comparing the economics of human vs. AI labor: instead of comparing per-task costs, treat the worker as an entity and estimate the minimum cost of "creating" one.
- For humans, that creation cost includes the full cycle of parenting, education, and sustenance from infancy to working adulthood — a long, uncertain process with a delayed and unpredictable payoff.
- For AI, the equivalent cost is manufacturing "intelligence" — framed as fundamentally different from human intelligence but functionally comparable for economic purposes.
- Argues AI's manufacturing process is highly replicable and follows a classic learning curve (cost declines predictably with scale/repetition), unlike human development.
- Flags this as an early, unfinished framework (explicitly a "sketchbook" entry) that will be expanded in the author's forthcoming second book.
