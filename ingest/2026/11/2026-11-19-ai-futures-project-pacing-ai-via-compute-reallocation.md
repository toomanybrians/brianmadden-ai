# Pacing AI via Compute Reallocation

## Insights

- Proposes "Verified Compute Allocation Disclosures" (VCAD): AI companies log all compute usage, classify it as capabilities vs. non-capabilities R&D, and publish periodic disclosures verified by independent auditors.
- Estimates that capping capabilities-R&D compute at 15% starting January 2027 would delay the "intelligence explosion" by roughly a year — framed as insufficient alone but a bridge to a more durable governance regime.
- Models a one-time 3x compute reduction (e.g., 20% cap plus 6% cheating error) as slowing near-term AI progress by ~1.5-2x, but argues the effect fades over time since growth rate converges back to the unconstrained trajectory — it mainly delays milestones rather than permanently slowing pace.
- Distinguishes "baseline auditing" (financial checks, spot-checks, feasible today, sufficient against passive non-compliance) from "robust auditing" (KYC on external users, AI classifiers, auditor-managed logging) needed to keep error near 6% even against a leadership-backed conspiracy to cheat.
- Argues restricting capabilities inputs without also limiting algorithmic progress risks a dangerous "algorithmic overhang" — built-up unused algorithmic gains that could cause a sudden capability jump once limits lift, or leak to competitors/China given weak security.
- On geopolitics: argues the US could pace AI development while retaining a lead over China, partly because poor security already means China can steal IP regardless, and slowing down could buy time to improve security or negotiate a mutual agreement.
- Frames the proposal as needing voluntary adoption first (by OpenAI, Anthropic, etc.) to build trust and public benchmarks, with domestic/international regulation as a longer-term escalation path.

## Quote

> Timelines to recursive self-improvement (RSI) are most likely short... AIs will fully automate the process of AI R&D before EOY 2027.
