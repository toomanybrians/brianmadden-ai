# As I argued in my new book, automation has a playbook and a long history, augmen

## Insights

- Distinguishes two paths for AI-driven change: automation (replacing human tasks, has an established "playbook" and long history) versus augmentation (keeping humans central, requires experimentation and is harder to execute).
- Warns that organizations may default to automation because it's the easier, well-trodden path, rather than doing the harder work of augmentation.
- Expresses concern that as AI capabilities improve, there will be a tendency to retreat from human-centered work design rather than actively redesigning tasks and UX to keep humans central.
- Notes this human-centering strategy only makes sense "as long as AI stays jagged" (i.e., uneven in capability) — implying the strategy's validity is contingent on AI's current uneven skill profile.
- Frames the stakes starkly: without deliberate effort, the remaining human roles risk being reduced to low-value residual tasks like "greet customers" and "keep carts clean and tidy."

## Quote

> We don't want a world where the top human tasks are to "greet customers" & "keep carts clean and tidy." — @emollick
