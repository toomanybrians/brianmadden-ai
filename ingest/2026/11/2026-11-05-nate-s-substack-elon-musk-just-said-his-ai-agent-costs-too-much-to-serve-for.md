# Elon Musk just said his AI agent costs too much to serve for what it earns. If your AI bill goes up next, here's how to know which jobs are worth paying for.

## Insights

- Musk shut off a Grok Bot customer's unlimited usage early, stating the cost of serving the agent was "unsustainably high relative to revenue" — even though xAI's own procurement write-up credited the same bot with finding $14,220 in savings from unused software seats.
- This illustrates that cost to supply an AI agent, price charged for it, and value it generates are three distinct, independent numbers — an agent can be valuable to the user while being unprofitable for the provider at current pricing.
- The piece argues agents let individuals personally direct work that previously required assembling a team, shifting decisions about what work to fund, how much effort it deserves, and whether results justified the expense down to the individual level.
- This creates a double-edged outcome: people can now investigate previously-impractical ideas and surface problems earlier, or alternatively overspend generating unneeded work and lose time reviewing it.
- The article frames this as a emerging practical skill: individuals need frameworks to value their own time (e.g., testing decisions against an hourly rate range) and to budget experiments against uncertain payoff.

## Quote

> The cost of serving Grok Bot was unsustainably high relative to revenue.
