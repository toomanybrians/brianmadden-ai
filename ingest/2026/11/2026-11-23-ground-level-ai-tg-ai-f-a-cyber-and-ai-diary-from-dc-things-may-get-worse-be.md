# TG-AI-F: A cyber and AI diary from DC — things may get worse before they get better

## Insights

- CoreWeave's CISO described the cybersecurity threat facing AI infrastructure as a "zombie apocalypse," and argued recent high-profile tech outages may be self-inflicted — companies rushing to deploy AI and patch systems under management pressure, causing errors rather than being caused by attacks.
- He also flagged risks of putting critical AI workloads in shared data centers, plus physical threats like drone attacks on data centers.
- A gap exists between frontier AI labs' cybersecurity initiatives and the critical-infrastructure operators who need them: Anthropic's Project Glasswing reportedly launched without any electric utilities or grid equipment manufacturers as participants, according to an American Electric Power security exec.
- Smaller critical infrastructure operators (water utilities, rural telecoms) often lack dedicated cybersecurity staff or expertise — one example cited: a hometown water utility's four-person team had no one responsible for cybersecurity.
- AI is described as lowering the barrier for finding and exploiting vulnerabilities, intensifying pressure on under-resourced infrastructure operators.
- Despite the warnings, the piece notes a "surprising amount of optimism" among DC policy/security circles about how AI-cybersecurity risks will ultimately play out, though specifics aren't detailed in this excerpt.

## Quote

> I don't think that we're under a massive cyberattack at the moment. But I think we're doing it to ourselves. — James Higgins, CISO, CoreWeave
