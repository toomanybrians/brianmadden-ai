# Nov. 20 - AI agents can move money — who’s really in control? | Power isn’t the only hurdle for AI data centers

## Insights

- AI agents are now executing financial transactions, raising a governance gap: a transaction can pass technical/security checks yet still violate business policy, meaning CIOs need policy-layer controls, not just technical authorization checks.
- Data center developers are making commitments to utilities and communities (beyond power supply itself); the real test cited is whether those promises actually change operational practices, not just secure capacity.
- Microsoft introduced "Copilotforce" and deeper Office integration within Copilot at Ignite, adding more dimensions (cost, value, output quality) for CIOs to evaluate before adoption.
- A case study highlights German energy provider Uniper using hybrid cloud to deploy agentic AI for procurement while concurrently migrating to SAP S/4HANA — an example of running new AI capability atop legacy ERP transition.
- Separate reporting flags a recent Binance hack as a cautionary case, with implied lessons for CIOs on security practices before year-end.
- Industry commentary (via linked "top stories") notes a push among tech leaders for enterprises to sharpen focus on AI's demonstrable business value heading into 2027, including attention to hidden costs and ownership of AI initiatives.
