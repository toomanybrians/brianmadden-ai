# ⚙️ The cost of keeping the internet human

## Insights

- Tools for Humanity's World ID uses iris-scanning orbs to generate a "Proof of Human" credential, with 18M users verified across 100+ physical orb locations since launch three years ago.
- The system claims privacy via anonymized multi-party computation (AMPC): iris data is converted to a code, split into fragments stored across independent institutions (e.g., UC Berkeley, KAIST), which never recombine; users hold their own photos locally rather than the company storing them centrally — a contrast drawn explicitly with CLEAR's server-stored biometric model.
- After initial verification, users prove humanity via zero-knowledge proofs (confirming membership in the verified set without revealing which specific ID is theirs), rather than re-sharing biometric data.
- An outside cybersecurity expert cautions that entropy claims (iris ~200+ bits vs. fingerprint ~8.3-char password equivalent) can be misleading since biometric traits come from overlapping human variation rather than true randomness, and that ZKP/AMPC are promising but not "magic" — real-world security depends on system operation.
- Enterprise/consumer integrations already exist or are planned: Tinder human badges, concert ticket anti-scalping access, Zoom's "Deep Face" live selfie-matching to flag deepfake impersonation in meetings, plus modular "Proof of Age," "Proof of Attributes," and "Proof of Presence" products being marketed to banking/financial services for deepfake-fraud mitigation; a speculative use case includes passport-free identity verification (e.g., hotel check-in, airport security) via zero-knowledge proofs of government-issued credentials.
- The piece notes the structural irony that Sam Altman co-founded both Tools for Humanity (the human-verification solution) and OpenAI (a maker of the generative tools driving fraud/impersonation risk), raising the question of AI industry responsibility for harms its own tools enable — citing a Deloitte estimate of AI-driven fraud losses reaching $40B by 2027, up from $12.3B in 2023.
