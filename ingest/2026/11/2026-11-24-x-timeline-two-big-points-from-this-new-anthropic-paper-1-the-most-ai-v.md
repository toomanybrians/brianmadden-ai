# Two big points from this new Anthropic paper:

## Insights

- Across 66 interviews at 58 companies, the highest-value AI applications were strongly correlated with significant organizational change (merged/cut roles, altered reporting lines, changed processes) — light-touch AI use that fit into existing structures tended to be rated lower-value by employees themselves.
- Most AI value came from increasing output (accelerating work, improving quality, or enabling new products/services) rather than from cost-cutting; only about one-tenth of use cases delivered value directly captured by employees as reduced workload or more enjoyable work.
- Layoffs directly caused by AI automation were rare (only two companies reported a position fully eliminated by automation), but two-thirds of companies reported reduced hiring, and nine companies cut contracted/offshore roles (customer service, legal, dev work) as AI substituted for modular, outsourced tasks.
- Worker resistance to AI was a notable bottleneck — at five companies, employees who wouldn't adopt AI were pushed out, and performance gaps between AI users and resisters created visible organizational tension and manager anxiety.
- Three adoption strategies emerged: top-down automation (efficiency but risk of job displacement/distrust), bottom-up experimentation (preserves jobs but adoption is uneven and gains are capped), and deliberate reorganization around AI (broadening roles, redeploying staff to AI's "bottleneck" tasks) — the last associated with the highest reported value.
- Case examples illustrate the contrast: one firm automated a mailroom workflow outright (job-displacing), another struggled with uneven voluntary adoption, and a third redesigned associate/senior-attorney review workflows to use AI without eliminating jobs.
