# Weekly Dose of Optimism #220

## Insights

- SpaceX caught an orbital Starship upper stage (not just the booster) for the first time, which the piece frames as the "final boss" of full rapid reusability — projected to cut per-launch marginal cost from a ~$30-40M floor to roughly $2-10M, or $20-100/kg vs. Falcon 9's ~$1,000/kg.
- SpaceX's stated cadence targets: ~2 Starship launches/week by end of 2027, hundreds/year by 2029, multiple per day beyond that — intended to carry satellites, data centers, and eventually drug factories into orbit.
- A Phase 3 trial of an off-the-shelf leukemia vaccine (GPS) showed a 41% reduction in death risk for relapsed AML patients (24.0 vs. 14.7 months median survival); unlike a prior personalized mRNA melanoma vaccine, this one isn't custom-built per patient, making it potentially faster/cheaper to scale.
- Earth AI's "Alpheus" AI model re-analyzed decades-old exploration data and identified a 3km+ (possibly 8km) continuous copper-gold-platinum mineral system that earlier human explorers had misread as isolated, uneconomical pockets — an example of AI reinterpreting existing datasets to find missed value.
- Palmer Luckey disclosed SLAB, a privately-funded, decade-old seasteading project (1,000-year design life, ~$1B valuation) meant to host heavy industry (metal processing, desalination, refining, medical research) outside conventional land-based regulation.
- Tesla announced a 7.4MW solid-state transformer ("Powercore"), framed as another piece of vertically-integrated infrastructure stack-building, with commentary that design leadership without in-house manufacturing is becoming strategically incoherent as capability stacks (energy, compute) tighten.

## Quote

> "The final boss is dead."
