# Being a pain in the ass to (or in) AI companies is a real job now

## Insights

- Anthropic privately drafted federal AI legislation that would preempt state AI safety laws (including California, Illinois, New York) and centralize the power to halt dangerous model training in a single path: the Commerce Secretary petitioning one DC federal court, with a 45-day expiration on stop orders and no deference to the regulator — a structure described as making intervention very hard even for catastrophic/extinction-level risk.
- OpenAI fired three safety researchers (Tomek Korbak, Mikita Balesni, Jasmine Wang) amid a leak investigation tied to reporting on a "looped transformers" technique in its Astra model — a technique criticized industry-wide for reducing chain-of-thought monitorability; evidence tying the firings to the actual leak was reportedly weak to nonexistent, and the episode is framed as damaging trust in whether safety-focused staff can speak candidly internally or externally.
- Google DeepMind UK employment contracts reportedly contain lifelong anti-criticism clauses barring former staff from publicly criticizing the company at all, surfaced via BBC reporting connected to staff objections over AI sales to the US Department of War — distinguished from OpenAI's earlier equity-forfeiture non-disparagement scandal by timing (start vs. end of employment) and severity of consequence.
- The piece catalogs an ecosystem of accountability mechanisms already showing results: insider dissent via public Slack channels, whistleblower support orgs (AI Whistleblower Initiative, Psst.org), investigative journalism (Tarbell Fellowship, Nightingale Collective, Midas Project), legal/policy analysis groups (Legal Advocates for Safe Science and Technology, Guidelight AI Standards), and public advocacy campaigns (Encode AI, PauseAI/ControlAI) — citing concrete wins like the "Not For Private Gain" campaign reportedly shifting ~$75B in OpenAI nonprofit stake value and locking in Safety and Security Committee override powers.
- Argues that AI company insiders have outsized but time-limited leverage, since their own roles may be automated within a few years, making this a case for urgency in using internal voice now rather than later.
- Notes companies also take credit-worthy safety actions in parallel (e.g., Anthropic/OpenAI compute grants to an AI Biosecurity Lab, Anthropic publishing red-team results on safety monitors, OpenAI pausing training for two weeks after a containment incident), framing company behavior as consistently mixed rather than uniformly adversarial.

## Quote

> The single highest-stakes issue in AI regulation is who gets to stop AI companies engaging in really reckless R&D.
