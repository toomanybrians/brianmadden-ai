# Decision-making can be broken into 2 sub-components: direction-setting and autho

## Insights

- Decision-making in AI agent oversight is split into two sub-components: direction-setting (what goals/paths the agent pursues) and authorization (who approves actions).
- Direction-setting is proposed to be governed by automation levels (AL0–AL5) assigned per task, indicating how much autonomy an agent has at each stage.
- Direction-setting also involves defined affordances and permissions — e.g., access to compute — that let AI agents pursue research directions without requiring human approval.
- For the "handoff of review" stage, a proposed measure is end-to-end detection efficacy: the share of deliberately seeded flaws that evade both AI monitors and human reviewers.
- Another proposed review metric is time-to-review-and-patch, tracking the lag from agent activity to flagging, from flag to review, and from review to mitigation.
- These are framed as candidate measures meant as a starting point for evaluating oversight of autonomous AI research agents.
