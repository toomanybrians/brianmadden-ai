# RT @w01fe: I really like @EvanHub 's new pacing proposal here https://t.co/sglqF

## Insights

- The author retracts prior support for compute-reallocation-based pacing of frontier AI development, proposing instead that regulators get blocking authority over "internal deployments" of new frontier models (i.e., a lab's own internal use of a model, not just public release).
- Proposed mechanism: labs submit a review/report on a new internal deployment, a mandatory short waiting period applies, and a regulator can extend the block if concerned — potentially requiring court approval for longer blocks.
- Internal deployment blocking is framed as easier to explain to policymakers/public than compute-based metrics, better precedented (similar to existing external-deployment review regimes), and more effective at interrupting recursive self-improvement (RSI) loops where new models accelerate AI R&D.
- The approach is pitched as flexible: it can support "output-based" pacing (via safety case review) or "input-based" pacing (via capability thresholds), and scales with political will — at the extreme becoming an outright ban on using frontier models for AI R&D.
- Acknowledged downside: blocking internal deployment could create an "algorithmic progress overhang" (accumulated unexploited capability gains), raising security/theft risk, though the author argues this is partially mitigated since blocking also slows the R&D-acceleration feedback loop itself.
- A concrete phased rollout is suggested (credited to Sam Marks): regulators start with uniform deployment delays industry-wide, then move to case-by-case delays based on assessed danger/capability as their technical understanding matures.

## Quote

> Internal deployment blocking is very straightforward and reasonable-sounding to policy-makers and the general public.
