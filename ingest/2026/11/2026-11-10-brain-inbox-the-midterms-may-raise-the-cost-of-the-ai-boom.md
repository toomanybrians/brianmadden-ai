# ⚙️ The midterms may raise the cost of the AI boom

## Insights

- Post-midterm backlash against AI data centers has triggered real financial consequences: data center/semiconductor stocks (IREN, AMD, Nvidia, CoreWeave, Core Scientific) dropped sharply, and Microsoft is reportedly pulling back on multi-billion-dollar neocloud deals (Nebius, IREN, Nscale) citing cost and regulatory uncertainty.
- An industry analyst (Valence Howden, Infotech) frames this as "a slowdown with higher costs" rather than a retreat — companies will likely shift projects to friendlier states/municipalities, absorb more costs, and make more concessions, but won't stop building.
- OpenAI responded preemptively with a public pledge on responsible data center practices (no NDAs, resident input rights, paying own electricity costs) plus a policy blueprint recommending mandated environmental reporting and tax-incentive transparency — a signal that AI labs anticipate sustained regulatory pressure.
- The piece speculates that rising data center costs from regulation could increase pressure on AI companies to extract more revenue from frontier models.
- Visa's new Agentic Commerce Benchmark found AI shopping agents still fail often (57.2% pass rate across 22 models/12 labs on 15 shopping tasks), with 54% of failed runs still resulting in an unwanted purchase — suggesting agents struggle to know when *not* to buy.
- Visa's separate Trust Index found 72% of US consumers use AI assistants but only 23% trust AI to make payments on their behalf, suggesting the agentic commerce bottleneck may be more about consumer trust than model capability.
- In a GitHub Universe interview, GitHub's CPO frames AI-driven coding democratization as "lower the floor, raise the ceiling, and widen the walls" — arguing that easier entry for non-technical builders can coexist with professional developer growth and stronger open source, while infrastructure/security/trust become the harder bottleneck once code generation itself is cheap.

## Quote

> "This looks more like a slowdown with higher costs than a wholesale retreat from infrastructure investment." — Valence Howden, Infotech
