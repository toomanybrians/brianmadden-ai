# 13 Videos from CVAI: Igor Babuschkin, Ryan Daniels, Guillermo Rauch & More

## Insights

- Multiple enterprise leaders (Vercel, Databricks) described a shift away from relying on a single "frontier" model toward mixing small, large, fine-tuned, and open-weight models to cut costs — Databricks' router reportedly directs over four quadrillion tokens across providers, sending simpler requests to cheaper models.
- Open-weight model adoption is reportedly accelerating fast inside enterprise workflows: Factory's CEO said open models went from near-zero at the start of 2026 to over 10% of tokens running through the platform, with some customers using them more than half the time.
- River AI (founded by Igor Babuschkin) is positioning itself around decentralizing AI capability — letting businesses and eventually individuals own and post-train their own models on their own hardware, arguing concentration in a few labs is the industry's biggest risk and that many moderately capable models is a safer distribution of power than a few dominant ones.
- Mercor's CEO said the type of human labor needed to train AI has shifted from large pools of workers writing clean text for chatbots to specialized experts (engineers, lawyers, doctors, bankers) designing evaluations and RL environments — and that this market is capped by how many tasks humans still outperform models on.
- Crosby, described as an "agentic law firm," is selling legal AI directly to non-lawyer consumers and structuring itself around eliminating the billable hour by hiring lawyers willing to hand work to AI agents rather than acquiring existing law firms.
- Etched's co-founder predicted the largest growth in white-collar work in history will be driven by agentic workers rather than people, while expecting highly capable agents to commoditize chip design even as physical integration problems (racks, interconnects) remain hard.
