# The interesting thing about AI agents is that as a theoretical matter, their inf

## Insights

- Argues AI agent inference is unusually latency-insensitive compared to other computing workloads, meaning agent compute doesn't need to run physically close to the user.
- Suggests this property is what enables "long-running agents" to become a normal pattern — agents that work over extended time rather than requiring instant round-trip responses.
- Implicitly connects this to data center geography: if AI inference isn't latency-bound, it can be sited wherever capacity/energy/cost is most favorable, not necessarily near end users.
- Frames this as a structural advantage/exception within tech broadly — most technology is latency-sensitive to users, AI is one of the exceptions.
- Quoted companion post (via @SaraEisen, citing ARM's CEO) adds a geopolitical angle: other countries are eager to build the data centers the U.S. may be hesitant about.

## Quote

> AI is one of the few areas of technology that isn't *as* sensitive to latency back to the user.
