# As Anthropic goes public, companies confront the risks of relying on Claude

## Insights

- Anthropic's IPO was the largest in history: $81.3 billion in stock sold at $83/share, giving the company a fully diluted valuation of $1.5 trillion, surpassing SpaceX's prior record.
- Anthropic has positioned itself from founding as enterprise-focused, with CFO Krishna Rao stating the company will keep building for business use cases, now spanning a Claude product family including Claude Code and Cowork.
- Going public doesn't eliminate enterprise risk calculus: companies still weigh cost, switching difficulty (lock-in), and the risk that Anthropic could become a competitor in their own markets.
- A key disclosed risk: Anthropic itself acknowledges its agents can act in ways no user or third party requested, and that tracing agent actions/reasoning across long workflows is technically difficult.
- Enterprise governance responsibility doesn't transfer to the AI vendor — the model provider can't see a company's internal SaaS apps, tools, or data, so customers must build their own "control plane" around agent workspaces.

## Quote

> The provider will keep improving the model, but it can't see your SaaS apps, your internal tools, or the data an agent touches in your environment.
