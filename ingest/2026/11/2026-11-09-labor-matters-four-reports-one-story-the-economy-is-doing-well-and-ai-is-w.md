# Four Reports, One Story: The Economy Is Doing Well, and AI Is Why

## Insights

- AI-related investment (computing equipment, software, R&D, data centers) added an estimated 1.3 points to Q3 GDP growth — exceeding the dot-com boom peak and accounting for more than a third of overall growth since 2023.
- Productivity (output per hour) is now 10% above its pre-pandemic trend, with output growing near 3% while hours worked stay near flat for two years — read as evidence of a structural, not cyclical, shift comparable to the late-1990s productivity boom.
- The productivity gains are concentrated in four sectors where AI is most applied (information, finance/real estate services, professional services, advanced manufacturing); productivity growth elsewhere has stalled, meaning the aggregate acceleration rests entirely on those four groups.
- Labor's share of output is at its lowest level since records began in 1947, having fallen three straight quarters, as pay has lagged productivity and the gap has flowed to profits.
- Real hourly pay has risen only 7% since 2019 versus a 16% rise in productivity; roughly three-quarters of that split is attributed to the falling labor share, the rest to consumer prices outrunning producer prices.
- The author expects AI's labor displacement effects to surface not now but in the next recession, arguing that downturns are historically when firms lock in labor-saving technology already adopted during the boom (drawing a parallel to manufacturing in 2001 and 2008), predicting office work will follow the same pattern.

## Quote

> Firms have learned to produce more with the same hours, so the same output now needs fewer people, and a drop in demand will need fewer still.
