# There is a lot of focus on AI-aided discoveries in quantitative fields, but they

## Insights

- Historian Mark Humphries gave GPT-6.2 Astra images of an anonymous 1808 sketchbook (Yale's Beinecke Library) with minimal prompting; in 22 minutes it identified the likely artist as Louis-Guy-Charles-Guillaume de Rohan-Chabot, an obscure French aristocrat and British Army officer — a conclusion no fur trade historian had reached in over 200 years.
- The model's process resembled human historical research (reading sources, forming and testing hypotheses against dated evidence) but surpassed human capability by cross-referencing an enormous number of obscure, unindexed primary sources simultaneously — including a footnote in a 2014 PhD thesis and an untranslated diary entry — that a human researcher would have no logical reason to consult.
- The discovery led to locating and ordering a previously unknown, undiscovered 162-page diary by Chabot documenting his 1808 journey, independently confirmed by matching handwriting, itinerary, and a diary entry explicitly describing "I took a sketch" at a location matching a drawing.
- A second model (Fable 5.2) given the identical prompt reached the same identification of Chabot in about 35 minutes, suggesting this research capability exists across frontier models, not just one — though it failed to find the corroborating diary that Astra located.
- The author notes GPT-6.2 Astra showed a peculiar tendency toward excessive hedging and self-doubt when presenting conclusions in open-ended, non-deterministic research tasks (e.g., calling the diary "attributed to" rather than "written by" Chabot despite strong evidence), speculating this stems from math/proof-focused post-training optimized for deterministic correctness.
- Piece frames this as evidence that AI-driven discovery, previously associated mainly with quantitative/deterministic fields (math proofs, Navier-Stokes, Riemann Hypothesis), is beginning to generalize to open-ended, non-deterministic domains like historical research.
