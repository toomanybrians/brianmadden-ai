# This is ultimately a No True Scotsman argument, similar to those from @GaryMarcu

## Insights

- The author argues that claims "AI isn't intelligence" rely on a No True Scotsman fallacy — redefining "intelligence" narrowly enough that AI can never qualify, similar to how one could argue "flying" requires feathers and flapping.
- Draws an analogy: just as flight turned out to be about the underlying function (moving through air) rather than the specific mechanism (feathers/flapping), intelligence may be about function rather than a specific biological implementation.
- Distinguishes two separate claims often conflated: (1) "AI isn't intelligence" (which the author calls false) versus (2) "AI isn't finished/complete" (which the author calls true) — treating these as very different assertions.
- Characterizes anyone denying current AI qualifies as intelligence as relying on an "extremely out of touch" definition of the term.
- References Gary Marcus and Richard Sutton by name as examples of people making this kind of argument, without detailing their specific positions beyond this framing.

## Quote

> Anyone saying current AI isn't intelligence is using an extremely out of touch definition.
