# ChinAI #383: China’s struggling robotics companies

## Insights

- China's robotics/embodied-AI sector has seen a wave of bankruptcies and operational crises in 2026 (DeepBlue Robotics, Yinghe Robotics among them), with Huxiu compiling data on nearly 50 struggling or exited firms over five years.
- A recurring failure pattern: companies chased whatever category was trending (cleaning, delivery, disinfection, gardening robots) rather than building deep capability in one area, leading to shallow, unexecuted deals — e.g., a widely-touted LG disinfection-robot order that was never fulfilled at scale.
- Some firms reportedly masked technology gaps by quietly substituting human labor when robots failed to complete tasks, folding the labor cost into project budgets rather than disclosing the shortfall.
- Industrial and real-world deployment settings demand cost-effectiveness and reliability that current humanoid/embodied robots often can't meet; per Huxiu's data, ~55.5% of 218 disclosed embodied-intelligence/humanoid robot projects in China (H1 2026) were procured by educational and research institutions rather than commercial end users — suggesting demand is more demo/pilot-driven than market-driven.
- Investor enthusiasm appears to be cooling: Chinese embodied-AI financing fell to 33.3 billion RMB in Q3 2026, down ~29% from the H1 quarterly average.
- Counterpoint noted: IDC data shows real market growth in narrower, practical applications like robotic lawnmowers and pool cleaners, suggesting the failures may reflect normal competitive shakeout rather than sector-wide nonviability.
