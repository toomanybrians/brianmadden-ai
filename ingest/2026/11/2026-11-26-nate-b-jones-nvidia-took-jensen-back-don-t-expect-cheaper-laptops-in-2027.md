## Insights

### Memory and hardware price squeeze
- Memory now makes up 54% of the parts cost of a budget smartphone, up from 20% a year ago, per Counterpoint, cited by Jones.
- Global smartphone shipments fell 20% year-over-year, the lowest Q3 total since 2013; average retail phone price is up 20% so far this year (Counterpoint, via Jones).
- Gartner forecasts a 17% PC price rise for 2026, with Jones predicting further increases in 2027.
- Memory industry revenue hit nearly $200B in Q3 2026, up 369% year-over-year (Counterpoint), driven almost entirely by price, not volume — shipped memory grew only about 4% quarter-over-quarter.
- Contract price for a single 16Gb DDR5 chip rose from $8.70 to $48 in 11 months.
- Jones's mechanism for the squeeze: agentic AI workloads (browsing, tool-calling, overnight code generation) consume general-purpose CPU server memory (DDR5, LPDDR), not just specialized GPU-adjacent high-bandwidth memory, putting cloud providers in direct competition with phone and laptop makers for the same supply.
- Micron reportedly expects memory supply to get tighter relative to demand in both 2027 and 2028, per Jones.

### Power, grid, and physical constraints
- GE Vernova's gas power equipment backlog and reservations nearly doubled in a year, from 62GW to 122GW.
- Berkeley Lab found new power projects often take more than five years from grid-connection request to actually running.
- An industry trade group estimates US construction needs roughly 350,000 additional workers this year to meet data center build-out demand.
- Data Center Watch found roughly 120 US data center projects blocked or delayed amid local opposition in the first half of 2026, with about $200B of planned build-out tied up.

### Demand signals and financing
- OpenRouter weekly token volume rose from roughly 0.5 trillion (Jan 2025) to more than 128 trillion (Sept 2026); agent traffic reportedly overtook human traffic in early February 2026, and by October the platform routed 790 trillion tokens/month (~25x the Jan 2026 volume), about 75% from agentic activity.
- Anthropic's quarterly revenue (per IPO disclosures cited by Jones) went from about $1.38B (Q3 2025) to about $16.96B (Q3 2026) — over 12x in one year.
- Combined backlogs and contracted future revenue at Microsoft, Alphabet, Oracle, and Amazon total roughly $2.5 trillion (includes non-AI business).
- Combined Q3 2026 capital spending across Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft, and Meta topped $200B in a single quarter.
- An unnamed economist's estimate puts the planned US AI build-out through 2032 at more than 3% of GDP/year on average — larger than 1870s-80s US railroad construction (~2.2% of GDP) and the dot-com/telecom fiber boom (~1.1% of GDP).
- Free cash flow went negative in the latest reported quarter at Alphabet, Meta, and Oracle, and over the trailing 12 months at Amazon; Meta paused stock buybacks.
- The Federal Reserve counted more than $680B in debt issued through October 2026 to fund advanced computing (bonds, loans, project financing); Fed Governor Michael Barr said balance-sheet debt at seven of the largest AI companies was on track to exceed $800B by year-end.
- The Fed put those companies' off-balance-sheet commitments and guarantees at over $3 trillion, including more than $1 trillion in leases not yet started plus over $2 trillion in take-or-pay contracts and equipment-value guarantees.
- The European Central Bank warned in November that tech companies' debt issuance in Europe's bond market could squeeze out other borrowers.
- A structural mismatch is flagged: AI data center buildings last decades, but the chips and servers inside can lose most economic value in three to six years, creating duration risk for lenders.
- Epic AI found the price of achieving the same level of model performance fell at least 13-fold in a year.
- A claim (possibly referring to open-weight rather than "OpenAI" models) states models largely from China accounted for more than 70% of OpenRouter's tokens in September 2026, up sharply from a year earlier.
- A Columbia economist's Brookings paper estimates the US AI infrastructure built through 2032 would need roughly $3.7 trillion in annual revenue (~9% of projected US GDP) to earn a 10% return before financing costs, assuming a 50% operating margin — requiring about 80% revenue growth every year for six years from a $100B base.

### Practical implications offered
- Jones advises planning hardware purchases around continued tight memory supply rather than expecting price drops, and extending the life of existing devices.
- He predicts tighter limits on subsidized frontier AI access — usage caps, pricing tiers, more workplace pressure to justify AI spend — and advises using cheaper or open-weight models where sufficient, reserving frontier models for harder tasks.
- He recommends checking how AI vendors and employers are financed before committing, drawing a parallel to 1870s railroad builders who were wiped out despite durable demand because they built on fragile debt.
- He frames AI efficiency — measurable results per gigabyte of memory, per prompt, per dollar of API spend, per agent, per hour of human attention — as the decisive skill for 2027-2028 as brute-force scaling gets more expensive.
