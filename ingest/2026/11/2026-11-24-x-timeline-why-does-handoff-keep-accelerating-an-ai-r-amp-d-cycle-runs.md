# Why does Handoff keep accelerating?

An AI R&amp;D cycle runs only as fast as it

## Insights

- Frames AI R&D automation progress as a pipeline-speed problem: cycle speed is capped by whichever stage remains most manual, so progress is nonlinear — automating the last bottleneck produces disproportionately large speedups ("Handoff" accelerating).
- Argues this creates a compounding dynamic: as automation share increases, each further increment of automation removes an increasingly binding constraint, which is why the pace of change itself keeps increasing rather than staying steady.
- Cites companies reporting that the remaining human-performed steps (not the AI-automated ones) are now the limiting factor in R&D throughput.
- Breaks the AI R&D cycle into three stages with distinct automation maturity: work execution (nearly fully automated), review (automation catching up quickly), and decision-making (least automated but progressing quickly).
- Implies decision-making is the stage most likely to be the next bottleneck to fall, given it's explicitly flagged as moving fast despite being furthest behind.

## Quote

> Companies say the human steps are already the bottleneck.
