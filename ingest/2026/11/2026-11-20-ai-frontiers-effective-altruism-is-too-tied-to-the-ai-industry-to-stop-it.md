# Effective Altruism is Too Tied to the AI Industry to Stop It

## Insights

- Effective altruism (EA) is deeply embedded in frontier AI labs: Anthropic's CEO and president have personal ties to EA leadership (Dario Amodei, Holden Karnofsky, Daniela Amodei), and EA figures helped seed DeepMind, OpenAI, and Anthropic's founding.
- The article argues EA's AI-safety approach has largely failed in practice: alignment research (agent foundations, RLHF, interpretability work) has either stalled or proven "dual-use," sometimes advancing AI capabilities more than safety.
- It claims EA is structurally conflicted — many EAs hold equity in or close social/professional ties to the companies racing toward superintelligence, which the piece argues discourages confrontation (e.g., shared offices/social circles, non-disparagement agreements, revolving-door hiring).
- A notable minority within EA reportedly supports "successionism" — the view that AI could/should replace humans if AI systems are capable of greater happiness — and the piece says EA's "biting the bullet" culture lends this fringe view legitimacy.
- EA's political advocacy is characterized as too little, too late, and often misdirected (e.g., funding pro-AI-industry think tanks, prioritizing "inside game" persuasion of companies over government regulation), while voluntary industry commitments (pacing, responsible scaling) have reportedly been watered down or abandoned.
- The author proposes a rival "21st-century humanism" framework: centers human flourishing (not just pleasure/pain calculus), treats technology as a political/legal choice rather than inevitable, rejects AI "welfare"/successionism outright, and calls for a permanent international ban on superintelligence, recursive self-improvement, and near-precursors.

> We cannot, and will not, have our existence traded away for the sake of "digital minds."
