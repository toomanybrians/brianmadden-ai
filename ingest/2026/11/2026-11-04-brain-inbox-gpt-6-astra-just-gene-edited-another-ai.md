# 🧬 GPT-6 Astra Just Gene-Edited Another AI

## Insights

- MIT researchers had GPT-6 Astra directly edit another model's weights (no gradient descent/training), changing just 0.016% of Qwen3.5-0.8B's parameters to fix a repetition loop, raising its MATH-500 score from 7.9% to 48.1% — framed as a step toward "recursive self-improvement."
- The same model also wrote small game-playing neural networks from scratch, outperforming networks trained with hundreds of millions of steps of conventional reinforcement learning — suggesting frontier models may out-write human-designed training pipelines in narrow domains.
- Anthropic's amended IPO filing warns investors that future (not current) models may soon trigger its internal Responsible Scaling Policy threshold for "dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI," and that past such speedups were only recognized after the fact — prompting consideration of mandatory human review of AI-driven research decisions.
- OpenAI's new LifeVal benchmark tests models on real-life tasks (caregiving, finances, government services); scores jumped from ~49% (GPT-5.2) to ~83% (GPT-5.6), with the newer GPT-6 Astra scoring slightly lower (82%) but faster — while GPT-5.6 delivers similar results at roughly half the cost, raising a cost-vs-capability tradeoff question for deployment.
- Chip stocks have lost $3.7 trillion in value since October 6 amid investor doubts about how quickly AI infrastructure spending converts to profit, even as broader markets rose — signaling growing skepticism about AI capex economics.
- US humanoid robotics projects (Nvidia's reference design, Meta's prototype) rely heavily on Chinese-made robotic hands and manufacturing partners, despite US leadership in chips and AI software — hands remain the hardest unsolved physical component.
