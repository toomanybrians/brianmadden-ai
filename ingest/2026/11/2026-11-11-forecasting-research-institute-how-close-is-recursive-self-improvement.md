# How Close Is Recursive Self-Improvement?

## Insights

- AI companies report rapidly growing AI contribution to AI R&D itself: Anthropic says Claude wrote over 90% of codebase contributions in September 2026; OpenAI reports AI agents did 3.1 workdays of work per human workday in August 2026, up from ~0.5 in May 2026.
- Despite this, humans still retain most research judgment — Anthropic states no measured part of its AI R&D is fully autonomous, and performance gaps persist in AI "choosing goals" in engineering and research.
- AI agents excel at well-defined ML benchmarks but struggle with open-ended research: in one evaluation, AI-written papers reinventing unpublished research were rejected by the original authors in all four cases tested, even with newer models and more resources.
- Public capability metrics (Epoch Capabilities Index) show no clear acceleration yet (~14 points/year since Sept 2024), but internal company assessments suggest a potential speedup — METR estimated ~1.5–2x capability acceleration at Anthropic attributable to AI, with a later report raising this to ~2x with 80% attributed to AI.
- The report frames full "recursive self-improvement" as requiring three contested steps — automation of AI R&D, sharp acceleration of capability progress, and breadth of gains beyond AI R&D — with expert forecasts for full automation ranging from 2027 to 2052.
- An ensemble of frontier LLMs forecasts ~40% chance of a "fast year" (capability frontier rising 30+ ECI points in 12 months) by 2030; conditional on that happening, their forecast for AI catastrophe by 2050 (>10% of people dying from an AI-caused event) rises from 2.9% to 8.4%, and expert AGI likelihood rises from 55.7% to 84.3%.

## Quote

> Large performance gaps persist when it comes to Claude exercising judgement in choosing goals in both engineering and research.
