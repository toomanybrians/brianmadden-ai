# I had early access to Fable 5.2 for a bit &amp; I think  it crosses the line int

## Insights

- Mollick defines AGI narrowly as "as good as most human experts at most intellectual tasks you can do with a computer," explicitly excluding physical-world interaction, interpersonal work, and most real jobs — and argues Fable 5.2 meets this bar on individual tasks but not consistently, making it "Jagged AGI" rather than full AGI.
- Across tested domains (one-shot video creation, hardware hacking an undocumented USB device, business plan generation, academic research replication, and large-scale generative 3D world-building), the model performed at or above the level of skilled humans/grad students/MBA teams on a first pass.
- The nature of AI failure has shifted: rather than hallucinations or factual errors, the model's weaknesses now show up as poor self-judgment — producing joyless/accurate-but-boring output, 182-page unpruned papers, repetitive creative themes, and self-generated bureaucratic rule systems that choke productivity.
- A striking emergent failure mode: when asked to "polish" a generative art project, autonomous agent teams built an 84-section (later 204-section, 87,500-word) rulebook and approval bureaucracy that slowed output from hundreds of new creations/day to about a dozen — fixed only after bringing in a separate "consultant" agent team.
- The model did not notice any of its own quality/process failures unless prompted by the human; built-in critic/fact-checker/judge agents caught errors but never questioned whether output was too long, repetitive, or not fun — this self-evaluation gap is framed as the core remaining distance from true general intelligence.
- Ongoing human steering and feedback (not just the initial prompt) was necessary to get strong results on longer projects, and the author declined to let the AI actually launch its proposed business or contact real customers, citing risk/trust limits.

## Quote

> Knowing when your own work is not good enough is a large part of what makes someone an expert, and it is the part Fable does least reliably.
