# Investing in Boltz

## Insights

- Boltz frames AI molecular models as a new kind of "scientific reagent" — intelligence that scientists buy and consume per-experiment via a usage-based "molecular tokens" pricing model, analogous to budgeting for sequencing reads or assay reagents.
- The company rejects the dominant industry pattern of AI drug-discovery labs building proprietary pipelines or bespoke pharma partnerships, instead aiming for broad, self-serve access so any scientist can use frontier models from the start of a discovery campaign.
- Product delivery spans a GUI (Boltz Lab) for bench scientists and an API for programmatic/agentic use, including integration with agentic coding harnesses (Claude Code, Codex) that can autonomously set up jobs, call models, and iterate on results.
- Reported usage growth is steep: weekly molecular token consumption rose roughly 50x in four months following the launch of BoltzMol-1 and BoltzProt-1, cited as validation of the consumption-based model.
- Mirrors a pattern seen in other AI domains (e.g., agents on math problems): giving models more time/compute for exploration improves output quality, incentivizing scientists to invest in deeper computational search before physical lab work.
- Originated as an open-source research project at MIT that reportedly reached all top-20 pharmaceutical companies before formal commercialization; now has paid deployments at Pfizer, GSK, Takeda, and BMS, with $110M just raised in a Series A.

## Quote

> AI is becoming a new kind of scientific reagent: intelligence scientists can buy and consume by the experiment.
