# No Shirt, No Shoes, No Service for Bots

## Insights

- Bot traffic has surpassed half of global web activity (51% in 2024, 54% in 2026 per one source, 60.6% per Cloudflare), with AI agent requests specifically up 16.3x from Feb to Oct 2026, 8x in just Aug–Oct.
- Major consumer platforms are actively blocking AI shopping agents rather than accommodating them: United Airlines now bans bots from booking/modifying tickets and can cancel bot-purchased reservations even if a human supervised the transaction; Amazon has blocked third-party shopping agents (e.g., Perplexity's Comet) via robots.txt changes and cease-and-desist notices.
- The stated rationale is security, but the underlying economic driver is that many consumer businesses run on a "loss leader" model — the base product/service breaks even or loses money, and actual profit comes from upsells, ads, fees, or warranties that AI agents are disinclined to accept (agents skip sponsored listings, decline upgrades, uncheck add-on insurance).
- Concrete financial breakdowns: Amazon's entire non-AWS operating profit effectively comes from advertising revenue; United's ancillary fees (seat/bag upgrades, credit card/loyalty, insurance commissions) exceed its total operating income; Dell's hardware is sold near/below cost while ProSupport warranties generate most of its operating profit.
- Shopify is the counter-example: because it takes a flat percentage (~3.1%) of transaction volume regardless of who buys or what they buy, it has no incentive to resist agents and has opened checkout to browser-based AI agents, reporting faster/cheaper completion rates than standard browser automation.
- The piece frames this as a forced reckoning for "loss-leader" business models: companies built on upsell-dependent profit must choose to raise base prices, add explicit agent-checkout fees, or redesign incentives — otherwise they'll keep excluding bots from transactions.
