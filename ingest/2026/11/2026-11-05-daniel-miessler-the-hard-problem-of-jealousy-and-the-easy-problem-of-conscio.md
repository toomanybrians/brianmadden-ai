# The Hard Problem of Jealousy and the Easy Problem of Consciousness

## Insights

- Miessler argues the "hard problem of consciousness" isn't actually different in kind from other evolved sensations (jealousy, fear, hunger) — all are evolution's way of driving behavior, so if jealousy doesn't need a special explanation, neither does subjective experience.
- He frames consciousness as a "container" sensation that holds other sensations, with a nested "sensation of authorship" inside it that gives rise to praise, blame, and felt responsibility.
- Central claim: subjective experience plus the authorship/responsibility bundle functions as a "creativity exponent" — felt stakes (mortality, legacy, praise/blame) are what push organisms (and people) to strive and create at a high level.
- He proposes this is empirically testable: if AI systems with no felt stakes (no sense of authorship, no internal praise/blame, no legacy concern) eventually produce paradigm-shifting creative work at elite human level, his exponent theory is falsified.
- His prediction is that AI lacking felt stakes will fall short of that level of creative achievement, because "cold" computation of stakes won't generate the same drive as lived/felt stakes do.
- Piece also addresses the "zombie" thought experiment (a being with identical function but no inner experience), arguing that if something truly replicated all the same functional/experiential components, it would simply be conscious — the zombie scenario is dismissed as incoherent.

## Quote

> If AI systems with no felt stakes end up doing paradigm-shifting creative work at the level of the best humans, then I'm wrong about the exponent.
