# Future 50 is back

## Insights

- Of the prior two years' 100 Future 50 awardees, 63 have raised further funding and 6 were acquired, with 17 now valued above $1 billion (including two decacorns) — suggesting the selection methodology (VC-partner nominations below a $200M valuation threshold) has had meaningful hit-rate for identifying future breakouts.
- More than two-thirds of this year's cohort were founded in 2024 or later — over twice the share in the inaugural 2024 class — indicating a compressing timeline from company founding to meaningful enterprise/government/frontier-lab traction.
- Team sizes are shrinking: nearly half of this year's awardees have 20 or fewer employees, up from 37% in 2025 and 24% in 2024, offered as evidence that AI tooling is giving small teams outsized leverage.
- Geographic concentration in the US is intensifying (Silicon Valley still leads, NYC a close second but shrinking, LA also shrinking) while "Rest of World" has sharply contracted; Europe grew but still trails the US.
- Sectoral mix is now dominated by AI with some defense/deeptech, while fintech and crypto — previously leading categories — have collapsed to a minor share (just one crypto company this year).
- The Generalist is launching a companion list, "Generational 25," for later-stage companies (≤$5B valuation) with "generational potential," reflecting a view that ceilings for tech companies are higher than before and multi-billion-dollar firms may still be early-stage.

## Quote

> Companies are increasingly capable of jumping from idea to meaningful traction.
