# Monopoly Round-Up: The AI Bubble Pop is Going to Claim More Victims

## Insights

- Hedge funds, not households, are carrying the leverage underlying the AI bubble: gross hedge fund assets are $13T against $4T net, with top-15 funds borrowing $11 for every $1 managed, concentrated at Goldman, Morgan Stanley, and JP Morgan.
- The core economic flaw: AI-hosting corporations (chips, land, power, water) run on debt-financed models whose revenue hasn't materialized to cover debt service, so borrowing has substituted for cash flow.
- Nvidia's CEO resignation is framed as likely tied to alleged accounting fraud (claiming chip sales that weren't real) and alleged illegal chip diversion to China (e.g., to firm Megaspeed), with smuggling-related revenue possibly 20-25% of sales — not the publicly stated "espionage" narrative.
- Nvidia's "circular financing" — funding customers (neoclouds like CoreWeave, Nebius, Firmus; also a $10B stake in Anthropic) to buy its own chips — artificially propped up demand signals, and this mechanism is now breaking down.
- Anthropic's IPO is described as a "rug pull": insiders maximized their own take while retail investors got a stock that fell from $115 to near its $83 IPO price within a day; the company is highly unprofitable with ~$1T in committed data center spending.
- Oracle's finances exemplify the interlocking risk: ~$465B in debt/obligations, negative $16B annual cash flow, and a recovery plan dependent on a few customers — primarily OpenAI, which itself can't raise needed capital and whose path to an IPO is now blocked by Anthropic's failed listing.
- Projected contagion effects if the bubble fully unwinds: hits to consumer spending, pensions, university/nonprofit endowments, state/city budgets reliant on capital gains taxes, and allied economies (South Korea, Japan, Taiwan); also expects adjacent fraud to surface as institutions scramble for liquidity.
