# RT @TechPowerUp: NVIDIA Allegedly Engineered Scarcity By Funding AI Startups To

## Insights

- A WSJ report (via Wccftech) alleges NVIDIA deliberately engineered perceived GPU/memory scarcity by investing in AI startups that would in turn buy NVIDIA GPUs and build data centers, sustaining the appearance of high demand.
- The motive described: NVIDIA faced an oversupply of semiconductors from TSMC and needed buyers to absorb this "avalanche" of GPU inventory.
- An internal August meeting reportedly saw then-CFO/acting CEO Colette Kress push to scale back circular financing deals (including the $250B OpenAI arrangement), with Jensen Huang responding by calling her a "deer in the headlights."
- A flagged structural problem: many of the smaller cloud providers NVIDIA was funding reportedly could not actually afford to buy NVIDIA's own GPUs, raising doubts about whether this investment strategy was financially sustainable — a potential "dead end."
- Context: this follows Huang's departure as NVIDIA CEO, and the report adds to other controversies (e.g., investigation into Huang's ties to a suspected Chinese spy) circulating around NVIDIA and AI infrastructure buildout economics.
- Broader implication raised by commenters (not the article's own claim): questions about whether this circular financing behavior could carry antitrust or market-manipulation exposure, given NVIDIA's dominant position in AI compute supply.

## Quote

> NVIDIA artificially inflated the ongoing GPU and memory shortage by investing in AI startups that would go on to buy NVIDIA GPUs.
