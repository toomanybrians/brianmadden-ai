# We Are All Junior Talent Now

## Insights

- OpenAI's Chief People Officer KC argues the terms "junior" and "senior" are losing meaning: in a fast-moving AI landscape, experience from even three months ago can be more relevant than experience from years ago, so tenure-based seniority breaks down.
- He frames the stack of modern work as: the model (brain), the harness/agent tooling like Codex or Claude Code (an "exoskeleton" of skills, tools, and runtime context), and above that "incarnations" — assistants, then specialists.
- Much current human work is "work on work" — people acting as middleware, doing tasks and then logging/updating systems about them; he predicts agents will absorb this system-updating role, freeing humans to work directly with humans.
- This shift implies every role effectively gains a suffix of "agent manager" (e.g., salesperson becomes sales agent manager).
- He argues emotional intelligence — how one "shows up," acts with integrity, and demonstrates humility/willingness to learn — becomes the key human differentiator once everyone has access to comparable AI intelligence, illustrated via a story about a patient choosing an oncologist for bedside manner.
- Tunguz separates two strategic options for companies given AI model progress: using AI to do the same tasks faster (boosts earnings/profit) versus reimagining what's possible (transforms the business), noting the two paths look similar initially before diverging sharply.

## Quote

> The moment you relegate the system updating to the agents, humans start working with humans.
