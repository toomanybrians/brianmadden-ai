# How AI Applications Earn Their Premium

## Insights

- As frontier models get cheaper and more capable, parts of AI applications become replaceable by a direct prompt to a general-purpose model — the core risk is any product whose output a customer can reproduce with little ongoing effort.
- Vendors defend pricing power through four levers: domain expertise, deep integrations, reliable execution, and hard-to-access context — and must actively price around these rather than deprioritizing pricing during fast growth.
- A real-world build-vs-buy test (Repsol legal, comparing Harvey vs. Microsoft Copilot vs. an internal prototype) found the specialized vendor won on quality, accuracy, and time savings, leading to full rollout with 96% adoption and 4–6 hours/week saved per lawyer.
- Three rules for sustained pricing power: operate the workflow better than the customer could in-house, make proprietary/prepared context cheaper to rent than to rebuild, and prove measurable value before pricing to capture it.
- Context monetization splits into two models: selling exclusive private data (e.g., a vendor with proprietary benchmarks across ~3,000 companies offering MCP access) versus charging for the preparation work (permissions, normalization, validation) on data the customer already owns.
- Recommended pricing practice: measure full cost per completed unit of work (including failures/retries), price at the highest defensible layer of value rather than per-token, and separate volatile model-cost pass-throughs from fixed-price orchestration (per Clay's approach) so efficiency gains can be shared with customers without eroding margin.

## Quote

> Your advantage must widen faster for the application to keep earning its premium.
