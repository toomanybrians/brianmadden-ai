# Nov. 25 - Black Friday shopping gets an AI makeover | Governance gaps and the Bengals’ GenAI playbook

## Insights

- Customer-facing AI shopping agents are emerging to the point where they may transact directly with retailers' own AI bots, raising the prospect that CIOs will need to manage machine-to-machine commerce disputes and liability, not just system uptime, during peak shopping periods.
- Supplier offboarding often fails to revoke access: terminated vendors can retain unused credentials, creating a lingering security risk that existing access-management processes don't reliably catch.
- Traditional IT governance controls (risk reviews, third-party oversight, stop controls) remain necessary for AI but are insufficient on their own — a former CIO argues AI introduces strain points the old playbook wasn't built for.
- Post-quantum cryptography migration is surfacing a visibility gap for CIOs: per NIST transition guidance, organizations often can't identify or control vulnerable cryptographic implementations buried in legacy systems or vendor-controlled infrastructure.
- The Cincinnati Bengals replaced manually scripted fan-chatbot responses with a generative AI system that draws on internal team guides to answer questions, escalating to a human representative only when needed.
- A separate piece frames Gen Z's AI fluency as a forcing function requiring organizations to rethink leadership style, productivity measurement, and the structure of future work.
