# The forest of philanthropy vs. the trees of charity

## Insights

- Draws a distinction between philanthropy (institutional, scalable, quantifiable) and charity (human-to-human, "positive friction" that AI cannot properly quantify) — framing AI's analytical power as fundamentally mismatched to judging human value.
- Raises governance concerns about Anthropic's founders' pledge to donate 80% of wealth to "charitable initiatives," questioning whether routing it through a self-controlled nonprofit vehicle preserves founder control rather than relinquishing power.
- Critiques Effective Altruism (popular in the AI community) for prioritizing investment/compounding returns over immediate giving, and critiques the "request for startups" model (cited via Stripe's Nan Ransohoff) where AI leaders dictate which problems are worth funding.
- Argues AI should be used to make philanthropic operations more efficient (fundraising, analysis) but warns against outsourcing the human, spiritual act of giving/judgment itself to AI or donor-advised funds.
- Profiles real-world nonprofit examples (Play & Learn in Muskegon, mRelief's SNAP-benefit tools) where AI is already used in the background (e.g., an "End-to-End SNAP Agent") to reduce friction, while emphasizing that human staff and connection remain the actual value delivered.
- Concludes AI has "the hammer of intelligence, but the nails are not always where the AI community may think they are" — suggesting AI-driven efficiency assumptions about where help is needed may be misaligned with actual human needs.

## Quote

> AI has the hammer of intelligence, but the nails are not always where the AI community may think they are.
