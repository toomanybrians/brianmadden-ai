# The Top Takes from Newcomer’s Cerebral Valley AI Summit

## Insights

- Cost-of-compute dominated the summit: companies are responding via purpose-built chips, application-specific inference, open-weight models, and real-time price arbitrage routing queries to cheaper models based on task complexity.
- Jensen Huang's departure from Nvidia is widely seen as destabilizing — Nvidia had acted as a financial "backstop" for the AI ecosystem (Ghodsi), and several speakers framed it as an opening for chip-market competitors (Taneja, Wachen, Babuschkin).
- Multiple speakers argued against concentration of AI power in a few labs: Hemant Taneja warned the industry risks a capitalism/trust backlash over value concentration; Babuschkin and Foody argued no single company can or should own a "duopoly on intelligence."
- Training/evaluation work is shifting from low-skill content workers to high-skill domain experts (lawyers, doctors, bankers, sommeliers) building reinforcement-learning environments and evals — Mercor pays contributors over $7M/day across 100+ countries.
- Legal services (Crosby) and coding (Factory, Vercel) are cited as leading verticals for agentic disruption; open-weight model usage at coding-agent startup Factory grew from ~0.01% of tokens to "double-digit percent" of volume in under a year.
- Bessemer/Newcomer's AI 100 index shows $3.6T total valuation (36x the 2016 Cloud 100 figure), with OpenAI and Anthropic alone accounting for 55% of that value; ~$8T in planned AI capex over five years would require ~$6T in annual revenue to justify.
- Audience survey: ~79-80% believe there's an AI bubble likely to pop in 2027, yet there's near-unanimous optimism about Anthropic's IPO; 64% of attendees said they were hiring fewer people due to AI, and none reported hiring significantly more.

## Quote

> "I just simply cannot see a future where we'll just have one giant AI company and we all have to ask for permission if we want to use intelligence." — Igor Babuschkin
