# ⚙️ GPT-6.2’s biggest leap is in work per dollar

## Insights

- OpenAI's GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol launch reframes AI model competition around cost-efficiency ("work per dollar") rather than raw capability alone — Astra scored 5.5 points higher than Anthropic's Fable 5.2 on the Agent's Last Exam benchmark while costing just 5% as much in tokens.
- Sol, OpenAI's efficiency-focused model, matches Anthropic's second-tier Opus on intelligence but is priced at the level of Anthropic's third-tier Sonnet, and OpenAI says many of its own employees default to it for both technical and non-technical tasks.
- Microsoft's Ignite keynote centered on proving AI ROI to fatigued enterprises: it claims Copilot is 84% cheaper than Claude and 63% cheaper than ChatGPT for average users, and launched Agent 365 Business Analytics, a tool that ties token spend to actual completed work and business KPIs rather than just tracking usage volume.
- Microsoft's new Microsoft-Decision-1.1 model (10x faster, 200x cheaper than traditional LLMs) joins a wave of low-cost "decision models" from OpenAI and Google, signaling a split between frontier reasoning models and cheap, fast models for simpler decisioning tasks.
- Mistral is moving away from the price-competition race, instead building sovereign, ownable frontier models targeted at regulated/technical sectors (industrial engineering, finance, energy) where enterprises want full control and no vendor lock-in risk.
- The piece notes that as models commoditize on raw capability, value is shifting to routing/orchestration layers (directing tasks to the most cost-effective model) — a capability Anthropic has built in but which OpenAI's GPT-6.2 currently lacks.

## Quote

> "Anything you do in Claude or ChatGPT, you can do in Copilot for less." — Judson Althoff, Microsoft
