# China’s AI runs on American chips

## Insights

- Despite DeepSeek's "Sputnik moment" narrative, China's best AI models remain persistently ~6-6.5 months behind the US frontier, even though the US has roughly 16x China's AI compute — the gap has not closed over two years.
- China's AI access runs heavily through American channels: roughly two-thirds of Chinese AI compute will come from US chips by end of next year, obtained via legal sales, gaming-card workarounds, smuggling, and (the largest channel) renting compute in data centers abroad (Malaysia, Singapore, Norway) that US export rules don't restrict.
- "Distillation" — training on massive volumes of output from US frontier models (e.g., Alibaba exchanged 150M+ messages with Claude in three months) — is estimated to close roughly half the compute gap, equivalent to giving China 4x more chips; model providers' countermeasures are only partially effective and create a financial disincentive to block paying "customers" who are actually copiers.
- China is reframed as running a different competition than "build the smartest model" — it's optimizing for ubiquitous deployment (factories, hospitals, government), backed by major advantages in electricity capacity build-out and dominance in industrial/humanoid robotics manufacturing and supply chains.
- Frontier AI model security is weak (industry rated ~2-3 out of 5 on a scale where 5 stops nation-state spies, vs. strict mandatory rules for nuclear plants), making outright theft of model weights or training breakthroughs a live risk that could erode any US lead even if chip/compute gaps are addressed.
- Policy levers to widen the US lead are described as mostly already available (restricting offshore chip rental, tightening smuggling enforcement, cutting legacy ASML tool/maintenance sales) but politically stalled, partly due to industry incentives (e.g., NVIDIA, policymakers with AI-sector financial stakes) favoring continued sales to China.

## Quote

> China is renting American chips.
