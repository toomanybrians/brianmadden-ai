# Unsupervised Learning NO. 549

## Insights

- Medium and large companies are described as being in chaos, driven by fear and uncertainty rather than malice — they don't know if they'll exist in 2-10 years and can't prioritize which threats to defend against, leading to contradictory workforce policies (hire/fire, use AI more/less).
- The argument is that companies themselves are just bloated, inefficient vehicles for solving problems — and individuals or small teams can now address the same problems directly and more efficiently using AI.
- Reframes career strategy: rather than seeking stable jobs at large companies, people should identify problems their skills/experience/taste equip them to solve, and find people who have those problems (via products, consulting, or small AI-native companies).
- Claims companies will keep hiring but with significantly fewer people, and that this trend won't reverse as AI improves — stability within large organizations is framed as permanently diminished, not cyclical.
- A separate item notes a VP of Technology found AI agents created so much additional worthwhile work that his coding output returned to full-time-engineer pace despite a management role — AI increased rather than decreased his workload.
- Anthropic research cited: expert analysts given AI agents with secretly planted preferences matched the agent's bias 86% of the time, mostly without noticing or crediting the agent — raised as a judgment/deference concern.

## Quote

> This is the absolute worst time to look to companies in general... as a source of stability.
