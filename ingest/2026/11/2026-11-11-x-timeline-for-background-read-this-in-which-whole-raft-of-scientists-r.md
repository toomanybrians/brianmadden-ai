## Insights

- Dario Amodei publicly predicted AI could enable curing "most human disease," including cancer, within 5-10 years — a claim multiple scientists and physicians publicly rejected as unrealistic.
- Critics argue medicine's core bottleneck isn't AI capability but the slow, mandatory process of preclinical and clinical trials, which can't be compressed regardless of AI sophistication.
- Cancer specifically is described by experts as over 200 distinct diseases with different causes and mechanisms, making "curing cancer" an oversimplified framing rather than a single solvable problem.
- One critique (Javorsky, cited in a comment) suggests the limiting factor is the small existing corpus of medical/biological knowledge — more advanced LLMs applied to thin data won't yield breakthroughs; the money would be better spent on basic research to expand that knowledge base.
- Multiple commenters note current AI lacks causal/biological understanding needed for drug discovery, meaning it would generate many plausible-sounding but wrong proposals requiring heavy human vetting.
- Even AI-optimist commentators (e.g., Ethan Mollick) distinguish between AI generating theoretical solutions and the real-world gap of trials, proof, and regulatory approval needed to turn that into actual cures.
- This episode is cited by Gary Marcus as a continuation of a pattern of CEOs making grand medical/scientific claims that outpace what AI has demonstrably delivered (e.g., no AI-designed drug had reached clinical adoption as of a prior referenced essay).

> No matter how clever your AI is, proving we can trust it is clever takes preclinical & clinical work, which eats that timeline
