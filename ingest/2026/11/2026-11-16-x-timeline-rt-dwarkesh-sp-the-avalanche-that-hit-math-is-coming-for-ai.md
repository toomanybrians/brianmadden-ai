# RT @dwarkesh_sp: The avalanche that hit math is coming for AI development.

http

## Insights

- Author argues AI capability in math went from unable to reliably add four-digit numbers (2023) to solving a Millennium Prize problem in 88 hours (around late 2026), and expects the same trajectory to hit AI research itself.
- Cites concrete internal lab data: METR found Anthropic's R&D progress rate has doubled in a year, largely attributable to AI use; OpenAI's heaviest users went from $1,000/day to $7,000/day of internal coding agent usage over one summer; Claude reportedly "leads" over 26% of Anthropic's AI R&D work, up from under 1% in February.
- Frames AI research as unusually well-suited to automation because, unlike pure math (which values new theory), ML progress is empirically driven and reachable via persistent recombination of existing ideas — illustrated by the claim that the transformer architecture was a combination of known pieces nobody had assembled yet.
- Predicts automated AI researchers that match the best humans by a median of 2028 (range: as early as 2027, 25% chance not until after 2032), driven by AIs working ~10x human speed around the clock (~50x more weekly output) plus parallelization across modular research tasks.
- Lays out two possible outcomes once AI research is automated: a "software-only singularity" (compounding self-improvement without new compute) vs. a "spark" (a compressed burst of 3-6 years of progress in one year before plateauing until more compute is built) — assigns 50/50 odds.
- Addresses the objection that real-world expertise (law, trading, business, politics) can't be verified/trained in a datacenter the way math can; argues scaled RL will still transfer general competencies (planning, persistence, learning from mistakes) via vastly more numerous, higher-quality training environments built by automated engineers.

## Quote

> AI research is closer to formalizing a theorem than to writing a novel, because there is a lot of independent modular work to do.
