# The Bitter Lesson, Generalized

## Insights

- The piece generalizes Richard Sutton's 2019 "Bitter Lesson" (that general, compute-scaled methods outperform hand-built human knowledge in AI) into a broader claim about progress and gatekeeping.
- Reframes the metaphor: "compounding progress and new ideas" plays the role of the scaling AI method, while entrenched experts (lawmakers, principal engineers, veteran practitioners) play the role of the old, hand-crafted knowledge being displaced.
- Characterizes expert resistance to new ideas as often reflexive — rejecting change simply because "that's not how it's been done" or "I have the experience," rather than evaluating the idea on merit.
- Explicitly qualifies the claim: not every new idea or AI-proposed change is actually better than the status quo — the generalization isn't a blanket endorsement of novelty.
- States the core generalized thesis: when experts become gatekeepers to progress, that is "generally (but not always) a bad thing."
