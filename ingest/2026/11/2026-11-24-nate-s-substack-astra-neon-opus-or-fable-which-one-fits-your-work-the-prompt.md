# Astra, Neon, Opus or Fable: which one fits your work? The prompt guide that answers it for your actual tasks, plus the model I start with for each kind of work.

## Insights

- The author argues that model quality judgments are becoming task-dependent rather than universal: a model can feel worse for someone's daily work while being more capable at the specific problem that matters for a different task.
- Direct comparison: GPT-6.2 Astra (OpenAI, released Nov 17) frustrates for writing and general coding by over-indexing on details and producing explanations that feel like extra work, but excels on messy, detail-heavy problems where exhaustive inspection is valuable.
- Gemini 4 Neon (Google, released Nov 18) is described as fast, clear, and easy to use for everyday writing/thinking tasks — a positive experience contrasted directly with Astra.
- The piece challenges the assumption that "smarter = better to talk to," suggesting that ease-of-use and raw capability on novel/messy problems can diverge, making single-axis leaderboards or reviews misleading.
- Proposes that evaluating models now requires understanding what work is being delegated, what judgment is retained by the human, and running tests designed to surface capabilities that routine daily use won't reveal.
- Frames model selection as a multi-model workflow problem: choosing which model fits which kind of task, and designing handoffs between models that preserve context (contradictions, constraints, open questions) from one model's work to the next.

## Quote

> A model can get worse at the work you do every day while becoming more useful for work that changes your business.
