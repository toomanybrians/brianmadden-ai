# Can AI help build a healthier future?

## Insights

- Microsoft's agentic diagnostic system (described in a Science paper) reportedly nearly doubled diagnostic accuracy over physicians while lowering cost — framed as reversing the usual AI pattern of raising costs via upcoding.
- Despite strong randomized-trial evidence for AI in medical imaging (mammography, colonoscopy, retina, ECG), clinical implementation lags badly; the bottleneck is adoption, not technology — attributed to thin hospital margins, risk aversion, and lack of demand from physicians or patients.
- "Whole-patient models" (integrating EHR, imaging, genomics, proteomics, exposome data) are described as closer to near-term feasibility than "virtual cell" models, which face a combinatorial explosion of cell types and possible perturbations.
- Panelists argue biological data, not compute, is now the binding constraint (unlike the AlphaFold/Protein Data Bank case) — citing a lack of large-scale "activity-based" or functional datasets linking molecular states to outcomes, as opposed to static structural data.
- US healthcare's short-term insurance/reimbursement structure is identified as structurally misaligned with prevention-oriented AI applications, in contrast to health systems in other countries with population-health incentives.
- Speakers highlight unexpected findings: patients report chatbots as more empathetic than overburdened clinicians, and AI-driven ambient scribes are freeing clinician time that could restore the doctor-patient relationship.
- Two "frontier of excitement" areas flagged beyond diagnostics: the immune system/immunome as an underexplored but central lever for cancer, neurodegeneration, and aging; and using AI to guide which real-world experiments to run (compute as experimental-design compass) rather than replacing experimentation.
