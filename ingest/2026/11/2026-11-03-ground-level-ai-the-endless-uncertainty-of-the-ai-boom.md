# The Endless Uncertainty of the AI Boom

## Insights

- The author contrasts the 2010s "future-proofing" era (cloud, digital transformation, data-driven) with today's AI era, arguing the former had a clear, predictable direction even if execution was uncertain, while AI's direction itself is unclear.
- Companies are now described as practicing "ambiguity tolerance" or "technological adaptability" rather than future-proofing, since the technology changes faster than strategy can be set.
- Businesses continue investing heavily in AI adoption and infrastructure despite not knowing if they'll need to pivot within months — driven more by fear of falling behind than confidence in payoff.
- Investors are described as nervous about climbing Big Tech AI spending commitments, with no clear consensus on whether the investment will pay off.
- The author notes a shift in sentiment among the C-suite executives, founders, investors, and researchers she interviews: early boom-era optimism and bold predictions have given way to nervous laughter and reluctance to forecast even a few months ahead.
- Despite AI models becoming demonstrably more capable and widely used over the nearly four years since ChatGPT's release, the author states this increased capability hasn't reduced overall uncertainty ("the fog") about AI's trajectory or implications.

## Quote

> It seems like the risk of being left behind looms larger than making the wrong bet.
