# We Won A Battle In The Data Center War

## Insights

- A wave of 2026 midterm results showed data centers becoming a decisive, cross-partisan electoral issue, with anti-data-center sentiment uniting otherwise politically divided communities in states like Georgia, Arizona, and Wisconsin.
- Georgia, now a leading global data center hub (300+ facilities), saw strong public opposition (83% favoring a statewide moratorium per Science for Georgia survey); the governor's race turned on this issue, with the winning Democratic candidate pledging to sign a moratorium.
- Ballot measures in ten states and outright local bans in cities across Florida, Maine, California, Ohio, and Kansas reflected a broad, organized regulatory pushback — covering zoning restrictions, energy caps, voter-approval requirements, and moratoriums.
- Water and power strain is a concrete grievance: Arizona data centers were linked (per Forbes reporting) to water shortages severe enough to trigger mandated usage cuts for residents, even as the state hosts 180+ facilities.
- In response to the political backlash, OpenAI signaled it would reduce NDA use in data center development and emphasized better "communicating" the benefits to local communities — framed by the author as a messaging fix rather than a substantive change.
- The piece notes an irony: San Francisco, home to OpenAI's headquarters and executives, itself has a data center moratorium, suggesting a gap between where AI companies site infrastructure and where they personally live.
