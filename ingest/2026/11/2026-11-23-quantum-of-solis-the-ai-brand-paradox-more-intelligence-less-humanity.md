# The AI Brand Paradox: More Intelligence, Less Humanity

## Insights

- Argues a core paradox: as AI makes content production easier and faster, it becomes harder to create something that produces genuine emotional resonance with people.
- Distinguishes "competing against AI" (using AI to accelerate existing work) from "competing with AI" (using it to question assumptions and create what wasn't previously possible) — framed as fundamentally different mindsets and games.
- Cites WordPress VIP's Future of the Web 2026 research: 74% of consumers say the internet feels less human than 10 years ago; "bot fatigue" sets in after ~40 minutes of interaction; 60% say AI in brand messaging is a turnoff; 61% can't name a brand using AI well.
- Introduces "digital warmth" as a concept — AI-driven experience that feels attentive and personal without faking human presence or manufacturing false intimacy.
- Proposes organizations need an "experience style guide" alongside brand guidelines — rules for how AI should behave, where humans must stay present, when to disclose AI's role, and what a brand's "relational promise" is.
- Describes a personal practice called WWAID ("What Would AI Do") — a set of reflective questions used before prompting AI, aimed at using it to expand thinking rather than just retrieve answers faster.
- Frames organizational culture (fear of being wrong, reward for certainty, risk-averse review structures) as the real constraint on AI-era innovation, since AI amplifies existing culture rather than replacing it.

## Quote

> As intelligence becomes abundant, being intelligent will not, by itself, make a brand remarkable.
