# 🕵️ Huang's Affair Triggers Nvidia Spy Probe

## Insights

- October's jobs report shows consulting (+17,300), legal services (+2,400), and accounting (+2,300) jobs still growing despite being ranked among the most AI-exposed professions; losses instead concentrated in routine functions like document-preparation services (-17% YoY) and call centers (-4.7%).
- Harvard economist David Deming argues AI remains "broad but not that deep" — it can absorb pieces of many jobs but rarely replaces an entire role, which may explain continued hiring in exposed fields.
- Independent auditor METR found Anthropic's internal capability metric (AECI) has nearly doubled its yearly rate of improvement since October 2025 (29 points/year vs. 15 previously), with ~80% of that acceleration attributed to Anthropic using its own AI to speed up AI research rather than more training compute; Anthropic redacted much of the evidence and cancelled part of the audit.
- Drugmaker executives (Vertex, AstraZeneca, Regeneron) are pushing back on claims that AI will soon cure most diseases — Vertex had ~50 AI companies screen its 10,000-compound library with zero resulting drugs, and no AI-designed drug has yet won FDA approval, despite $8.4B invested in AI drug discovery in 2025.
- OpenAI raised $11B at a $1.25T valuation, below its earlier $1.4T target, reflecting investor concern that AI usage is rising while spending growth has slowed sharply (Ramp data: AI model spend grew just 1.5% across Aug-Sept vs. 42% in July) as cheaper models and price cuts let companies "use more AI, but use it for less."
- Google DeepMind's AI agents found new attack methods against the retired 1024-bit RSA encryption standard (1,600x more efficient signature forgery in one case), though modern 2048-bit+ systems are unaffected.
