# Compute Crunch? Startups Aim to Financialize It

## Insights

- A cluster of startups (Ornn, Silicon Data, Architect, Liquid Compute) are trying to turn GPU compute capacity into a tradeable financial commodity, with standardized pricing indexes, futures, options, and brokerage services.
- Ornn brokers short-term GPU contracts between neoclouds/data centers and compute-hungry startups, and is reportedly raising a new round at roughly a $1B valuation (Thrive Capital, General Catalyst in talks), up from a $33M a16z crypto-led seed in June.
- Silicon Data has built an hourly GPU rental price index (H100, B200) from ~100 data sources, positioning itself as a pure data/index provider rather than a broker; CME plans to list futures on its indexes via NYMEX, pending regulatory approval.
- ICE similarly plans contracts referencing Ornn's Compute Price index; both CME and ICE listings are delayed pending regulatory sign-off, and newer entrants like Liquid Compute want to become regulated exchanges themselves rather than list through CME/ICE.
- The CFTC has opened public comment on compute futures, surfacing a core unresolved question: whether GPU compute is standardized, interchangeable, and liquid enough to function as a commodity, especially since GPUs depreciate fast as new chips launch (unlike metals).
- Investors are split — some are funding this space aggressively while at least two told Newcomer they passed on deals, uncertain whether today's compute shortage is structural or a temporary phase that would undercut the market's rationale.
