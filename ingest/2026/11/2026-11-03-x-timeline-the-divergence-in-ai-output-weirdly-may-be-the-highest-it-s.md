# The divergence in AI output weirdly may be the highest it’s ever been, even thou

## Insights

- Claims that variance in AI output quality/results is currently at its highest point ever, despite AI adoption also being at its broadest point — productivity gains are becoming more unevenly distributed, not less, as usage spreads.
- Frames this as counterintuitive: wider adoption would typically be expected to narrow the gap between best and worst users, not widen it.
- Cites a historical baseline: a few years ago, even in a highly capability-aligned field like coding, the best achievable productivity gains topped out around 30-50%.
- Implies current gains can now far exceed that historical ceiling, via a quoted anecdote describing a full six-person dev team being replaceable by a single Claude Code prompt.
- Suggests the gap between frontier/skilled users and average users of AI tools is widening rather than converging as the technology matures.

## Quote

> The divergence in AI output weirdly may be the highest it's ever been, even though adoption is the broadest it's ever been.
