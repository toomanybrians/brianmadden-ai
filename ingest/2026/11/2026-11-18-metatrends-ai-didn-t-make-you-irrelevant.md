# AI Didn’t Make You Irrelevant

## Insights

- Cites Stanford Digital Economy Lab research (Erik Brynjolfsson et al.) finding employment among 22-25 year-olds in highly AI-exposed occupations was ~19% below projected trend through June 2026, while noting the researchers themselves caution this doesn't prove causation.
- Draws a distinction between AI devaluing specific tasks/work versus devaluing the person doing them — argues experience, judgment, and curiosity remain intact even as the economic value of certain skills shifts.
- Frames AI as a tool for interrogating and stress-testing a problem/idea (e.g., "argue both sides and cite sources") rather than a source of final answers — explicitly warns it can be "confidently wrong."
- Proposes a concrete method for starting something despite feeling behind: pick a problem driven by "awe or pain," use AI to investigate and challenge assumptions, build a rough first version, test it with someone who has the actual problem, then reach out to one specific person.
- Uses a personal pre-AI anecdote (founding a student space advocacy group via letters in 1980) to argue that finding collaborators for a cause has always been less about resources/standing and more about naming the problem publicly.
- Suggests current tools (AI, networked outreach) make the "finding people who care" step faster than it was in a pre-digital era, though the article doesn't elaborate on specific AI capabilities beyond research/argumentation support.

## Quote

> AI may make some of the work we do today less valuable. That is not the same as making us less valuable.
