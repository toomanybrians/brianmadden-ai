# What Kind of Factory Will You Build?

## Insights

- The piece argues AI-driven work is converging on a four-stage "factory" loop: shared context (specs/docs/history), an agent that executes, an execution trace (what shipped, broke, or cost), and a curator that rewrites specs/skills based on the trace.
- The trace and curator stages are framed as the key differentiators: without them, AI use stays a one-off workflow; with them, the system compounds and improves over successive runs.
- Two vendor posts published the same day (Notion's "A New Shape for Work" and LangChain's "How to Build a Knowledge Factory") are cited as independently describing the identical architecture, suggesting convergent thinking in the industry — though the author notes neither includes benchmarks or verified outcomes, calling both "vendor arguments about architecture."
- The factory model is presented as generalizable beyond engineering: marketing (campaign performance → better copy), ad operations (ROAS data → new creative), sales (call transcripts → sharper decks).
- Satya Nadella is quoted describing a reimagined company structure where a founder and subsequent hires each operate with a "token budget," blending compute costs with wages as a build resource.
- The author frames building a factory as fundamentally an operations challenge, not a technology purchase — requiring someone to own context, write specs, review traces, and run the curator loop on a regular cadence.

## Quote

> The question is no longer whether AI can do the work: it is what kind of factory you will build.
