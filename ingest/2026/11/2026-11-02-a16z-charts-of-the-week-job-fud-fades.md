# Charts of the Week: Job FUD Fades

## Insights

- Microsoft's buyout offer to ~60,000 employees (~25% of workforce, ~$10B potential cost) was framed by its CFO as giving "employee agency" rather than an AI-driven cut; outside speculation tied it more to offsetting capex commitments than to AI substitution.
- News coverage of "AI and job loss" has receded to roughly year-ago levels, and explicit AI-attributed layoff announcements hit their lowest point of the year in August-September, suggesting the acute "AI jobs apocalypse" narrative has cooled since a peak around May.
- Job postings data (Indeed) show rising demand for software engineers, manufacturing, and especially engineering roles, with engineering up ~30%, SWE ~26%, and manufacturing ~13% year-over-year since October — read as AI driving demand for both infrastructure builders and AI users rather than displacing them.
- On the AI monetization/ROI side: Adobe data shows AI-referred ecommerce traffic has ~50% higher revenue per visit than non-AI-referred traffic, and Meta's average ad prices have grown 20%+ YoY for two straight quarters, suggested as partly AI-driven efficiency.
- Market "AI bubble" fear is increasingly centered on a "Rack Spread" (falling token prices vs. rising GPU rental costs), but the piece argues this is a flawed crude-oil/crack-spread analogy since token volume (and total spend) isn't fixed, unlike oil supply.
- Available token-spend data (from Ramp, OpenRouter, Silicon Data, Ornn, Atlanta Fed survey) is described as small-sample, self-selected, and wildly inconsistent (e.g., Ramp's $188/employee/year vs. Atlanta Fed's $1,000+/employee), making it unreliable as a definitive signal for AI ROI debates.
