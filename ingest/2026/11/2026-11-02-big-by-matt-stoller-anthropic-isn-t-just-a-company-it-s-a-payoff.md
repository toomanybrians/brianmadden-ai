# Anthropic Isn't Just a Company, It's a Payoff

## Insights

- Anthropic's IPO filing (S-1) shows unusually deep ownership and operational entanglement with its supposed rivals/suppliers: Amazon (~10-12% stake), Google (~15% stake), Microsoft (undisclosed stake), and Broadcom — all also serve as investors, customers, lenders, and infrastructure suppliers simultaneously.
- Mention-count comparison is used as evidence of complexity: Amazon appears 150+ times and Google 100+ times in Anthropic's S-1, versus a handful of mentions for comparable companies in Google's 2004 and Amazon's 1995 IPO filings.
- Anthropic depends on Amazon, Google, and Microsoft for ~50% of its distribution (e.g., Claude for Microsoft 365, Claude in Chrome), for internal operating tools, and for computing resources purchased via non-cancellable, multi-hundred-billion-dollar contracts.
- These same companies are also lenders to Anthropic (Amazon's convertible debt tied to AWS purchases, Broadcom guaranteeing $42B in borrowing) and stand to post massive equity gains if Anthropic's IPO prices high (Google alone shows $117B in unrealized gains tied largely to its Anthropic stake).
- The piece argues this cross-ownership structure creates incentives for "rivals" not to compete on price or capability, since undermining Anthropic's valuation would also hurt their own balance sheets — potentially explaining Google's and Amazon's relatively muted frontier-model competition.
- Big tech is characterized as having shifted from capital-light, cash-generating businesses to capital-intensive, cash-burning ones (Anthropic lost $80B last year; Amazon had -$42B free cash flow), making a systemic AI-bubble deflation a macroeconomic risk rather than a sector-specific one.
- Regulatory posture shifted: Biden-era FTC/antitrust efforts to separate these cross-investments (e.g., forcing Google to divest its Anthropic stake) were dropped under Trump, who instead encouraged cross-investment among ten major AI/tech firms.

## Quote

> Anthropic looks less like a stand-alone business and more like four monopolies in a trench coat.
