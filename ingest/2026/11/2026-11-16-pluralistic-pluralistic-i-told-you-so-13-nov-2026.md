# Pluralistic: I told you so (13 Nov 2026)

## Insights

- Microsoft's global Outlook/Azure/Teams/Copilot outage lasted roughly two days and halted government agencies and businesses worldwide — many organizations reportedly couldn't even convene a meeting or plan a response without these tools, illustrating deep enterprise dependency on a single vendor's infrastructure.
- Doctorow frames this as evidence of chronic underinvestment: in 2025-2026 Microsoft spent over $30b on stock buybacks and lost nearly $200b on AI initiatives the company itself now admits "will not pay off," while simultaneously cutting headcount by 25% (60,000 jobs) in 2026, including its first-ever voluntary retirement buyout.
- He argues the layoffs directly correlate with degraded reliability: when those workers were employed, the servers stayed up; after cutting them, outages followed — used as evidence that staffing (SREs and management) was sacrificed to fund AI spending and shareholder payouts.
- Proposes a "bright-line" test for illegal monopoly: if a dominant firm diverts surplus capital to investors (buybacks) and that precedes product/service degradation, it should be presumed an illegal monopoly subject to breakup — independent of intent or market-definition arguments.
- Cites Apple as a parallel case: despite receiving ~$20b/year from Google (deemed necessary to "deliver excellent products" by a judge), Apple had a string of bad product launches while spending $170b on its own stock buybacks — used to argue monopolists systematically underinvest in product quality regardless of resources.
- Broader argument: enterprise reliance on a single cloud/software monopolist creates sovereignty and continuity risk — Microsoft can allegedly grant foreign governments' and firms' data to political actors without guaranteed data sovereignty, raising questions about what happens if access is cut off for geopolitical reasons.

## Quote

> If you can't manage to keep your servers online, you have no business incinerating tens of billions on stock buybacks.
