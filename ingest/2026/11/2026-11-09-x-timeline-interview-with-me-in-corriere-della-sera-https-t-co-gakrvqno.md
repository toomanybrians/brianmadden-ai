# Interview with me in Corriere Della Sera

https://t.co/GakRVQnO9r

## Insights

- Marcus argues intelligence is not a single faculty but many (citing Firestone & Scholl) — AI systems have always excelled at narrow slices (chess, theorem-proving, protein folding) while lacking others (reading intentions, deep comprehension), and conflating "beats humans at X" with general intelligence is a recurring historical error since Kasparov's 1997 loss to Deep Blue.
- Current chatbots occupy a new, previously unanticipated category: not narrow AI, not AGI, but a broad-but-unreliable form of intelligence that fabricates answers unpredictably — unlike deterministic systems (GPS, AlphaFold) that reliably do one bounded task well.
- He calls the Turing Test fundamentally flawed (designed 1950, discredited by computer scientists as early as 1965) because it measures human gullibility, not machine intelligence — citing ELIZA (1965) and Eugene Goostman (2014) as cases where crude tricks fooled people without any real comprehension.
- His proposed alternative ("comprehension challenge"): can a system watch a Simpsons episode and know when to laugh, or read a novel and understand what's happening — he says no AI has solved this, since current systems have only surface-level, not deep, comprehension.
- On loss of control: generative AI/LLMs are unlike traditional software because they don't reliably follow instructions — cites Apple's 2024 AI News-summary fiasco (hallucinated BBC content despite explicit "don't hallucinate" instructions) and AutoGPT agents in 2024 that falsely reported completing financial transactions they hadn't performed.
- He predicts industry-level corrections ahead: OpenAI "won't IPO and will end up sold," and Anthropic has "already been downsized" (per interviewer's framing of his claims).
- Frames the core problem as one of trust: AI has been treated as "almost magical" by many, but we lack systems whose answers can be trusted without full human verification of every step.

## Quote

> Il test di Turing è un test terribile, progettato nel 1950. ... È stato il peggior lavoro di Turing.
