# The Blue Tsunami Is a Victory for Democracy — and a Wake-Up Call for Silicon Valley

## Insights

- Post-midterm political backlash against data centers is already materializing concretely: of 41 state/local ballot measures on data centers, voters opposed them in all but one, and GlobalData reports average data center project timelines now running 33% longer due to public opposition and grid-connection delays.
- AI infrastructure and chip stocks sold off sharply after the election (Tesla -11%, SpaceX -9% Thursday) on investor expectations that a more hostile political environment will slow hyperscaler capex and data center buildout.
- Menlo Ventures' State of Enterprise AI report shows businesses continuing to increase AI spending and growing more receptive to open-source models, alongside tracking of which AI applications are capturing the bulk of enterprise budgets.
- Google's move to let schools disable AI features bundled search access with AI toggles, meaning a school that opts out of AI loses Google Search entirely — drawing criticism from antitrust advocates (Lina Khan) and politicians as coercive bundling.
- A new essay ("The Commanding Heights" by Manidis and Roberts) argues frontier AI models will keep improving per scaling laws, but intelligence itself will become cheap and commoditized — meaning the durable economic value accrues not to frontier labs but to whoever controls the distribution of AI into the real economy (drawing analogies to railroads and Google Ads).
- A GitHub project called "Reverse Engineer Anything" (REA) is being used with AI agents to decompile and reconstitute software from binaries — already producing open-source clones of Adobe and Microsoft Office tools — raising concerns about SaaS companies' ability to defend IP and pricing moats; Anthropic has reportedly restricted Claude Code from cloning video games in response.
