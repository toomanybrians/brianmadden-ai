# The AI data center boom is entering its bargaining era

## Insights

- Post-election data shows a majority of winning congressional candidates (62%) support regulation or conditional development of data centers, with 16% backing outright bans or moratoriums — signaling growing political friction around AI infrastructure buildout.
- Community opposition to data centers is coalescing around concrete local concerns: electricity costs, water use, environmental impacts, and loss of local control — not abstract AI anxieties.
- A Texas gubernatorial upset was partly attributed by a local opposition leader to the incumbent's inaction on data center regulation despite community petitions, suggesting the issue has real electoral consequences.
- Industry perspective (via a developer with projects underway) suggests the backlash won't halt data center development broadly, but will create a bifurcation: late-stage/already-approved projects proceed largely unaffected, while early-stage/speculative projects face delays.
- The piece frames the core problem as having shifted from "how to pay for and power" data centers to "how to deal with the communities expected to host them."
- Negotiated concessions to nearby residents were used successfully to secure zoning approval for at least one contested project, suggesting a template for how developers may adapt going forward.
