# Where Are the “Killer Apps” of AI?

## Insights

- O'Reilly argues historically the "platform" (browser, OS, BASIC interpreter) is never the killer app — value accrues to applications built on top of it; by this logic current AI model providers (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) and coding agents (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor) are plumbing/platform, not the killer app itself.
- Drawing timeline comparisons (transformer paper 2017 ≈ Berners-Lee's 1989 web proposal; ChatGPT 2022 ≈ Mosaic 1993), he argues AI's pace from invention to platform breakout (~5.5 years) roughly matches the PC and web eras — contrary to the popular narrative that AI is moving unusually fast.
- He contends AI may actually be moving slower than hype suggests relative to historical pattern: Claude Code (the "BASIC equivalent") didn't arrive until 2025, and current AI-era conversation is dominated by developer-tooling productivity talk rather than visible end-user "killer apps" like Amazon, Google, or Wikipedia.
- Proposes that killer apps may be blocked by "missing mechanisms" — infrastructure like payment, identity/accountability, and value-settlement systems for autonomous agents (citing his own "Missing Mechanisms of the Agentic Economy" argument) — analogous to how ecommerce needed HTTPS before Amazon could work.
- Invokes Carlota Perez's framework: capital floods into infrastructure buildout, a bubble forms and bursts, and the real transformative applications get built afterward by different players on the cheapened leftover infrastructure (e.g., YouTube built on cheap post-crash bandwidth).
- Suggests massive AI capital expenditure (~$700B among hyperscalers) may reflect premature monopoly-seeking and bubble groupthink focused on backward-looking value capture rather than new value creation, potentially slowing genuine innovation.
- Closes with an open question/call for examples: is he simply not hearing about AI killer apps that already exist, or have they genuinely not yet appeared beyond chatbots and coding agents?

## Quote

> A killer app is at first a startling window onto an undreamed-of possibility... that rapidly gets taken for granted.
