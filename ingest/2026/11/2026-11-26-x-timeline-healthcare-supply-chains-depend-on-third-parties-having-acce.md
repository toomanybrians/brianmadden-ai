# Healthcare supply chains depend on third parties having access to critical appli

## Insights

- Frames healthcare third-party access (device makers, suppliers, GPOs, consultants) as a growing enterprise security gap distinct from patient-data security, driven by the need for real-time supply chain visibility.
- Argues that legacy VPN-based network access models are mismatched to this need: granting network-level trust to fulfill a single application need, which increases lateral-movement risk if credentials are compromised or access isn't revoked promptly.
- Describes a shift in the underlying enterprise security question from "how do we grant network access" to "what specific application does this person need" — i.e., zero trust reframed around application-level least privilege rather than perimeter/network access.
- Positions access control as multidimensional: identity, device posture, location, risk level, role, and session context, rather than a binary network-in/network-out decision.
- Cites concrete stats: average healthcare breach cost of $7.42M, 279 days average to identify/contain breaches, and 46% of surveyed healthcare organizations citing material shortages as the top supply chain concern.
- Uses a real-world example (Atlantic Health System during a CrowdStrike outage, 15,000+ users) to argue that decoupling application delivery from specific devices/networks aids operational resilience during disruption, not just security.

## Quote

> The traditional security question was: "How do we give external users access to our network?" The zero trust question is fundamentally different.
