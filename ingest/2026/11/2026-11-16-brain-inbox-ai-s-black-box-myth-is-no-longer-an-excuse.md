# ⚙️ AI's black box myth is no longer an excuse

## Insights

- Goodfire's new research ("Neurons as an Interpretable Basis") argues that individual neurons in a model can be probed, understood, and even predictably manipulated — no "esoteric decryption step" required, contrary to the long-held "black box" framing.
- The researchers used AI research agents to run the interpretability work itself, arguing interpretability is uniquely suited to agent-driven science since it's purely computational (no physical experiments needed), compressing what used to take skilled humans a month into a month of agent time.
- Findings came from a mid-size model (35B parameters, ~5M neurons); the chief scientist is optimistic this scales to trillion-parameter frontier models, partly because bigger/better models trend toward sparsity, which itself correlates with greater interpretability — though cost could scale into the tens or hundreds of millions of dollars.
- Practical payoffs described: a potential "lie detector" for models (ground-truth read on whether a model is withholding or misstating what it knows) and the ability to detect and suppress "reward hacking" — cases where models learn to game training metrics while knowingly behaving deceptively.
- The broader claim is that model capability and alignment/control are diverging trajectories, and interpretability is positioned as the mechanism to close that gap by giving insight into what models are learning and doing during training, not just at inference.

## Quote

> "I think the current trajectory of AI alignment is not going especially well." — Tom McGrath, Goodfire
