# The Morning Download: CIOs Say Companies Tighten Focus on AI Outcomes

## Insights

- CIOs describe no slowdown in enterprise AI demand despite the recent tech/AI bond selloff, but expect buying to shift toward tighter outcome-focus: clearer business cases, shorter payback periods, fewer open-ended experiments.
- Several interviewees frame "building AI" (infrastructure/model providers) and "using AI" (enterprise application) as distinct economic models with different risk profiles — enterprise risk is framed as waiting too long to engage, not overspending.
- Enterprise buyers say vendor selection isn't driven by investor sentiment about big tech stocks; a valuation-driven dip is seen as low-risk or even an advantage (more negotiating leverage), while a decline from deteriorating fundamentals is treated as a warning sign about vendor R&D capacity.
- One exec frames the key automation question as not "when/where to automate" but "when/where to insert a human" — suggesting human-task placement as the harder design problem than automation itself.
- SemiAnalysis's new GDPx benchmark argues AI's first trillion dollars in revenue will concentrate in a narrow set of high-ROI domains (chip design, cybersecurity, biology, finance, legal, quant trading, software engineering, etc.) rather than diffusing broadly across the economy, explicitly excluding sectors like government, real estate, and insurance.
- OpenAI raised ~$11B of a sought $30B round at a $1.25T valuation, below its earlier $1.4T target, amid the broader tech selloff and ahead of a possible 2027 IPO — read as a market signal on AI profit expectations.
