## Insights

- Pope Leo XIV, addressing researchers and experts convened by the Pontifical Academy for Life and the Future of Life Institute, argued AI's future "is not predetermined" and that humans bear responsibility to direct, modify, or refuse its applications.
- He reframed the core question from "what can AI do?" to "what purpose should it serve?", warning that treating AI development as a race to win risks ignoring those left behind.
- He explicitly rejected granting AI systems rights or legal personhood, arguing such systems don't have consciousness, don't experience joy or pain, and that personhood claims risk obscuring human responsibility for harms caused.
- He called for "disarming" AI, urging states meeting in Geneva in November to move toward banning lethal autonomous weapons, and insisted decisions about human life, liberty, and the use of force must always remain under meaningful human oversight.
- On governance, he said the ethical principles for AI are largely known, but what's missing is effective international institutions with shared standards, independent oversight, and clear accountability mechanisms so AI decisions can be reviewed, challenged, and remedied.
- A Vatican AI commission led by Cardinal Michael Czerny is taking a "transdisciplinary approach," engaging researchers, business, and policymakers to ensure vulnerable people and communities aren't left behind by AI development.
