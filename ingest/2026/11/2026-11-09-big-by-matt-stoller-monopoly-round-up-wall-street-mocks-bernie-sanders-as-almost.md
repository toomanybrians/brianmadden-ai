# Monopoly Round-Up: Wall Street Mocks Bernie Sanders as “Almost Dead”

## Insights

- Markets saw their biggest single-day drop in 18 months after the midterms (S&P -4.36%, NASDAQ -5.52%, ~$3 trillion in S&P market cap erased), driven largely by fear that a "Democratic wave" signals the end of the AI trade.
- Despite the crash, prominent investors and AI executives (Jason Calacanis, Ron Baron, OpenAI's CFO Sarah Friars, Meta's president) publicly dismissed the relevance of the election to their AI investment plans, with Baron saying he doesn't "think about elections."
- The AI industry's response to political pressure has reportedly included rebranding "data centers" as "compute campuses" to soften opposition.
- Midterm ballot initiatives in numerous local jurisdictions rejected new data center construction, including in Texas, a key data center hub — suggesting growing bipartisan local resistance to AI infrastructure buildout.
- On Election Day, Google announced it would cut off schools' access to Google Search if those schools restricted access to Google's AI tools — a move characterized as possible illegal tying, occurring amid NYC and LA school districts restricting AI in classrooms over concerns about learning outcomes and child psychology.
- Anthropic's IPO is described as "tottering" and "existential" for Wall Street/Silicon Valley, cited alongside a scandal involving Nvidia's CEO as signs that the AI investment narrative may be destabilizing.
- Pending federal permitting reform legislation (described as favorable to data center construction) may stall in Congress following the midterms, adding further uncertainty to AI infrastructure financing.
