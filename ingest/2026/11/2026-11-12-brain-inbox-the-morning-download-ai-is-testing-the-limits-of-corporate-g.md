# The Morning Download: AI Is Testing the Limits of Corporate Governance

## Insights

- Corporate boards are grappling with the pace mismatch between AI-driven change and traditional governance cycles — board materials prepared weeks in advance can be stale by the time meetings happen, per Jing Ulrich (SLB, Adidas, Swarovski, KKR's Wella board member).
- Board directors argue AI oversight has moved beyond a technical/IT issue into core board-level strategy, risk, succession planning, cybersecurity, and reputational risk discussions.
- The framing of AI at the board level has shifted from headcount-reduction/cost-savings conversations to questions of how AI drives competitive strategy (Shellye Archambeau, ExCo Group).
- A recurring concern among directors: AI's elimination or transformation of entry-level roles threatens the traditional pipeline through which future managers/leaders gain subject-matter expertise and collaboration skills (Joe Jimenez, boards of GM/P&G/Quantinuum).
- Clara Shih (former Salesforce AI CEO) warned that without early hands-on work experience, junior employees risk becoming mere "AI pass-throughs" lacking the judgment to critique AI-generated output.
- Separately noted: OpenAI reported Q3 revenue of ~$10B (up ~50% QoQ) with $10.9B operating losses, trailing Anthropic's $17B revenue/$567M loss; Anthropic founders plan to cash out ~$250M each in its upcoming IPO; TSMC is investing up to $500M each in Anthropic and OpenAI.
