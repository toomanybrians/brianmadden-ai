# #AI is moving from experimentation into everyday business, making adaptability e

## Insights

- Enterprise AI adoption is now happening diffusely and often invisibly: through approved tools, AI features embedded in existing apps, employee experimentation, developer workflows, and emerging agents — frequently without leaders having visibility into where it's occurring.
- The piece argues "shadow AI" differs fundamentally from shadow IT: offering an approved enterprise tool doesn't close the gap, because different AI models serve different needs and AI capability keeps surfacing inside apps the company already owns, so banning tools alone isn't a viable strategy.
- Recommended starting point is visibility, not transformation: understand where AI already touches workflows, data, and permissions before committing to large AI initiatives, using existing infrastructure (apps, browsers, connections) to surface current activity.
- Framed the "adaptive enterprise" as one that can absorb disruptions — mergers, regulatory shifts, cyber incidents, outages, or AI agent errors — without needing to rebuild core systems each time.
- Advocates for flexibility in AI tool choice (e.g., one AI service for developers, another for customer service, private environments for proprietary data), enabled by consistent access policies and governance so tools can be swapped as costs/options change.
- Notes that observability of AI/agent activity must come with its own boundaries — monitoring agent actions doesn't justify broad employee surveillance; purpose, access, and retention need governance too.
- Positions Citrix's own offerings (e.g., NetScaler AI Gateway, governed paths connecting AI to applications not originally built for it) as infrastructure for applying control and visibility to AI traffic.

## Quote

> AI changes who or what performs the work. It does not remove the need to deliver, govern, secure, and observe that work.
