# Humans on AI #73 || November 24, 2026

## Insights

- Anthropic's interviews with 58 enterprise customers found AI's value comes mainly from increasing output rather than cutting costs, with roles broadening (non-technical staff coding, specialists taking on full workflows) and bottlenecks shifting to code review, sales, and strategic decision-making.
- The same Anthropic report notes hiring is slowing (not usually layoffs directly), overseas contractor work is shrinking, and one executive observed that "the closer you are to the money, the harder it is for the models to excel in that job" — implying entry-level and junior work is more exposed to automation than roles tied closely to revenue decisions.
- DeepMind researcher Julian Jacobs' Work Values Index found a third of workers are intrinsically motivated by their jobs, and a fifth would keep working even for 120% of salary as guaranteed income — suggesting basic income alone won't replace the non-monetary meaning work provides as jobs are automated.
- OpenAI's chief scientist proposed pacing AI development using base-model perplexity (compression capability) as a shared threshold metric, arguing capabilities are mostly latent in pretraining and merely "elicited" by post-training/RL — a claim contested by critics who note the data is code-only and omits confounds like data-mix shifts.
- A new study found swarms of AI agents complete tasks 6x faster than solo agents but underperform by roughly 7% in relative performance on equal compute, with larger swarms prone to an "echo-chamber" effect of over-optimizing on flawed shared metrics.
- Axios reporting indicates Anthropic and OpenAI have unreleased frontier models considered too powerful/unsafe for public release, prompting internal debate over whether to reserve future models for internal use only or halt training entirely.

## Quote

> "The closer you are to the money, the harder it is for the models to excel in that job."
