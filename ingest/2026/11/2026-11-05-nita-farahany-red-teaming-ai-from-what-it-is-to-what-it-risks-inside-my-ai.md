# Red-Teaming AI: From What it is to What it Risks (Inside my AI Law and Policy Class #19)

## Insights

- Three OpenAI safety researchers (Tomek Korbak, Mikita Balesni, Jasmine Wang) were terminated after allegedly raising safety concerns through internal and external channels; OpenAI cites mishandling of sensitive information, while the researchers frame it as retaliation for red-teaming disclosures, raising the risk that internal safety whistleblowing gets punished rather than protected.
- AI red-teaming lacks the clear success/failure criteria of cybersecurity red-teaming — it's often unclear whether a harmful output represents a system "break" or the system "working as intended" with a bad result.
- Anthropic's October 2025 research found poisoning attacks may require only a small fixed number of malicious documents rather than a percentage of training data, undermining assumptions about how much corrupted data it takes to compromise a model.
- Hands-on testing of jailbreak techniques (direct asks, hypothetical/fictional framing, role-play) on current models (year not specified beyond "this year") showed these approaches now largely fail where they previously succeeded, suggesting guardrails have measurably tightened.
- U.S. legal frameworks not designed for AI — CFAA, DMCA Section 1201, and private Terms of Service — create real criminal and civil liability risk for independent researchers who responsibly disclose AI vulnerabilities, chilling external safety research.
- A proposed "safe harbor" model (legal + technical protections, modeled on coordinated vulnerability disclosure in early-2000s software security) aims to protect good-faith red-teamers, but risks creating a two-tier system where only well-funded institutions can safely participate, leaving independent/grad-student researchers exposed.
- California's SB53 is noted as a current law providing whistleblower protections specifically for reporting AI risks, applicable to "frontier" and "large frontier" developers based on compute and revenue thresholds.

## Quote

> If OpenAI's employees no longer feel like they can raise safety issues internally... we are all at greater risk that something truly catastrophic will happen.
