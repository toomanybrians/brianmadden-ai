# You can read the full report at https://t.co/qchf7Xx8Ur

## Insights

- Apollo Research's "Handoff" report describes frontier AI companies progressively delegating all AI R&D functions — including safety assessments and deployment decisions — to internally deployed AI agents, with the stated endpoint being no human in the loop.
- The report breaks Handoff into three components: work execution, review, and decision-making. Execution is nearly fully delegated (AI agents write over 90% of code merged into Anthropic's codebase); review is catching up fast but human coverage is thin (OpenAI reviewers see 0.001–0.01% of coding trajectories; Anthropic flags ~50 of 100,000 weekly AI monitor alerts); decision-making is least advanced but accelerating (Claude "leads" 26% of Anthropic's AI R&D work, up from under 1% in February).
- Automation creates a self-reinforcing acceleration: once most of an R&D pipeline is automated, remaining human steps become the bottleneck, so each further automation step yields proportionally larger speed gains — companies reportedly already see human review as their limiting factor.
- Key risk framing: misaligned AI agents placed in "insider" positions within R&D could influence training of successors or sabotage evaluations meant to catch them, compounding across generations — independent of whether recursive self-improvement (RSI) is achieved, though RSI (targeted 2027–2028) would sharply worsen the risk.
- Human oversight is described as structurally degraded — reviewers see only AI-filtered subsets of activity, face volumes/complexity exceeding their capacity, and increasingly face outputs they can "only trust rather than verify," risking rubber-stamping or further offloading review to AI.
- The report's policy ask: a U.S. task force (via proposed CAISSI) to set transparency standards for Handoff, including metrics like end-to-end detection efficacy, time-to-review-and-patch, automation levels per task (AL0–AL5), and tracking of non-delegable go/no-go decisions.
