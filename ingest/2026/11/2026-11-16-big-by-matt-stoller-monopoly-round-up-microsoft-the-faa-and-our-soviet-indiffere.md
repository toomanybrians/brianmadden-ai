# Monopoly Round-Up: Microsoft, the FAA, and Our Soviet Indifference to System Collapse.

## Insights

- Microsoft Outlook's 21-24 hour outage (affecting 450M+ commercial and 95M consumer subscribers) and the FAA's nationwide ground stop the following day were both attributed to "cascading failures" — small technical glitches that propagated through tightly-coupled, interdependent systems.
- Microsoft has cut roughly 35,000 jobs since October 2022, disproportionately targeting software engineers while increasing project management staff — a pattern the piece links to reduced institutional capacity to monitor and mitigate systemic risk.
- Satya Nadella stated up to 30% of Microsoft's code is now AI-written, a figure described as much higher at other firms including Anthropic; executives reportedly acknowledge the risk of small teams reviewing code they didn't write or have time to vet.
- Anthropic's pre-IPO S-1 filing is cited admitting the company "may deploy models whose behavior we cannot adequately verify" — framed as evidence that frontier AI labs are knowingly shipping systems beyond their own ability to monitor for harm or misalignment.
- The FAA cut its air traffic controller target by 2,000 even as air traffic rose 10% and controller headcount fell 6% since 2015; it rolled out an AI-assisted air traffic system (SMART) three days before the outage, over union and congressional safety objections.
- The piece frames weak antitrust enforcement and lack of corporate liability as root causes enabling monopolistic "chokepoints" (e.g., Microsoft's enterprise lock-in) that make cascading failures more likely and more damaging when efficiency is prioritized over redundancy.
