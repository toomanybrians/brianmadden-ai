# The Financial Engineering Essential for AI in 2027

## Insights

- GPU rental prices on short-term contracts have surged from roughly $3/hour to $12–13/hour in six months, pushing Anthropic's blended compute cost per GPU-equivalent hour from $3.10 to $5.65.
- Anthropic's Q3 2026 results show the strain directly: $5.0b of its $9.86b quarterly compute bill went to short-term compute, flipping a $974m operating profit in Q2 into a $567m operating loss in Q3 despite revenue growing to $16.96b.
- Structural supply constraints look durable rather than temporary: 40–50% of data center projects are delayed, GPUs sit idle waiting for power, and transformer/turbine lead times run three to five years (turbine production reportedly sold out through 2029).
- Frontier labs face a margin squeeze they can't easily resolve through pricing because competitive price wars make raising token prices risky (any hike cedes market share), forcing the burden onto efficiency gains instead.
- Labs are responding via three levers: efficiency improvements (e.g., a cited 40% cost-per-task reduction in a newer model version), tightening free/subscription usage allowances (OpenAI pooling ChatGPT and Codex into one shared token limit), and shifting enterprise customers from pay-as-you-go API usage to multi-year committed contracts.
- The core strategic shift framed for 2027: labs need to match the duration of customer contracts to the duration of their underlying GPU leases, rather than simply chasing raw GPU availability as in 2026.

## Quote

> In 2026, the race was to find GPUs at any price. In 2027, the winners will be the labs that match the duration of their customers' contracts to the duration of their compute.
