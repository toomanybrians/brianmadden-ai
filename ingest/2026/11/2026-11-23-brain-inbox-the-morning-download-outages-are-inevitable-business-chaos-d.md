# The Morning Download: Outages Are Inevitable. Business Chaos Doesn’t Have to Be.

## Insights

- A cluster of major tech outages (Microsoft 365 affecting 450M+ users, plus disruptions to air travel, banking, and national security comms) is prompting enterprise leaders to shift focus from outage prevention to organizational resilience.
- Tech leaders interviewed emphasize that security and IT can't make remediation decisions in isolation — cross-functional alignment (IT, security, risk, business, HR, procurement) before and during incidents is treated as essential.
- Recommended practices include pre-mapping critical systems and dependencies, running "uncomfortably realistic" tabletop exercises, and pre-assigning decision rights (who can shut down a system, who talks to customers/board) before a crisis hits.
- Several leaders stress building rollback/"undo" capability into IT systems and staged rollouts (testing changes on small user batches first) as practical resilience mechanisms rather than bureaucratic overhead.
- AI is described as double-edged: it's accelerating discovery of software vulnerabilities and enabling attackers to operate at "machine speed," while also offering defensive automation and help mapping complex systems — but automation must stay governed by human oversight.
- Context note: the National Cyber Director overseeing AI-related cybersecurity policy was replaced amid criticism of the administration's handling of the outages, following earlier concern over an Anthropic model ("Mythos") demonstrating cyberattack capability.

## Quote

> "Tested automation can accelerate recovery, but it must be governed by clear procedures and human oversight to avoid introducing additional risk." — Grant Yacomeni, Gigamon CISO
