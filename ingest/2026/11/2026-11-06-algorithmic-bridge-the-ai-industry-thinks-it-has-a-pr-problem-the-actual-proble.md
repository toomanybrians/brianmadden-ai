# The AI Industry Thinks It Has a PR Problem. The Actual Problem Is Much Worse

## Insights

- American hostility toward AI is sharply higher than in the rest of the world, where reactions skew toward cautious optimism or neutrality; the piece treats this as a distinctly US phenomenon ahead of the 2028 election.
- Many Americans simultaneously use AI daily and demand the industry be restrained — framed not as contradiction or confusion, but as people clearly signaling which trade-offs they're willing to accept.
- The AI industry's dominant response (treating backlash as a "messaging" or PR problem) is characterized as mistaken: the resentment stems from real, present-tense harms (job loss, cost of living, disrupted lives), not from miscommunication.
- Industry justifications are consistently deferred to the future tense (future cures, future abundance, future alignment), which fails to answer immediate harms experienced today — described as "changing the subject" rather than addressing the problem.
- A historical parallel is drawn to railroads: transformative technologies can still deserve populist fury over the terms of their arrival, independent of their eventual long-term value.
- Public hostility is reframed not as an obstacle to AI's future but as a possible corrective mechanism — something that could "deflate the bubble" and force better terms, rather than kill the technology outright.

## Quote

> People do not so much resent a lack of honesty as an unwillingness to do better today.
