# Build Where AI Labs Can’t Follow ♟️

## Insights

- Sequoia now frames the addressable AI opportunity against a $10–20 trillion services market rather than the ~$650 billion software market, a reframing the piece treats as the reason the "application layer" is bigger than skeptics assume.
- Frontier model releases are repeatedly killing off a category of AI startups — wrappers, copilots, "chat with your docs" tools, and horizontal agent builders — because labs are shipping AI coworkers, coding agents, SaaS connectors, app stores, and forward-deployed enterprise teams that absorb those use cases.
- The startups cited as winning (Harvey, Legora, EvenUp, OpenEvidence, Abridge, Basis, Sierra, Cursor, 11x, FurtherAI) share a pattern: they run regulated, multi-step workflows and own the customer's records or outcomes, rather than sitting as a thin layer on top of a model.
- The article's central claim is that defensibility against lab encroachment comes down to a single measurable property of a product — one that cuts across the usual vertical-vs-horizontal framing — such that getting it wrong means execution can't save a company, and getting it right means each new model release makes the product stronger rather than obsolete.
- U.S. business applications hit a record 5.67 million in 2025 (cited to Census BFS data), offered as evidence of market scale for AI tools serving businesses.
- The piece references a "4-layer system of work" and a 2x2 "Work Map" as frameworks for identifying which parts of a workflow labs have no advantage in, plus a 7-stage "encroachment sequence" describing how labs expand into startup territory.

## Quote

> Every model release now wipes out a cohort of AI startups.
