# Interface Friction Is AI's Most Underrated Problem

## Insights

- Miessler frames AI's practical value as a function of capability minus "interface friction" (the cost of context-switching to a separate AI app and back), proposing Usefulness = Capability - Friction, and estimates current state as roughly 6 - 5.
- He argues friction, not capability, is the binding constraint on AI's perceived value today, and expects usefulness to expand "exponentially" once friction drops starting around 2027.
- Claude Tag (launched by Anthropic, June 2026) is cited as a concrete friction-reduction example: it runs inside Slack, has persistent memory/context about the business, and can execute and schedule recurring tasks (e.g., a daily 9am report) without the user leaving the app or "doing an AI thing."
- He predicts the end-state is voice-first, always-on AI accessed through a personal "DA" (digital assistant) rather than discrete apps, citing OpenAI hardware efforts, Apple's Siri AI work, and sci-fi references (Her, Interstellar, Iron Man) as directional signals.
- Explicit advice to AI builders: the central design question should be "how can I reduce the friction of people using this," with the goal of making AI something people just talk to rather than a separate tool they visit.
