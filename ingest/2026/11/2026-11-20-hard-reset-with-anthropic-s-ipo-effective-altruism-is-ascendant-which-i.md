# With Anthropic’s IPO, Effective Altruism Is Ascendant. Which Is Bad.

## Insights

- Anthropic's IPO created mass wealth (average employee ~$40M, 400+ employees worth $100M+, co-founders at $20B+ each), concentrating capital among people with ties to effective altruism (EA) and longtermism.
- Multiple Anthropic co-founders have documented EA ties: Dario Amodei's involvement traces to 2008 via GiveWell; Daniela Amodei married GiveWell co-founder Holden Karnofsky; other co-founders lived together in an EA-interested SF house — despite both Amodeis publicly distancing themselves from the "EA" label in recent interviews.
- Anthropic structurally encourages EA-aligned giving: new hires are asked to commit equity to charity, and the company has a public benefit fund allocating shares to charitable/specific populations; post-IPO employee conversations reportedly center on directing new wealth to donation accounts, often EA-oriented.
- An Anthropic researcher (Tao Lin), self-described longtermist, predicts "superhuman AI" (better than top experts in any field) by end of 2027, estimates ~20% chance of human extinction from AI and under 50% chance of AI "taking over the world," and forecasts mass, permanent unemployment requiring state support.
- The piece frames EA/longtermist ideology as enabling a belief among AI leaders that they uniquely understand existential risk and are therefore justified in self-directed, utilitarian control over AI's development and its consequences.
- Separately noted: an AI evaluation study (Vals) found frontier models, including Anthropic's (which performed worst), misidentified civilians, ambulances, surrendering soldiers, and a school as military targets in simulated autonomous drone-strike scenarios.

> "People should be angry at me and everyone else developing AI... We live in a bubble, I won't meet those people." — Tao Lin, Anthropic researcher
