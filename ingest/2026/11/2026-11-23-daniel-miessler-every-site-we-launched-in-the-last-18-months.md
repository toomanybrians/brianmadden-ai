# Every Site We Launched in the Last 18 Months

## Insights

- The core economic claim: building a website has dropped in cost to roughly the cost of having the idea, which shifts the limiting factor from production effort to whether an idea is worth pursuing at all — explaining a jump from ~5 sites in 7 months to 35 sites in 5 months.
- Several sites are explicitly built as verifiable evidence infrastructure: quote archives tying every claim to a primary source (for Sam Altman, Dario Amodei, and Miessler himself), an AI-hacking-incident ledger with evidence grading, and a vulnerability dataset tied to real breach outcomes rather than raw CVE counts.
- A cluster of tools attempts to directly measure AI's effect on labor/output: "Deeds" reads commit histories to categorize AI-assisted work in repos (caps, fixes, tends), and "Who Does the Work?" simulates how concentrated a team's output is among few contributors.
- "Daemon" pages (for both Miessler and his AI assistant Kai) are built as machine-readable identity/status pages specifically so AI agents, not just humans, can parse a person's mission, goals, and projects.
- The post itself was produced almost entirely by an AI agent (Kai) — researching, screenshotting, writing, and designing — presented as a demonstration of the same AI-driven production shift the sites describe.
- Several entries are framed as public, falsifiable arguments or debates (open-source AI guardrails, a safety-labeling standard, Palantir's trustworthiness) rather than simple blog posts, each designed to let readers check sources and vote.

## Quote

> Now building one costs about as much as having the idea, so the question itself is what decides it.
