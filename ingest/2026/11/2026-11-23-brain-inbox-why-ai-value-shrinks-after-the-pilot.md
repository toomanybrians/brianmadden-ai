# ⚙️ Why AI value shrinks after the pilot

## Insights

- A vendor-sourced model (OneTrust) shows a $100M AI pilot opportunity shrinking to roughly $30M in production once reliability (80%), adoption (75%), and realized benefit (80%) are applied alongside operating costs and risk-weighted failure costs — still a 250% return, but far below the pilot-stage projection.
- The gap between pilot and production value isn't typically driven by catastrophic failure; it comes from compounding, unglamorous shortfalls in reliability, adoption, and benefit realization at scale.
- Gartner is cited projecting that by 2027, 60% of organizations won't realize expected AI value without a cohesive governance framework, with the usual root cause being fragmented ownership, inconsistent controls, and reactive (post-incident) oversight.
- In this framing, governance doesn't enlarge the AI opportunity — it determines how much of the original value is actually retained, improving production reach, oversight scalability, and early risk detection simultaneously.
- With stronger assumptions under integrated governance (90% reliability, 85% adoption, 85% realization), the same $100M opportunity nets ~$51M retained value (460% risk-adjusted return), with most of the gain attributed to better realization rather than cost-cutting.
- OneTrust's CFO frames governance as an enabler of bigger, faster bets: he'd rather fund two well-governed AI initiatives than ten loosely overseen ones, treating governance cost as nonnegotiable "insurance."
