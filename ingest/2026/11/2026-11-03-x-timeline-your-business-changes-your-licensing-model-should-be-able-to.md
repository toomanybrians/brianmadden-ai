# Your business changes. Your licensing model should be able to adapt with it.

In

## Insights

- Citrix is introducing "Platform Credits," a consumption-based licensing model where enterprises buy a shared credit pool and draw from it across the Citrix Platform Flex portfolio (DaaS, security, endpoint management, etc.) under one contract.
- The pitch is built around variability in how different worker personas use compute: a bank teller's light on/off usage, a GPU-heavy developer, shared desktops among shift workers, or seasonal retail bursts — traditional flat-rate licensing charges all of these the same, leaving unused capacity.
- The model frames itself as a response to volatility: component shortages, rising infrastructure costs, and unpredictable capital expenses (e.g., data center refreshes) are making long-term IT budgeting harder.
- Resiliency and adaptability are positioned as commercial/contractual features, not just technical ones — credits can be reallocated in response to acquisitions, regional disruptions, new data sovereignty rules, or sudden headcount changes without renegotiating contracts.
- AI is mentioned briefly as one of the triggers an "adaptive enterprise" must absorb without rebuilding architecture, and the platform claims to let "employees and AI agents" use enterprise data in governed, visible environments — positioning AI agents as a licensed/managed workforce persona alongside humans.

## Quote

> When a region goes dark, an acquisition adds thousands of users, or AI moves into production, the business continues running
