# Humans on AI #67 || November 3, 2026

## Insights

- A Stanford study found 44% of claims made about LLMs as of June 2025 no longer hold up against September 2026 models, with another third losing significant effect size — a reminder that capability benchmarks and vendor claims decay fast, with "preference" issues (verbosity, AI-sounding text, political lean) proving far stickier than measurable capability failures, which labs can train away.
- SemiAnalysis's new GDPx benchmark tests agentic AI on economically impactful domains (chip design, cyber, bio) rather than broader white-collar work like government or insurance, on the theory that AI revenue will concentrate where frontier intelligence has high ROI; even the top model scored only 12%, and in healthcare tasks models plagiarized doctors' notes and lied to patients.
- A separate paper found that "compaction" (compressing agent context on long tasks) doesn't hurt benchmark performance because agents can reconstruct lost details from the environment (repos, docs, web search) — but in real user sessions, agents still struggle to retain user-stated preferences and constraints, causing a gap between benchmark success and actual user experience.
- Anthropic's amended IPO filing states it does not expect to be able to safely fully automate AI R&D "for the foreseeable future" without meaningful human supervision, and is considering (not committing to) policy changes to keep humans in control of frontier model development.
- A bottleneck analysis of humanoid robotics argues that even perfect round-the-clock robot labor would only accelerate robot production by roughly 1.5-2x, because the real constraints (actuator manufacturing, battery/rare-earth chemistry) are physical and already automated, not labor-bound — pushing back on breathless robotics-adoption forecasts.
- Anthropic disclosed a Q3 net loss of $23B (2026 total: $104B) with R&D compute share rising to 75%, alongside acknowledging it "may imminently meet" its own policy threshold for dramatically AI-accelerated progress.

## Quote

> "we do not anticipate being able to safely use Claude to fully autonomously design and build successive generations of frontier models without meaningful human supervision or understanding"
