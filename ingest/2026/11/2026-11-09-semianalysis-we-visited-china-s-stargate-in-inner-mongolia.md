# We Visited China’s Stargate in Inner Mongolia

## Insights

- China's "Galaxy" campus in Inner Mongolia (owned by Envision Group) is being built at roughly one-fifth the cost of comparable US campuses like Stargate Abilene, with its first 120MW phase delivered in under 12 months.
- The developer, Envision, is a wind-turbine and renewable-energy company that has vertically integrated into datacenters — building its own wind farms, dedicated power lines, battery storage, low-voltage power modules, and prefabricated substations, rather than relying on third-party vendors.
- Galaxy uses an 800V DC solid-state-transformer architecture that converts grid AC directly to rack-level DC, skipping UPS/rectifier stages — a design still on the US roadmap but already running live in China, where roughly a quarter of recently built datacenters use DC power versus ~97% of US datacenters still on AC UPS.
- Cooling at Galaxy shares one water loop between liquid- and air-cooled servers with no central chiller plant, a northern-China design pattern offered as a real-world reference point for the US debate over chiller-free cooling (e.g., Nvidia's Vera Rubin claims).
- Chinese datacenter economics are intensifying competition: rents have roughly halved in five years (~$40/kW/month excluding power), capex is down ~25% (<$3M/MW), and Inner Mongolia power costs ~$0.055/kWh — pushing industrial players (energy, construction, battery firms) to vertically integrate into AI infrastructure as a differentiator, paralleling the BYD-style playbook from China's EV industry.
- A similar vertical-integration trend is forming via partnerships (e.g., CATL, VNET, Zhongheng Electric) rather than in-house builds, with CATL also holding stakes extending into AI model development (DeepSeek).
