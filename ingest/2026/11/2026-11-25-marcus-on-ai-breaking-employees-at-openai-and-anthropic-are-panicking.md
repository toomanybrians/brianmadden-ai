# BREAKING: Employees at OpenAI and Anthropic are panicking

## Insights

- Marcus argues model capability gains have consistently outpaced interpretability/control advances, across "hundreds" of models since GPT-4 — his long-running thesis, reasserted here rather than newly demonstrated.
- Central claim: giving LLMs agentic internet access has made them "fundamentally more dangerous" by combining unreliability with real-world reach, shifting the risk question from "how smart" to "how controllable."
- Cites Axios reporting (Madison Mills) that dozens of OpenAI/Anthropic employees are reportedly alarmed, with debate reaching the highest levels of these companies — Marcus flags this as potentially partly PR but likely reflecting genuine internal fear.
- Criticizes the absence of independent, FDA-style scientific review for AI releases, framing current self-policing as "neutered" and leaving release decisions in the hands of financially conflicted CEOs.
- Proposes a concrete near-term fix: voluntary withdrawal of internet-connected agents until safety solutions exist, plus an internal-consensus requirement before any new model release — with federal intervention as the fallback if companies won't self-regulate.
- Note: core evidence for "panic" rests on a single secondhand report characterized as "breaking," not on primary documentation — worth weighing against Marcus's default skepticism toward industry safety claims.

## Quote

> Generative AI has gotten harder to monitor, harder to control, and more reckless.
