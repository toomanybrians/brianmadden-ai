# A 700% Price Hike on A Key Component in Gaming Consoles? Blame A 25-Year Cartel.

## Insights

- DRAM memory chip prices rose roughly 700% over 18 months, driven partly by the three dominant manufacturers (Micron, Samsung, SK Hynix, ~90% market share) redirecting production from commodity DRAM to High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) sold exclusively to AI accelerator makers.
- A federal judge allowed a price-fixing class action against the three firms to proceed, finding their parallel conduct — synchronized production cuts, simultaneous DDR4 phase-outs, identical new customer-vetting procedures, and refusal to expand capacity despite record profitability — went beyond rational parallel market behavior ("hawks flying in formation").
- In October 2025, Samsung and SK Hynix reportedly locked up 40% of worldwide DRAM output for a single customer, OpenAI, without any corresponding plans to expand supply — illustrating how AI infrastructure demand is reshaping who gets access to a scarce, foundational hardware input.
- The same three companies pled guilty to a criminal DRAM price-fixing conspiracy in the mid-2000s (18 executives charged, $730M in fines), yet one convicted executive was later promoted to President of Samsung Europe — raising questions about deterrence and accountability in concentrated tech supply chains.
- Rising memory costs are cited by Microsoft and Sony as reasons for console price hikes and delays to next-gen hardware, and are estimated to be adding 8-15% to smartphone and laptop prices — showing how AI-driven component demand ripples into costs for ordinary enterprise and consumer computing equipment.
- The piece argues a 2007 Supreme Court standard (Twombly) made it structurally harder to bring price-fixing suits early, which it says allowed this DRAM oligopoly's behavior to persist largely unchecked for 25 years despite a prior criminal conviction.
