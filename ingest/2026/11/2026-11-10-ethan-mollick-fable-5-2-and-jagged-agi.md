# Fable 5.2 and Jagged AGI

## Insights

- Mollick proposes a working definition of AGI — "as good as most human experts at most intellectual tasks you can do with a computer" — deliberately excluding physical-world interaction, interpersonal work, and most real jobs where computer use is just one task among many.
- Testing Claude Fable 5.2 across six domains (filmmaking, hardware hacking, business launch, humanities course design, academic research, generative world-building), the model performed at or near human-expert level on individual tasks, but Mollick still calls the result only "almost AGI" — "Jagged AGI."
- The nature of the jaggedness has shifted: rather than factual hallucinations or math errors, failures now show up as lack of taste/judgment — a joyless course design, a bloated 182-page research paper, repetitive creative themes, and an AI-built "bureaucracy" of self-generated rules (87,500 words, 204 sections) that throttled its own output.
- The model's agents ran extensive self-checking (critics, fact-checkers, judges) but these checks only catch errors, not meta-problems like "is this too long, repetitive, or boring" — that judgment consistently had to come from the human.
- In an autonomous business-launch test, Fable produced an MBA-level business plan (and estimated its own 20-40% success odds) but Mollick stopped short of letting it contact real customers or spend money, suggesting a trust/autonomy ceiling even when planning quality is high.
- Mollick notes AI can now produce research output faster than anyone can verify it, flagging a emerging verification bottleneck for fields that rely on human review.

## Quote

> Knowing when your own work is not good enough is a large part of what makes someone an expert, and it is the part Fable does least reliably.
