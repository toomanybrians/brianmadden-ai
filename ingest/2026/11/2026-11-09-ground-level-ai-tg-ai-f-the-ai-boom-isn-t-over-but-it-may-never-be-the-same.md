# TG-AI-F: The AI boom isn't over. But it may never be the same

## Insights

- Backlash against AI data centers became a significant electoral factor in the midterms, even in a race otherwise dominated by tariffs, inflation, and the Iran war — candidates ran on data center moratoriums and construction pauses.
- Newly elected Ohio Democratic governor Amy Acton publicly framed data center expansion as exploitation of local communities, signaling a political stance against industry-friendly siting.
- Stock markets reacted sharply to the election results: the Nasdaq 100 fell 6.3% (wiping out $2.3 trillion) and the S&P 500 dropped 4.4% ($2.8 trillion lost), both the worst single-day drops since Trump's April 2025 tariff announcement.
- Investors appear to be pricing in doubts about the sustainability of the AI infrastructure boom now that politicians opposing data centers are winning office.
- The author frames this moment as communities gaining real leverage — demanding a say in what data center infrastructure gets built, where, and who bears the cost — a shift described elsewhere as the industry's "bargaining era."
