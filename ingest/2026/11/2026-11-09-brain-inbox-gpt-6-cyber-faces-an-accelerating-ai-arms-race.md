# ⚙️ GPT-6 Cyber faces an accelerating AI arms race

## Insights

- Deloitte's State of AI in the Enterprise report (3,500 global business leaders) finds a wide gap between AI activity and transformation: 81% used AI to speed decision-making, but only 25% say it has meaningfully transformed the business.
- Job redesign is shallow in most organizations — 60% have redesigned jobs around AI to some degree, but only 15% report extensive or complete redesign, and only 15% report a real shift in decision-making authority.
- Deloitte's head of AI argues real ROI requires redesigning work and operations, not just speeding up existing tasks — treating AI as a transformation initiative rather than a technology rollout (new workflows, governance structures, accountability mechanisms).
- Separately, Wharton's AI report finds ~40% of organizations measure AI returns by hours saved, while net-new revenue is the least-tracked success metric (27%) — suggesting most enterprises are still measuring efficiency, not business transformation.
- Deloitte reports enterprise revenue gains attributed to AI rose to 28% (from 20% in January), but warns this won't compound automatically without structural redesign — risk of reaching an "activity plateau" where spending rises without proportional value.
- Separate item: OpenAI released GPT-6 Cyber, a model aimed at cybersecurity defenders, which it claims is far better at staying within set operational boundaries following a prior safety breach (HuggingFace incident) — framed as part of an ongoing arms-race dynamic between AI capability growth and the capacity to secure/contain it.

## Quote

> "Without addressing those challenges, organizations risk reaching a plateau where AI increases activity and spending without producing proportional business value." — Jim Rowan, Deloitte
