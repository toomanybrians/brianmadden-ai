# Saving Money with ChatGPT

## Insights

- OpenAI estimates nearly 2 million Americans used ChatGPT in one week in October to look for ways to save money, with roughly 4 million savings-related messages sent that week (education ~900k; groceries/household essentials/meal budgeting ~700k; housing ~600k; recurring expense review ~600k).
- Usage examples cited include people asking ChatGPT to negotiate bills (internet, mobile, pest control, subscription services), plan budget-constrained meals, and research/compare car purchases (comparing models, drafting dealer emails, surfacing fees and lease equity).
- OpenAI's own framing: ChatGPT "can't solve the affordability challenge on its own" but helps people spot fees, compare offers, and prepare for negotiations.
- Separately, OpenAI's AI Sentiment Tracker (built with Codex) shows a narrowing but still net-negative view of AI among Americans: 56.3% negative vs 39.3% positive, a "negative-17" score improved from "negative-24" the prior month.
- OpenAI Academy ran "AI Skills Jams for Caregivers" in five U.S. cities (~400 attendees) teaching caregivers to use ChatGPT for scheduling, checklists, automating recurring tasks, finding resources, and prepping questions for medical visits; workshops also covered protecting personal health information and verifying AI responses.
- OpenAI analysis estimates over 40 million weekly ChatGPT messages in the U.S. relate to healthcare questions asked on behalf of someone else (i.e., caregiving-related).
