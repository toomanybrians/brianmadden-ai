# OpenAI, Meta Float Post-Midterm PR Strategy: More Condescension

## Insights

- OpenAI and Meta executives (Greg Brockman, Dina Powell McCormick) are responding to Republican midterm losses tied to data center backlash by framing the problem as one of messaging/PR rather than substance — Brockman says the industry needs to "do a better job of communicating" why data centers benefit local communities.
- Brockman's pitch leans on personal narrative (growing up near a smelly-but-valued sugar beet factory in North Dakota) to argue data centers are similarly disruptive-but-beneficial, while citing examples like OpenAI-sponsored free lunches in Georgia.
- Brockman continues to make sweeping near-term predictions about AI curing diseases and solving death within 12-18 months, a pattern the author argues undermines efforts to appear humble post-election.
- Meta's McCormick frames data center investment as needing to be "bipartisan" and locally driven, pointing to Richland Parish, Louisiana as a success story — but a prior NYT investigation documented a secretive, fast-tracked deal process with NDAs and limited public transparency.
- Louisiana's Senate race (won narrowly by a Republican who'd signed an NDA with a Meta shell company) is cited as evidence that data center deals are becoming a live political liability even in deep-red areas.
- The Pentagon's CTO is quoted separately calling the AI industry's problem fundamentally a "PR problem," echoing the same framing used by Brockman and McCormick.

## Quote

> "I think that helping people understand not just that data centers... the investment, the community benefits... AI technology is also something that is important for them."
