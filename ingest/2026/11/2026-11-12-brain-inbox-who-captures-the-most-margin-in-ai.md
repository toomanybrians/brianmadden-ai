# Who Captures the Most Margin in AI?

## Insights

- The AI value chain has five layers — power plant, data center operator, neocloud (GPU owner/operator), inference vendor, and AI application — each billing in a different unit, which the piece normalizes to dollars per watt per year to compare margin capture.
- At a 40% application gross margin, modeled gross profit splits roughly: power 3%, data center 5%, neocloud 28% (pre-depreciation), inference 24%, application 40% — applications and neoclouds capture the most margin, though neocloud margin shrinks sharply once GPU depreciation is included (to near $0–2/w/yr).
- Compute pricing is rising fast: Anthropic disclosed burst compute costs up to $13/GPU-hour, and CoreWeave's CEO confirmed new multi-year contracts are pricing at $20–25/w/yr versus a ~$9/w/yr blended fleet rate today — more than double current costs, raising the question of who absorbs higher compute prices.
- Pricing power across the stack will depend on whether applications can pass rising compute costs to end customers based on demonstrated ROI; an application saving 30% won't command the same pricing power as one saving 70%, and techniques like small models, fine-tuning, and routing are framed as margin-preservation tools.
- Vertical integration is actively consolidating margin: CoreWeave acquired Weights & Biases ($1.03B), OpenPipe, Marimo, and Monolith AI ($185M) to move into training/evaluation/enterprise workflows; Nebius spent over $1B in 2026 acquiring four companies to build an integrated "Token Factory" spanning search, optimization, scaling, and orchestration.
- The argument concludes that unlike the SaaS era, different vertical AI software markets will support structurally different gross-margin pools rather than a single industry-wide norm.

## Quote

> Selling capacity at more than $40m per megawatt annually while pricing for new infrastructure on four & five year contracts is moving to $20m to $25m per megawatt.
