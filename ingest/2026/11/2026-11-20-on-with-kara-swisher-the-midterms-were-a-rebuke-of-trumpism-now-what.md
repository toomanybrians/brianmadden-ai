## Insights

- A panelist states that 40 of 41 local ballot measures aimed at restricting data centers passed in the midterms, framed as evidence that opposition to data centers is now broad-based and geographically close to home ("a proxy" for discomfort with AI generally).
- Polling shows AI/data centers as unusually cross-partisan: panelists say more voters are unsure which party to trust on AI than on any other issue, and the issue hasn't "sorted" into one party the way abortion or immigration have.
- A panelist describes a partisan "switcheroo" on AI: Republicans, historically more skeptical of tech, reportedly became marginally more favorable, while AI grew significantly less popular among Democrats and independents over the past two years.
- Candidates visibly adjusted their messaging on data centers and AI mid-race as the issue rose in salience, according to the panel, with Democratic candidates James Talarico (TX) and Sherrod Brown (OH) specifically cited as benefiting from attacks tied to Elon Musk and AI/tech backlash.
- Despite the backlash, panelists report no public appetite for an outright moratorium on AI or data centers — survey respondents reportedly want more regulation and more deliberate rollout, not a full stop.
- A panelist frames the opposition coalition as united by shared skepticism but divergent underlying motives: job-loss fears, environmental/energy concerns, and environmental-justice objections to data centers being sited disproportionately in rural communities and communities of color.
