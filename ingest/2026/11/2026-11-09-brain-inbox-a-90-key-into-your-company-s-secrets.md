# A $90 Key into Your Company's Secrets

## Insights

- The cost of an AI model autonomously executing a full corporate network break-in (32-step, 20-host simulated environment) fell 99% in five months — from $5k–$10k in April 2026 to $90 with an open-weight model (GLM-5.3) by August.
- Attackers now run an expected-value calculation on compute spend; cheap defensive filters that block low-effort attacks push rational attackers to concentrate AI resources on the highest-value targets rather than spray smaller ones.
- Defensive AI is already compressing security operations work — autonomous investigation of alerts drops a 70-minute manual review to minutes at roughly $2 of compute per alert, addressing the historical problem of SOC teams being unable to review most of their daily alert volume (cited at 4,400+/day).
- Three factors are compounding the defensive compute burden: speed (intrusion response time dropping from months to minutes, breakouts as fast as 27 seconds), scale (a documented incident used a swarm of ~700 coordinated AI agents executing 17,600 actions over 4.5 days to harvest 136 production keys), and breadth (non-human/agent identities already outnumber human employees 82-to-1 in enterprise environments, a ratio expected to keep climbing).
- Framing shift proposed: a CISO's role is no longer buying layered "insurance policy" defenses per threat type, but continuously tuning defensive compute spend to keep the attacker's expected value negative.

## Quote

> The cost of breaking into a corporate network with AI has fallen 99% in five months.
