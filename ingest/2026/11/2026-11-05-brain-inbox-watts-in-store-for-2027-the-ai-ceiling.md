# Watts in Store for 2027 : The AI Ceiling

## Insights

- Physical compute buildout is hitting a hard ceiling: roughly 80GW possible by 2027, doubling the entire US compute base built to date — yet GPU rental costs still doubled in six months ($3 to $12-13/hr), showing demand is outpacing supply even as supply surges. Finished server racks are reportedly sitting unpowered because utility power isn't available to energize them.
- This points to a structural, not temporary, ceiling on energized compute — pricing pressure on GPU-hours is expected to persist rather than ease as buildout continues.
- A theoretical revenue ceiling is estimated for AI: today's ~38GW fleet earns ~$140B annually; new 2027 capacity (3-5x more powerful per watt) could add ~$350B, for a total near $500B — but this depends on assumptions about inference/training split and pricing per gigawatt.
- Rising GPU costs may force foundation model labs to shift GPU-hours from training (currently 60%) toward inference (currently 40%), potentially slowing frontier model development pace.
- Predicted consequences of compute scarcity: AI pricing may bifurcate by latency/speed (like internet bandwidth pricing), algorithmic efficiency innovation will be incentivized (cited example: a 27B-parameter model scoring near-parity with a 753B-parameter model), and "harness" software wrapped around models becomes critical — claimed to account for over half of system performance gains and able to cut task costs by 71% with no accuracy loss.
- Economic implication: as inference costs rise, off-the-shelf AI-embedded software may become cheaper than custom-built/maintained internal ("vibe-coded") software, affecting build-vs-buy decisions for enterprises.

## Quote

> When supply doubles & prices still double, demand is fundamentally outrunning physical reality.
