# Voters Hit Pause on Data Centers. What Happens Next?

## Insights

- In last week's elections, 41 local ballot measures on data centers were decided, with voters rejecting the industry in all but one (a small North Dakota town with 430 voters), despite tech companies and allies outspending opposition roughly 100 to 1.
- An NBC exit poll found 76% of voters said they would oppose a new data center being built in their community, signaling broad public sentiment against local buildout.
- The local ballot measures themselves have limited practical effect: they directly impact about 2 gigawatts of reported project capacity, roughly half a percent of the nationwide development pipeline.
- Governors matter far more than local votes: at least nine states now have a governor or governor-elect who has promised or imposed a pause on new data centers, and those states account for 41% of the national pipeline.
- States hold the most governmental control over data center approval, and that level of government shifted in many of the industry's key planned-construction locations following the election.
- The Nasdaq 100 lost $2.3 trillion in market value in a single day (its largest one-day loss on record), which the piece ties to investor reaction to the governors' race outcomes and pause threats.

## Quote

> For AI companies whose futures hinge on the data center buildout, this is very bad news — unless public sentiment drastically changes.
