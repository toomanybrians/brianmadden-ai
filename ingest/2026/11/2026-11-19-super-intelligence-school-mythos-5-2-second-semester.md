# Mythos 5.2 Second Semester

## Insights

- Mythos 5.2 and Fable 5.2 share the same underlying model, but Mythos grants verified organizations greater scope for specialist cybersecurity and life-sciences work, while Fable serves a broader audience with additional safeguards in those domains.
- The model is positioned for tasks requiring careful reading, technical judgment, and repeated verification — specifically security investigation and research work, not general use.
- The piece distinguishes between a model correctly identifying a problem and the harder, separate question of whether an organization can trust it to act on that finding (e.g., proposing fixes, confirming version scope, validating evidence).
- It highlights specific failure modes: a correct finding can still produce a bad repair, a passing test can leave the underlying flaw intact, and a research explanation can sound complete while resting on an unverified assumption.
- Benchmark gains are framed as useful for deciding where/how much to trust the model, not as proof of end-to-end reliability — the "details behind the gains" matter more than the headline scores.
- The full piece (not fully included here) reportedly walks through a security review case end-to-end, runs Python checks, and covers API usage, pricing, and access requirements.
