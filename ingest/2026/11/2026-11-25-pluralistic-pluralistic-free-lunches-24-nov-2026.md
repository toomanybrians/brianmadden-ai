# Pluralistic: Free lunches (24 Nov 2026)

## Insights

- Argues that persistently high profit margins and market dominance (not "market distortions") are themselves evidence of monopoly power blocking competition — directly applied to Big Tech's AI spending.
- Notes that multiple Big Tech firms "pissed away hundreds of billions of dollars" on stock buybacks and AI data centers that the piece claims can never turn a profit, framing this as proof of monopoly insulation from competitive discipline rather than business genius.
- Describes Amazon's profit structure: AWS is no longer its most profitable segment — the $80B/year "pay-to-play" search placement/ad business extracting fees from sellers has overtaken it.
- Describes Meta/Google's ad-tech duopoly as acting as both buyer and seller in markets they control, generating profits large enough to fund what the piece calls "AI misadventures."
- Claims U.S. Trade Representative policy for 25 years has exported legal protections (IP law, anti-reverse-engineering rules) that shield American tech monopolies from foreign competition, extending their market power globally.
- Frames the broader argument as: Big Tech's business model (nondisclosure/noncompete agreements, binding arbitration, regulatory capture, IP lock-in) blocks the "information flow" that would otherwise let smaller competitors or workers challenge its margins.

## Quote

> America's tech margins are the world's opportunity.
