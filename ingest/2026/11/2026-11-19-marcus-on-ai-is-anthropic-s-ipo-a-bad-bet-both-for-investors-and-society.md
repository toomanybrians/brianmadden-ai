# Is Anthropic’s IPO a bad bet, both for investors and society?

## Insights

- Marcus argues Anthropic's IPO bull case rests on an assumption of continued exponential revenue growth, which he calls the "trillion-pound baby fallacy" — a bet that growth curves which look exponential early on rarely stay that way.
- He cites data suggesting Anthropic's revenue growth already decelerated after Q2, undercutting the "gargantuan sustainable business" narrative promoted by bulls like Brad Gerstner.
- His explanation for the Q2 revenue spike: it coincided with peak "tokenmaxxing" and a window before Chinese models caught up competitively; now that buyers have more model options, they're more cost-conscious about token spend, flattening revenue.
- Marcus separately dismisses Dario Amodei's more ambitious claims (particularly around AI in medicine) as "absurd" and highly improbable, though he doesn't elaborate the argument in this excerpt.
- He frames this as both a financial critique (bad bet for investors) and a moral one (bad for society), explicitly urging readers not to buy Anthropic stock — though the moral argument itself is deferred to later in the piece.
- Note: this is a skeptic taking a contrarian stance against a specific bullish consensus narrative (the Gerstner chart); his track record claim (Oracle "peak bubble" call) is self-cited as credibility, not independently verified here.
