# 🧊 Who Is Hacking Europe's Supermarket Freezers?

## Insights

- A new SemiAnalysis benchmark (GDPx) testing AI agents on 206 high-value professional tasks (chip design, quant trading, law, cybersecurity) found top models scoring only 9.6–12%, with safety classifiers reportedly suppressing the scores of leading models like Claude Opus and GPT-6 Astra — suggesting current "frontier" capability on real professional work is still narrow even where hype is highest.
- Price-performance gaps are widening faster than capability gaps: a cheaper model (Gemini "Carbon") reportedly reaches ~90% of the top model's benchmark score at roughly a third of the per-task cost, a dynamic with direct implications for enterprise AI tooling decisions.
- A survey of 268 early-career mathematicians (MIT) found 75.5% believe their odds of staying in academic math have declined in recent months, with many citing demotivation from AI's encroachment on research work — a concrete data point on AI's psychological/career effects on a knowledge-work field.
- Anthropic quietly added prompt restrictions to Claude Code barring recreation of existing games, intended narrowly, but developers report the rule is also blocking legitimate professional work (client projects, reverse engineering) — an example of alignment/safety rules producing collateral restrictions on knowledge work.
- DeepSeek released an open-source coding agent (dsh-superagent) explicitly designed to minimize demands on human attention — confirming once, working silently, and only messaging when a decision is needed or work is done — reflecting a design philosophy around reducing human-in-the-loop overhead for agentic coding tools.
- Unrelated but context-setting: suspected low-skill cyberattacks disrupted supermarket refrigeration across seven European countries via unsecured systems/default passwords; no AI involvement confirmed, but framed alongside claims (from CrowdStrike's CEO) that AI increasingly gives small actors nation-state-level attack capability.
