# You don’t have to love powerlooms or hate hand weaving to recognize a powerloom

## Insights

- Uses the analogy of powerlooms replacing hand-weaving to argue that recognizing a technology's transformative capability doesn't require endorsing it or condemning the craft it displaces.
- Claims that many intellectual/knowledge-work tasks have historically resisted industrialization because they were considered "pure craft" — individualized, non-standardized work.
- Argues this assumption may no longer hold: such craft-like intellectual tasks could soon become subject to industrial logic (standardization, scaling, mechanization of process).
- Explicitly flags this as a difficult, disruptive transition ("not an easy change") rather than a purely positive or neutral shift.

## Quote

> You don't have to love powerlooms or hate hand weaving to recognize a powerloom when you see one.
