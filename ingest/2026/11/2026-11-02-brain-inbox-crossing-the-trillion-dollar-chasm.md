# Crossing the Trillion Dollar Chasm

## Insights

- Amazon (AWS) and SpaceX executives have both publicly set ~$1 trillion annualized revenue targets for their AI/cloud compute businesses, with AWS projected to hit it around 2032 and Google Cloud/SpaceX around 2030 under current growth-decay assumptions.
- Hyperscalers face a structural tension: their legacy cash-generating businesses (Google Search ads, Amazon retail/ads, ServiceNow seat licenses) must fund massive AI infrastructure capex while AI itself erodes those same legacy revenue streams.
- Google's Search & advertising growth decelerated to 12% YoY (from 19% in Q1) even as query volume hit five-year highs, because AI Mode queries run 3x longer but monetize at a lower rate than traditional search results.
- Amazon's ad growth also slowed (12% in Q3, down from 26% in Q2); Jassy claims external AI shopping agents aren't yet meaningfully diverting traffic from Amazon's search results pages, calling current agentic shopping experiences "not great."
- Microsoft stands out as the only hyperscaler where AI-driven ARPU expansion (Copilot, E5/E7 bundles) is outpacing the slowdown in seat growth, while ServiceNow's stock dropped 29% on investor fears that agentic AI will undercut seat-based software pricing.
- Market reaction to Q3 earnings was sharply negative for Amazon (-12%) and Alphabet (-12.6%), reflecting investor doubt that legacy cash flows can sustain capex spending long enough to realize returns on AI infrastructure investment.

## Quote

> The first $10t company will be the one whose new compute engine reaches $1t before its old monopoly pays the tab.
