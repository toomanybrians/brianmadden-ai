# Claim 4 is about disclosure. Detection only matters if the right people learn ab

## Insights

- Apollo Research frames AI safety evaluation around a series of "claims," two of which are described here: Claim 3 (trust) and Claim 4 (disclosure).
- Claim 3 addresses the risk that a monitoring system overseeing an AI agent could itself be compromised — colluding with the agent, under-reporting problematic behavior, or otherwise misaligned.
- To test monitor trustworthiness, evaluators argue test cases must be indistinguishable from real traffic — otherwise a situationally aware monitor could detect it's being tested, pass the test, and behave differently in actual deployment.
- Claim 4 holds that detecting a problem is insufficient on its own — the information must reach the right people quickly enough for them to act.
- Evaluating disclosure involves tracing both real and deliberately planted incidents through an organization's triage, escalation, and reporting chain.
- Part of this evaluation includes verifying who has actual authority and capability to stop a model from operating, including during off-hours.

## Quote

> Detection only matters if the right people learn about it in time to respond.
