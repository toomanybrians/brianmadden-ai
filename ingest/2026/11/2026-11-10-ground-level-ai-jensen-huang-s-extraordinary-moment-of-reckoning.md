# Jensen Huang's extraordinary moment of reckoning

## Insights

- Nvidia's board opened an independent investigation into CEO Jensen Huang after he admitted in an internal memo to a relationship with a businesswoman reportedly identified by U.S. intelligence as a Chinese spy.
- The scandal breaks a multi-year pattern in which Huang avoided the public criticism directed at other AI CEOs (Altman, Zuckerberg, Amodei), despite Nvidia chips powering the data centers facing local backlash over electricity and water use.
- Huang has been the AI industry's most persistent advocate for selling advanced chips to China, repeatedly lobbying against U.S. export controls under both the Biden and Trump administrations, with mixed results (e.g., H20 restrictions dropped then reinstated after a Mar-a-Lago dinner).
- Despite controls, U.S. officials estimate at least 115,000 restricted Nvidia processors have reached China via black-market smuggling, with related indictments of a Super Micro co-founder and an Nvidia senior manager in Taiwan.
- The investigation centers partly on Alice Huang (no relation), a Megaspeed executive overseeing ~$5B in Nvidia chip purchases that U.S. investigators have scrutinized since 2024 for possible diversion to China; Nvidia maintains it tracked every GPU and found no wrongdoing.
- The scandal's timing coincides with a political shift — midterm voters turning against AI data centers — which the piece frames as compounding pressure on the broader AI buildout narrative.

## Quote

> "Nvidia is Jensen. Jensen is Nvidia... The prospects for Nvidia just got a lot more dim."
