# 🔮 Don’t waste a good backlash

## Insights

- After the Democrats' midterm win, markets shed over $2.5 trillion from AI-adjacent ("picks-and-shovels") stocks on expectations of new scrutiny around AI safety, labor market impact, power concentration, and data centers.
- US voters passed 43 of 44 local measures to slow data center construction; the author frames this as genuine democratic engagement rather than a death knell for AI buildout, and argues it's actually healthy for the industry.
- The core leverage point in data center negotiations is time, not money: grid connection waits (~4 years average) dwarf construction time (18-24 months), and idle chips cost real money — each B300 chip can earn ~$8/hour and pay for itself in under a year.
- Communities sitting on long grid queues have significant negotiating power; the piece contrasts a Scottish deal (DataVita paying up to $100k/MW/year, running to $543M over 15 years) favorably against a New Mexico Oracle deal, calculated at roughly 18x stingier per megawatt.
- Data centers create very few permanent jobs relative to capital invested — around 16-20 jobs per billion dollars spent (Meta Louisiana, Anthropic NY/Texas examples) — making locally negotiated revenue levies more valuable to communities than job promises.
- The argument is that infrastructure operators can afford generous community levies given extreme per-gigawatt revenues (cited $10-40B/GW, with some deals near $50B/GW/year), meaning current community benefit asks are a small fraction of what's possible.

## Quote

> Getting the wrong end of the voters' ire is not a reason to stop building everywhere. It is a reason to strike a deal.
