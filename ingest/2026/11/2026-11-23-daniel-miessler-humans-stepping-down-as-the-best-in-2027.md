# Humans Stepping Down as the Best in 2027

## Insights

- Miessler frames 2027 as the year humans "step down" as the best across multiple domains: programming went first, followed by security testing (his own field), mathematics, and now music.
- He argues that valuing skills based on scarcity is an inherited evolutionary habit, not a fixed law, and questions whether it's worth continuing now that scarcity is disappearing for cognitive/creative skills.
- Uses chess as a precedent: a free app has beaten the world champion-level play consistently since 1997, yet human interest in playing and improving at chess has only grown — suggesting AI superiority doesn't necessarily kill human enjoyment of an activity.
- Draws an analogy to calculators and arithmetic: nobody feels diminished that calculators outperform humans at long division, implying some forms of AI superiority provoke no identity threat at all.
- Proposes that the sense of "loss" people feel as AI surpasses them isn't really about the output being beaten — it's about which part of the work (elite status, time invested, the output itself) a person has attached their identity to.
- Concludes that how much this transition "hurts" is substantially a matter of perspective/framing, not an inevitable consequence of AI capability itself.

## Quote

> Why did we value programmers so highly? Because there were so few of them who could do that job and do it so well.
