# After the Click: Who Gets Paid in the New Internet Era 💸

## Insights

- Alphabet lost roughly $500B in market value in a single session (Oct 29), followed by Reddit falling 29% despite revenue growing 53% in the same quarter — framed as two data points on the same underlying shift rather than unrelated stories.
- The traditional web value exchange (content → Google traffic → website revenue) is described as ending, replaced by a "toll economy" organized around three choke points: ownership of user intent, ownership of content that can't be faked, and ownership of transaction completion.
- Illustrative example: a shopper gets a full AI-generated answer (comparisons, pricing, a buy button) without visiting any website, then separately searches Reddit directly for human validation — meaning the answer engine keeps the query, Reddit gets trust-driven traffic but no referral credit, the original publisher whose content trained the answer gets nothing, and the merchant pays for a sponsored slot to get the sale.
- The piece positions itself as offering a layer-by-layer map of the "answer-first internet" value chain, five rules for operating in a post-click economy, and distinct playbooks for AI founders, traffic-dependent businesses, and investors.
- Argues traffic that disappeared from Google is not simply migrating to another channel (e.g., ChatGPT) — implying a structural loss rather than a redistribution of referral traffic.

## Quote

> For 25 years the web ran on a simple deal: Create useful content → Google sends traffic → websites make money. That deal is now over.
