# The Morning Download: Ready to Start Your Next Car Lease With an AI Chatbot? BMW Wants to Help.

## Insights

- BMW launched a ChatGPT app allowing users to browse local inventory, configure vehicles, build lease financing packages, and schedule test drives — though the lease itself must still be completed at a dealership.
- BMW executives frame this as defensive positioning: customers already research vehicle purchases via AI chatbots, so brands feel compelled to be present within those chatbot ecosystems or risk being bypassed in the "agentic commerce" shift.
- Accuracy/hallucination control was a major engineering focus, particularly around financial calculations (e.g., how down payments affect lease costs); BMW relied on extensive testing and guardrails rather than any stated technical guarantee.
- BMW receives only aggregate usage insights from the chatbot interactions, not transcripts of individual customer conversations, until a test drive is scheduled and synced to the dealer's CRM.
- Agentic commerce is raising brand anxiety more broadly — some companies (e.g., Amazon blocking Meta's Muse) are actively locking out third-party AI shopping agents rather than integrating with them.
- Separately, new Labor Department data cited in the issue shows professional/business services added 70,000 jobs (largest monthly gain since July 2022), with gains even in AI-exposed fields like consulting, accounting, and law — while job losses are concentrated in more routine categories (document preparation down 17% year-over-year, call centers and PR down ~4.7%, advertising agencies down 3.9%).

## Quote

> "AI is only going to become more important in shopping journeys, not less. So we have to be here." — Leigh Moynihan, BMW of North America
