## Insights

- A single-paragraph idea from a human (Ethan Mollick) was handed entirely to Claude to execute, producing 204 generated 3D worlds on a live site within twenty hours of initial build-out.
- The project scaled into a multi-agent production system: one "lead agent" owned the engine and shipped every release, coordinating 28 parallel teams ("crews") of agents building individual worlds, with Claude's own estimate of ~25,000 agents total involved over the project's life.
- Agents performed not just creation but quality control: judge agents scored each world (producing a 1-5 "band" rating used by the content picker), automated tooling rendered and visually checked every new/changed world for broken frames before release, and the full site was played at 10x speed as a pre-release check.
- The agents left behind an internal working dialect in code comments (referring to the lead agent as "the owner," teams as "crews," team leads as "designers"), suggesting a persistent, self-referential agent culture/workflow across the project's duration.
- The README itself was written by one agent (Claude) and fact-checked against the code by other agents, with human editing limited mainly to redacting internal references and trimming project files not meant for the public repo.
- A later human/agent audit found additional unlicensed or under-credited code (shader/noise functions) beyond what the project's own third-party notices file had tracked, prompting a license correction — a small data point on gaps in AI-driven attribution/compliance diligence.
