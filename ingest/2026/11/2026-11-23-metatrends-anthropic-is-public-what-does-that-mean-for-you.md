# Anthropic Is Public. What Does That Mean for You?

## Insights

- Anthropic's IPO priced at $83/share, closed day one at $97.17 (~$1.76T market value), raising ~$107B including a $26B placement from Google and Nvidia — described as the largest IPO in history, surpassing SpaceX's record set just 5 months earlier.
- Anthropic's revenue trajectory: $41M total in 2023 vs. $17B in Q3 2026 alone, with an annualized run rate of $81B by October 31, 2026.
- The time required for companies to reach $1B valuation has compressed sharply: ~21 years in 2000, ~11 in 2010, ~4.5 in 2020, ~1 year today; some AI startups now raise at multibillion-dollar valuations pre-product or pre-incorporation, based on founder pedigree alone.
- A cited Kurzweil claim: 2026–2036 will see as much change as the prior 100 years combined (Model T to Starship in a decade) — framed as entering "hyper-exponential growth."
- Wealth concentration critique addressed: Bloomberg Businessweek estimates SF AI-startup insiders hold ~$1.2T in equity (average Anthropic employee stake ~$72M at IPO midpoint), and SF absorbs over a third of global VC; author argues this money will recirculate rapidly via lockup expirations, borrowing against equity, and reinvestment by new angel investors into AI, robotics, biotech, and other frontier sectors.
- Anthropic's seven co-founders pledged 80% of personal equity to philanthropy; the IPO allocated up to 20% to retail investors (vs. typical 5–10%), framed as broadening public access to frontier AI ownership.
- Practical framing offered: entrepreneurs should prioritize assembling exceptional teams and a "moonshot-sized vision"; individuals should use accessible AI tools and build independent ventures to regain agency over their own livelihoods rather than relying on traditional jobs.

## Quote

> Concentrated wealth that sits still is a problem. Concentrated wealth that reinvests at this speed is an accelerant.
