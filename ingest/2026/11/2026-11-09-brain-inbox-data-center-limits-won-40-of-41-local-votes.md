# Data Center Limits Won 40 of 41 Local Votes

## Insights

- Of 41 local data-center ballot measures tracked nationally on November 3, voters sided with restrictions (bans, moratoriums, added review requirements) in 40 — the only exception was Harwood, ND, where a referendum tied to an Oracle/Applied Digital campus failed to pass.
- Across all measures, the restriction side won roughly 73% of over 250,000 votes cast, with the median measure passing at 77%; five of the successful measures were outright bans.
- These votes are not necessarily representative: ballot measures only arise where residents organize signature drives or councils refer them (11 of 41 here were council-referred), and half the measures were concentrated in Ohio.
- Broader polling shows similar sentiment: a Gallup survey found 71% of Americans oppose having an AI data center in their area, compared to 53% opposing a local nuclear plant.
- Despite this opposition, salience is low relative to other issues — NBC's midterm exit poll found AI/data centers were rated "crucial" or "a major factor" by 56% of voters combined, the lowest of five issues polled.
- The piece frames local consent (alongside chips and electricity) as now a hard constraint on AI infrastructure buildout, and sets up a claim-by-claim fact-check of common objections (power prices, water use, noise, pollution, grid strain, jobs, tax exemptions, land use, secrecy) — though the detailed analysis is behind a paywall.

## Quote

> Until now the hard limits on the AI buildout were chips and electricity; local consent has joined them.
