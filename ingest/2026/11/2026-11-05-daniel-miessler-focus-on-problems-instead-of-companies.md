# Focus on Problems Instead of Companies

## Insights

- Large and mid-sized companies are reacting to AI-driven disruption with fear and panic rather than malice, producing contradictory and erratic workforce policies (hiring freezes paired with hiring pushes, mandates to use AI followed by walkbacks).
- This instability stems from companies not knowing whether they'll exist in 2-10 years and being unable to prioritize which threats to address, leading to constantly shifting strategy rather than a stable plan.
- The argument reframes work: companies are just bloated, inefficient vehicles for solving problems, and AI now lets individuals or small teams solve the same problems companies solve, but faster and with less overhead.
- Individual value is framed as "taste" — a person's unique perspective and approach to solving a problem — which is argued to be increasingly more valuable than being an interchangeable skilled employee inside a large organization.
- Recommended practical shift: don't quit a job, but start identifying problems your skills/experience qualify you to solve, find people who have those problems, and test offering solutions (product or consulting) on the side.
- Underlying structural claim: businesses provide jobs only because they need people to make money, not as an obligation, so job security erodes as AI reduces that need.

## Quote

> Businesses don't owe people jobs—they provide jobs because they need people to make their own money.
