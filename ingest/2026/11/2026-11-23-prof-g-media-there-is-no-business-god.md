# There Is No (Business) God

## Insights

- Nvidia's board ousted Jensen Huang over a relationship with a subordinate and a customer suspected of Chinese espionage, after initially trying to protect him; a petition with 200,000 signatures (10,000+ claiming to be Nvidia employees/shareholders) demands his reinstatement, which the board has rejected.
- Draws on Ernest Becker's "immortality projects" theory: humans build businesses, nations, and ideologies to cope with mortality, and transfer childhood dependence on parents onto charismatic leaders/founders as substitute gods.
- Argues Silicon Valley has become a literal "Technopoly"/religion (citing Postman, Epstein, Harari's "Dataism") with its own messianic language — chief evangelists, Musk's "Technoking" title, Altman's "magic intelligence in the sky," Andreessen's manifesto "patron saints."
- Notes a structural trend: founder-god companies concentrate all communication and decision-making through one person (Huang had ~60 direct reports and called 1:1s "impossible"), while essential operators (e.g., Gwynne Shotwell at SpaceX) remain invisible.
- Connects this to governance mechanics: rising use of super-voting shares (35% of 2025 US IPOs, 64% of tech IPOs, up from 12%/36% in 2016) entrenches founders as unremovable, framing this as "the ultimate immortality project."
- Contrasts Nvidia's cheap forward P/E (9.2 vs. S&P's 18.3) despite blowout earnings with Anthropic's record $1.5T IPO valuation under a seven-founder super-voting structure — framed as a bet on whether AI capex will pay off and whether founder-god governance is sustainable at scale.
