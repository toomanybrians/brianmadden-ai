# For authorization:
-Which consequential go/no-go decisions are reserved for huma

## Insights

- Frames AI agent decision-making as splitting into two sub-components: direction-setting (what research/action paths to pursue) and authorization (whether a consequential action is approved to proceed).
- For authorization, proposes tracking which go/no-go decisions are explicitly reserved for human personnel rather than delegated to AI.
- Proposes a specific audit practice: deliberately feeding AI agents flawed proposals for reserved decisions and measuring how often humans catch and override them — a test of human oversight robustness.
- Flags as a distinct risk category: cases where an AI agent made a "reserved" decision anyway, i.e., bypassed the human-authorization gate.
- For direction-setting, proposes Automation Levels (AL0–AL5) per task, plus explicit affordances/permissions (e.g., compute access) defining what AI agents can do autonomously without human approval.
