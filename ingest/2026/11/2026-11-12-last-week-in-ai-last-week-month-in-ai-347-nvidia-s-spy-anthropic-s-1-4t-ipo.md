# Last Week (Month) in AI #347 - Nvidia's spy, Anthropic's $1.4T IPO, AI's blue wave

## Insights

- Anthropic's S-1/A filing added a risk disclosure warning it "may imminently meet" its own Responsible Scaling Policy threshold for recursive self-improvement (dramatically accelerated AI-driven progress), while admitting it has historically failed to detect such acceleration until after it occurred.
- Anthropic's assessment partner METR found internal models were already speeding up the company's own AI R&D by roughly 2x, prompting Anthropic to consider revising its scaling policy to keep humans in control of frontier model development.
- Anthropic's IPO filing shows run-rate revenue hit $81B (end of October) with nine-month net loss of $104B (mostly non-cash charges) and compute commitments near $900.9B against ~$132B cash on hand — illustrating the scale of spend/burn behind frontier AI labs.
- Enterprise/consumer AI spending growth may be decelerating: Ramp data shows token spending grew only 1.5% across August-September versus 42% growth in July, cited alongside a broader "neocloud" stock selloff over debt-funded data center buildouts.
- Frontier labs (Google, OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral) are explicitly positioning new model releases around cybersecurity/offensive-defensive capability (bug discovery, exploit generation), with Preparedness Framework risk classifications rising (e.g., GPT-6 Cyber moved from "High" to "Critical").
- Public/political backlash against AI infrastructure (data centers) is emerging as a material business risk: a wave of US midterm results was driven substantially by anti-data-center sentiment, and new state-level AI safety regulation (e.g., NY's RAISE Act) is taking effect, requiring safety frameworks and incident reporting from large AI developers.

## Quote

> Anthropic states that no current model has been designated as exceeding the threshold, and that it does not expect Claude to be able to design successive frontier models on its own, safely, without meaningful human supervision.
