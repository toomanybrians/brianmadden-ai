# 🛡️ AI Hacking Incidents and the OFFSEC Harness Index

## Insights

- Daniel Miessler launched hackingincidents.ai, a tracker of cyberattacks since 2023 where AI was confirmed or suspected to be used, with each incident graded by evidence level (confirmed/strong/suspected) and tagged by model used and AI's role.
- The tracker shows a sharp rise in AI-driven attacks: in 2024, 0 of 27 reported incidents involved AI actually running the attack; in 2026 so far, 78 of 174 incidents do — suggesting a shift from AI-assisted to AI-executed attacks.
- A companion site, offsecharnesses.ai, catalogs open-source offensive AI agent harnesses verifiable on GitHub and cross-references which ones have appeared in real-world attacks.
- Of 154 tracked harnesses, 14 have been used in actual attacks (by 26 distinct attacker groups), and 7 are tied to confirmed breaches — indicating a small but real subset of open-source AI tooling is being weaponized.
- The two sites are linked so users can trace a specific harness/tool to the documented incidents it was used in, built from vendor, IR firm, CERT, and government reports.
- Miessler frames the project as an attempt to empirically answer "how much are attackers really using AI, and with what tools" rather than rely on speculation.

## Quote

> In 2024, none of the 27 reported attacks describe AI running the attack. In 2026 so far, 78 of 174 do.
