# Executive Briefing: If the Data-Center Backlash Leaves Fewer AI Suppliers, Who Will Your Company Rent Capacity From in 2027, and at What Price?

## Insights

- Local ballot measures in early November showed strong opposition to new data centers (e.g., over 75% support for a prohibition in Edgewater, FL), while an October poll found 72% opposed a data center in their own community.
- Despite infrastructure backlash, AI usage keeps climbing — weekly adult usage rose from 52% (February) to 65% (May), per Edison Research/SSRS survey data.
- The piece argues a backlash against local data-center construction will consolidate supply: developers facing delays and high debt loads (e.g., projects carrying $500M in debt) may be forced to sell cheaply to larger, cash-rich operators, reducing competition and favoring Big Tech incumbents.
- Slower construction timelines are framed as reducing overbuilding risk, giving demand time to catch up with supply.
- Blocked data centers domestically may push investment and jobs to other jurisdictions (Canada, EU, UK, Singapore) actively courting developers — the argument being that keeping a data center out of a town doesn't keep AI out of the workplace.
- The author frames the backlash partly as a messaging failure by the AI industry — people's economic anxiety about AI's impact on jobs converts opposition to data centers into a proxy vote for protecting economic opportunity.
- Companies are advised to audit AI commitments that depend on capacity from projects that may not materialize, and to pressure-test alternatives within a 90-day window.

## Quote

> A vote against a data center starts to feel like a vote for the American dream.
