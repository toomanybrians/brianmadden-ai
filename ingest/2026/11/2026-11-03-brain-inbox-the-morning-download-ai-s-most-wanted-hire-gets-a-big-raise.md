# The Morning Download: AI’s Most Wanted Hire Gets a Big Raise

## Insights

- "Forward-deployed engineers" (FDEs) — technical staff embedded with clients to get AI systems into production — have become one of the hottest-paid roles in tech, with elite practitioners earning $586K–$1.45M total comp, per executive search firm Christian & Timbers.
- "Elite" FDE status is defined by having shipped agentic AI into production with at least $10M in documented enterprise impact, ability to consult on strategy, and experience building/leading an FDE team; roughly 80% of such people have a Palantir background.
- Enterprises (Target, Barclays, Bausch and Lomb, American Express) are now hiring FDEs directly as full-time staff rather than relying on vendor-supplied talent, aiming to turn "ambiguous goals into production agentic systems."
- This has triggered a three-way talent scramble among enterprises, tech vendors, and consulting firms: OpenAI and Anthropic have launched dedicated FDE services units, Microsoft and Amazon run internal programs, and Google/Accenture are jointly training up to 1,000 FDEs; demand is expected to persist through at least 2028.
- Underlying pressure driving this: Gartner data cited shows 44% of CEOs struggle to demonstrate AI-driven financial returns to stakeholders, and 58% of CIOs report rising pressure to deliver cost improvements from AI.
- Separately, Palantir's Q3 revenue rose 96% (U.S. commercial revenue up 148%, now exceeding government revenue), which CEO Alex Karp attributed to businesses wanting direct control over AI rather than depending on AI labs — though analysts warn frontier LLMs may increasingly replicate Palantir's data-integration work more cheaply.
