# The Morning Download: Enterprise AI Is on the Ballot Too

## Insights

- Tech policy fights nominally aimed at frontier AI developers (curbing model capabilities, slowing data center build-out) risk spilling over into enterprise AI deployment even though most CIOs aren't tracking midterm outcomes directly, per KPMG's Todd Lohr.
- Because AI is a general-purpose technology, regulations framed as targeting AI companies can inadvertently sweep in unrelated industries — e.g., age-verification laws for AI chatbots could capture ordinary enterprise customer-service bots, per Cato's Jennifer Huddleston.
- Absent a federal framework, a state-by-state regulatory patchwork creates interstate complications for enterprises around data access, product development, and availability of AI tools — issues inherently cross-border in nature.
- Legal challenges are already underway (xAI vs. Colorado and Minnesota AI policies), suggesting courts may end up resolving some of this regulatory ambiguity rather than legislatures.
- Labor-aligned policy pushes are emerging around job guarantees and union input into how AI is deployed, including proposals to ban companies from eliminating jobs purely via AI substitution — framed as popular with voters (Guardrails Alliance's Shaunna Thomas).
- Beyond regulation, there's a consumer-sentiment dimension: some advocates argue customers actively prefer interacting with real humans over AI in products and services, a factor enterprises may need to weigh alongside compliance.
