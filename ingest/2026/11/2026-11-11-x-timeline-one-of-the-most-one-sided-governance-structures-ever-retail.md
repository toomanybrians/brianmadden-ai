# one of the most one-sided governance structures ever; retail gets screwed 

tota

## Insights

- Anthropic's IPO filing reportedly includes a governance structure in which the company remains controlled by two shares valued at less than a penny each, while public (retail) shareholders get almost no ability to influence corporate decisions.
- The claim is that this voting structure is being read in detail (via the IPO's voting section) by a third party (@Ric_RTP), who characterizes it as one of the most one-sided governance arrangements seen.
- Marcus frames this as evidence of hypocrisy: a company that publicly positions itself as safety-focused/ethical shows, in his view, its "true colors" when financial control and shareholder power are at stake.
- The underlying structural claim: retail investors in the IPO would have capital exposure without corresponding voting/control rights, concentrating power with existing insiders.

> "Anthropic is about to sell the public a trillion-dollar company controlled by 2 shares worth less than a penny." — @Ric_RTP
