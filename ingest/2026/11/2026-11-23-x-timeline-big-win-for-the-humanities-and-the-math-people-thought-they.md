# Big win for the humanities. And the math people thought they had the key to ever

## Insights

- Argues that the surprising success of LLMs is actually a win for the humanities, not just for math/computer science, because the systems work by modeling language, not pure numerical abstraction.
- Claims matrix multiplication alone is not what makes LLMs powerful — it's that this computation is applied to representations of human language specifically.
- Points out that the foundational "textbooks" of knowledge (implicitly: human knowledge generally) were themselves written in words, reinforcing language's centrality.
- Raises the broader claim (via a quoted/replied post) that human language has proven "unreasonably effective" at modeling the universe, to the point that next-token prediction training produced systems with this much capability.
- Frames this as unexpected: a discipline often seen as less rigorous than math/STEM (humanities, language study) turns out to be foundational to the technology's power.

## Quote

> It is amazing that human language turned out to be so unreasonably effective at modeling the universe.
