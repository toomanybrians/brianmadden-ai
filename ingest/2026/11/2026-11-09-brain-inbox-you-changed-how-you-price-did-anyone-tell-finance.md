# ⚙️ You changed how you price. Did anyone tell finance?

## Insights

- AI has broken traditional software pricing models (seat/license/annual contract), with companies like Intercom (per resolved conversation) and Salesforce/Agentforce (credits per action) moving to outcome- and usage-based pricing.
- Revenue recognition rules (ASC 606) were designed for fixed-price contracts and don't cleanly map to these new pricing structures, forcing judgment calls on finance teams.
- A key ambiguity: when a product is built on a third-party frontier model and passes through most of the revenue, companies must decide whether to report gross revenue (full customer payment) or net revenue (amount retained) — same underlying business can show 3x difference in reported revenue depending on this call.
- Pay-per-outcome pricing means revenue can't be booked upfront at contract signing (e.g., sales wanting to count a full year of ticket resolutions immediately); it must be recognized incrementally as outcomes actually occur, since resolution rates are uncertain for new customers.
- Prepaid credit packs introduce another wrinkle: unused/expired credits aren't recognized as a lump sum windfall — revenue is recognized gradually, and in some jurisdictions unclaimed balances legally belong to the state rather than the company.
- The piece argues these aren't edge cases but routine friction points that recur at every financial close once usage-based AI pricing is adopted, and suggests AI agents themselves (ingesting contracts, running rev-rec, drafting auditable reasoning) are being positioned as the solution to managing this complexity at scale.
