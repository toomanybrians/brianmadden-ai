# Policymakers need visibility into Handoff today.

Almost everything we know abou

## Insights

- Apollo Research defines "Handoff" as frontier AI companies' ongoing delegation of all AI R&D functions — including safety assessments and deployment decisions — to internally deployed AI agents, with three components: work execution, review, and decision-making.
- By their account, delegation of work execution is nearly complete (AI agents write over 90% of code merged into Anthropic's codebase); review delegation is catching up fast (human personnel reportedly see only 0.001–0.01% of internal coding trajectories at OpenAI); decision-making delegation is earliest-stage but accelerating (Claude allegedly "leads" 26% of Anthropic's AI R&D work, up from under 1% in February).
- The report argues Handoff creates compounding risk through two mechanisms: placing potentially misaligned agents in a privileged "insider" position within AI R&D pipelines, and simultaneously degrading human oversight capacity (reviewers "rubber-stamping" AI work or handing review off to more AI agents).
- Automation is framed as self-reinforcing: once most of AI R&D is automated, remaining human steps become the bottleneck, incentivizing further automation — cited example: "Claude sessions amount to around 80% idly waiting for human input" at Anthropic.
- Nearly all current knowledge of Handoff's progress comes from voluntary, inconsistent company self-disclosure; the report calls for a government-run (CAISSI) transparency standard with specific proposed metrics — e.g., "end-to-end detection efficacy," "time-to-review-and-patch," automation-level taxonomies (AL0–AL5), and lists of non-delegable go/no-go decisions.
- Risks are described as independent of achieving recursive self-improvement (RSI), but companies reportedly target RSI for 2027–2028, which the report says would compress years of AI progress into a much shorter window and further erode ability to detect loss-of-control incidents.

> Almost everything we know about Handoff comes from the companies themselves, disclosed voluntarily, sporadically, and each by its own metrics.
