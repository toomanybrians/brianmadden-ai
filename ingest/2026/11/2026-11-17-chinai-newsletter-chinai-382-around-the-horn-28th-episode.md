# ChinAI #382: Around the Horn (28th episode)

## Insights

- China's robotics manufacturing sector has seen roughly 30 companies face severe operational crises in the past two years, suggesting a shakeout among hardware startups.
- Compute scarcity is viewed as the main barrier to Chinese tech giants (BAT) building competitive "personal agents," since always-on agents retaining context and running long tasks are token-hungry; one account describes Tencent renting GPUs from a rival and the rival later clawing them back (paying a penalty) due to intensifying scarcity.
- Egocentric (first-person camera) data collection has become a paid side-gig in embodied AI training — factory workers reportedly earn extra pay (cited as ~$10/hour) wearing head-mounted cameras to generate training footage for robotics.
- DeepSeek released a research prototype "super-agent" that supervises and coordinates multiple sub-agents, reporting results back to a human controller — described as a step toward layered AI-managing-AI systems.
- A hedge fund manager's view holds that the real risk in AI infrastructure isn't a bursting bubble but the end of a three-year hardware bull market, since trillions already invested would require demand growth in AI services that may not materialize.
- AI-generated content made up 70% of Douyin's top 100 trending short dramas as of August, coinciding with reported job losses among human short-drama workers; separately, Chinese computer science graduates face a historically tough job market, with some programmers shifting toward electrical engineering/factory-floor roles amid AI-driven structural changes and prior CS program over-expansion.

## Quote

> Every unit of compute allocated to a personal agent is one less unit available for other purposes.
