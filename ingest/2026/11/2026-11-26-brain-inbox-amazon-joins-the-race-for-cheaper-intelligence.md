# ⚙️ Amazon joins the race for cheaper intelligence

## Insights

- Anthropic's research (58 enterprise customers, ~300 use cases across finance, real estate, medicine, engineering, logistics, media) found the biggest organizational gains come from using AI as a force multiplier for existing employees, not from headcount reduction.
- The largest current use case is extending coding agents/software development capability to nontechnical roles, creating value through increased output rather than cost-cutting.
- AI hasn't caused significant layoffs so far, but is slowing new hiring and reducing reliance on contractors, especially offshore ones.
- Two bottlenecks limit AI gains: employee resistance to deployment, and tasks AI hasn't sped up yet (code review, sales, decision-making), even though code creation itself is faster.
- Top-down automation mandates breed employee anxiety and resistance from the experts needed to build durable workflows; bottom-up adoption is inconsistent. Anthropic says the most value comes from "meaningful reorganization" — restructuring job roles and task ownership rather than applying a fixed approach.
- Separately: Amazon launched Strands Decider v2, open "decision models" (2B–31B parameters, built on Gemma 4) that return probabilities across multiple-choice options instead of generating text — part of a broader industry trend (following Jev) toward cheaper, narrower models for agent-routing decisions, which could undercut frontier labs' token-based revenue economics.

## Quote

> "The wide variation in how employers are deploying the same technology suggests they have some say in how AI changes work." — Anthropic
