# ⚙️ Trump’s tax threat tests tech’s global AI plans

## Insights

- Trump proposed an unspecified "tremendous tax" on US companies building AI data centers abroad, framed as preventing China from capturing that infrastructure, though no administration details or scope have been worked out yet.
- Hyperscalers have tens of billions in overseas AI data center commitments already in motion (Google ~$15B each in Malaysia and Finland; Microsoft in Australia/Japan/India; Amazon in Spain/Germany/India), creating potential conflict with the proposed tax.
- Domestic data centers have become politically contentious, with public backlash and the topic emerging as a hot issue in recent US midterm elections, raising the prospect of a wave of state-level regulation affecting AI labs' compute strategies.
- One possible effect of a tax on overseas data centers: it could push AI companies toward less compute-intensive model architectures if expanding physical infrastructure becomes harder or costlier.
- Separately, a Chargebee CEO interview argues that AI-era reinvention for SaaS companies requires more than adding AI features — it demands rethinking business models, pricing (hybrid vs. pure usage-based), and how humans and AI agents collaborate on work like finance, where accuracy below 100% still requires human accountability and traceability.
- A hardware review notes new MacBook Pro hardware (M5 Max, up to 128GB unified memory) is now capable of running local AI models (4B–70B+ parameters) via tools like Ollama and LM Studio, enabling private, token-budget-free local inference for knowledge work.

## Quote

> "We’re not allowing data centers to go into other countries because China will take all of them, and we’re not allowing that to happen." — Donald Trump
