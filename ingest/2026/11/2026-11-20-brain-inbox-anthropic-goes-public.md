# Anthropic goes public

## Insights

- Anthropic's Nasdaq IPO raised $81.3B — the largest IPO ever — closing with a $1.76 trillion valuation, making it the largest public benefit corporation and one of the largest public companies overall.
- The IPO sharpens a core tension for frontier AI labs: Anthropic's founders have repeatedly warned their own technology could be catastrophic, yet the company just converted that technology into the biggest capital raise in history.
- A METR audit found Anthropic's AI-driven R&D rate had roughly doubled — which by the company's own Responsible Scaling Policy should trigger a self-declared "Automated R&D" risk threshold requiring new safety auditing and transparency, but the policy is non-binding.
- After California's SB 53 passed requiring companies to publish legally binding safety policies, Anthropic reportedly submitted a weaker framework that recast its more ambitious safety commitments as voluntary rather than required.
- Financial analysis cited in the piece estimates Anthropic would need over $600B in annual revenue to match peer returns on invested capital (vs. Apple's ~$478B revenue), raising questions about how the scaling/investment trajectory squares with Amodei's stated intent to "pace" development.
- Early investor Dustin Moskovitz publicly stated he invested in Anthropic (and earlier OpenAI) "to promote safety, not racing" and now argues the whole frontier industry should slow down; he and his wife donated their Anthropic stock to a nonprofit, forgoing personal profit.
- The IPO coincided with a 100+ person protest at Anthropic's SF headquarters and a public "MisAnthropic Statement" signed by figures across the AI debate (from AI skeptics to accelerationists) accusing the company of using safety branding while racing toward riskier capability and lobbying for weaker regulation.

## Quote

> "Anthropic is not a safe AI company, and we must reject its reckless attempt to amass power in the name of the greater good." — The MisAnthropic Statement
