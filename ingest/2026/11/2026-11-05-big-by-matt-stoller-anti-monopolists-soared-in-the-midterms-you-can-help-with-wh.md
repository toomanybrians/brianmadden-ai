# Anti-Monopolists Soared in the Midterms. You Can Help With What's Next.

## Insights

- Pro-AI political spending collapsed under populist backlash: the AI-industry SuperPAC "Leading the Future" (backed by VC Ron Conway and lobbyist Chris Lehane) fell apart as anti-monopoly, anti-Big-Tech messaging gained traction with voters.
- Data center construction was rejected at the ballot box in every one of 41 local measures tracked across eight states, often by 75-87% margins, including in low-income communities — signaling broad public resistance to AI infrastructure buildout, not just wealthy NIMBYism.
- Stock market reaction to the election was bifurcated for tech: companies with existing data centers (big hyperscalers) rose, while firms still in the data-center buildout phase fell on expectations of higher financing/permitting costs.
- Several winning candidates explicitly campaigned against AI data centers and "Wall Street landlords" (e.g., Michigan's Will Lawrence) and against utility cost-shifting from data centers onto consumers (Arizona Corporation Commission winners).
- A major corporate restructuring (CVS/Aetna/Caremark break-up) is framed as a preemptive response to anticipated regulatory/antitrust scrutiny tied to the new political mandate — illustrating how enterprises are already adjusting structure in anticipation of policy shifts.
- The piece frames the election as an "anti-oligarchy" mandate broadly skeptical of concentrated corporate power (including AI/tech capital), rather than a specific referendum on AI policy per se.
