# Unsupervised Learning NO. 550

## Insights

- Employment for 22- to 25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed jobs is running about 19% below trend; the pattern looks less like layoffs and more like companies simply not hiring juniors, framed internally as "efficiency."
- Young job seekers are leaning on chatbots to write résumés and cover letters while employers use AI avatars and one-way video interviews on the hiring side; research cited suggests AI screeners actually rate AI-written applications more favorably.
- Misumi Group (Japan) scrapped degree-based starting pay, now paying all new hires the same flat salary, with HR leadership citing AI's takeover of entry-level "grunt work" as the rationale.
- A developer survey finds half of software engineers now feel they manage AI rather than write code directly, and half say they use AI partly to visibly signal usage to managers; junior developers reportedly worry about long-term career prospects.
- One engineer's account describes PR throughput jumping from ~20/month to 200+/month at an AI-native startup, with human code review disappearing and the new bottleneck becoming the engineer's own ability to keep track of what's happening.
- A Microsoft-built diagnostic system (MAI-DxO, using GPT-5 as a debating panel of virtual specialists) reportedly scored 83% on hard NEJM cases versus 22% for doctors working alone, at roughly a quarter of the cost per case.
