# Shorting Kalshi and Polymarket

## Insights

- AI-run news sites are already publishing real scoops: RuntimeWire ingests court transcripts and documents and publishes stories faster than human reporters, though its output is described as bland, poorly structured, and lacking robust editing/fact-checking/legal review processes.
- A second AI-run outlet, The Dissent, uses fake bylines to give AI aggregation agents an anthropomorphic identity, and was built without consulting any journalists.
- Sam Altman described a near-future AI assistant (a "descendant of ChatGPT") that could continuously watch a user's screen, meetings, and calls, and integrate with texts, email, docs, and Slack to maintain full context of a person's work life — framed as especially useful for CEOs who can't track all customer feedback or context themselves.
- Rep. Ro Khanna, positioning for a possible presidential run, frames tech leaders as would-be "conquerors" who see themselves as above ordinary people, arguing that his familiarity with them gives him standing to challenge their power.
- California gubernatorial nominee Xavier Becerra floated (then walked back) the idea of distributing AI company shares to Californians, while separately noting the state has few AI regulations despite being ahead of other states.
