# ⚙️ I almost said no to this partnership

## Insights

- The piece frames AI-integrated browsers as a response to "tab overload" in knowledge work — the described pain point is dozens of open tabs, parallel research threads, and the sense of missing information, which an AI browser addresses by auto-grouping tabs by topic and letting users reference open tabs directly in an AI prompt.
- Norton's Neo browser is positioned against the "five tools to answer one question" problem, pitching consolidation of search, browsing, and AI assistance into a single workflow rather than a chatbot layered on top of existing tools.
- The product's differentiation claim is architectural, not just feature-based: chat history and context are stored locally on-device rather than in the cloud, contrasted with typical "free" AI tools that monetize user data.
- Security is bundled as a core value prop via Norton's legacy malware/phishing protection (Web Shield), suggesting AI browser adoption is being pitched partly on trust/safety grounds, not just productivity.
- Explicit positioning note: Neo is framed as "the browser you already wanted, with an AI that knows what's in front of you" — distancing it from general-purpose chatbot products like ChatGPT.

## Quote

> Neo doesn't try to be ChatGPT. It tries to be the browser you already wanted, with an AI that knows what's in front of you.
