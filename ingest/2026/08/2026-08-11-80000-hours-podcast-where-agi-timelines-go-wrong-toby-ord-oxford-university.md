# Where AGI timelines go wrong | Toby Ord, Oxford University

## Insights

- Ord frames AGI timeline forecasting as systematically error-prone, cataloguing 14 recurring mistakes — e.g., treating benchmarks as final tests, assuming capabilities scale together, conflating intelligence with capability, and losing error bars when repeating point estimates.
- He distinguishes "could" from "will" forecasting and warns against extrapolating trends toward an undefined finish line, arguing much AGI discourse smuggles in unstated assumptions about what counts as "arrival."
- Recursive self-improvement is presented as dangerous along four distinct dimensions, but Ord is skeptical it will actually work as commonly imagined — danger and likelihood are treated as separate questions.
- He argues policy bans are more feasible than assumed: both a unilateral ban on superintelligence and a US-China treaty on superintelligence are live possibilities, not just idealistic proposals.
- Ord advocates banning unmonitorable chain-of-thought reasoning now, tying interpretability/oversight directly to near-term governance action rather than waiting for future capability thresholds.
- His personal estimate places transformative AI roughly a decade out, but he defends "broad timelines" — a distribution of plausible arrival times — as more useful for planning than a single date, including for individual career decisions.

## Quote

> Transformative AI is likely a decade away.
