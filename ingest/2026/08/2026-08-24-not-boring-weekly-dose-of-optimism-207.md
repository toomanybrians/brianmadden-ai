# Weekly Dose of Optimism #207

## Insights

- Robotics company Generalist claims its GEN-1.5 model is a one-shot learner — able to generalize from a few seconds of demonstration data, learning from simulation, human video, or chained sub-tasks rather than requiring extensive task-specific training.
- "Routers" — infrastructure that dynamically routes AI workloads to the cheapest/most capable model for a given task — had a breakout week: Stripe acquired OpenRouter for a reported $7.5 billion, and portfolio companies Merge and Ramp both launched their own routing products in the same week.
- Merge's new "Merge for Workforce" router claims to let companies use 75x fewer tokens for comparable or better results, framed as a cost/efficiency play on model usage.
- Ramp purchased the domain router.com to house its own AI routing product, positioning cost-optimized "intelligence routing" as a companion to its existing time/money-saving tools.
- The piece frames model routing as a response to companies increasingly caring about "Return on Tokens" (ROT) — treating AI inference spend as a cost center to be optimized, not just a capability to be maximized.
- A cited study (Mizrachi, Rottem & Rozenkrantz, Nature Human Behavior) found voluntary attention regulates acute immune responses, suggesting attention functions as a biological control mechanism, not just a cognitive/experiential one.
