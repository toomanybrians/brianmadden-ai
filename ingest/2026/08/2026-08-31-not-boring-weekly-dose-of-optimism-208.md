# Weekly Dose of Optimism #208

## Insights

- Anthropic previewed a "Model Hardware Standard" (MHS) that lets AI agents operate multiple lab/manufacturing instruments in parallel (microscopes, liquid handlers, robotic arms), applied to tasks like drug discovery experiments and quantum computer laser calibration.
- Commentary cited on MHS frames its value as automating routine, tedious lab tasks (e.g., laser alignment) so human scientists can focus on higher-order scientific work.
- Separately noted: examples exist of US manufacturing companies already using Claude and Codex for practical work tasks, though specifics aren't detailed in this piece.
- Nvidia acquired Hugging Face, the open-source model repository, for $12.9B — framed as a "commoditize your complements" strategic move for Nvidia's business.
- Hugging Face's Pollen Robotics unveiled "Microduck," a $399 accessible robot trainable via reinforcement learning, capable out-of-the-box of behaviors like walking, object manipulation, and self-recovery.
- Other items in the roundup (cancer drug approval, nuclear microreactor contracts, drone startup funding) are not directly AI/knowledge-work related and are outside this note's scope.

## Quote

> "This is aiming to automate boring stuff, like laser alignment, so humans can do science, which is core to the human spirit." — paraphrased framing attributed to Ruxandra Teslo's commentary in the piece
