# On Substack-Pangram Partnership: It's About Sending a Message

## Insights

- Substack's partnership with AI-detection company Pangram is framed as a non-binding disclosure mechanism, not enforcement: flagged writers face no ban, can opt out entirely, and the goal is giving readers information to decide who to read, not policing content.
- Pangram's own reported false-positive rate is extremely low (0.0041%, ~1 in 24,000) versus a higher false-negative rate — a deliberate design trade-off favoring not punishing honest writers over catching every AI user.
- Pangram 4.0 reportedly detects AI involvement in "humanized" (adversarially disguised) text 98.83% of the time across 13 commercial tools, and generalizes across model families with sub-0.7% false-negative rates, addressing prior critiques of detector brittleness on mixed human-AI authorship.
- The piece argues detection tools work via stylometric "fingerprints" AI models leave in aggregate writing style (from training + post-training), not via matching text against training-corpus membership.
- A key risk raised is social, not technical: a "mob" of readers/writers, unfamiliar with detector nuances (chunk-based scoring, brittleness on short texts, inability to measure how much human care went into a text), may make absolute judgments from a tool that only measures relative/partial AI involvement.
- Frames the underlying problem as a collective-action/commons issue: individually small acts of outsourcing writing to AI compound into platform-wide trust erosion, drawing a parallel to a university cheating scandal that forced a professor to abandon take-home exams.
