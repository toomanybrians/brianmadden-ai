# ⚙️ Android's big leap is from apps to agents

## Insights

- Google's Sameer Samat frames Android's next evolution as moving from an "operating system" to an "intelligence system" — from users tapping through apps to simply telling devices what outcome they want, with AI agents handling the navigation and multi-step execution.
- This shift requires agents to work consistently across a whole device ecosystem (phones, computers, cars, watches, glasses), not just on a single screen — device continuity is treated as core to the agent vision, not an afterthought.
- Google is already testing this in smaller, concrete features: app automations and "Rambler" (a keyboard tool that converts spoken voice brain-dumps into polished text), suggesting agentic AI is being introduced piecemeal rather than as one big relaunch.
- Samat raises governance questions the industry hasn't settled — permissions, sandboxing, and human oversight — as a design challenge once agents can take actions on a user's behalf, not just suggest them.
- Samat also claims AI is already changing internal work at Google, specifically noting product managers can now build functional prototypes themselves without engineering handoff — a concrete deskilling/reskilling data point for a knowledge-work role.
- Separately in the issue: Ramp enterprise spending data shows businesses favor cheaper/mid-tier AI models over top frontier ones (Anthropic's flagship "Fable 5" got only 6% of token spend despite Anthropic leading overall adoption), suggesting enterprise buyers are pushing back against paying for maximum model capability for routine work.
