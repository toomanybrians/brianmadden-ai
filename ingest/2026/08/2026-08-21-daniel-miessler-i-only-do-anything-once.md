# I Only Do Anything Once

## Insights

- Frames a personal productivity philosophy: any task done more than once should be captured as a reusable "skill" so it's never manually repeated — the goal is eliminating whole categories of recurring work, not just speeding them up.
- Distinguishes between "hard problems," which are memorable and inherently one-off, and small repeated lookups/retrievals, which are the real hidden cost because they're frequent, low-attention, and never accounted for.
- Argues the real difficulty isn't building the automation ("skill") itself but noticing which tasks have become invisible friction — repeated small annoyances get normalized and stop registering as costs, like a heavy pack you stop feeling.
- Proposes a concrete detection heuristic: if you catch yourself thinking "I wish I could just do that automatically," that thought is a signal your brain has already priced the task as too expensive to keep doing manually — treat it as an item for a "build queue."
- Critiques the common pattern of using AI to go faster at existing manual tasks ("faster on the treadmill") rather than restructuring around a higher-level question of what you actually want as a human, and building systems (skills/harness) toward that.
- Describes personal implementation: domain-specific "skills" (e.g., home automation, network) that let an AI assistant retain and apply context (like device names/models) without the user needing to re-supply it each time.

## Quote

> Most people are pointing AI at going faster on the treadmill. Get off it instead.
