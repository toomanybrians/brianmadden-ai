# Wisconsin versus (AI) Goliath

## Insights

- A closed paper mill site in Wisconsin Rapids (pop. ~18,000) is being proposed for conversion into an AI data center by Russian-founded PNK Group, following the loss of the town's main employer for over a century.
- Local opposition (South Wood County Neighbors for Responsible Development) reports the city and developer have been unwilling or unable to answer basic questions: NDA status, water/chemical usage in the "closed loop" cooling system, number of local jobs, or construction hiring — construction and modular assembly reportedly won't use local labor at all.
- Residents cite a precedent where a Meta data center's closed-loop system flush released antibacterial-resistant bacteria into a Wyoming city's water supply, raising concern about similar risk locally.
- Economic tension centers on tax-incentive promises (a Wisconsin sales tax exemption passed by the legislature and governor) versus real costs to residents — 40% of the county falls in the "ALICE" (asset-limited, income-constrained, employed) population, where even small utility bill increases are described as significant.
- The fight has produced unusual cross-partisan alignment — a socialist political group and conservative-leaning residents in a red county opposing the same project — and has sparked a local alderman recall effort after alleged intimidation of a business distributing "Stop the Data Center" signs.
- Disgraced former NYC mayor Eric Adams appeared at a town hall to advocate for the data center, reportedly telling residents "data centers are going to be here" — met with public hostility.
- Organizers frame the core question as one of regulatory/political leverage: weak state environmental enforcement (linked to prior budget cuts to Wisconsin's DNR), unclear enforceability of proposed "Community Benefit Agreements," and doubts that fines would meaningfully deter a billion-dollar developer.
