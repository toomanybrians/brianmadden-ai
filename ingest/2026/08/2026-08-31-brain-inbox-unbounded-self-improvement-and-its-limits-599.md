# 🔮 Unbounded self-improvement and its limits #599

## Insights

- Philosopher Toby Ord's analysis argues unbounded recursive self-improvement (RSI) in AI is theoretically possible but practically unachievable, because the "generation time" for each improvement loop (research, training, deployment) can't shrink to zero — real-world constraints like experiment time, training runs, and chip fabrication impose hard floors.
- Beyond practical limits, physical limits also cap RSI: the speed of light bounds communication, the Bekenstein bound caps information density in finite space, and Landauer's principle imposes an energy cost on irreversible computation.
- Open-weight models are rapidly gaining enterprise adoption: Vercel's open-weight token share hit 62% (up from 28% two months prior), and some Western firms (e.g., Thomson Reuters) are building in-house models on Chinese open weights like Qwen to cut costs.
- Bridgewater, working with Thinking Machines, fine-tuned an open Qwen model that beat every frontier model tested on internal information-filtering tasks — ~30% fewer errors at 1/14th the inference cost — illustrating that small, fine-tuned open models can outperform frontier closed models on narrow tasks.
- OpenAI's new "Jalapeño" chip was designed partly by OpenAI's own models (helping write kernels, cutting ~10% from a compute block) and went from first hire to tape-out in ~16 months, beating comparable Nvidia silicon 1.5–1.9x on tokens per megawatt — suggesting AI can compress hardware design cycles.
- The compute market is fragmenting into specialized, non-commodity chips optimized for different needs (latency, power, training vs. inference), with examples like Cerebras powering ChatGPT's fast-response mode and Fractile's deal with Anthropic — a trend expected to reduce Nvidia's relative (not absolute) dominance.
- Miscellaneous item: Meta reportedly considered shrinking some teams by up to 60% to become "AI native."

## Quote

> The Universe, it seems, agrees with me. Unbounded RSI has its limits.
