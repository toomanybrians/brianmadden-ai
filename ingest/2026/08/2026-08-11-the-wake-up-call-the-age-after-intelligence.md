# The age after intelligence

## Insights

- Argues that as AI makes intelligence abundant and commoditized (like electricity), intelligence itself is revealed as insufficient — it can optimize means but not choose ends or values.
- Frames the coming abundance of AI/AGI as forcing a cultural reckoning: humanity has implicitly treated intelligence as its highest virtue, but that framing breaks down once machines exceed human cognitive capacity.
- Distinguishes "calculating best next step" (what LLMs/AI do) from a form of embodied, non-analytical "instinct" or intuitive knowing that guides human action without conscious deliberation.
- Suggests AI has surpassed brain-based intelligence while having none of the "intelligence of being alive" — perception, interoception, emotion, hormonal/autonomic signals — implying a gap AI cannot close.
- Positions the real challenge of an AI-abundant future as philosophical/existential (what is worth pointing intelligence at, what is worth becoming) rather than technical or economic.
- Draws a parallel between society's growing dependence on AI and historical religious devotion, suggesting AI risks becoming an unexamined belief system people offload agency to.

## Quote

> It’s like A.I. has surpassed the intelligence contained in our minds while possessing almost none of the intelligence contained in being alive.
