# Enough Debate about the AI Jobpocalypse. We Need To Plan for the Messy Middle.

## Insights

- Frames current AI-and-jobs discourse as a stalemated binary: critics/boosters both predict mass automation and unemployment, some AI CEOs counter with a post-labor "abundance" utopia via universal high income, while techno-optimist economists argue AI creates more jobs than it destroys — and this deadlock prevents any actual planning or action.
- Introduces economist Molly Kinder's concept of the "messy middle" — a near-term period of concentrated, uneven workforce disruption rather than a clean utopia/dystopia outcome.
- Kinder's approach is described as evidence-based: identifying specific sectors/populations where AI will hit the labor force, and tracing second- and third-order consequences from those effects.
- The piece frames the core actionable question as what should be done today to protect people's livelihoods, rather than continuing to debate whether disruption will happen at all.
- Notes real-world stakes for workers and families caught in the debate — e.g., decisions like whether children should pursue college — as the human cost of the discourse's inaction.
- Kinder left a senior fellow role at Brookings Institution to found a new organization focused specifically on AI's impact on jobs, signaling institutional momentum around this "messy middle" framing.

## Quote

> Molly Kinder... argues that we're entering what she calls the "messy middle": a period of concentrated and uneven workforce disruption.
