# That feeling you’re having about AI? It's telling you something important.

## Insights

- CHT argues public discourse on AI has shifted from abstract utopia/doom predictions to lived experience — people react to how AI actually shows up in daily work and relationships, not forecasts about it.
- The piece frames a broader "humanization" trend (companies marketing "made by humans," rehiring after AI-driven layoffs, IBM/Ford emphasizing "human capital") as evidence of public backlash against AI-first strategies.
- CHT's framework identifies five domains of human life exposed to "AI erosion": relationships, work/contributions, cognitive capacities, inner life, and identity — positioning workplace AI adoption as one facet of a single, larger threat rather than an isolated issue.
- Draws an explicit analogy to social media harms: argues AI's problems stem from underlying business incentives and design choices, not any single product or company — implying policy/design intervention, not just user behavior change, is the lever.
- Signals upcoming output: a multi-institution working group (Harvard, Oxford, Brookings, Columbia, Duke) developing proposed legal/rights frameworks for the "AI era," explicitly compared to labor rights after the Industrial Revolution and privacy law after Kodak.
- Rejects "humans vs. AI" framing as a trap that recreates prior utopia/doom polarization; positions the real question as what human capacities/experiences are worth deliberately preserving as AI becomes infrastructural.

## Quote

> AI is not an invading force we can repel. It is already woven into our daily lives.
