# Weekly Dose of Optimism #205

## Insights

- A model referred to as "ChatGPT Astra" reportedly solved ten significant unsolved math/CS problems, prompting claims (disputed) that AI progress is entering a "singularity" phase with accelerating timelines.
- A well-regarded AI leaker suggested the underlying model may actually be an early version of GPT-6 rather than an incremental GPT-5.x update, with possible public access as soon as the following week.
- Tesla's planned "Terafab" chip facility in Texas (targeting 1 terawatt of compute/year, ~$16.8B initial investment) reportedly includes a particle accelerator to generate EUV light via a free-electron-laser approach, potentially challenging ASML/TSMC's lithography dominance through vertical integration.
- Large capital raises for hard-tech/infrastructure companies (Base Power, Hadrian, Valar Atomics) signal continued investor conviction that AI-era compute and energy demand require corresponding buildout in physical infrastructure ("atoms," not just "bits").
- Oklo achieving criticality for its advanced reactor design is framed as a milestone for nuclear power's role in meeting rising energy demand tied to compute/AI growth, after years navigating NRC regulatory hurdles.

## Quote

> "We can't forget the world of atoms is 100x bigger than the world of bits." — Chris Power, Hadrian CEO
