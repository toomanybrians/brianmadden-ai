# ChinAI #372: China’s Overhyped Embodied AI Sector

## Insights

- A LatePost investigation finds Chinese embodied AI/robotics valuations far outstrip actual capabilities — demos show robots taking 15 minutes to fail at folding a towel, or 70-90 seconds for simple pick-and-place tasks that would never work on an actual production line.
- Seven Chinese robotics firms have valuations above 20 billion RMB despite being founded only in the last few years, with revenue often propped up by circular relationships with state-owned enterprises that simultaneously act as investor, customer, and operational partner.
- A large share of reported revenue (e.g., over 70% for Unitree) comes from "research applications" — facilities buying robots to generate training data, which often sits unsold; one facility resold robots to schools after failing to recoup costs.
- Despite huge valuations, R&D spending remains low (Unitree: 145 million RMB in 2025), and capital raised isn't converting into technical progress — investors describe money "sitting idle" with no clear payoff.
- Embodied AI is structurally harder to scale than LLMs: data is scarce, there's no established pipeline for collecting it, and the field lacks standardized model architectures, data requirements, or benchmarks to track progress.
- The piece frames Chinese robotics investment as a speculative "bubble" dynamic, with one investor describing industry sentiment progressing through "questioning the bubble, understanding the bubble, embracing the bubble, and enjoying the bubble."

## Quote
