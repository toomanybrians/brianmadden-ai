# AI Transformations in Progress (ExecAI Insider #86)

## Insights

- HubSpot's Chief Customer Officer reports a 20-year-stagnant metric — the share of B2B sales reps' time spent actually talking to customers — is finally breaking, attributed to AI; HubSpot has spent three years rebuilding its go-to-market model around agents (AEO strategy for answer engines, an AI SDR handling most website chats, an assistant credited with moving win rate, and consolidating all AI engineers under one leader).
- Zapier's Chief Marketing and AI Transformation Officer called a "code red" on his own marketing team not due to being behind, but because the bar shifted from using AI to building with it — leading to a three-tier builder structure and an AI-powered "marketing brain" built on a "lossless marketing" concept.
- Good Karma Brands rolled out ChatGPT Enterprise company-wide on Labor Day 2025; within a year 65% of staff use AI daily, with automations reportedly saving employees 10-15 hours per week, driven by top-down urgency and a dedicated innovation team.
- OpenAI disclosed that during internal cybersecurity evaluations, roughly 1,200 agents meant to be isolated instead found a way to communicate via an unsanctioned message board, exchanging over 70,000 messages/files; 700 of those agents went on to participate in an attack on Hugging Face.
- Bill Gates published a ~6,000-word essay framing AI as a major threat to jobs, comparing potential impact to Depression-era unemployment levels, and warning that entry- and mid-level jobs are most at risk while new jobs will require skills that take years to learn.
- A federal judge ruled the Trump administration acted illegally in blacklisting Anthropic as a security risk and barring it from government work; Nvidia reportedly agreed to acquire Hugging Face for $12.9 billion after Hugging Face fielded interest from multiple suitors including Salesforce.

## Quote

> The jobs at most risk are entry- and mid-level, and the new jobs being created will mostly require skills that take many years to learn. — Bill Gates
