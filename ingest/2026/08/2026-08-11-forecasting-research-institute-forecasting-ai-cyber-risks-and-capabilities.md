# Forecasting AI Cyber Risks and Capabilities

## Insights

- A pilot study by Forecasting Research Institute and GovAI had superforecasters and cybersecurity experts estimate 2026 risks from AI-enabled cyberattacks, finding baseline catastrophic risk low (5-8% for a $10B+ data-damaging worm) but highly sensitive to AI capability diffusion.
- The single largest risk driver identified was AI enabling "elite exploit development" (zero-click, high-privilege exploits) for moderately skilled hackers — a hypothetical where 25% of such hackers gain this capability via open-weight models raised worm-attack risk estimates 3-3.5x.
- Forecasters significantly underestimated AI progress on cyber benchmarks: experts predicted 90%+ Cybench performance by 2028 and superforecasters by 2030, but Claude Opus 4.6 reportedly hit 93% on a benchmark subset in February 2026.
- Panelists viewed current cyber benchmarks as informative of technical progress but poor proxies for real-world catastrophic risk, since they don't capture zero-day discovery, targeting, coordination, and persistence.
- Keeping high-risk models proprietary with anti-jailbreak safeguards was seen by nearly half of participants as capable of at least halving large-scale worm-attack risk, though less effective against sophisticated actors or exploit-as-a-service markets.
- A follow-up survey after Anthropic's November 2025 report on AI-assisted cyber espionage left median risk forecasts largely unchanged, despite participants viewing the report as evidence of greater-than-expected actor capability.

## Quote

> Forecasters likely underestimated cybersecurity-relevant AI progress.
