# The Growing Impact Of Data Centers

## Insights

- The US hosts nearly 40% of the world's data centers (4,000–5,000 facilities) despite 174 countries having some presence, driven by cheap electricity, fiber infrastructure, historical interconnection points, and tax incentives; Virginia has the highest concentration, with Loudoun County alone housing ~200 (117 more in development).
- AI companies frequently promise local economic benefits (jobs, growth) to host communities, but the industry's lack of transparency means no state tracks actual job numbers, and construction jobs often go to outside workers rather than locals.
- Decades-long tax breaks for data centers strain local government finances — Texas and Virginia each spent ~$1 billion subsidizing data centers in 2025 — reducing funds available for education and infrastructure; Crook County, Oregon gave away $29 million in property tax exemptions in 2024 alone versus far smaller corporate community contributions.
- Environmental/health costs are substantial: hyper-scale data centers use up to 20 million liters of water per day (equivalent to a town of 50,000); ~60% of data center power comes from fossil fuels; US data centers generate 105+ million tons of CO2e (2% of 2023 US emissions); backup diesel generators emit pollutants tied to respiratory harm, with health costs projected to exceed $20 billion by 2028, disproportionately burdening disadvantaged communities (up to 200x more per household).
- US data centers currently use ~4.5% of national electricity (equivalent to 17 million households), projected to reach 12% by 2030 — tripling current usage — straining local grids and raising residential electricity costs, especially in Virginia (35% of global hyper-scale capacity).
- Financial risk is escalating: data center infrastructure spending is projected to exceed $1 trillion by 2029 (up from $455 billion in 2024); Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet, and Meta invested ~$344 billion in 2025 alone (1.1% of US GDP), raising concerns among analysts that a burst AI bubble could destabilize pensions, retirement savings, and insurance, and trigger broader recession.
