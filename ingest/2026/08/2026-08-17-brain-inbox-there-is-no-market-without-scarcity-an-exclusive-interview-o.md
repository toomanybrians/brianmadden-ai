# "There is no market without scarcity": An Exclusive Interview on Cloudflare's Stephanie Cohen on making AI pay for the open web

## Insights

- Cloudflare will change default settings on September 15, 2026: new domains and untouched free-tier sites will block AI "training" and "agent" crawlers on ad-supported pages by default, while "search" crawlers remain allowed — a private infrastructure decision now functioning as de facto internet policy given Cloudflare sits in front of over 20% of the web.
- The traffic economics have shifted sharply: crawlers now take roughly 9.6 pages for every visitor sent back to publishers (up from ~7.5 a year prior), and nearly 60% of all traffic on Cloudflare's network is now non-human.
- Cloudflare is building payment infrastructure around the HTTP 402 "payment required" status code — over 2 billion such responses fire daily across its network, but the vast majority don't convert into actual payment, revealing a market with high demand but almost no settlement infrastructure yet.
- The model is shifting from "pay per crawl" (paying for access/input) toward "pay per use" (paying only when content is actually used in an AI-generated answer/output), alongside efforts to reduce redundant crawling of unchanged pages.
- Google is described as the unresolved structural problem: it captures 87.8% of referral traffic and its mixed-purpose crawler reportedly gives it roughly twice the content access of leading AI companies, since publishers can't separate its search/training/agent functions — Cloudflare's exec avoided direct policy commitments on this.
- Despite Cloudflare's stated optimism that "the open internet is thriving," its own reported data shows absolute human traffic fell 35–40% across retail, software, IT, and financial services between June 2025 and April 2026, and licensing deals remain bespoke/negotiated rather than programmatic, unlikely to fully replace lost ad/referral/affiliate revenue.

## Quote

> "There is no market without scarcity." — Stephanie Cohen, Cloudflare
