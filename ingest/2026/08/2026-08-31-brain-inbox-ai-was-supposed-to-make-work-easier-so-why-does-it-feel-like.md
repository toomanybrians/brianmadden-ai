# AI Was Supposed to Make Work Easier, So Why Does It Feel Like We’re Working Harder?

## Insights

- Draws a distinction between "iterative AI" (making existing processes faster/cheaper) and "innovative AI" (creating new capabilities that weren't possible before); argues most enterprises are stuck in the former, wrapping legacy processes in AI interfaces rather than redesigning work.
- Introduces "cognitive dAIrwinism" — a cluster of emerging failure modes (cognitive offloading, AI atrophy, cognitive debt, digital amnesia, AI sycophancy, AI slop, cognitive surrender) resulting from unguided AI adoption, and ties this to a hidden "AI Tax": the labor cost of reviewing, correcting, and rewriting low-quality AI output.
- Cites OpenAI's concept of a "capability overhang" — the gap between frontier model capability and typical usage — noting power users reportedly draw on ~7x more advanced model capability than typical users, creating an internal skills/capability gap even among AI adopters.
- Proposes reframing AIQ from "Artificial Intelligence Quotient" (tool proficiency) to "Augmented Intelligence Quotient" (human capability expansion), positioned alongside IQ, EQ, SQ, and AQ as quotients needed for the AI era.
- Uses IKEA's chatbot "Billie" as a case study: after Billie deflected ~47% of routine inquiries, IKEA analyzed unresolved cases, found demand for design/product judgment, and reskilled service staff into remote design advisors, generating ~€1.3B in incremental sales — framed as augmentation vs. pure automation/headcount reduction.
- Cites WEF projections: 170 million new roles created this decade alongside 92 million displaced, 39% of core skills changing by 2030, and 63% of employers citing skills gaps as the top transformation barrier.

## Quote

> "But faster old work is still old work."
