# We Have the Most Powerful Technology of Our Lifetime, Yet AI Is Exposing the Limits of How We Think

## Insights

- Employee anxiety about AI is framed as a communication and trust failure: executives talk "transformation" and "efficiency" while employees hear "replacement" and "more work with fewer people," and this gap can't be closed with training modules or demos.
- AI lacks institutional memory, moral responsibility, and a sense of what matters; it can generate options and patterns but can't resolve tradeoffs between efficiency, trust, and experience quality on its own.
- Proposes "AIQ" (Augmented Intelligence Quotient) as a successor to AI fluency — the ability to combine human judgment/empathy/creativity with machine speed and pattern recognition, e.g. using AI to challenge a strategy from multiple stakeholder perspectives rather than just draft it.
- Distinguishes "iterative AI" (speeding up/optimizing existing processes) from "innovative AI" (questioning why a process exists and what new models become possible) — warns that treating optimization as the whole strategy is a common organizational failure point.
- Recommends starting AI implementation with one workflow, but redesigning around the underlying outcome/customer need rather than automating existing (often outdated) steps as-is; stresses governance defining what requires human approval vs. what can be automated.
- Argues real competitive advantage lies between people/companies who use AI to extend judgment versus those who outsource thinking to it entirely, risking erosion of critical thinking, synthesis, and original point of view.

## Quote

> You cannot automate your way to imagination, and you cannot cut your way to growth.
