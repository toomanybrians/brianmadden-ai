# YOUR CHILD IS NOT READY

## Insights

- Diamandis's Moonshots community survey (parents, teachers, students, professionals — self-selected, likely biased) rated education's preparation for the future at just 4.3/10 on average, with teachers (3.5) and parents of teens (3.8) rating it even lower.
- 87% of surveyed parents of teens named "not being prepared for an AI future" as their top concern for their child — ahead of bullying, grades, or college admissions.
- On AI in classrooms: 42% said AI is allowed but not taught, 41% said it's banned outright, and only 2% said it's a core part of curriculum — meaning 83% of children are in schools where AI is banned or merely tolerated.
- Parents ranked desired future skills as AI literacy (78%), critical thinking (72%), adaptability (68%), and entrepreneurship (63%) — a mismatch with standard school subjects (algebra, history, biology, standardized testing).
- 79% of surveyed students/teachers/professionals believe a college degree will become less important in an AI world, with 45% saying "significantly less important."
- Diamandis proposes a three-pillar "new social contract" for education: Mindsets (purpose, curiosity, abundance, moonshot, exponential thinking), Entrepreneurship (building/selling/creating value as a life skill), and AI skills (prompting/reasoning with AI, not coding) — citing Fei-Fei Li's claim that Socrates was "humanity's best prompter."
- 84% of parents believe AI will increase human opportunity (73% said "greatly increase"), suggesting optimism about AI itself coexists with fear that institutions will fail to prepare kids for it.

## Quote

> The parents are not afraid of AI. They are afraid their kids will miss it.
