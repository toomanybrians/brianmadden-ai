# The AI-Native Company

## Insights

- Miessler argues the central story in tech isn't attacker/defender dynamics or AI governance debates, but the wholesale restructuring of how companies operate internally.
- He frames this restructuring as converting company knowledge into formal artifacts: constitutional documents (goals/metrics/strategy), context domains (internal knowledge), SOPs (codified policy), and agentic workflows that execute against those SOPs.
- Internal transparency becomes a byproduct: company activity gets published to internal "daemons" visible across people, agents, and departments.
- Human roles shift from doing the work to architecting, stewarding, and orchestrating systems that do the work — with distinct functions (idea, engineering, product, go-to-market) starting to merge into generalist roles.
- He predicts companies define an "ideal state" via these primitives and let AI manage the transition from current to ideal state, under human leadership oversight.
- Companies that don't adopt this model are predicted to largely fail to survive the transition; he compares the shift's magnitude to moving from paper/pencil to spreadsheets/databases, but more extreme.

## Quote

> The question won't be how we can find someone to do X or Y work with AI; it'll be how we can turn that work into an agent workflow.
