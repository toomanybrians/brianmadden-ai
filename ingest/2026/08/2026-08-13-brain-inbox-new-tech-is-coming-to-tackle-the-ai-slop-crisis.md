# ⚙️ New tech is coming to tackle the AI slop crisis

## Insights

- Major AI/content platforms (Anthropic, Spotify, Suno, Substack) are converging on transparency measures — watermarking, AI-persona badges, fingerprinting, AI-detection integration — partly driven by EU AI Act compliance requirements taking effect for content generated after August 2.
- Anthropic's new watermarking uses the C2PA open standard and will eventually persist through copy-paste and extend to older models; OpenAI, Microsoft, Google, Cohere, Meta, and Synthesia have also committed to the EU's transparency code.
- Detection studies suggest the problem transparency tools are addressing is severe: one study found 97% of listeners can't distinguish AI-generated music from human-made, and roughly half of one newsletter's readers can't spot AI images in a recurring quiz.
- A framed but unresolved question: watermarking is an arms-race measure (bad actors will adapt), and it's unclear whether consumer preference for "human-made" content will persist or fade over time.
- Separately, enterprises are shifting from renting AI (frontier lab APIs) to owning custom models — Oumi's new platform lets companies deploy, retrain on production data, and manage custom models via one console, cutting build time from weeks/months to potentially minutes of human effort (hours of compute).
- Thomson Reuters' CTO is cited as an example of an enterprise building its own foundation model to avoid outsourcing "intelligence" to frontier labs, resulting in a smaller, cheaper-to-run model — illustrating the underlying motive of cost control, customization, and data ownership.
- Databricks acquired Electric (maker of lightweight Postgres variant PGlite), enabling AI coding agents to run locally on phones/laptops with low latency and then sync to a central enterprise database — framed by Databricks as shifting company workflows from "human speed" to "machine speed."

## Quote

> "Before, we were bottlenecked on people typing code... But now they go at machine speed… If you don't embrace that as company, your competitors will and you'll be left in the dust." — Nikita Shamgunov, Databricks
