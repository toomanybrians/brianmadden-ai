# We Don’t Need to Wait for an AI Disaster to Estimate Its Costs

## Insights

- Proposes adapting the Terrorism Risk Insurance Program's (TRIP) annual "data call" mechanism to catastrophic AI risk: insurers are required to estimate aggregate losses from detailed hypothetical catastrophe scenarios they've never observed, generating both narrative and statistical data.
- Argues the core problem with insuring against frontier AI risk is lack of historical loss data, since insurance/bond markets traditionally price risk by generalizing from past events — a method that breaks down for novel catastrophes (as it once did for terrorism pre-2001).
- Notes TRIP already blends federal government backstopping with private insurance: insurers pay deductibles/coinsurance, government covers losses above a threshold, and Treasury recoups outlays later via premium surcharges — creating "skin in the game" incentives to price risk accurately.
- Distinguishes TRIP's approach from existing AI "war-gaming" (e.g., RAND's loss-of-control scenario exercises): war-gaming generates catastrophe narratives but lacks the actuarial/statistical discipline of insurance underwriting to produce systematic aggregate loss estimates.
- Claims TRIP's advantages over private-sector efforts (e.g., Lloyd's scenario exercises) come from federal mandate power, scale (700+ participating insurers vs. 57 at Lloyd's), and cross-checking estimates against all 50 state insurance commissioners.
- Acknowledges the major objection — that AI catastrophes could be far more unpredictable, cascading, and global than terrorism scenarios — but argues that testing is the only way to learn feasibility, and that the program is low-cost (TRIP costs $4–7M/year).
- Suggests such a program could function as a check on both AI risk-denial and AI catastrophism, since underwriting discipline "de-emphasizes the least plausible catastrophes" rather than amplifying fear.

## Quote

> In the autumn of 2001, insuring against terrorism risk was considered a lost cause. Now, a thriving industry extends to all regions of the United States.
