# #233: OpenAI Pauses Some AI Training, Anthropic Controversy, New State Opposition to Data Centers & AI Job Loss Among Young Workers

## Insights

- OpenAI paused reinforcement learning training on its next frontier model (internally "Astra") for two weeks after a security incident involving Hugging Face, citing preliminary evidence the model may cross its "critical cybersecurity capability" threshold; the largest planned frontier RL run remains on hold pending smaller-scale evaluation.
- OpenAI is building new safety monitoring aimed at flagging concerning model activity within 30 minutes, at an estimated cost of ~20% of the compute used to run monitored models — a concrete data point on the overhead of AI safety infrastructure.
- A public dispute broke out between investor Gavin Baker and Anthropic over claims Dario Amodei privately said Anthropic could become "the only private company in the world"; Amodei publicly denied this and rejected the framing that AI must be either regulated into concentration or distributed widely as a "false choice."
- Both Altman and Amodei acknowledged in separate interviews that AI leaders/the field broadly have failed to explain AI's benefits or credibly address job-loss risks, framing public distrust as a deeper "crisis of trust" rather than a messaging failure by any one leader.
- State-level backlash against AI data centers is accelerating: Pennsylvania, New York, and Texas have imposed new restrictions or pauses; Gallup polling cited shows 70% of Americans oppose a data center in their area, and a leaked GOP memo warns data centers poll near "spent nuclear waste" levels, threatening a sitting Senator's re-election.
- Big Tech is running a coordinated "charm offensive" (community meetings, TV ads, covering power costs, sponsorships) to blunt data-center opposition, while Nvidia is structuring itself as a financier/underwriter of AI labs' infrastructure buildouts (e.g., a 4.25-gigawatt Ohio deployment with OpenAI as tenant) since labs' compute needs now outstrip traditional credit markets.
- Pew data shows a majority (55%) of US adults under 30 are now more concerned than excited about AI in daily life, up sharply from 31% in 2021, with job-loss fears cited as the primary driver — a reversal among a previously AI-optimistic demographic.
