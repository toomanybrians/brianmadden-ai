# The Actual Reason Why Google “Fell Out” of the AI Race Changes Everything

## Insights

- Central thesis: Google DeepMind (via Demis Hassabis) has strategically diverged from OpenAI/Anthropic's shared bet on recursive self-improvement (RSI) via coding agents, instead pursuing world models as the path to AGI — a deliberate withdrawal from the "race" rather than a failure to compete.
- OpenAI and Anthropic both increasingly treat human engineers as managers/supervisors of coding agents (Claude Code, Codex) rather than as direct code producers — described as a near-total shift in how technical work happens inside frontier labs.
- Both leading labs anchor their strategy in Sutton's "Bitter Lesson" — betting that scaling compute/search will surface any needed algorithmic breakthroughs, rather than requiring new architectures.
- Anthropic co-founder Jack Clark reportedly estimated a 60% chance of RSI being achieved by 2028; some insiders privately favor a coordinated capabilities slowdown despite pushing the pace themselves.
- Karpathy's move to Anthropic is framed as evidence he independently observed early RSI dynamics (an autoresearch agent improving his own code efficiency by ~10%) and joined to help scale that loop into pretraining.
- Frames the AI competitive landscape as a structural divide between cash-constrained startups (OpenAI, Anthropic) that need AI to become the business immediately, versus an incumbent (Google) that can subsidize a longer, differently-theorized bet.

## Quote

> Google DeepMind's leadership sees OpenAI and Anthropic's bet at best as an off-ramp, and at worst as a dead end.
