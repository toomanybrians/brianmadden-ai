# Open-source is NOT the same as open-weight

## Insights

- Draws a technical distinction between open-source (full pipeline: raw training data, preprocessing, algorithms, code) and open-weight (only the trained parameters), arguing the terms are being conflated in mainstream coverage — including, per Marcus, by the New York Times.
- Argues open-weight release lets users run or "post-train" a model but blocks deeper interventions: removing specific training data, filtering by domain (e.g., legal reasoning), auditing for bias, checking bias, benchmark contamination, or copyrighted content, or investigating bioweapon-relevant content.
- Frames this as materially limiting three distinct constituencies: developers (can't customize training data/process), scientists (can't test hypotheses about memorization vs. generalization), and regulators (can't audit for bias, safety, or legal exposure).
- Names AllenAI's Olmo and Nvidia's Nemotron as examples of genuinely open-source models (weights + training data + recipes), contrasting them with Meta's practice.
- Alleges Meta/Zuckerberg deliberately trades on open-source's reputational goodwill while releasing only open-weight models — using precise "open-source" language when advantageous, then releasing closed-data models under the same halo.
- Useful skeptical-but-correct point for enterprise readers: "open" AI model claims often overstate real transparency/customizability, which matters for any org evaluating vendor claims, compliance exposure, or build-vs-buy decisions around model provenance.

## Quote

> Open-weight is open-source with all the good press but far fewer advantages.
