# The EU AI Act Newsletter #109: The Watermarking Era

## Insights

- Under the EU AI Act, since 2 August 2026 providers of leading generative AI models (e.g., Anthropic, OpenAI) must disclose to users that they're interacting with AI and mark AI-generated output as such — moving the norm from voluntary provenance labeling to mandatory disclosure.
- Anthropic's implementation for Claude embeds a text watermark that requires no extra tokens, adds no visible or hidden characters, doesn't affect output quality/cost, and can't be traced to a specific person or conversation — other Code of Practice signatories will roll out their own versions.
- Experts (Bontcheva, Pedreschi) rebut claims that watermarking degrades text quality, saying degradation is only measurable in very short outputs (under ~200 tokens/150 words) — a threshold negotiated directly with major AI providers.
- Commentators (Casovan, Pasquarelli) suggest the shift is a bigger behavioral change than people realize — moving users from "educated guessing" about AI content to seeing explicit verified symbols/overlays — and note engagement with content drops significantly once flagged as AI-generated.
- The AI Act does not treat "AI agents" as a separate legal category; they're covered under existing definitions of AI systems and GPAI models. Transparency rules for agents interacting with people or generating content apply from August 2026, with high-risk obligations phased in for 2027–2028, and agent autonomy/tool-use may factor into "systemic risk" model classification.
- GPAI Code of Practice discussions flagged that "marginal-risk" clauses (allowing a provider to match competitors deploying unsafe models) could only be invoked in exceptional, tightly safeguarded circumstances.
