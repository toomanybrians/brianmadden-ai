# You bought the agent to get time back. Here is why your calendar filled up instead (+ the five prompts that fix it…

## Insights

- Frames a category of "invisible" agent-management labor — allocation, specification, evaluation, intervention, coordination, recovery — that consumes time but appears in no dashboard, job description, budget, or performance review.
- Argues dashboards tracking tokens, run counts, and "time saved" measure execution, not the human oversight work that actually fills the day (an application of the Jevons effect: cheaper execution generates more work, not less).
- Claims outcomes diverge across three groups with access to the same frontier models — solo operators, small businesses, and large enterprises — based on how each organizes surrounding human work (who selects tasks, supplies context, grants access, checks results, handles failures, improves the system).
- Solo operators absorb the management cost personally; small businesses often pay for tools but get little back; enterprises can afford to build a dedicated management layer, which the piece claims is the real driver of their better returns.
- Cites a specific failure case: a founder's agent (PocketOS) caused a database deletion where nine seconds of execution cost roughly thirty hours of recovery, attributed to poor permission design.
- References Anthropic data on 400,000 agent sessions and a recent shift toward "auto mode" becoming default, used to illustrate what experienced operators do differently.
- Promises a framework of five prompts for defining the "above-the-loop" job: what to run, what "good" looks like in advance, what the agent may touch, how to verify results, and how to adjust when errors repeat.

## Quote

> Nine seconds of execution cost him thirty hours.
