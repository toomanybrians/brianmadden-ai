# OpenAI and Anthropic want the government’s help to slow things down

## Insights

- Over 1,300 employees at major AI labs — including chief scientists at Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta, and Google DeepMind — signed an open letter ("Pacing the Frontier") asking the US government to build tools capable of slowing frontier AI development, marking a notable shift from industry insiders openly requesting external constraints on their own work.
- The letter doesn't call for an immediate pause; it asks governments to develop the technical and regulatory capacity now, so options exist if a slowdown becomes necessary later — framed by advocates as preserving "optionality" against unpredictable capability jumps.
- The core risk cited is AI automating AI research itself, which could accelerate capability gains faster than humans' ability to understand or control resulting systems — Anthropic already reports over 80% of code merged into its codebase is AI-authored (as of May 2026).
- Voluntary restraint is seen as unstable: any single company willing to move fast undermines collective slowdown efforts, so signatories argue only government-enforced rules (ideally international) can make coordination durable.
- Major unresolved obstacles include US-China competitive dynamics, the practical difficulty of coordinating multiple governments, and antitrust-related barriers to companies coordinating pace amongst themselves without legal cover.
- Some signatories (e.g., John Schulman of Thinking Machines) note the letter conspicuously asks only for government action, not voluntary commitments from the labs themselves — highlighting a gap between rhetoric and corporate follow-through.

## Quote

> AI may end up progressing far more quickly than societies and states can adapt.
