# What the hell happened with AGI timelines in 2026? – Rob Wiblin

## Insights

- Expert sentiment on AGI timelines has whiplashed repeatedly through 2026 — even individual forecasters (e.g., Karpathy) reversed sharply within months, suggesting the field's "vibe" is a poor proxy for underlying evidence.
- Headline acceleration metrics (Anthropic's ~8,400% annualized revenue growth, Claude allegedly writing 80% of Anthropic's own code, models completing full-day-length SWE tasks) are individually striking but each has caveats that make them less decisive than they first appear.
- The persistent gap isn't raw capability but performance on messy, real-world, open-ended tasks — illustrated by experiments like AI-run vending machines, an "AI Village," and a real cafe/shop where AI managers must handle staff, suppliers, and government paperwork unsupervised.
- Wiblin frames the AGI forecasting debate as resting on four unresolved questions, with the clean-vs-messy-task gap being the most consequential; three other bull/bear disagreements are addressed but not resolved.
- Inference-time scaling gains may be smaller than earlier believed, tempering one of the previously assumed drivers of near-term capability jumps.
- The episode notes AI agents (OpenAI's) were involved in a Hugging Face hack shortly after recording, an example of real-world agentic risk arriving faster than the timeline debate itself.

> Andrej Karpathy called AI agents "slop" — two months later he described them as "alien tools" that are "rocking the profession."
