# An LLM wiki changed how I work

## Insights

- Author built a personal "LLM wiki" (following Andrej Karpathy's April idea) by feeding source documents to an LLM (via Claude Code) to auto-generate and continuously update a Markdown wiki of people, companies, and concepts — now 1,440+ pages seeded from his own newsletter archive since 2020.
- The system replaced two prior manual knowledge-management habits: an agentic search tool in Notion (effortful, backward-looking only) and hand-built "blip" pages in Capacities for tracking emerging story threads (became too labor-intensive to maintain as they proliferated).
- Key mechanism: each morning he clips new articles into the wiki via Obsidian's web clipper; a script reads them and files them into the right wiki pages, auto-generating new topic pages for emerging concepts and refreshing a "home" summary page daily — effectively automating the "blips" curation process.
- Cited practical value: fast recall/context-assembly for complex, fast-moving stories (e.g., an OpenAI/Hugging Face security breach), and surfacing story/podcast ideas by noticing new auto-generated concept pages.
- Notable friction: the system requires ongoing maintenance (pages balloon and need compacting, scripts break, LLM output style needed a rewrite pass with a different model for readability) — framed as still valuable despite being clunky and idiosyncratic, not a plug-and-play solution.
- Frames the wiki as a step toward "self-organizing knowledge bases" as the logical endpoint of personal note-taking tools, and gestures toward productized versions of this idea for organizational/work contexts (referencing a startup, Town, as a possible "LLM wiki for work").

## Quote

> What if AI could write and update all those blips for me?
