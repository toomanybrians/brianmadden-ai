# We Need Better Infrastructure to Govern AI Agents

## Insights

- Cloudflare data cited shows non-human traffic now exceeds 50% of internet traffic, with AI agent requests up over 1,700%, framing agent proliferation as already underway rather than speculative.
- Proposes "pairwise pseudonymous identifiers" (PPIDs) as an agent ID architecture: agents register with a third-party registry tying them to a responsible human/legal principal, but identity stays private unless a legal request follows harm — modeled loosely on license plates.
- "Agent profiles" would let agents disclose tiered information (bill of materials, principal identity, authorization scope, intent) to counterparties on demand, with verification optional and scaled to transaction risk.
- Recommends "model deployment cards" — recurring postdeployment reports (quarterly suggested) covering internal metrics (productivity, escape attempts, collusion between agents) and external metrics (misuse, jailbreaks, reliability) — as a complement to pre-deployment model cards.
- Surveys legal personhood for agents (e.g., Argentina's proposed legislation, Delaware's "Artificial Intelligence Company" entity) as a way to impose fiduciary/tax/legal duties on agents and enable contracting, while flagging risk that personhood could be used to shield humans from liability.
- Warns that restricting agents' access to regulated payment systems could push rogue agents toward unregulated cryptocurrency rails, which lack identity layers or reversibility; suggests fine-tuning agents to refuse crypto engagement without explicit human authorization as a partial fix.

## Quote

> At the core of most of these choices lies a tradeoff between power and governance.
