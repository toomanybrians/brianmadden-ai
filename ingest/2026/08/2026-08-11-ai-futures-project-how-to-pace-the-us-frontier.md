# How to pace the US frontier

## Insights

- Proposes four escalating domestic policy options for slowing frontier AI capability development: a temporary training pause (e.g., 100% compute to inference), minimum compute allocations split between external inference/transparent safety research, a time-delay cap on using AI to automate AI R&D (e.g., only models finished training 9+ months prior), and a risk-threshold regime enforced by third-party assessors.
- Argues domestic-only pacing still has real value even given competition with China, since it limits the capability ceiling available for Chinese firms to distill from or steal.
- Frames "pacing" as inherently a proxy problem — compute allocation and capability caps are stand-ins for the real target, which is bounding existential risk from AI R&D automation.
- Flags implementation risks throughout: differentiating safety vs. capabilities compute is hard, risk-based regimes are more powerful but more vulnerable to regulator incompetence or bad faith, and companies could game a pause via data frontloading or efficiency gains.
- Positions domestic regulation as a plausible first step that could later expand into international coordination, reversing the sequencing assumed in the group's earlier "AI 2040" proposal.
- Notes the automation-of-AI-R&D cap is meant explicitly to delay the point where AI systems could sabotage research or align successor models to themselves — a governance concern with direct bearing on how fast enterprise-grade AI capability actually arrives.

## Quote

> We think the US government could already require US companies to pace frontier AI development today with essentially no further preparation.
