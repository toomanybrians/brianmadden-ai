# GitHub picked a bad week to go down

## Insights

- Cursor launched "Origin," its own code-hosting platform, defaulting on for paid plans unless an admin opts out (pricing/data retention terms unpublished); GitHub suffered a ~6h42m outage hours later. With SpaceX having closed a $60B purchase of Cursor's parent company on Aug 14, a single owner now controls the editor, the code repository, and the underlying model — a vertical consolidation in the AI coding stack.
- Stripe finalized acquiring OpenRouter (an AI model routing gateway serving ~8M developers across 400+ models) for $7B+, up from a $1.3B valuation just three months earlier in May. Combined with Stripe's January purchase of usage-billing company Metronome, this puts model selection, metering, and payment infrastructure under one company — positioning Stripe as the transaction layer for agent-initiated spending.
- Anthropic gave Claude's Gmail connector the ability to send, reply, and forward email (not just read), plus file management in Drive; approval is on by default but can be disabled. This marks a shift from AI as passive reader to active sender — a distinct risk category since a bad draft costs a minute but a bad sent email costs a relationship.
- Nine major tech companies carry roughly $3 trillion in off-balance-sheet commitments tied mostly to AI infrastructure buildout (per WSJ), versus ~$600B in reported capex — Alphabet alone holds $811B, up from $332B three months prior. These are largely non-cancellable purchase commitments and leases, meaning the bill comes due regardless of whether revenue materializes.
- An essay titled "AI;DR (AI; Didn't Read)" — arguing recipients have no obligation to read unedited AI-generated output sent to them — hit Hacker News's front page with 500+ comments, suggesting an emerging social/etiquette norm is forming in real time around AI-generated content in professional communication.

## Quote

> A drafting assistant that misreads a thread costs you a minute. A sending one costs you a relationship.
