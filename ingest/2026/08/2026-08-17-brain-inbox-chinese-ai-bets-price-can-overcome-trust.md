# ⚙️ Chinese AI bets price can overcome trust

## Insights

- Chinese AI labs (DeepSeek, Alibaba, Z.ai) released a wave of new models/tools targeting enterprise and developer audiences: DeepSeek's open-source "Harness v0.1" agent framework (MIT license) and DeepSeek-V4-Pro directly challenge Anthropic's Claude Code; Alibaba's Qwen-3.8 emphasizes coding/office workflows; Z.ai's GLM-5.3 claims major coding gains plus "emergent" cybersecurity capabilities.
- The strategic bet: these firms are competing on cost and customizability against pricier US frontier models (GPT-5.6, Anthropic's Fable 5), at a moment when enterprises are increasingly strained by Claude Code/Codex bills.
- The trade-off enterprises face is explicitly framed as cost vs. trust — US labs accuse Chinese competitors of model distillation, and the open models reportedly carry weaker guardrails and higher jailbreak risk.
- Frontier US labs' enterprise revenue is enormous and growing (OpenAI's run-rate reportedly nearing $40B pre-IPO; Anthropic eyeing a $2T valuation), which explains why Chinese firms want a piece of that market.
- Separately, enterprise agent adoption is hitting real friction: Deloitte survey finds only 15% of orgs have scaled multi-agent systems despite high ambition, with just 21% saying their processes are agent-ready — most are bolting agents onto existing workflows rather than redesigning around them.
- A Google/MIT Tech Review study ties agent output quality directly to data access: orgs exposing agents to over 70% of enterprise data report consistently accurate outputs, versus only 22% trust among orgs limiting access to 30% or less — average enterprise data exposure is just 45%.
