# In This Labor Market, Humanities May Be More Desirable Than STEM

## Insights

- Argues the labor market is pivoting away from its historical premium on STEM/analytical skills toward humanities and people skills, citing a viral FT anecdote in which a financier described AI-native young hires as "wildly impressive" but "alarmingly shallow," lacking critical thinking.
- Frames AI as a tool that automates "the predictable parts of a job," which raises the relative value of humanities training because it centers on the historically unpredictable human being — adaptability, nuance, and reading context AI can't access.
- Cites supporting statements from other tech figures: Reed Hastings (Netflix) predicting demand for human emotional experiences over AI-generated ones (e.g., no one wants to watch robots play basketball), and Daniela Amodei (Anthropic) saying the company hires for EQ, communication, kindness, and curiosity over pure technical skill.
- Notes employer demand is increasingly rooted in persuasion and communication skills, partly because the public is skeptical of tech/data-center narratives and can sense inauthentic or "unoriginal" (AI-generated) output — referencing Gen Alpha's slang "that's so AI" as a term for unoriginality becoming culturally uncool.
- Suggests the future may not be a "humanities bootcamp" but a blending of disciplines — pointing to a University of Colorado Boulder course co-taught by a mathematician and a Renaissance scholar as a model for merging STEM and humanities rather than treating them as competing silos.
- Secondary/aside items flagged in the "what else we're paying attention to" roundup: 404 Media tracked a rare book via AirTag to an Amazon facility shredding books to train LLMs; AI companies reportedly repositioning their PR message toward "curing cancer" amid unpopularity; Google bought Spirit Airlines' customer data for $10M for AI training; Walmart workers reportedly must correct inefficient AI-generated warehouse picking routes.

## Quote

> Critical thinking is the most important hireable skill—a skill that humanities graduates seem to have more of at the present than STEM ones.
