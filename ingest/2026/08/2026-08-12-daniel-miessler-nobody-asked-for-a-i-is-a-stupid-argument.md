# "Nobody Asked for A.I." Is a Stupid Argument

## Insights

- Miessler rebuts the "nobody asked for AI" argument (attributed to Cory Doctorow) by arguing that no invention is ever explicitly requested in advance — people didn't ask for penicillin, cars, or electricity either; solutions arise from problems, not consumer demand.
- He argues people have been articulating the underlying problems AI addresses for decades: soul-crushing corporate knowledge work, incompetent/lazy coworkers, bad customer service, unfair performance reviews and compensation, and broken hiring processes.
- He extends the argument to healthcare and social wellbeing: most of the world lacks basic medical access, and hundreds of millions suffer loneliness, mental illness, cancer, and dementia with no support.
- His core framing: AI's value proposition is providing "billions or trillions more brains and hands" and near-infinite time to work on long-standing human problems, not a manufactured/marketed need.
- He includes a self-authored 2022/2025/2026 progression joke: complaints shift from hating corporate jobs, to fearing AI will take those jobs, to accepting AI enables people to do what they "were supposed to do in the first place."
- Piece is framed as a direct response to a Jacobin essay linking AI to monopoly power and bubble dynamics.

## Quote

> Inventions arise naturally out of problems. AI isn't going parabolic because of some crazy marketing campaign.
