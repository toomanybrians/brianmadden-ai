# 🔮 The curious economics of a $6 AI agent #597

## Insights

- Azhar's personal AI agent ("RMA," an OpenClaw agent) once spiked to $500/day when it ran on higher-tier legacy models by default; a manual audit traced this to inefficient model routing, and per-token costs have since fallen even as capability rose.
- Cost control now hinges on dynamically routing tasks to the cheapest sufficient model — defaulting to subscription-included models (e.g., OpenAI Codex) before falling back to cheaper open-weight or specialized models, and escalating to premium models only for hard tasks.
- Price competition between Anthropic and OpenAI, intensified by Chinese model advances, is a direct driver of falling agent operating costs.
- Amazon reportedly spent ~$1.8M over five months on a Claude-based project, with a senior employee noting it's genuinely hard to track AI-related costs — echoing the "hard to audit" theme at enterprise scale.
- Ramp data shows wide dispersion in AI spend per employee: top 1% of firms spent a median $7,400/employee in July vs. $11.95/employee at the median firm — suggesting AI spending intensity is highly concentrated, not evenly diffused.
- Even at optimized ~$6/day, one power user's annual AI agent spend (~$2,000/year) is "substantial" for someone not writing code, hinting at real budget exposure as personal/professional AI agent use scales.

## Quote

> It's difficult to figure out how much anything [AI-related] costs.
