# Last Week in AI #251 - Mythos Back, Sonnet 5, Etched, LongCat

## Insights

- Anthropic's regulatory posture is loosening: after talks with the US government, the company redeployed its Claude Fable/Mythos models with new cybersecurity classifiers and a jailbreak-severity framework co-developed with major partners, though this highlights uneven release constraints compared to OpenAI's approach.
- Claude Sonnet 5 is positioned explicitly as a cost play for agentic workloads — cheaper agent execution, better coding/benchmark performance, and reduced misaligned behavior, but with weaker cybersecurity capability than top-tier competitors, suggesting a deliberate tradeoff of capability ceiling for safety/cost.
- Etched is making a serious hardware bet on full-stack inference, pulling 400+ engineers from Nvidia and TSMC with contracts already implying $1B in demand — a signal that specialized inference silicon (vs. general GPUs) is becoming a real competitive front.
- LongCat-2.0 from China is an open-source MoE model notable for large-scale training and efficiency techniques, part of a broader wave of Chinese open releases (alongside DeepSeek's staff-doubling expansion) that keeps pressure on US labs' pricing and openness norms.
- New benchmarks (OSWorld2.0, TUA-Bench, SWE-Together) reflect the field's shift toward evaluating agents on long-horizon, real-world computer-use and coding tasks rather than one-shot Q&A — a maturation signal for enterprise-agent readiness.
- Consumer/creative tooling continues advancing in parallel: Google's NotebookLM now generates TikTok-style video summaries of research, and Nano Banana 2 Lite offers faster, cheaper image generation via API — both lowering the barrier to AI-assisted content production.
