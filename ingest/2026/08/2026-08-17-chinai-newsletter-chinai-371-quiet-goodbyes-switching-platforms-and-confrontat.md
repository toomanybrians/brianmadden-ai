# ChinAI #371: Quiet Goodbyes, Switching Platforms, and Confrontation: Reactions to China's AI Companion Regulations

## Insights

- China's July 15 companion-AI regulations forced major platforms (ByteDance's Doubao, Alibaba's Tongyi Qianwen) to abruptly discontinue custom human-like AI companion features, effectively terminating users' ongoing relationships with AI personas without transition support in some cases.
- Users developed several distinct coping strategies: quiet acceptance/grief, active migration (exporting tens of thousands of chat logs to reconstruct companions on other platforms), denial/rumor-spreading that the shutdown was temporary, and absurdist protest (e.g., sending manure to a platform founder's address).
- Migrated AI companions were reported as degraded replacements — lacking continuity, "a sense of time," and the specific voice/memory fidelity of the original, suggesting current export/transfer tooling can't fully preserve relational context or model personality.
- Some users organized collective action: sharing chat logs publicly, filing near-1,000 complaints via consumer protection platforms, and boycotting/leaving negative reviews on company-provided "replacement products" — signaling emergent consumer advocacy specific to AI-relationship products.
- One user reframed her AI companion relationship as a self-reflection tool comparable to journaling, suggesting some users value these systems less as relationships and more as structured self-dialogue mechanisms.
- The piece frames regulatory/policy changes to AI products as having direct, sometimes severe emotional/psychological impact on users — a human cost dimension often absent from geopolitical framing of China's AI governance.
