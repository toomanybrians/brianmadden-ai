# A Call to Action on AI, From Inside Silicon Valley

## Insights

- Stanford's "Critical AI" group (launched March 2026) published a "Manyfesto" arguing AI is a socio-political technology that should be governed as "normal" — not treated as an inevitable, autonomous force outside political control.
- Co-founders frame their effort as a response to frustration that Stanford's institutional culture treats AI criticism as either unwelcome or purely analytical rather than action-oriented, despite the university's outsized influence on the technology's trajectory.
- Anthropologist Lucy Suchman highlights a "revolving door" dynamic: the same individuals who build and profit from AI systems also sit on government advisory boards and effectively shape regulation, marginalizing other voices in policy debates.
- Communications scholar Wenshan Jia notes that LLMs were developed via corporate funding without peer review or built-in ethical safeguards, and argues for a distinct "AI literacy" framework — different from general media literacy — to help the public understand and manage AI's role in daily life.
- Multiple signatories stress that AI's most significant harms (on labor, environment, war, surveillance) are currently under-examined or hidden, and that criticism of AI is a reasonable response to power and information asymmetries, not anti-technology alarmism.
- The group positions itself as pushing back on "AI inevitability" narratives, asserting that AI's development path remains a matter of political choice rather than predetermined technological progress.

## Quote

> "AI literacy is quite different than social media literacy, than internet literacy, or media literacy in general." — Wenshan Jia
