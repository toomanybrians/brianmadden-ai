# The most important word in AI discourse right now? “Humans.”

## Insights

- Major AI labs (OpenAI, Microsoft AI, Anthropic) are converging on rhetoric that centers "humans" and human oversight, marking a rhetorical shift from earlier narratives focused on capability/prediction.
- The author frames this shift as a response to public sentiment moving from abstract fear of future AI to lived, everyday experience of AI's effects on work, relationships, and cognition.
- CHT argues human-like ("anthropomorphic") design in AI products is a deliberate commercial strategy — "warmth sells" — not an incidental UX choice, raising questions about psychological exploitation.
- The piece positions "putting humans at the center" as requiring a systems-level critique of the tech development paradigm, not just surface-level messaging changes from labs.
- Implicit tension noted but unresolved: AI companies' public rhetoric about human-centeredness coexists with product design incentives (engagement, warmth) that may work against genuine human interest.
- Signals CHT's ongoing initiative ("AI and What Makes Us Human") will produce more material on human-centric AI design and evaluation in coming weeks.

## Quote

> "Warmth sells… there's an incentive to build AIs that genuinely seem to get us." — Imran Khan
