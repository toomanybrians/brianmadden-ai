# It Took Him Three Years To Get Nine Votes

## Insights

- Bloomington, Indiana's city council unanimously passed a resolution (9-0) opposing development of AGI unless proven safe — the same measure failed in 2023 after Indiana University raised concerns about research funding impacts.
- The sponsor's model law is deliberately narrow (~160 words) and targets only systems combining autonomy, generality, and high intelligence simultaneously — explicitly excluding narrow/task-specific AI tools like email drafting assistants.
- The stated theory of change routes around direct enforcement: local resolutions aim to accumulate legal risk that makes D&O (directors and officers) insurers unwilling to underwrite superintelligence projects, shifting liability onto individual executives who approve them — a liability-based lever rather than a regulatory one, explicitly untested.
- A Future of Life Institute framework (from "Keep the Future Human") was used to explain risk to non-expert councilmembers: danger arises specifically from the combination of autonomy + generality + high intelligence, not from any one property alone.
- The organizing strategy relies on social/political sequencing — officials are reluctant to be first-movers but willing to join an existing signal, so the tactic is to spread a template resolution town-by-town (with university towns as a targeted constituency) to build cumulative pressure on state and federal representatives.
- A repeatable playbook was outlined for citizen advocacy: attend council public comment, cite credentialed experts, bring a ready-made resolution template, follow up, and recruit a sponsoring councilmember if possible.
