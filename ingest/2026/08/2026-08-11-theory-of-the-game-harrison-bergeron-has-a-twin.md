# Harrison Bergeron has a twin

## Insights

- Hoffman frames current AI discourse as failing to distinguish between technologies used to "lift people" versus those used to "make them kneel" — arguing society's judgment mechanisms (media, law, discourse) can't yet tell liberators from tyrants and end up punishing both.
- He argues the field is over-indexed on debates about who builds AI (labs, ownership, credentialing) and under-indexed on how it gets shaped and what gets built with it — a plea to redirect attention from provenance to application.
- Slop is reframed as a normal transitional cost of any transformative technology, not evidence against AI's legitimacy — a rhetorical move to inoculate AI from dismissal based on low-quality output.
- The "theft" framing around training data is challenged by analogy: human learning from a shared cultural commons is called education, while a machine doing structurally the same thing is called larceny — Hoffman says the line isn't obvious.
- The accompanying fiction ("Harrison and Garrison") updates Vonnegut's equality-enforcement dystopia into an AI-alignment allegory: universal "smoothing" of outputs to an average, a mandatory AI assistant that interrupts thought with commercial suggestions, and tiered access (free vs. paid) as the new mechanism of enforced sameness.
- The story's twist — twin rebels, one using tech to elevate and one to dominate, both shot by an indiscriminate "collective judgment" — is offered as a parable for how uncritical anti-AI or pro-AI reflexes fail to discriminate between beneficial and exploitative uses.

## Quote

> We are currently over-invested in who creates artificial intelligence and under-invested in how we can shape it.
