# Unsupervised Learning NO. 540

## Insights

- AI agents now operate across nearly every piece of enterprise technology, raising urgent questions about verifying agent identity; a layered security approach is described as the only viable way to address this as the technology evolves (discussion with Okta's SVP of AI Security).
- A recommended on-ramp for non-engineer knowledge workers into AI coding is "software-shaped" ordinary tasks — automation, upgrades, and small tool-building — rather than starting with complex engineering problems.
- A specific security risk is flagged: a potential "prompt injection worm" that could propagate through AI systems, described as a near-term concern.
- A tier-list ranking of major AI models is circulating (via Theo), offering a snapshot of the current competitive AI model landscape.
- Practical resilience advice for AI-dependent workflows: prepare for the possibility of cloud AI providers or model hosts (e.g., Hugging Face) being cut off due to government action, by regularly downloading and locally storing the latest open models as a backup.
- Separately, testing of six chatbots against state-aligned propaganda narratives found they correctly debunked false claims about 75% of the time, while AI-generated search summaries were notably less reliable at filtering misinformation.
