# Import AI 468: 23 RSI ideas; PostTrainBench+; and how trust and transparency interplay with AI racing

## Insights

- IFP's policy proposal frames recursive self-improvement (RSI) risk not as something to halt but to instrument — the goal is building "pedals and sensors" (transparency, verification tech, state capacity) so slowdown becomes possible later if needed, rather than trying to slow things now.
- A game-theoretic model (MIT/Columbia) of AI racing firms finds that coordinated slowdowns require both transparency (fast, credible detection of a rival's behavior) and trust (believing the rival is a rational actor) — neither alone is sufficient.
- Counterintuitively, more transparency can temporarily destabilize cooperation: faster detection makes it cheaper to "free-ride" by waiting to confirm a rival stopped rather than stopping unconditionally, before eventually restoring stability at very high transparency.
- With low trust between firms, the model finds racing to disaster is the only equilibrium; with high trust, the probability of mutual ruin between rational firms shrinks quadratically — trust is the dominant variable.
- Intology's Locus system hit 44.7% on PostTrainBench (automated post-training/model-improvement benchmark), beating prior frontier-agent baselines, and reached 51.6% in an extended-compute variant — surpassing the human instruction-tuning baseline for the first time, a concrete data point on AI automating AI R&D itself.

## Quote

> With high trust, in every equilibrium, the probability that two rational firms race forever vanishes quadratically in the prior odds ratio of rationality.
