# Hyperlaw: AI Will Change How Law Evolves

## Insights

- AI is boosting legal task productivity substantially: Choi et al. found 12–32% gains in task completion speed, while Schwarcz et al. found AI increased law student productivity on legal tasks by up to 130%.
- The author predicts AI agents will soon be able to fully draft first versions of most legal writing, shifting lawyers' role toward reviewing and editing rather than originating text.
- Cheaper contract-writing (via AI) and cheaper litigation pull in opposite directions: more complete contracts reduce disputes ("contracting effect"), while cheaper trial costs reduce incentives to settle and push more disputes to court ("litigation effect").
- Because trial becomes relatively cheaper than settlement, AI is expected to shrink the range of mutually acceptable settlements, increasing the share of disputes that go to trial rather than settle.
- The net effect on how fast law evolves varies by domain: tort law (where contracts are rare) is predicted to see faster legal change, while property/contract law outcomes are ambiguous depending on which cost — gap-filling or litigation — falls faster.
- A hypothesized "hyperlaw" future — law that updates continuously and quickly (e.g., liability rules for autonomous vehicles evolving in a decade instead of decades) — carries risks: investment uncertainty, incentives to relabel disputes to exploit faster-evolving legal categories, and courts potentially raising filing barriers to throttle litigation volume.

## Quote

> A hyperlaw system of torts might adjust to autonomous vehicles, for example, in a decade or less.
