# Greg Brockman: Cyber Defenders’ AI Moment Is Now

## Insights

- Greg Brockman argues cybersecurity has hit a watershed: AI can now automate real-world attack chains (finding vulnerabilities, chaining minor weaknesses, operating at machine speed/scale), but the same capabilities can equally empower defenders — the outcome depends on who moves faster.
- In a live demo, ChatGPT Work (GPT-5.6 Sol) audited Brockman's personal website in ~15 minutes, surfacing 13 issues (outdated jQuery, email-security gaps, an unencrypted Cloudflare–AWS connection), then autonomously remediated most of them within an hour (reconfiguring security settings, removing jQuery, migrating to Cloudflare Pages, improving email protections).
- OpenAI reports it already uses AI to triage nearly all initial internal security alerts before human specialists get involved, freeing humans to focus on higher-judgment decisions.
- Brockman's prescribed defender playbook: deploy capable AI agents now, assess critical systems, clear vulnerability backlogs, embed AI into the software development and incident-response pipeline, and automate incrementally while keeping humans accountable for consequential decisions.
- Separately, OpenAI announced $1M in grants plus up to $1M in API credits across 14 independent organizations (US, EU, Brazil, Singapore, South Korea) testing ideas from its "Industrial Policy for the Intelligence Age" report — topics include AI's effect on jobs/workforce, portable benefits for non-traditional work, AI and tax/revenue systems, energy infrastructure for data centers, and AI safety/governance capacity-building.
- A K-12 "AI Jam" in Salt Lake City (155 teachers, 7th such event this summer) reported 98% of educators left with a usable AI workflow — e.g., a teacher used ChatGPT to instantly adapt worksheets for mixed-level classrooms, a task that previously took much longer manually.

## Quote

> Give security teams capable AI agents. Assess critical systems. Attack vulnerability backlogs.
