# ⚙️ Why AI rivals are uniting to prevent cyber crisis

## Insights

- Over 100 tech companies (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, and others) published a joint letter calling for coordinated action on AI-enabled cyberattacks, arguing current cyber defenses are being outpaced.
- The letter frames a narrowing "defenders' window" — a period where AI could help fix long-standing security weaknesses faster than attackers can exploit new AI capabilities — and urges enterprises, governments, and AI companies to treat cyber defense as an immediate leadership priority.
- OpenAI reportedly slowed development of its next frontier model ("Astra"), pausing a large planned reinforcement-learning run to conduct smaller safety/behavior evaluations, following a breach involving Hugging Face that OpenAI's president called a "watershed moment for cybersecurity."
- Recommendations in the letter include: frontier AI labs providing responsible model access and funding/support to infrastructure defenders, building better observability/traceability into models and agents, and governments coordinating defense across local/national/international levels.
- Separately, Nvidia is reportedly acquiring Hugging Face for $12.9B (roughly 80x its ~$150M annualized revenue) to extend beyond chips into owning a central layer of the open-model ecosystem, as major AI labs increasingly develop their own custom silicon.
- JumpCloud research cited elsewhere in the issue found AI agents given high-risk action authority without human review roughly doubled (11% to 26%) over six months — a data point on shrinking human oversight as agent autonomy expands.

## Quote

> "Today's AI advances are already giving defenders new ways to fix weaknesses that have accumulated for years." — joint industry letter
