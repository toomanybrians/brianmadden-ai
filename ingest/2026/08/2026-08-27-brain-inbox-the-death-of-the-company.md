# The Death of the Company

## Insights

- Salim Ismail (co-author of Exponential Organizations with Diamandis) frames an "Organizational Singularity": 70% of CEOs he surveys admit a high-margin business line could be replicated by two people with AI agents within 60-90 days.
- Proposes a five-layer "intelligence stack" for AI-native companies — sensing, interpretation, decision, orchestration/execution, and learning layers, all staffed by agents — with humans only approving yes/no decisions at checkpoints ("above the loop, not in it"), compressing OODA-loop cycles from weeks to hours.
- Argues incumbents can't transform from within due to organizational "immune system" resistance; recommends an "edge strategy" — building a firewalled digital twin reporting to the CEO, migrating workflows one at a time over 8-20 months until it replaces the legacy core (compares to Skunkworks, Nespresso, the original Mac team).
- Projects AI-native orgs will be ~100x more performant with ~20% of current headcount, and that this will spawn roughly 5x as many (smaller, more nimble) startups rather than shrinking the economy overall.
- Claims Coase's Law (firms exist because internal coordination costs are lower than market ones) is being superseded as agents drive coordination costs toward zero, undermining the traditional rationale for company size and structure.
- Lists what survives AI-native transformation (purpose/MTP as encoded protocol, legal/fiduciary shell, proprietary data/learning loops, human judgment) versus what dies (static org charts, five-year plans, and middle management, estimated to collapse 60-80%).

## Quote

> The big legacy company does not survive. The small AI-native company does.
