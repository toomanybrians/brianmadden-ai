# White House’s Response to China Lacks Confidence in America

## Insights

- The White House accused Moonshot AI of covertly distilling Anthropic's "Fable" model to build Kimi K3, threatening sanctions/Entity List action — but offered no public evidence, and officials' own timeline is inconsistent (Fable was unavailable for most of K3's training window).
- The episode highlights a structural conflict of interest: Anthropic has strong financial incentive to frame Chinese open-weight competitors as derivative/theft-based, since open, cheap alternatives directly threaten its subscription/API revenue model.
- Chinese AI firms (Moonshot, DeepSeek, Zhipu) are pursuing a distinct business logic — open-sourcing weights while monetizing efficient hosted inference — trading direct model-access revenue for global standard-setting and adoption, especially where US models are costly or restricted.
- The author argues the more consequential question isn't whether distillation occurred, but what it reveals: if a frontier lab like Anthropic can't defend against distillation by a smaller rival, its case for keeping models closed "for safety" and its claims of an unassailable AGI lead both weaken.
- Argues the strategic implication is that open-sourcing may be less a moral choice for American labs and more a competitive necessity, since gains diffuse to open Chinese models regardless of restriction efforts.

## Quote

> If China will always steal the gains and release them into the world, then Anthropic should do it first.
