# This is the best way to choose an AI model

## Insights

- Argues the common approach to model selection — asking "which model should I use?" — starts from the wrong place; it should start with understanding one's own thinking/working process, not the model itself or a leaderboard.
- Suggests identifying how your best work actually gets made: e.g., via long unstructured brain-dumps later refined, tight iterative back-and-forth, or sketch-then-refine cycles.
- Once someone can see their own thinking loop clearly, the model question becomes answerable: which model helps you move through that specific loop faster.
- Different working styles call for different models — someone who works via rambling prompts needs a model that can absorb and run with unstructured input, while someone who thinks through debate needs a model that pushes back/argues.
- Frames model choice as personalized to individual cognitive process rather than a universal "best" answer, rejecting the premise that one model fits all users.
- References an external tool (linked) designed to map a person's working style to a corresponding model recommendation.

## Quote

> Do not look at the model first. Look at your best work, and look at how you actually got there.
