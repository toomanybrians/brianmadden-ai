# AI Content Must Now Carry a Label. Cameras Are Next.

## Insights

- Two AI content-transparency laws took effect simultaneously on August 2, 2026 — EU AI Act Article 50 and California's AI Transparency Act — creating a de facto global compliance baseline since neither law depends on company location, only on market access/reach into that jurisdiction.
- The compliance ecosystem is converging on C2PA (Content Credentials), an open cryptographic standard that hashes and signs content plus metadata; Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI have committed to adopting it, but Apple and Midjourney notably have not.
- Provenance metadata is not proof of authenticity — it's an "evidentiary layer" with known failure modes: metadata stripping on upload/conversion, the "analog hole" (screenshotting/rerecording erases records), "signing-through-camera" (a camera honestly signs a deepfake photographed off a screen), and forged manifests.
- California's law escalates over time: embedded disclosure/detection tools by 2026, a ban on platforms stripping compliant provenance data by 2027, and a requirement that hardware (smartphones, cameras, voice recorders) generate provenance records by default by 2028.
- Enforcement carries real teeth: EU fines up to €15M or 3% of global turnover; California fines are only $5,000 per violation but accrue daily, compounding quickly across noncompliant products.
- U.S. courts are separately drafting Federal Rule of Evidence 901(c) to handle AI-fabrication claims in litigation — a lower "sufficient evidence of fabrication" bar shifts burden to the evidence's proponent to show likely authenticity, and provenance records (or their suspicious absence) will factor into that test.

## Quote

> Content provenance is the evidentiary layer the AI era has lacked.
