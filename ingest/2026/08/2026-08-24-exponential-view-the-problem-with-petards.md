# 🏦 The problem with petards

## Insights

- Frames the AI industry's decade-long messaging as a rhetorical "petard": labs and investors argued AI was simultaneously essential to economic growth and disease eradication, yet so dangerous and complex that only a select few could be trusted to steward it.
- Identifies the core contradiction in industry rhetoric: AI could take jobs, cause harm, or spiral out of control, but the same firms insisted they needed to build it fast and be left alone in the meantime.
- Argues this messaging has now "backfired" at the local/county level in the US, tying the industry's self-serving safety-vs-urgency narrative to a broader political backlash.
- Suggests datacenter buildout is perceived not as an abstract economic or healthcare benefit but as tangibly serving an "out-group" — a driver of local resistance separate from the technology's merits.
- Piece is presented as one installment; the full essay (not included here) covers community attitudes toward datacenters and whether they deliver real local benefit.

## Quote

> This was the petard: AI is too important for America not to let us get on with it.
