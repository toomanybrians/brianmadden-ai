# Weekly Dose of Optimism #204

## Insights

- Enigma raised $71M and launched robots.online, letting anyone remotely control real robot arms; the founders (non-roboticists from Israeli tech/military backgrounds) are betting that studying raw human-robot interaction patterns yields better interfaces and possibly novel training data, sidestepping the usual robotics-PhD path.
- Google DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2 family (VLA model, embodied-reasoning model, on-device model) now supports whole-body humanoid control — walking, crouching, dexterous manipulation — with one checkpoint generalizing across different robot bodies (Apollo, Franka Duo), though speed and fine dexterity remain unsolved.
- DoorDash secured FAA Part 135 air-carrier certification for in-house drone delivery (DoorDash Air), framed less as a proprietary hardware bet and more as an attempt to commoditize the delivery "last mile" — owning the demand network while standardizing/competing across delivery methods (Dashers, robots, drones, AV partners).
- Amazon's Zoox received first-ever US approval for a commercial robotaxi with no steering wheel or driver's seat, marking a shift from retrofitted autonomous cars to vehicles designed natively for a driverless paradigm — reimagining car interiors as multi-purpose "moving rooms."
- Underlying theme across items: physical-world AI (robotics, drones, AVs) is advancing via infrastructure/platform plays (commoditizing complements) rather than single-company vertical hardware bets, echoing patterns seen in software AI competition.

## Quote

> Like Nvidia and Microsoft signing the letter, DoorDash is trying to commoditize its complements.
