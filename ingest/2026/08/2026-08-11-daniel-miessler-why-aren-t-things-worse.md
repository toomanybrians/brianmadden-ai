# Why Aren’t Things Worse?

## Insights

- Proposes applying Theory of Constraints to security/risk: instead of asking why bad things happen, ask what specific frictions currently prevent bad things from happening more often.
- Argues this constraint-mapping exercise is largely absent from mainstream security discourse despite being foundational to understanding risk.
- Raises a labor-market/security intersection: displaced junior developers, especially in regions with weak law enforcement, could become a new supply of technically skilled people with fewer legitimate options.
- Suggests AI — particularly unrestricted open-weight models and pre-packaged "crime harnesses" — could drastically lower the skill and traceability barriers to cybercrime and other tech-enabled organized crime.
- Frames the risk as a convergence problem: the timing of AI-driven ease-of-crime coinciding with AI-driven junior-dev job losses and weak new-grad hiring could simultaneously increase both capability and motive.
- Positions this as a general analytical lens for forecasting change: identify the flow points holding a system in equilibrium, then ask how new forces shift pressure on each one.

## Quote

> What if AI ... were able to make it 20x easier to do cyber crime without getting caught?
