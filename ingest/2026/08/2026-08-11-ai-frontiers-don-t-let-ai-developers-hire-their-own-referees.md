# Don’t Let AI Developers Hire Their Own Referees

## Insights

- Proposed AI governance models (regulatory markets, "private governance," the FRONTIER Act, SB 813, Connecticut's IVO pilot) let developers select and pay their own independent verification organizations (IVOs), creating a conflict of interest similar to pre-2008 credit-rating agencies grading their own clients.
- Competition among IVOs, meant to improve rigor, instead pushes toward leniency because developers can shop for whichever verifier grades most easily — worsened when certification also confers a liability shield, narrowing incentives to only what's needed to pass.
- Government licensing of IVOs to police this incentive problem just relocates the expertise/independence burden back onto regulators — the very gap the IVO model was designed to avoid.
- Underwriters Laboratories is often cited as proof private certification works, but its legitimacy historically came from being funded and overseen by insurers with capital at risk, not from manufacturer-paid testing alone.
- Because much of frontier AI's risk falls on third parties (not the developer or user), ordinary customer-driven safety demand is too weak a check — unlike consumer products, where buyers are also the ones at risk.
- Proposed alternative: mandatory liability insurance for AI developers, mirroring auto insurance and IIHS — insurers bear claims costs, giving them real financial incentive to price risk accurately and to keep monitoring/enforcing fixes over the life of a policy.

## Quote

> Competition—the feature that is meant to make the IVO model effective—instead drives it toward laxity.
