# Venture Capital Has Never Been This Concentrated

## Insights

- AI captured 86% of U.S. venture capital in H1 2026, up from 65% the year before — a concentration level with no historical precedent, exceeding even the 2001 dot-com era's 38% allocation to B2B software.
- Two companies, OpenAI and Anthropic, alone absorbed 53% of all venture dollars raised in H1 2026; mega-deals (>$100M rounds) made up 88% of capital deployed, versus deals under $100M shrinking to 12.5% from 44% two years ago.
- Non-AI sectors are being starved: funding for biotech, fintech, healthtech, and cybersecurity roughly halved since 2021, and AI-backed companies are now twice as likely to raise follow-on rounds as non-AI companies.
- Concentration is happening at the fund level too — the 10 largest VC funds captured 43% of committed capital in 2025 (up from 13% in 2021), and the three biggest brand firms (a16z, Founders Fund, Thrive) took 48% of H1 2026 capital; first-time fund formation is at its lowest since 2016, and the total number of U.S. VC firms declined for the first time on record.
- This private-market narrowing is compounding a public-sector pullback: federal R&D funding has fallen from 67% of national R&D in 1964 to 18% in 2022 (0.63% of GDP vs. near 2% in the 1960s), with thousands of NIH/NSF grants terminated and remaining federal science budgets redirected toward AI, quantum, and nuclear.
- The piece frames this dual concentration as a systemic risk: venture capital has historically been causally responsible for a large share of top U.S. public companies, so narrowing bets to one sector/few firms could shrink the pipeline of future major innovations if the AI bet underperforms.
