# AI CEO Building Platform Based On Human Nature Is Confused By Human Nature

## Insights

- Anthropic is posting mid-level corporate roles (storyteller, events manager) at $200K–$400K salaries, even as the broader tech labor market skews heavily toward employers, with layoffs widely attributed to AI replacement or used as AI-justified cover.
- Dario Amodei has expressed concern that people are joining Anthropic for salary rather than mission — a stance the author finds oddly disconnected from basic incentives, given Anthropic's near-$1 trillion valuation and looming IPO as a "golden carrot" of generational wealth.
- Some recruits publicly frame joining Anthropic as mission-driven, tied to prestige, ethos adjacent to effective altruism, desire to influence a consequential technology from inside, or self-perceived alignment with "superintelligence" — though ego/status motives are noted as plausible alongside altruism.
- Anthropic's public benefit corporation structure is positioned as prioritizing long-term societal gain over short-term investor returns, but the author argues it still must compete via the same capital-intensive infrastructure spending as OpenAI and others, constraining how "different" its practices can actually be.
- The piece cites Anthropic's public refusal to work with U.S. military/Iran war efforts as a PR move plausibly aimed at differentiating from ChatGPT/OpenAI, despite prior involvement with government tech use — questioning how much moral positioning is strategic versus principled.
- Frames AI-sector job-hopping as following prior tech hype cycles (rideshare, scooters, crypto), suggesting some workers may view the current AI wave as a last window for insider wealth before further job losses.

## Quote

> How is a company that is compiling billions of data points on human nature so confused about human nature?
