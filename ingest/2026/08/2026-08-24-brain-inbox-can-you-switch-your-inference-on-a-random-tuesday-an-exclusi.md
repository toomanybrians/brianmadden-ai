# Can you switch your inference on a random Tuesday? (An Exclusive with PandaOS on what European AI sovereignty actually costs)

## Insights

- The June 2026 Anthropic export-control suspension of Fable 5/Mythos 5 (lasting under three weeks) served as a "warning shot" showing European companies how exposed they are when a single US provider is load-bearing for AI operations.
- PandaOS founders argue "sovereignty" is misdefined by political responses (e.g., Austria lobbying to host Anthropic in the EU) — a European postcode doesn't remove US jurisdiction, since the CLOUD Act still applies to US entities regardless of server location; Microsoft France reportedly couldn't guarantee EU data was shielded from US authorities, and Airbus migrated workloads from AWS to French provider Scaleway as a result.
- Real sovereignty is reframed as operational redundancy/optionality rather than ownership of chips or models: the practical test ("litmus test") is whether a company can switch inference providers/models on short notice without breaking operations — not whether infrastructure sits on European soil.
- Model capability is described as commoditizing quickly (parity between six-month-old frontier models and current open-source ones), so the founders argue building resilient, swappable infrastructure matters more than chasing the "best" model.
- On-premise/self-hosting is deemed unnecessary for ~99% of companies (per Philipp) — the exceptions being extreme IP sensitivity, government contracts, or scale that justifies compute costs; most companies are better served owning their data layer and maintaining switchable access rather than owning infrastructure.
- Observed enterprise dysfunction: large German corporates default to Microsoft Copilot with curtailed features due to IT risk-aversion, or exhaust AI workshop credits on day one — described as a procurement/governance failure distinct from the sovereignty/jurisdiction debate.
- Europe's plausible competitive opening is framed as specialized/vertical models (e.g., Black Forest Labs, Prior Labs' tabular models acquired by SAP) rather than competing head-on at the general frontier model layer, which is dominated by the US and China (Europe has no distinct share in large-scale model counts per Epoch AI data).
