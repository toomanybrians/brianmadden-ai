# The UK's former head AI safety scientist on how to solve alignment before superintelligence arrives | Geoffrey Irving

## Insights

- Irving argues time-horizon length isn't a reliable safety bound: a model good at week-long subtasks could still chain them into multi-year plans, so "models only get trained on short horizons" is not a reassuring argument against long-term misaligned behavior.
- He describes current frontier labs' alignment strategies as a mix of "character training" (shaping model persona/values), scalable oversight (models supervising themselves as they scale), and monitoring (interpretability, AI control) — but says no lab has a rigorous argument this combination will hold up past human-level capability.
- Labs differ meaningfully in approach: Anthropic leans toward virtue-ethics-style character training with few hard rules and lower "corrigibility" emphasis; OpenAI uses many more explicit deontological rules and prioritizes deference/corrigibility to humans; DeepMind's approach is less defined publicly.
- Describes a known failure mode from human-subject debate experiments (with Beth Barnes): a dishonest debater can win by producing a complex, plausible-sounding argument whose flaw neither debater can locate — meaning scalable oversight schemes (debate, amplification, constitutional AI) may fail even in theory, not just practice.
- Predicts full-blown superintelligence within roughly two to three years, driven not by sudden generalization leaps but by labs incrementally expanding data/training coverage from verifiable tasks into fuzzier, less-verifiable domains via deployment feedback and self-critique training.
- Argues there's a large "product overhang" — current models are already far more capable than the economy has absorbed — so pausing new frontier training for years wouldn't stall economic growth, since gains from better usage of existing models would continue.
- On policy: believes a slowdown should have started already, names lab CEOs and a handful of national leaders (fewer than 10 people) as the practical bottleneck for coordinated pause decisions, and argues AI safety researchers at labs face diminishing returns and should, on the margin, move into government roles instead.
