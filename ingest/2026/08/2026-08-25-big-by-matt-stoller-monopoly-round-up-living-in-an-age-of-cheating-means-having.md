# Monopoly Round-Up: Living in an Age of Cheating Means Having Your Time Stolen Constantly

## Insights

- Stoller frames "time theft" as an uncounted economic cost: consumers and small businesses increasingly bear unpaid "second jobs" resolving fraud, junk fees, and platform errors, which standard economic statistics (CPI, unemployment, GDP) don't capture.
- AI chatbots are cited as a mechanism of this time theft: in his account, an AI support bot looped through repeated "sincere" apologies while never resolving a booking dispute, effectively substituting automated stalling for accountability.
- He argues AI itself is presented as neutral technology, but current use cases mostly serve to let companies "dump" the cost of mistakes and disputes onto workers, consumers, and business owners rather than absorbing them internally.
- He connects public anger at AI and data centers not to opposition to the technology per se, but to lived frustration with automated systems (chatbots, dummy credit card systems, opaque commission structures) that offload resolution labor onto ordinary people.
- The broader argument: platform business models (travel booking intermediaries, tech distribution layers) create arbitrage opportunities by cheating both consumers and small business partners (e.g., independent hotels), betting that enough people will give up pursuing refunds.
- He attributes the rise in such scams to lack of regulatory enforcement (specifically FTC Chair Andrew Ferguson's non-enforcement), not to inherent flaws in AI or platform technology.

## Quote

> We all know, at some level, that the mistakes AI fosters are going to be dumped on us.
