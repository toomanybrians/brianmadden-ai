# ⚙️ What America’s most AI-exposed cities reveal

## Insights

- Indeed's new metro-level AI exposure metric (combining job-ad composition with skill-level AI exposure data) finds average exposure of 44 across US metros, ranging 40–60; San Jose (59), Seattle (57), DC (54), San Francisco (53), and Austin (52) are most exposed, all driven by software/data-heavy economies.
- Metros built around hands-on, physical labor show lower exposure scores, reinforcing a broader pattern across labor research that physical work is comparatively insulated from AI disruption.
- Indeed's economist stresses exposure scores indicate which skills/tasks are likely to be reshaped or augmented, not which jobs will disappear — outcome depends on how individual employers choose to deploy AI.
- OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Sol pricing over 20% (to $4/$20 per million input/output tokens), undercutting Anthropic's Fable 5 and Opus 5, amid enterprise pushback on frontier-model costs and Gartner projections that agentic AI could raise inference costs up to 5x by 2028.
- Enterprise spending data (via Ramp) shows companies favoring cheaper models over top-tier frontier ones — Anthropic's Fable 5 was only 6% of token spend, while its lower-cost Opus 5 has overtaken it in business usage.
- Thomson Reuters built a proprietary in-house LLM ("Thomson") on an open-source base, trained on decades of proprietary legal/financial content, for $40M — aimed at reducing dependence on external AI vendors and cutting costs while matching frontier-model performance on domain tasks.

## Quote

> "A high exposure score means more of the work could be reshaped, or augmented, by AI, not that the job will go away." — An Nguyen, Indeed Hiring Lab
