# The Loop and the Cut

## Insights

- Proposes that meaning/interpretation is not a static property but an ongoing biological process: a "write-read-rewrite" loop where symbolic marks (rate-independent, like DNA) get built, interpreted as instructions, and then revised so future readings differ.
- Draws on Howard Pattee's "epistemic cut" (symbolic vs. dynamical) and "semantic closure" (symbols must both be read by machinery and specify how that machinery is built) as the dividing line between mere causation and true interpretation.
- Argues the classic cut (DNA=symbolic, protein=dynamic) is too static; in reality the cut "rotates" over time in things like transcriptional condensates, stem cell differentiation, CRISPR immunity, and plant vernalization — the same material can play both instructional and executive roles across phases.
- Distinguishes this "autosemiosis" (self-production of the interpretive process itself) from Maturana/Varela's autopoiesis (self-production of a bodily boundary) — a system can maintain itself physically without maintaining a meaning-making loop.
- Frames "semiotic freedom" (slack between sign and response) as always constrained by the system's own history of prior writes — freedom is never absolute, only freedom from specific past constraints ("endogenous unfreedom").
- Sets up (in a later, truncated section) a direct application of these criteria to large language models, arguing that simulating the dynamics of meaning is not the same as instantiating the interpretive loop — relevant to debates about whether AI systems "understand" anything.

## Quote

> Wherever one material organization refuses to stay on one side of the line, the cut has to be understood as something the system keeps remaking in time.
