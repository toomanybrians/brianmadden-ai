# Why AT&T Is Betting Big on Open-Weight AI

## Insights

- AT&T runs open models for roughly 25% of its total AI usage today and expects that share to climb to 70-80% over time, citing cost control and data protection as the drivers.
- The company built a "smart router" that automatically directs prompts to the most cost-effective model for a given task, and reports 80-90% cost savings in certain applications from switching off proprietary models.
- AT&T processes an average of 45 billion AI tokens daily; open models fully handle customer-service transcript analysis and network operations, including OTel, a telecom-customized open model powering root-cause-detection agents.
- Open models can run on AT&T's own data centers rather than rented cloud infrastructure, further cutting costs; the company plans a future mix of cloud-hosted and self-hosted open models.
- Motivations beyond cost include avoiding vendor lock-in (including use of Chinese open models) and "AI sovereignty" — keeping control over the flow of proprietary enterprise data and IP, since that data is viewed as the real asset.
- Gartner analyst cited: enterprises hit a cost inflection point with frontier proprietary models and increasingly fear single-vendor risk; Gartner projects open models will underpin over 50% of business AI use cases within two years, up from under 10% today.
