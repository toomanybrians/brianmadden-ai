# ChinAI #370: Why is there No Star AI Company from Guangdong?

## Insights

- Several of China's top AI figures (Kimi K3's Jianlin Su and Guangyu Chen, Moonshot's Yang Zhilin, DeepSeek's Liang Wenfeng) trace back to Guangdong province, yet no frontier AI company is headquartered there — talent origin and company location have decoupled.
- China's frontier AI industry has clustered almost entirely in Beijing and Shanghai (and increasingly Hangzhou, via DeepSeek), despite Shenzhen's status as the country's broader tech hub.
- Shenzhen reportedly tried to lure two of the "Six Little Tigers" LLM startups with incentives like free office space, but the deal collapsed — illustrating how Chinese cities actively compete to host AI champions as an economic-development strategy.
- Tencent is Shenzhen's main AI contender (its WorkBuddy desktop agent reportedly outranks ByteDance's TraeIDE) but is still seen as falling short of frontier-lab status.
- The piece frames the gap as partly cultural: Beijing's "political mindset" (control/power) vs. Guangdong's "business mindset" (freedom/growth), suggesting regional culture shapes which ecosystems produce AI leaders even when talent originates elsewhere.
- Inter-city rivalry for hosting AI/tech champions is described as a broader pattern driving China's industrialization, per economist Zhang Jun, though it carries risks of resource misallocation and overcapacity.

## Quote

> If Beijing embodies a 'political mindset,' then Guangdong embodies a 'business mindset'... one focuses on power, the other on growth.
