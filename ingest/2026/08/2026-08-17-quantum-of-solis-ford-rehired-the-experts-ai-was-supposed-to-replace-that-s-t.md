# Ford Rehired the Experts AI Was Supposed to Replace, That’s the Story

## Insights

- Ford brought back, hired, or promoted roughly 350 experienced technical specialists ("gray beards") after concluding AI and automated quality systems alone weren't delivering results — they now mentor younger staff, lead design reviews, and improve Ford's defect-detection AI.
- Ford COO Kumar Galhotra and VP Charles Poon both attribute the shift to a flawed assumption: that ingesting existing design requirements into AI would automatically produce high-quality output. The real gap was training data quality — AI needed to be trained by the most experienced people, not just given existing documentation.
- Institutional knowledge (judgment, edge cases, undocumented exceptions, cross-departmental handoffs) lives in experienced workers' heads, not in process documents or data; when that knowledge isn't captured, AI "automates around the absence" rather than replacing it.
- Klarna is cited as a parallel case: after its AI assistant reportedly did the work of 700 agents, Klarna reinvested in human customer service after its CEO admitted cost had become "a too predominant evaluation factor," leading to lower quality.
- Following the reinstatement of veteran engineers, Ford ranked highest among mass-market brands in the 2026 J.D. Power U.S. Initial Quality Study (152 problems per 100 vehicles) — its first No. 1 ranking among mainstream brands since 2010.
- Piece argues for shifting from "org charts" (who reports to whom) to "work charts" (how value actually flows across functions, systems, data, and human/AI handoffs) to identify where AI should and shouldn't act autonomously; recommends measuring AI by value creation rather than headcount reduction.

## Quote

> "Artificial intelligence is a fantastic tool, but it's only as good as the information you use to train it." — Charles Poon, Ford VP of vehicle hardware engineering
