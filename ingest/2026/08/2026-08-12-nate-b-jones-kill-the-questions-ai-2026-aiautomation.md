# Kill the questions ... #AI #2026 #aiautomation

## Insights

- Support teams have historically optimized for response speed, but AI has made fast replies cheap/commoditized, eliminating speed as a differentiator.
- Most of the actual labor in a support interaction is invisible pre-reply work: finding emails, checking payments, digging through Slack, sending invites, writing apologies — work that only exists because a customer had to ask.
- The framing shift proposed: instead of asking "how do we answer faster," ask "how do we make sure they never had to ask at all" — targeting the root cause, not the response.
- Applying AI to the entire process (not just the reply step) is framed as producing a smaller inbox/fewer tickets overall, rather than just a faster-churning one.
- The author distinguishes between AI applied narrowly (speeding up replies) versus AI applied structurally (redesigning the process to prevent the need for tickets), positioning the latter as the more valuable use.

## Quote

> When you point AI at the whole process instead of just the reply, the win is not a faster inbox. It is a smaller one.
