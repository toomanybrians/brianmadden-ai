# 🔮 Why one AI is better than four #598

## Insights

- Anthropic's multi-agent experiment found that when correct answers depend on private information held by only a few agents, group discussion overrides that signal in favor of the shared-but-wrong consensus: most model families got it right only 17-36% of the time, versus near-100% for a single agent given all the evidence.
- This replicates a "hidden-profile problem" seen earlier in blending multiple models' answers, where combining outputs preserved only about a quarter of the good ideas any single model produced alone — suggesting aggregation/consensus mechanisms actively destroy valuable minority information.
- One model (Mythos 5) notably outperformed others at ~85% accuracy on the task, with the cause unexplained.
- Two structural causes proposed: LLMs have low variance/diversity (e.g., 30 agents given the same coding task will independently converge on identical git branch names), and multi-agent systems lack human-style institutions — reputation, recourse, protection for dissenters — that let minority-information-holders be heard and trusted.
- One proposed fix (from Thinking Machines) is deliberately cultivating an ecosystem of differently-"raised" AI systems with different values/purposes to preserve useful weirdness/diversity, though no fix is yet proven.
- Separately, on AI economics: token-price elasticity is underwhelming (a 10% price cut raises usage only 12-18%) possibly because "cost per token" isn't the right unit — what matters is cost per completed useful unit of work, which knowledge work has historically resisted pricing (hence salaries/retainers instead of piecework).
- Evidence of uneven adoption: since October 2023, the top 1% of firms raised AI spend per employee by $6,542, while the median firm's per-employee AI spend rose only $9.63 — likely concentrated in software, where output (commits, PRs, releases) is most measurable.

## Quote

> The cost per token is irrelevant. What matters is the cost of completing a useful unit of work.
