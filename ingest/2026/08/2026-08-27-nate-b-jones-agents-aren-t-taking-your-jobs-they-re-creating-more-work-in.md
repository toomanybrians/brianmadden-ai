# Agents Aren't Taking Your Jobs. They're Creating More Work Instead.

## Insights

- Central claim: AI agents generate more human work than they eliminate (allocation, review, and recovery tasks), and this offsetting labor is largely unmeasured.
- Agent management and outcomes differ by scale — solo operator, small business, and enterprise — because each absorbs the human overhead differently.
- Legal industry agentic AI adoption is up 108x, while many other small businesses see stalled or minimal uptake.
- A specific case: nine seconds of agent execution generated roughly thirty hours of follow-on human work for one founder, illustrating hidden overhead costs.
- Enterprises reportedly see better agent ROI than smaller businesses, attributed to available capital to staff the surrounding human work rather than assuming automation is self-sufficient.
- Managers are increasingly shifting into a role of "agent allocators" — coordinating and directing agent work rather than doing tasks themselves.

## Quote

> Agents are real leverage, and the companies seeing returns are the ones staffing the human work around them instead of assuming it disappeared.
