# The Link Between Your Workplace Situation and Your Mental Health

## Insights

- Miessler argues that emotional swings at work — from euphoria to dread — track almost entirely with perceived appreciation from bosses, management, and peers, not with the actual nature of the work.
- He frames this as evolutionary: humans place primary value on their work being recognized, and appreciation from key stakeholders (boss, team, organization) functions as the "currency" for that validation.
- He proposes a diagnostic habit: when feeling low, first ask "what is my current state of respect/appreciation at work?" before assuming the cause is something else.
- He notes this dynamic can flip fast — a management change or new hire can end a long stretch of feeling valued almost overnight — and the response should be to assess whether the situation is fixable or requires exit.
- Central thesis: traditional corporate jobs are becoming a declining source of this fulfillment, and that decline will accelerate — recognition/appreciation will increasingly need to be self-generated rather than sought from employers.
- He links this to his "Human 3.0" concept: building a career/life structured around self-directed work people choose because it's worth doing, rather than depending on corporate structures for appreciation.

## Quote

> I think the amount of this type of fulfillment that we can get from traditional corporate jobs is declining.
