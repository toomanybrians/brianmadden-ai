# Gemini is Cooked but GCP is Cooking

## Insights

- Google restructured DeepMind leadership, with Demis Hassabis stepping back from day-to-day operations and a wave of top researchers (Jeff Dean, Sanjay Ghemawat, Quoc Le, Oriol Vinyals) departing to found a new independent lab, following a pattern of prior high-profile exits (Noam Shazeer, John Jumper).
- SemiAnalysis argues the real winner of this shakeup is Google Cloud, not DeepMind or rival labs — internal compute allocation battles between Gemini and GCP appear to have been resolved in GCP's favor under CEO Thomas Kurian.
- The piece frames Google's core strategic problem as a lack of organizational "conviction": GCP has been selling large volumes of TPU capacity on long-term contracts to Gemini's direct competitors (notably over 20% of TPU shipments through 2027 going to Anthropic), prioritizing cloud revenue over frontier-model advantage.
- Gemini's model performance and API growth are both described as decelerating sharply (token growth rate dropping from 60% to 38% quarter-over-quarter), while Gemini's enterprise platform (formerly Vertex) has grown mainly by hosting third-party models like Claude.
- The analysis situates this as a broader case study in talent retention and culture: Google is characterized as bureaucratic and risk-averse, historically slow to ship AI products despite early innovation (e.g., an internal chatbot built a year before ChatGPT but never released).
- The author is skeptical that new DeepMind leadership or acqui-hiring another "neolab" will restore frontier competitiveness, arguing the underlying cultural/organizational issues — not personnel — are the root cause.
