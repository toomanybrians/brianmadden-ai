# 🦆 The $399 Robot You Can Retrain

## Insights

- Pollen Robotics (Hugging Face's robotics arm) launched Microduck, a $399, 25cm/800g open-source bipedal robot with 15 motors, camera, LiDAR, and two inertial sensors, running its control policy 50 times/second onboard.
- The product ships with seven pre-trained reinforcement-learning behaviors (walking, kicking, roller-skating, self-righting after falls) but the core value proposition is retraining: the full stack (SDK, MuJoCo simulation, RL training code) is Apache 2.0 open source, trainable on a laptop or via Hugging Face Jobs.
- Microduck's pre-order launch coincided with reports that Nvidia agreed to acquire Hugging Face for $12.9 billion, framing the robot as a potential low-cost on-ramp to embodied AI within Nvidia's strategy.
- The piece frames this as lowering the price floor for hands-on robot learning from thousands of dollars to under $400, potentially turning robot training into an accessible hobbyist skill.
- Broader newsletter context notes Google's Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash made AI video generation priced per-second/resolution (a "utility" pricing model), and separately reports that a draft White House executive order to create a self-regulatory AI oversight body has stalled, leaving state legislatures (e.g., Illinois's mandatory third-party AI safety audits) and labs' internal policies as the practical rulemakers.
- Over 100 AI companies (OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft, Oracle, AWS, Google) signed a letter warning that AI-enabled cyberattacks will become significantly more widespread and sophisticated within months, urging urgent investment in cyber defense for critical infrastructure.
