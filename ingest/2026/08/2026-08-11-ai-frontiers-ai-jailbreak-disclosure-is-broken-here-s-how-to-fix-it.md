# AI Jailbreak Disclosure Is Broken. Here’s How to Fix It

## Insights

- AI jailbreak disclosure lacks the standardized infrastructure that cybersecurity vulnerability reporting has developed over decades — most frontier labs offer no formal channel at all, and where programs exist, restrictive NDAs block disclosure to governments, other labs, or the public indefinitely.
- Developers self-grade jailbreak severity against undisclosed, inconsistent rubrics, letting labs downplay findings with no external check — even well-connected safety orgs (e.g., FAR.AI) report labs unilaterally setting fix timelines and offering no guarantee of action.
- The disparity in disclosure programs (e.g., Anthropic's locked-down CBRN process vs. its more open cyber-jailbreak channel) appears driven by external/political pressure rather than actual risk severity — suggesting current incentive structures don't map to real-world harm.
- Real-world stakes are cited as already material: alleged jailbreak use by Boko Haram for weapons troubleshooting, Russian cybercriminals exploiting jailbreaks, and a US government shutdown of Claude access over an alleged cyber jailbreak.
- Universal jailbreaks — ones that bypass safeguards across many harmful query types — have been found in most tested frontier models, including GPT-5.6, using only simple public techniques, indicating safeguards remain broadly brittle at the frontier.
- Proposed fix borrows from cybersecurity norms: near-term public reporting mechanisms with transparent severity rubrics, plus a medium-term independent clearinghouse to intake, evaluate, and coordinate disclosure of jailbreaks across affected vendors.

## Quote

> Labs listen, but contest severity, set safeguard update timelines unilaterally, and provide no guarantee that they will act on a given finding.
