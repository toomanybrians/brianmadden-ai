# Seven reasons I wouldn’t count Google out

## Insights

- Marcus pushes back against a consensus narrative (talent exodus + delayed model = Google is finished), arguing structural advantages — proprietary data at unmatched scale, in-house TPUs reducing Nvidia dependency, and distribution via Android/Search/Mail/YouTube — outweigh leadership churn.
- His financial framing is notable: Google's $132B profit and cash reserves give it staying power that OpenAI and Anthropic lack if the LLM market ends in a "multi-way tie with modest profits" — a scenario where sheer runway matters more than model quality.
- He frames Hassabis's move to Chairman/Chief Science Officer as continuity rather than departure, given his decade-long working relationship with successor Koray Kavukcuoglu — a more charitable read than the "leadership vacuum" framing elsewhere.
- Implicit in his argument: competitors' weaknesses (OpenAI's reputational decline, Anthropic's regulatory friction, Microsoft/Amazon's thinner AI talent pools) matter as much as Google's own position — relative strength, not absolute strength.
- Worth flagging as a genuine contrarian take with real reasoning behind it, not just reflexive AI-skepticism — this is Marcus arguing against the herd in a direction that's actually pro-incumbent, which cuts against his usual doom framing.

## Quote

> Google may simply outlast the other two.
