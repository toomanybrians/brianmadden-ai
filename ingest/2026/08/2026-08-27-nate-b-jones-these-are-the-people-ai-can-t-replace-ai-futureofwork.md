# These are the people AI can't replace #AI #futureofwork

## Insights

- Argues that when AI removes the difficulty of building software, the hard problem doesn't vanish — it shifts to judgment: deciding what to build, for whom, and whether it should exist at all.
- Identifies the people this favors as those with gut-level customer understanding, systems thinking, comfort with ambiguity, and the ability to clearly articulate something that doesn't yet exist.
- Claims AI doesn't replace this type of person but instead extends their capacity — a product thinker who previously relied on five engineers now has effectively unlimited engineering capacity.
- Frames the prior bottleneck as execution capacity (how much could get built), not idea quality, and argues that ceiling has now been removed.
- Reframes the central professional question going forward as "should we build it" rather than "can we build it."
- References a companion newsletter piece on what product management becomes when software production is cheap/governance-driven.

## Quote

> The only question left is the good one: not can we build it, but should we.
