# Wisconsin versus AI Goliath Part II: Port Washington

## Insights

- A Vantage Data Centers facility (three square miles) is under construction in Port Washington, WI, on Lake Michigan, with OpenAI and Oracle slated to occupy it once built; residents say no one will clarify what will actually be stored or run there.
- No environmental impact report was conducted; FOIA records obtained by residents show Vantage told the mayor the project wouldn't proceed if an impact study were required, and the mayor intervened to have the requirement dropped, later approved by the governor's office.
- Residents describe intensive surveillance infrastructure around the site (seven license-plate-reading "Flock" cameras at the subdivision's single exit) and worry about the data center's potential military/security use given the scale of investment.
- Water usage is a major flashpoint: Vantage has reportedly used local well water without proper permitting or capping procedures, and a nearby power plant's cooling process discharges heated water into Lake Michigan, raising concerns about the Great Lakes' finite freshwater supply (20%+ of Earth's accessible fresh water, drinking source for 30M+ people).
- Local opposition (Great Lakes Neighbors United) has faced arrests at council meetings, intimidation by private security, anonymous threatening letters, and dismissal as "outside agitators" by the mayor, despite being lifelong or decades-long residents.
- Property values near the site have reportedly dropped $150K–$300K, with residents unable to sell (buyers rescind offers) or even shop for replacement homes due to contingency issues tied to proximity to the data center.
- Broader pattern noted: one resident had lived near three data centers in San Antonio before moving to Wisconsin for its environmental character, only to face the same dynamic again — suggesting a repeatable community-impact pattern as data center buildout accelerates.
