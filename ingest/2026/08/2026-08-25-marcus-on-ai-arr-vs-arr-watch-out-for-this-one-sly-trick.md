# ARR vs ARR. Watch out for this one sly trick.

## Insights

- Marcus flags a terminology conflation: "ARR" ambiguously refers to either Annual Recurring Revenue (durable, subscription-like) or Annualized Run Rate (a single strong period multiplied by 12, with no guarantee of repeating), and AI companies' boosters rarely specify which.
- He argues Anthropic's touted ARR figures are likely the latter (run-rate, not recurring revenue), and that this distinction is being obscured deliberately or carelessly by promoters of the number.
- His specific claim: Anthropic's recent quarterly revenue may not recur at the same scale, because enterprise customers (he cites AT&T) are shifting toward cheaper open-source models as "tokenmaxxing" (heavy, high-cost token usage) winds down — a cost-driven substitution away from frontier proprietary models.
- He draws a historical analogy to Netscape (1995): explosive, doubling-quarterly revenue and a massive IPO valuation despite no profit, followed by collapse once Microsoft offered a free competing product — framed as a cautionary parallel for AI revenue narratives.
- Underlying point: rapid top-line growth metrics in AI companies can mask non-recurring or unsustainable revenue composition, and audiences should demand clarity on which "ARR" is meant before treating growth claims as evidence of durable business strength.

## Quote

> Annualized Run Rate, alas, means no such thing [recurrence].
