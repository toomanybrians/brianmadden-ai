# 🌻 abdul el-sayed on AI

## Insights

- El-Sayed frames data center opposition as a referendum on AI's value proposition: communities see costs (water, power, noise) but no clear local upside, while executives openly acknowledge the technology may eliminate jobs.
- He notes a generational irony — the heaviest AI/chat users are young people who report resenting the technology even as they feel compelled to adopt it competitively ("keeping up with the Joneses").
- Building-trades labor leaders privately express skepticism about data center construction jobs despite strong pay, viewing them as short-term (a "three-month job") with long-tail costs (electricity bills, water contamination) borne by residents.
- He argues data center siting decisions let capital pit localities against each other for "least-common-denominator" concessions, and calls for federal-level standards rather than local/state moratoria alone.
- He invokes the industry's own AGI-race narrative plus Moore's Law to argue current infrastructure buildout is inherently duplicative — predicting a future of "dead," stranded data center assets once compute efficiency and consolidation catch up.
- He characterizes current AI-driven valuations and the political economy behind the buildout (tech billionaires seeking power, VCs seeking returns, corporations seeking automation) as speculative ("funny money") rather than reflecting real economic value.

## Quote

> If you ask me — data centers in the dirtiest form versus no data centers — I say no data centers.
