# 📈 Making sense of the AI capex logjam

## Insights

- The seven largest AI-infrastructure builders are guiding to $863B in 2026 capex, up 88% year-over-year, with roughly $550B estimated as AI-specific spending.
- There's a growing gap between capex outlays and their earnings impact: assets "not yet in service" across four major hyperscalers total $315B, up from $281B just one quarter earlier — a backlog of future depreciation not yet hitting income statements.
- Meta's capex-to-live-service lag has stretched to ~1.7 years, a full year longer than FY2024, meaning only about a third of dollars spent today become operational within the year.
- Other major spenders show the same lengthening lag pattern, though less pronounced than Meta's.
- This dynamic suggests reported earnings and margins may understate the true near-term cost burden of the AI buildout, since much of the spend is capitalized rather than expensed — a structural accounting lag that could delay when capex "reality" shows up in profitability, complicating comparisons of AI ROI across companies and time periods.

## Quote

> A dollar of capex spent by Meta now waits some 1.7 years before going live, a year more than in FY2024.
