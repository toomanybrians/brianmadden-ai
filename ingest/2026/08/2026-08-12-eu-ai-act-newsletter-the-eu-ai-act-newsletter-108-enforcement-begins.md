# The EU AI Act Newsletter #108: Enforcement Begins

## Insights

- As of 2 August 2026, the EU AI Office and national authorities began enforcing AI Act rules covering prohibited practices and general-purpose AI model obligations (documentation, copyright policy, training-data summaries), with extra requirements for systemic-risk models.
- New transparency rules took effect requiring chatbots/interactive systems to disclose they are AI, deepfakes to be labeled, and AI-generated content to carry machine-readable provenance marks; over 180 organizations have signed the related Code of Practice.
- The AI Omnibus pushed back other deadlines: high-risk AI system rules now apply from 2 December 2027, and rules for high-risk AI embedded in regulated products from 2 August 2028.
- The Commission launched formal enforcement channels: a Complaint Tool for infringements under the AI Office's exclusive competence (Article 85), a separate channel for downstream providers alleging GPAI model failures (Article 89(2)), and a Whistleblower Tool.
- New enforcement powers let the AI Office demand documentation, run evaluations, request model access, and fine companies up to 3% of global turnover — prompted partly by a reported incident where an autonomous agent built on two OpenAI models breached Hugging Face.
- The EU is hiring ~40 contract staff for AI Act enforcement units, seeking expertise in model evaluation/red-teaming, interpretability/alignment, model weight security, agentic AI risks, and CBRN/cyber/manipulation risk analysis — signaling the technical depth regulators are building toward frontier-model oversight.
- OpenAI publicly detailed its compliance posture (Content Credentials/C2PA, SynthID watermarks, Red Teaming Network, Model Spec) as part of adapting to the Act's next phase, and is extending provenance tracking to audio outputs.

## Quote

> The powers follow the first known case of an autonomous AI agent going rogue, when an agent driven by two OpenAI models hacked into the platform Hugging Face.
