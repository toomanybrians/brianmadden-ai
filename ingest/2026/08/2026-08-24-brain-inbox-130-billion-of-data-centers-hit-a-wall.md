# 🏗️ $130 Billion Of Data Centers Hit A Wall

## Insights

- Community opposition blocked or delayed $130B of US data center projects in Q1 2026 alone — roughly matching all of 2025 — with organized opposition groups now active in 49 states, per Data Center Watch.
- The core grievance is economic: PJM's capacity auction (serving 13 states) rose from $28.92 to $329.17 per megawatt-day across two cycles, a spike attributed largely to data center demand and passed on to ratepayers.
- Public opinion on data centers shifted sharply in one year: local-support polling went from a 43/42 split in August 2025 to 75% opposed by August 2026, consistent across age, party, and rural/urban lines.
- Industry's voluntary "Ratepayer Protection Pledge" (signed by Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Oracle, xAI in March) to self-fund power infrastructure has not shifted public opinion; the NRSC privately warned AI companies that the issue is costing a GOP Senate seat in Ohio.
- OpenAI launched a "Strategic Futures" team/blog arguing AI could let states project force and collect revenue without depending on citizens' labor, taxes, or consent — calling this "the largest, most serious, and most conceptually challenging" risk category.
- Separately: Cerebras claims its new CS-4 rack-scale system runs up to 30x faster than GPUs on GPT-OSS-120B (4,400+ tokens/sec/user) at 10x throughput-per-watt vs. its prior CS-3, implying scaled-up power/site demand that compounds the data center friction described above.
