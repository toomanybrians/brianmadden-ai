# An International AI Slowdown Is Ready Whenever Politicians Are

## Insights

- Over 1,000 employees at major AI labs, plus OpenAI and Anthropic leadership, have now formally endorsed the idea of a deliberately paced (slowed) international frontier AI development effort — a notable shift from pure acceleration rhetoric.
- The main objection to a slowdown isn't political will but verification: countries won't accept monitoring that leaks trade secrets, and current privacy-preserving verification tech can't reliably catch cheating (e.g., faked compute workloads, physical key extraction).
- The authors propose "Whole-Lab Inspection" (WLI) as a low-tech alternative: mutual US-China inspectors get physical access to labs plus read-only access to logs, communications, code, and compute telemetry — similar to nuclear or banking audits — rather than waiting for advanced cryptographic verification tools.
- WLI could enforce specific restrictions rather than a blanket freeze: banning autonomous AI R&D, capping how long AI agents work unsupervised, limiting compute for internal inference, and restricting post-training capability gains in domains like coding or medical diagnosis.
- The piece frames the core barrier to an international slowdown as political will, not technical feasibility — and argues that will could emerge quickly if AI-driven instability (job loss, WMD proliferation, loss-of-control incidents) intensifies.
- Implicit assumption: labs already have the internal surveillance infrastructure (keystroke/screen logging, compute telemetry) needed for this kind of external audit, meaning the technical lift for WLI is smaller than commonly assumed.

## Quote

> An international AI slowdown is already technically possible; it's just a question of whether the US and China have the political will.
