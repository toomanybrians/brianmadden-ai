# Dylan Patel – Anthropic & OpenAI will have most of the world’s compute by 2028

## Insights

- Anthropic and OpenAI's share of incremental global compute is projected to jump from ~30% this year to 40-50% next year, and toward controlling most of the world's usable flops by end of 2028 — driven by both leasing (e.g., SpaceX-built capacity) and self-built infrastructure (custom chips, TPUs).
- Lab economics have flipped from negative gross margins a year ago to strongly positive: base compute costs run ~$10-15M/megawatt, while Anthropic's revenue per megawatt has reportedly reached ~$50M, funding reinvestment into training rather than distributed profit.
- A non-consensus claim: labs are expected to shift an increasing share of compute away from external inference and toward internal R&D/training, because the internal value of frontier capability compounding exceeds near-term revenue from selling tokens.
- Financing the projected $3-4 trillion in AI CapEx by 2028 will require heavy debt issuance across hyperscalers and their supply chains, raising concern about a broader "crowding out" effect — higher market interest rates squeezing sovereign borrowers, consumer credit, and debt-heavy industries.
- The biggest brake on runaway AI progress/recursive self-improvement is framed as political and regulatory (delayed model releases, restrictions on internal deployment, public backlash) rather than compute or technical capability limits.
- China's AI compute remains under 10% of global incremental deployment due to export controls, with an expected inflection around 2028 via domestic fabs (SMIC/CXMT), though the resulting compute is described as materially lower quality than US/allied chips.

## Quote

> "The labs are going to allocate less and less compute to inference over time, which I think is very non-consensus." — Dylan Patel
