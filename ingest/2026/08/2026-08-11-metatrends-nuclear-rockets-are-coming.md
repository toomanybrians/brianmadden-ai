# Nuclear Rockets Are Coming

## Insights

- NASA under Administrator Jared Isaacman is reviving nuclear propulsion and power programs (fission reactors on the Moon, nuclear thermal engines for Mars) after decades of chemical-only propulsion.
- The strategic logic mirrors a broader innovation pattern: rather than building an all-new system, NASA is repurposing already-funded, "sunk cost" hardware (Gateway's Power and Propulsion Element, Idaho National Lab reactor components) into a working prototype — a lesson in shipping incremental, "good enough" solutions rather than waiting for the ideal one.
- Isaacman explicitly frames this as a Rickover-style strategy: solve one hard technical problem per iteration (reactor and vehicle separately) rather than attempting a breakthrough all at once — an approach relevant to how organizations sequence risky technology bets.
- The piece's executive takeaway — "audit what your organization has already paid for and abandoned" — treats the $2.5B repurposed-hardware move as a general lesson in surfacing sunk R&D investment inside large institutions.
- Frames nuclear engineering/materials science as an emerging high-leverage skill pair, and flags space-nuclear supply chains (HALEU fuel, compact power conversion) as a nascent commercial/procurement opportunity — an example of how national-lab-scale technology bets can cascade into new industries and talent demand.

> "The ultimate goal is to bring astronauts to Mars and back with the fewest miracles required." — Jared Isaacman, NASA Administrator
