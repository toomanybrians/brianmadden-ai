# SpaceX 10GW in 2027 – Why It’s Real, Will Drive $300B ARR for SpaceX, and Why Microsoft Will Be the Largest Offtaker

## Insights

- Microsoft is aggressively re-accelerating datacenter procurement (10GW+ signed in 2026 alone, ~$300B in binding commitments) after a 2024 pause, driven by a shift in incentives following the reworked OpenAI deal that dropped Microsoft's 20% revenue share.
- The piece frames serving frontier-model inference (API tokens) as an extremely high-margin business — potentially over $100B/GW/year in revenue vs. ~$12B/GW/year in compute cost — reshaping how hyperscalers value compute allocation between training, IaaS, and consumer/API products (Copilot, Foundry).
- Microsoft's compute has been largely tied up serving OpenAI's IaaS deal, leaving it unable to fully exploit its own access to OpenAI models for higher-margin uses like Copilot and its Foundry API business — a strategic bottleneck the new SpaceX capacity could resolve.
- SpaceX is positioned as a new, fast-turnaround compute supplier (3-5 month lead times) charging premium pricing, with Microsoft framed as SpaceX's largest likely offtaker — suggesting compute supply chains are diversifying beyond traditional hyperscaler self-build.
- The analysis implies Azure's revenue growth could more than double (from ~42% to 100%+) if Microsoft successfully redirects compute toward higher-margin inference/enterprise products rather than pure IaaS commitments to OpenAI — a signal of how compute allocation strategy directly drives enterprise AI product economics.
