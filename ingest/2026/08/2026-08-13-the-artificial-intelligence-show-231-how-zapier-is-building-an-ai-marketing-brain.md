# #231:  How Zapier Is Building an AI Marketing Brain

## Insights

- Zapier's marketing leadership identified a shift from AI chat tool usage to "AI builder" tool usage (Cursor, Codex, Claude Code, Zapier's MCP) as a new competitive threshold — marketers who could build their own apps, dashboards, and workflows were seen as a fundamentally different (and threatening) category of talent, distinct from those just using chatbots.
- The trigger for organizational change was a live demo: bringing in an outside "cursor expert" with a self-built digital twin to present to the team produced visible overwhelm/anxiety, which leadership used deliberately as a catalyst ("aha moment") rather than framing it as fear-based leadership.
- The rollout followed a staged pattern: mandate tool adoption (3-week deadline on Cursor), assign internal peer coaches who were already ahead, then shift focus from tool-learning to workflow/behavior change once baseline skill was reached.
- Zapier is building a department-level "marketing brain" — a shared, continuously updated context system pulling from Slack (public channels only), Granola notes, Zoom transcripts, Google Docs, and Coda — that feeds context to individual AI tools/agents team members use.
- A named organizational lever for feeding the brain is "working in public": Zapier tracks % of Slack messages sent in public channels vs. private DMs company-wide (posted execs' scores monthly); the interviewee personally shifted from ~50% private to 96% public over three months to make more of his communication visible/usable to the AI system.
- Framed challenges as primarily organizational/behavioral, not technical — e.g., unclear ownership of adoption accountability, resistance to public communication norms, and the need to tolerate rougher, less "buttoned up" work output while people learn new systems.
- Term introduced: "lossless marketing" / minimizing "generational loss" of information as work passes through people and tools — the stated design goal behind the marketing brain.
