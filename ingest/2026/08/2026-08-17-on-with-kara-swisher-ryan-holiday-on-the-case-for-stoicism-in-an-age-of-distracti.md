# Ryan Holiday on the Case for Stoicism in an Age of Distraction

## Insights

- Holiday argues Silicon Valley has "perverted" Stoicism into a hyper-masculine, shredded-physique caricature that strips out its actual grounding in reason, virtue, and obligation to others.
- On the Pope's writings on AI: Holiday praises it for asking a moral question ("is this right or wrong, what are our obligations to each other") rather than the usual framing of AI as either economically inevitable or a pure cost/benefit calculation.
- He contrasts that moral framing with what he calls today's "amoral" decision logic in tech/business — "can this get funding" or "will this go viral" — as the actual operative test for action, not right/wrong.
- Holiday criticizes leadership models (business and political) built around obligation to shareholder value, scale, or self-interest, calling this "fundamentally distortive" compared to a Stoic emperor's framing that good fortune equals good character and good intentions.
- He describes social media/personal-branding incentives as amplifying narcissistic and sociopathic tendencies, rewarding people who lack the shame or restraint that normally checks bad behavior.
- Holiday reflects on the tension of building a platform/audience under commercial and social-media incentives while trying not to "carry water" for bad actors or lend intellectual cover to harmful ideas.

## Quote

> Silicon Valley companies... have perverted [Stoicism] in a way that I find distressing.
