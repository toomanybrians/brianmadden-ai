# The UK's former head AI safety scientist on how to solve alignment before superintelligence arrives | Geoffrey Irving

## Insights

- Geoffrey Irving (ex-OpenAI, ex-DeepMind, former chief scientist at the UK AI Security Institute) expects full-blown superintelligence in roughly two to three years.
- Leading AI companies share a common alignment approach: train models for good character, use more capable AIs to supervise less capable ones, and monitor for deception or scheming — but Irving stresses no one has a rigorous argument that this combination will actually work.
- He anticipates a "phase shift" once models surpass human intelligence: below that threshold humans can judge and correct model errors, but above it, models themselves increasingly generate the feedback that trains their successors, removing human oversight as a check.
- Irving argues safety researchers may have more impact working inside governments than inside AI companies, and that governments should consider deliberately slowing frontier AI development until trustworthy alignment methods are established.
- His new nonprofit, Resolution, is pursuing a "portfolio of neglected research bets" on alignment rather than betting on a single technique.
- Discussion also touches on a perceived asymmetry between the pace of capabilities progress and the pace of alignment progress, and on post-superintelligence scenarios like accelerated science, nanotech, and uploaded minds.
