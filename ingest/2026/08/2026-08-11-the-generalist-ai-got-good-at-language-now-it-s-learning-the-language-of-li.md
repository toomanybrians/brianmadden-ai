# AI Got Good at Language. Now It’s Learning the Language of Life. (Eric Nguyen, Co-Founder and CEO of Radical Numerics)

## Insights

- Radical Numerics is extending the "language model" paradigm from text to biology, treating DNA/RNA/protein sequences as a language to model directly from raw biological data rather than relying on labeled datasets or literature.
- The founder argues that learning from primary biological data differs meaningfully from learning from scientific papers about that data — papers are a lossy, human-filtered summary, not the underlying signal.
- The company claims its model (Omnii) reproduced key findings from two years of Alzheimer's wet-lab research in days, suggesting AI could dramatically compress discovery timelines in life sciences — a knowledge-work productivity claim analogous to LLM gains in text-based fields.
- A core bottleneck flagged is verification: unlike text generation, validating AI-generated biological designs requires slow, expensive wet-lab experiments, limiting how fast these models can be trusted or iterated on.
- The same generative capability that accelerates drug discovery and synthetic biology also lowers the barrier to designing dangerous pathogens, prompting the company to pair capability work with biodefense/detection efforts as a dual-use safeguard.
- Frames a broader industry pattern: as general-purpose LLMs improve, specialized domain labs (biology, here) benefit indirectly, suggesting compounding effects across AI subfields rather than isolated progress.

## Quote

> "Giving AI the power to generate and create life carries a certain level of responsibility." — Eric Nguyen
