# 200GW Hiding in Grid, Sodium Batteries 10x Cheaper, Wave-Powered Datacenters w/ Ramez Naam | EP #280

## Insights

- Naam argues the binding constraint on AI scaling isn't power generation or chip cost but grid interconnection speed — in Texas (the fastest US grid), a new data-center power request today won't be fulfilled until 2031-2032, and the interconnection queue has stretched from ~15 months to 45 months over 20 years, driven more by regulatory/utility incentive structures than physical scarcity.
- Energy cost is framed as trivial relative to AI economics: on a $50B gigawatt data center, ~$35B goes to chips, and hyperscalers will pay double for power if it removes the bottleneck — the real lever is speed-to-power, not price.
- A specific quantified "hidden" fix: making the grid flexible (interruptible loads that draw down ~1-4% of hours/year) could unlock ~100-200GW of existing grid capacity — equivalent to roughly $5-10 trillion of AI data center capex — without building new generation, via software orchestration (e.g., Emerald AI) or on-site battery time-shifting (e.g., Naam's portfolio company using 4-hour batteries to exploit Dallas-Fort Worth's night/day demand gap).
- Solar+battery baseload plants are already operating (UAE, Chile) at ~$6/watt capex, competitive with Chinese nuclear ($4/watt) and far below US nuclear ($15/watt); sodium-ion batteries could cut battery costs another 10x, though seasonal/winter storage (especially at higher latitudes) remains economically unsolved.
- Nuclear fission is framed as cheap only through repetition/standardization (France, China's stamped-out AP1000 variants); the US nuclear renaissance depends on fleet-financing multiple reactors at once rather than one-offs, with SMRs realistically not commercial before the early 2030s despite hyperscaler enthusiasm.
- CUDA's moat is described as "broken this year and next" due to AI-assisted kernel recompilation tools letting code run efficiently on non-Nvidia chips (AMD, Cerebras) — a direct challenge to Nvidia's ~$5T market cap thesis, though co-guests note interconnect advantages still matter for training and multi-GPU inference.
- Space- and ocean-based data centers (Musk's stated ambition) are treated as directionally plausible but "prohibitive for 15-20 years" without an extreme launch-cadence exponential (10GW/year would require 5-6 Starship launches per day).

## Quote

> If you put in a request for hundreds of megawatts of power to build a data center today, good luck getting that power before 2031, 2032.
