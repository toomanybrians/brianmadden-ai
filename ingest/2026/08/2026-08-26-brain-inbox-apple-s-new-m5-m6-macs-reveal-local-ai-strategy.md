# ⚙️ Apple's new M5, M6 Macs reveal local AI strategy

## Insights

- OpenAI's first custom inference chip, Jalapeño (built with Broadcom), reportedly delivers 1.5–4x the performance-per-watt of Nvidia's Blackwell across varied workloads, per third-party SemiAnalysis benchmarks — tested on OpenAI's own model plus DeepSeek R1 and Kimi K2.5, not just tuned for its own stack.
- OpenAI says its own AI models accelerated Jalapeño's design/optimization process, helping take it from concept (late 2024) to working silicon in under two years; the same AI-assisted approach is being used for next-gen chips.
- OpenAI's hardware lead suggests early use cases will be latency-sensitive products (e.g., a faster "Codex Ultra Fast Mode"), with volume ramp expected into 2027 — not a wholesale replacement of Nvidia dependency yet.
- Apple's new Mac mini (M6) and Mac Studio (M5 Ultra/Max) are explicitly marketed around local AI performance (up to 4x/4.3x AI gains), reflecting demand from AI enthusiasts running always-on local agents (e.g., OpenClaw) that caused mini shortages earlier in 2026.
- Apple's Mac Studio messaging now includes guidance on clustering M5 Ultra units for scaled local AI inference — a notable shift from its traditional creative-pro-user framing toward AI infrastructure positioning ahead of a major iPhone/Siri 2.0 launch cycle.
- Perplexity, partnering with Nvidia, launched "Portable Computer" — a local-first version of its AI agent running on Nvidia's DGX Spark hardware — aimed at cutting token costs, improving speed, and keeping sensitive data off cloud frontier models; setup complexity remains a barrier to broader adoption.

## Quote

> "Jalapeño beats Blackwell on perf/W across almost all scenarios without being tuned for any specific point in the curve." — SemiAnalysis
