# Tech's Trust Problem Is a Product Problem

## Insights

- 71% of Americans oppose data centers being built in their community — more opposition than to a nuclear plant nearby — signaling the trust crisis is now operational, not just reputational (Texas and Pennsylvania governors both ordered data-center audits under constituent pressure).
- Anthropic's Dario Amodei attributes public distrust of AI not to safety fears but to a failure to deliver promised benefits; OpenAI reportedly paused frontier RL training because safety systems couldn't keep pace with capabilities.
- The piece frames public backlash as economic rather than philosophical: concern centers on affordability, wages, and visible wealth concentration among tech leaders rather than abstract AI alignment risk.
- Proposed remedy is "prove the abundance thesis" via visible, measurable results in health, education, cost of living, and energy — e.g., AI-designed protein binders outperforming human baselines, collapsing token costs (428x cheaper since 2020), and AI-driven grid optimization.
- A competing/complementary proposal ("make trust unnecessary") argues for radical transparency — open evals, real-time environmental data, published safety benchmarks — over reassurance, contrasting closed labs' obfuscated reasoning tokens with inspectable open models.
- Frames the fix as a product/execution problem for tech leaders, investors, entrepreneurs, and individual workers: build things that measurably improve ordinary lives rather than issuing safety pledges or PR messaging.

## Quote

> Trust us is not a strategy. It is a delay tactic. And the public knows the difference.
