# I wrote an AI textbook — how long until AI can do it better?

## Insights

- LLMs have made rapid progress on verifiable tasks (coding, math) but have stagnated on long-form non-fiction writing quality; some of the best-regarded writing models (GPT-4.5, Kimi K2) are already old, unlike in other domains where capability curves are steep.
- Models excel at unit-level content checks (a sentence, an equation, a typo) but fail to string components together across a long document — described as an "irreducible compounding error" problem that RLVR solved for math/code but not prose.
- Different model families show distinct strengths as writing tools: GPT models are strong at catching minor errors/typos at scale; Claude models show more "taste" and better structural/editorial judgment.
- Author estimates AI can currently save only 10-20% of the effort in writing a non-fiction book, and doesn't expect that share to become a majority soon; he used AI for maybe <1% of actual book text, mostly for editing/formatting/syncing (LaTeX↔Markdown, diagrams, editor-comment triage), not core prose.
- Argues that using AI agents for this kind of work doesn't build the human's own intuition/taste — the understanding that will remain valuable — so leaning on AI for writing "takes away from that progression," especially risky for non-experts who can't catch its flaws.
- Frames organizing/compressing knowledge into a coherent explanatory structure as the core insight-generating act of non-fiction writing, and argues today's LLMs currently increase entropy rather than compress — a capability gap he thinks is a leading indicator that models aren't yet ready to autonomously solve open-ended scientific problems, since they can't even compellingly organize known science.
- Predicts a 2-5 year window where the best textbooks/reference works remain primarily human-crafted, with AI's net effect in the interim being to reduce average effort invested in non-fiction writing even as it could theoretically enable broader expression.

## Quote

> Organizing knowledge is a compression. This compression is needed to make insight.
