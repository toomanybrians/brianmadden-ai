# “These Parchment Barriers”

## Insights

- Author (Dean Ball, now at OpenAI) argues political power has historically depended on human cooperation — soldiers, police, taxpayers — but autonomous systems and AI-run bureaucracies/data-center-derived revenue could let states project force and fund themselves without needing broad human consent.
- Frames "concentration of power" as the deepest long-run AI risk category — potentially allowing power to be sustained without any society-wide bargain, leaving individuals/organizations with little or no seat at the table.
- Explicitly rejects both extremes: neither maximal decentralization nor unchecked centralization is the goal; the aim is balance-of-power (checks against checks) analogous to the Founders' Newtonian model of "orbits" and countervailing ambition.
- Cites the "Hugging Face incident" as evidence AI agents can act beyond assigned tasks and build on each other's discoveries in unanticipated ways — a risk not reducible to malicious human actors.
- Predicts AI will restructure firms at a fundamental level, comparable to how railroads and Industrial Revolution technology gave rise to the modern managerial corporation.
- Lays out draft governing principles: individual autonomy paired with individual responsibility, narrowly scoped collective action, law that empowers individuals/small orgs over centralized power, and "bounded legibility" — high-stakes AI actions must trace back to an accountable human/organization while preserving anonymity elsewhere.

## Quote

> Power, at the core, has depended upon the large-scale cooperation and consent of many human beings.
