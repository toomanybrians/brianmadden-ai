# A trans-Atlantic mirror

## Insights

- Dyson contrasts US and European AI investment models: US has hyperscaler-led concentration with intertwined government/corporate power at the top and little agency at the bottom, while Europe's structure is more horizontal, with companies and governments acting as allies asserting local control.
- "Sovereign AI" means different things in each region: in the US it's framed as open-source/power-to-the-people; in Europe it's framed as national/cultural self-determination.
- Teravolt (UK-based, founders with Russia/Greece/Turkey/Armenia roots) is building decentralized AI compute infrastructure by converting existing industrial sites (old factories, crypto-mining facilities, land near power plants) to compress time-to-power from years to months, targeting underserved regions (Southeast Europe, Central Asia, Caucasus, Gulf).
- Teravolt's thesis: compute infrastructure is crystallizing into a four-tier hierarchy (gigafactories → regional hubs → hyperlocal urban nodes → devices), and pricing power/irreplaceability increases as you move down the tiers even though "the watts sit at the top" — the scarce, unbuildable asset is the local know-how to assemble land, grid access, permits, and community trust.
- Antefact (Manchester, solo founder Zack Dadfar) has developed a runtime reliability layer that detects signs an autonomous AI system is "struggling" or likely to fail *during* inference, before output is finalized — enabling intervention (stop, check, retry, reroute) earlier than existing guardrails/evaluators, starting with medical diagnosis use cases.
- Dyson argues the more urgent near-term AI danger is autonomous software acting outside its owning organization (interacting with third parties whose goals may not align), since accountability and security get far murkier once agentic systems leave a single organization's control — a domain with less scale economy than LLM training and less regulatory definition than sectors like healthcare (HIPAA) or finance.
- Closing framing: hierarchy/"stack" metaphors for AI power may be wrong — dependencies are two-way, and "sovereignty" language still resembles a land grab rather than negotiation between parties with real, if unequal, mutual dependency.
