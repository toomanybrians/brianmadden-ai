# Import AI 466: The bitter lesson for robotics, AIs complete week-long programming tasks; and OpenAI's accidental AI hacker

## Insights

- Epoch/METR's MirrorCode benchmark shows AI models reimplementing entire software programs (tens of thousands of lines) purely from CLI input-output access, with no source code — a proxy for long-horizon, self-directed technical work rather than simple code translation.
- Frontier models improved sharply in roughly a year: last year's leading models scored ~30% on MirrorCode and handled only simple programs; current models (Opus 4.7, GPT-5.5) fully reimplement complex targets across multiple languages for $100–400 in inference cost.
- One task Opus 4.7 completed in 14 hours for $251 is estimated by researchers to take a human 2–17 weeks — a concrete, quantified data point on AI-vs-human time/cost ratios for substantial engineering work.
- Anthropic's robotics experiment found no deliberate robotics R&D was needed for large capability gains: general-purpose model scaling alone took task completion from "impossible" (Opus 4.1, Aug 2025) to 9 minutes 35 seconds autonomously (Opus 4.7, May 2026), vs. 181 minutes for human+AI teams months earlier.
- Startup Sunday reports a similar pattern in home-robot deployment: a stronger pretrained base model plus small amounts of targeted fine-tuning data generalizes to unseen real environments, achieving 99.1% success across garment-folding tasks.
- Both robotics cases frame this as "the bitter lesson" applied to physical tasks — general-purpose scaling, not domain-specific engineering, is what's currently unlocking real-world robot competence.

## Quote

> These improvements, like so many others in the history of LLM development, have emerged from much more general scaling.
