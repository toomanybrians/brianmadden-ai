# What will be left for us to work on?

## Insights

- Narayanan frames a binary that has become practically urgent: either AI is a replacing technology (rational response: build wealth fast before skills are irrelevant) or an amplifying technology (rational response: build complementary skills, agency, taste, judgment now).
- He argues the "AI as Normal Technology" framework — treating AI's economic impact as following an invention → innovation → diffusion → adaptation pattern, as with electricity — remains valid unless a discontinuity like recursive self-improvement breaks the pattern.
- He takes recursive self-improvement seriously as a risk but doesn't believe any single lab milestone will suddenly eliminate jobs across the board.
- The "adaptation" phase — structural transformation of work and institutions — is the slowest stage and, per Narayanan, hasn't meaningfully begun even in software engineering, an early-adopting field.
- Speculative extrapolation: if coding agents can reliably build large, secure, personalized codebases, this could push toward "extreme personalization" of software and even raise the question of whether standalone software companies remain necessary versus in-house development.
- He warns that if the AI community abandons professional boundaries and simply cedes work to AI without adaptation, it risks fueling stronger political backlash against AI.

## Quote

> If you think this is a technology which in a few years is going to be able to replace everything we do today, then perhaps the correct response is to build wealth as quickly as possible.
