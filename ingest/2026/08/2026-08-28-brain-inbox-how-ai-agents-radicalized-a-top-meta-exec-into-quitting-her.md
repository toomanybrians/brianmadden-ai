# How AI agents "radicalized" a top Meta exec into quitting her job

## Insights

- Clara Shih (ex-Meta, ex-Salesforce AI CEO) describes watching a full product-development stack — user research, design, PM, multiple engineering roles — collapse into one or two people plus AI agents doing vibe-coding and prototyping; she began pulling down entry-level job postings as a result.
- She cites MIT economist David Autor's framework of three ways AI reshapes a job: (1) direct displacement when most tasks automate (translation, customer service), (2) the "happy case" where routine tasks automate and workers shift to higher-order work with demand growth offsetting losses (her example: software engineers, radiologists), and (3) a case where expert tasks get automated, entry barriers collapse, labor floods in, and jobs survive but degrade into non-living-wage work (her example: taxi driving after GPS + rideshare).
- She admits the optimistic thesis behind her own products (Salesforce Agentforce, Meta Business AI) — that automating rote support work would free staff for higher-value tasks — "has primarily not been true," despite it being the story she and colleagues told themselves while building.
- She estimates roughly one in five corporate roles — those centered on producing an artifact for someone else's review (briefs, decks, order forms) — are especially exposed, because the externally-accountable person (salesperson, recruiter, senior legal staff) may find it faster to use AI directly than to coordinate through intermediary roles.
- Contra conventional wisdom, she argues software engineers are not at risk because the CS mindset (algorithmic thinking, modularity) is exactly what's needed to build and evaluate agent systems — predicting software engineers will instead expand into legal, marketing, and accounting work.
- She disagrees with UBI as a sufficient policy response (citing Maslow: people need autonomy, mastery, and purpose from work, not just income), and points to a local volunteer-coordination nonprofit model (retirees helping elderly neighbors) as a possible template for purpose-generating "work" in a post-disruption economy.
- Her nonprofit (New Work Foundation / Dear CC) offers free tools for young job-seekers: a podcast on hiring-manager expectations, an "AI exposure by major/occupation" data tool (Field Report), and a mentorship-matching app (Game Plan); survey data reportedly shows self-worth questioning setting in by six months of unemployment.
