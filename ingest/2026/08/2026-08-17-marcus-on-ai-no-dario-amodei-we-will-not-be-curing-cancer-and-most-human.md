# No, Dario Amodei, we will not be curing cancer and “most human disease” in five to ten years

## Insights

- Dario Amodei publicly predicted AI could help cure "most human disease," including cancer, within 5-10 years; Marcus calls this timeline naive relative to how medical science and drug development actually work.
- Marcus points to a prior claim (from a March essay building on Emilia Javorsky's research) that, as of that writing, no AI-designed drug had reached clinical adoption despite over a decade of effort — used as evidence that AI drug discovery timelines are consistently overstated.
- Core argument: even substantially better AI cannot skip the time-consuming, expensive clinical trial process, which is a structural bottleneck independent of model capability.
- Marcus argues current AI lacks the "causally- and biologically-sophisticated" reasoning needed for this kind of scientific breakthrough — a capability gap, not just a data or compute gap.
- Notably, Marcus frames this as a shift in mood: a broad set of researchers and pro-AI voices (including physicians, computational biologists, and even AI optimist Ethan Mollick) publicly pushed back on Amodei's claim, which Marcus reads as growing mainstream skepticism toward CEO hype claims.
- Marcus also flags a pattern of overpromising from AI lab leaders beyond this instance (citing Amodei's earlier claim about AI doubling human lifespan, and a similar claim attributed to Demis Hassabis), suggesting this is a recurring credibility issue among leading AI executives rather than a one-off.

## Quote

> His timeline is so naive as to be absurd, both with respect to how medical science works and why it is challenging.
