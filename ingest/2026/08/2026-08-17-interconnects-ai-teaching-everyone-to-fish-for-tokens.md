# Teaching Everyone to Fish for Tokens

## Insights

- Open-source language models (full recipe: data, code, training process — e.g. Olmo, Pythia) are analogous to open-source operating systems, while open-weight models (just weights + inference code) are more like installed software versions with a "transient but long shelf life" — many companies still run on Llama 3 years after release.
- Nvidia is reportedly spending $26 billion to fund open(ish)-source model development (e.g. Nemotron) as a deliberate demand-generation strategy: more accessible model-building means more inference demand, which means more chip sales — described as "teaching everyone to fish for tokens."
- Two possible futures for open-source AI: (1) it "works" and generates enough Nvidia-chip demand/profit to sustain itself, requiring economic returns proportional to what Anthropic/OpenAI earn from APIs; or (2) it doesn't, and open models fork toward a different niche — efficiency, modifiability, specialization — becoming a long-tail complement to closed models rather than a competitor in high-value areas (knowledge-work collaboration, drug discovery, software engineering).
- The likely long-tail niche for open models: enterprise-specific agents running on-premises with private data on repetitive business tasks.
- Post-training's meaning is shifting — it used to cover the whole process of making a base model usable, but training a base model into a "general agentic reasoner" is becoming its own opaque, specialized stage, potentially reshaping the field's standard vocabulary from pretraining/midtraining/post-training toward pretraining/reasoning-training/post-training.
- Fewer open-model builders are releasing full base models, and some (e.g. via revenue-share licensing experiments) are seeking new financing models to keep near-frontier open-weight releases viable — a trend framed as necessary for Nvidia's open-source demand strategy to persist.
- Meta is cited as a different kind of open-weights actor: a hyperscaler with a massive balance sheet that releases very strong models (e.g. "Muse Spark 1.2") as open-weight specifically to undercut the token-selling revenue model of Anthropic and OpenAI — "flooding the zone with tokens" rather than cultivating a self-sustaining ecosystem.

## Quote

> Nvidia wants to teach everyone to fish for tokens, so the ecosystem is self-sustaining, but Meta is strategically flooding the zone with tokens.
