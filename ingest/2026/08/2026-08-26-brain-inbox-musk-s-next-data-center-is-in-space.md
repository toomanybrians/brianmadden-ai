# 🛰️ Musk's Next Data Center Is in Space

## Insights

- Nvidia and SpaceX designed a space-optimized Vera Rubin NVL72 AI server rack for launch aboard SpaceX's first Starmind satellite in Q4 2026, signaling an effort to site frontier-class compute in orbit rather than on the grid.
- The same announcement introduced Vera, a CPU built specifically for AI agent orchestration (code execution, tool calls, data movement) rather than model inference — targeting a bottleneck where cheap orchestration work was previously run on general-purpose x86 chips while expensive GPUs idled.
- Vendor claims (88 Olympus cores, 1.2TB/s memory bandwidth, 1.8x faster agentic task completion than x86) are unverified performance figures from the companies selling the hardware; orbital deployment still faces unresolved power, heat, bandwidth and reliability constraints with no on-site maintenance.
- Compute scarcity is showing up across the stack: Apple can't keep the Mac mini in stock because it became a popular local AI server amid a memory chip shortage; OpenAI reinstated a five-hour usage cap for Plus subscribers (while Pro tiers keep unlimited access) explicitly to manage compute load; Taiwan indicted nine people, including Nvidia and Supermicro employees, for smuggling restricted AI servers to China via Japan and Indonesia.
- SpaceX's vertical integration — rockets, satellite network, a $60B coding company acquisition (Cursor), and early access to Nvidia's newest silicon — is framed as a distinct competitive moat built around owning compute infrastructure rather than owning models.
- Separately: humanoid robots hit records in speed/jumping (a robot ran 100m in 9.39s, beating Usain Bolt) at a Beijing robotics games with heavy capital inflows (Unitree, XPeng), but the piece stresses this reflects solved locomotion problems, not general task competence in unfamiliar environments.
