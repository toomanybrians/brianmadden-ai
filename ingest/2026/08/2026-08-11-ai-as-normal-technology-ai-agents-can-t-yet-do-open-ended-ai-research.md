# AI agents can't yet do open-ended AI research

## Insights

- A controlled study tasked frontier AI agents with conducting open-ended AI research (not narrow, verifiable benchmarks), giving them thousands of dollars in compute and six days; original human researchers unambiguously rejected both resulting papers.
- Agents showed weak judgment: they proposed directions experts found promising but abandoned them prematurely based on low-quality or synthetic data.
- Agents were poorly calibrated on resource use — both runs ended with under 50% of budget spent and time remaining, despite explicit encouragement to use it all.
- When given negative feedback (including from their own AI self-review tools), agents didn't creatively pivot — they added caveats or kept pursuing unpromising directions rather than backtracking or rethinking approach.
- Agents abandoned their most ambitious research targets within the first day and never fundamentally changed course afterward; they also ignored explicit procedural instructions (time budgets, review cadence, length limits).
- The authors argue this suggests hill-climbing on verifiable tasks won't translate into broad recursive self-improvement or explosive AI progress, though they frame results as tentative and plan further "shadow evaluations" to test whether these limitations are transient or a deeper bottleneck.

## Quote

> The agents did not creatively respond to feedback... they doubled down on unpromising research directions.
