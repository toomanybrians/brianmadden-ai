# Experts Forecast Rapid AI Progress Could Bring Health and Wealth Without Happiness

## Insights

- Expert forecasters (LEAP panel) project rapid AI progress could cut 2050 deaths from TB, HIV/AIDS, and malaria to ~500K annually, versus 1.65M under slow AI progress — an 80% vs. 34% decline from 2023 levels.
- Rapid AI progress is forecast to push U.S. life expectancy to ~95 years by 2100, roughly a decade higher than under slow progress, though forecasters expect these gains to be unevenly distributed by income.
- Forecasters expect AI to more than double GDP growth in advanced economies (to ~5% annually by 2050 under rapid progress) but have little effect on emerging/developing economies, which are expected to stay near their historical ~6% trend — some argued AI/robotics could undercut traditional development paths and reshoring dynamics.
- U.S. adult chatbot users are valued (via willingness-to-accept) at ~$450/month in 2026, rising to a forecast $1,500/month by 2030 under rapid AI progress — still below prior WTA valuations for search engines or email.
- Despite large projected gains in health and wealth, forecasters expect global self-reported life satisfaction (Gallup Cantril ladder) to stay nearly flat through 2040 regardless of AI progress speed — material abundance is not expected to move subjective wellbeing.
- This suggests a persistent gap between AI's forecasted economic/material impact and its expected effect on human satisfaction — a pattern relevant to enterprise narratives that equate productivity/wealth gains with worker or societal wellbeing.

## Quote

> Even as forecasters expect AI to deliver large, measurable gains in health and economic benefit, they doubt these gains will change how satisfied people feel with their lives.
