# What the hell happened with AGI timelines in 2026?

## Insights

- The sudden 2026 sentiment reversal on AI agent capability (Karpathy and others going from "AI agents are slop" to "the profession is being refactored") is attributed to Claude Opus 4.5's release, not any architectural breakthrough — no progress was made on continual learning, world models, or other previously cited blockers.
- The Epoch Capabilities Index shows AI progress running at 2-3x its prior rate since reasoning models arrived in September 2024, with no sign of the repeatedly-predicted "wall" materializing.
- The likely explanation for agents suddenly becoming useful: continued scaling plus RL from verifiable rewards pushed per-step error rates low enough that delegating multi-step tasks to AI became faster than doing them manually — a threshold effect rather than a new capability.
- AI industry revenue growth is described as possibly the fastest for an industry of this size in history — combined OpenAI/Anthropic revenue up ~700% annualized over six months, with Anthropic's gross margins on inference rising from 38% to over 70% in a year, undercutting the "selling at a loss" explanation for growth.
- Public and expert sentiment about AI capability has swung sharply multiple times since 2021, largely independent of underlying technical trend lines — a caution against reading vibe shifts as reliable capability signals.
- The piece frames 2026 as an active, unresolved debate between "AGI bulls" and "AGI bears," with the evidence so far insufficient to settle core disagreements about reliability, generalization, and timelines.
