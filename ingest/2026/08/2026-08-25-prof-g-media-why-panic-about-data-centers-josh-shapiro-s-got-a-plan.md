# Why Panic About Data Centers? Josh Shapiro’s Got A Plan.

## Insights

- Gallup polling shows 70% of Americans oppose data centers being built in their local area, with nearly half "greatly" worried about environmental impact — concerns rank resources, quality-of-life, costs, and pollution in that order.
- Data centers are projected to consume 10-15% of U.S. electricity by 2030 (Berkeley estimates) and already use ~17 billion gallons of water annually, though this is less than several other industries (cattle, power plants, residential use).
- Construction pace is accelerating sharply: Texas has 537 data centers (most of any state), and both Texas and the combined Pennsylvania/New York total have more than doubled in number since July of the prior year.
- Data centers can deliver concrete local economic benefits — e.g., Loudoun County, VA funded new schools, a park, and a $102M recreation center from data center tax revenue; Richland Parish, LA teachers got $51,000 bonuses tied to a Meta facility.
- Land use is emerging as an unaddressable friction point: Loudoun County's 250+ warehouses/data centers occupy 53 million square feet (~920 football fields).
- Three governors have responded differently: NY's Hochul and TX's Abbott each imposed moratoriums (framed as reactive/panicked), while PA's Shapiro issued an executive order requiring local government buy-in and "good citizenship" commitments (paying for power, conserving water, hiring locally, transparency) rather than a blanket ban.
