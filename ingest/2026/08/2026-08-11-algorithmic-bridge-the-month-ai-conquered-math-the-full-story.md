# The Month AI Conquered Math: The Full Story

## Insights

- In a single month (July 2026), frontier AI models reportedly resolved or disproved multiple long-standing math conjectures (Cycle Double Cover, Jacobian, Dinitz-Garg-Goemans, Maxwell), some over a century old, marking a sharp jump from "good but not exceptional" to apparently superhuman performance in pure math research.
- Terence Tao's assessment that a key counterexample was "highly unlikely" to result from brute-force search suggests genuine creative/heuristic reasoning, not just exhaustive computation — a notable claim about the nature of AI's contribution.
- OpenAI's own framing (via Noam Brown) pushes back on "solved math" narratives: models found counterexamples/proofs but did not generate new conjectures, open new branches, or crack Millennium Prize problems — the frontier of mathematical creativity/direction-setting remains human for now.
- Cost framing is notable for enterprise/knowledge-work implications: OpenAI stated the compute for ten major results cost roughly $2,000 total, positioning elite research output as now commoditizable at trivial marginal cost.
- Reactions from mathematicians split sharply into "mad/sad" (threat to the meaning and labor of the field) versus "optimistic" (awe, acceleration of discovery) — a preview of the dual reaction pattern likely to recur across other expert knowledge domains as AI capability jumps arrive.
- The piece frames this as an analogy for what "AI conquering" a knowledge domain looks like from the inside — rapid, discontinuous, contested, and forcing a redefinition of what expertise/authorship even means afterward.
