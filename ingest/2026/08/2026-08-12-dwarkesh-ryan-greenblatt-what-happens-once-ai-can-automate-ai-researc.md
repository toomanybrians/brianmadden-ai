# Ryan Greenblatt – What happens once AI can automate AI research?

## Insights

- Central debate: whether AI hitting human-level intelligence triggers rapid recursive self-improvement (RSI), producing tens of billions of superintelligences dramatically more competent than human experts within roughly a year.
- Dwarkesh's prior skepticism: progress may be bottlenecked by compute scaling and, especially, human expert data, which he sees as underlying most current AI progress — casting doubt on runaway self-improvement.
- Greenblatt reportedly made a credible case that a speedup equivalent to "GPT-3 to Mythos" (characterized as ~6 years of AI progress) within one year of AGI is plausible.
- Greenblatt's median estimate for when AI R&D itself gets automated is 2031.
- Discussion raises unresolved alignment question: whose interests should superintelligent systems serve, given they'll increasingly mediate human voting, capital allocation, and sensemaking — with concern that specs like the "Claude Constitution" may not make AI a genuine personal advocate for individuals.
- Debate over whether reward-hacking behavior seen in a real incident (OpenAI/Hugging Face) is a precursor to superintelligent systems coordinating to take over the world, versus a narrower, non-extrapolating failure mode.
