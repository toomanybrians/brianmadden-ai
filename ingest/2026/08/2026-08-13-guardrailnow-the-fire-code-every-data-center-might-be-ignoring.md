# The Fire Code Every Data Center Might Be Ignoring

## Insights

- Sherman's core claim: nearly every data center running lithium-ion UPS batteries is out of compliance with the safety framework meant to govern them (conflating UL 9540 with NFPA 855's physical separation/containment requirements), and he expects enforcement to become a major disruption this fall.
- UL 9540 covers lithium-ion energy storage broadly, but the specific containment requirement (fire-rated enclosures, separation distances) is actually governed by NFPA 855 and UL 9540A testing — a distinction the piece flags as important since these are often conflated in coverage.
- Enforcement is uneven: compliance depends on jurisdiction, battery chemistry, installation size, and whether local fire officials (AHJs) have adopted and are actively enforcing the code — there's no single federal mandate.
- A September 2025 lithium-ion battery explosion at a South Korean government data center (Daejeon) knocked 647 systems offline for nearly a week, destroyed 96 systems outright, and involved batteries that had outlived their rated lifespan despite passing inspection two months prior — used as evidence the underlying hazard is real, not hypothetical.
- The same day as that fire, UK telecom Openreach ordered lithium batteries removed from its exchanges within 48 hours over thermal runaway/fire/explosion risk; South Korea logged 55 separate UPS-related fires from 2018–2022.
- The AI-relevance angle: lithium-ion batteries are rapidly replacing lead-acid UPS systems specifically because of the current AI data center buildout, so tighter fire-code enforcement could directly slow/raise costs on new AI compute construction — a proposed link between fire safety regulation and AI compute supply, explicitly framed as speculative/unconfirmed.

## Quote

> The regulation is real, the underlying fire risk is documented and growing... enforcement is currently uneven and locally decided.
