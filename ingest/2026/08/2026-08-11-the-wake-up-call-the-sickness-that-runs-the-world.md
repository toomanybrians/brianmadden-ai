# The sickness that runs the world

## Insights

- The story imagines a post-AI-revolution society stratified into four classes based on relationship to "the sickness" — an insatiable, status-driven ambition that becomes the only scarce human resource once AI automates most cognition and labor.
- Premise: mass reliance on AI caused human skills and original thought to atrophy, so "new" training data became increasingly synthetic/self-referential, degrading model quality and destabilizing capitalism and social order.
- Human-generated data becomes the scarce commodity; the ruling "Great Founders" prize ambitious, restless, status-obsessed individuals because they're the ones still generating novel data and driving growth.
- Society ranks people by contribution to the data/attention economy: creators ("born ones"), trained strivers ("learned sick"), passive consumers whose worth is measured in hours consumed, and disconnected "luddites" stripped of digital rights.
- Framed as speculative fiction rather than argument, but dramatizes a real anxiety: AI-driven automation could hollow out human initiative and turn continued human "value" into a function of data production and consumption rather than skill or labor.
- Depicts stillness, reflection, and disconnection as socially punished, inverting current productivity/wellness discourse — a caution about attention economies scaled up by AI incentives.
