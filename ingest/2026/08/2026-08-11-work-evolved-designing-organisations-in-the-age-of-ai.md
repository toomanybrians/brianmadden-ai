# Designing Organisations in the Age of AI

## Insights

- Argues it's premature for most large organizations to pursue AI-driven restructuring, since digital and data maturity to actually replace workers with agents is still lacking in most domains.
- Suggests that because labor laws and disruption costs limit how often teams can be reorganized, any reorg happening now may be the only window for years to bake in AI-readiness.
- Recommends designing around an external reference point (customer, product, or technical domain) rather than existing roles/reporting lines, arguing this maps better to how AI-enabled value flows across end-to-end work rather than sitting inside single job descriptions.
- Advocates generalizing job titles and writing outcome-based (rather than task-based) role profiles, so teams can be reshaped around problems as AI changes work — while acknowledging this trades away the ability to cleanly eliminate specialist roles later without triggering larger restructuring/consultation processes.
- Calls for flattening hierarchy and repositioning managers as "orchestrators" enabling teams and overseeing digital labor, rather than approval bottlenecks, as individual contributors gain more autonomous decision-making agency.
- Frames most team-level reorgs as actually driven by cultural/interpersonal issues rather than structural ones, cautioning that changing "sticks and boxes" alone rarely solves the underlying problem.

## Quote

> You can't solve a business problem by changing sticks and boxes alone.
