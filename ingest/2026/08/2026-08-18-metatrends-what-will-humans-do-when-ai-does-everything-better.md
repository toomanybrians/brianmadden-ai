# What Will Humans Do When AI Does Everything Better?

## Insights

- Diamandis frames a "purpose problem" distinct from the "unemployment problem": when AGI and robots outperform humans at all cognitive and physical tasks, policy fixes (UBI, redistribution) don't address the existential question of what gives life meaning.
- Uses historical precedent to argue leisure freed from labor (Greek "skholé") produces either civilizational flourishing (philosophy, art, Olympics) or decadent collapse (Roman "bread and circuses") — the outcome depends on culture and values, not technology itself.
- Points to Renaissance patronage (Medici) as a model where humans shift from producing to curating/directing resources toward what's valuable — suggesting "what should we build" becomes more important than "how do we build it."
- Cites research on how the ultra-wealthy already spend time (reading, exercise, patronage, collecting, mentoring, creative work, exploration, competition) as an empirical preview of post-labor human behavior — none of it economically motivated, all "autotelic."
- Draws on flow psychology (Csikszentmihalyi) to argue human satisfaction doesn't depend on being the best (AI will always be better) but on full absorption and challenge-skill matching — so AI superiority doesn't eliminate the value of human activity, just its economic necessity.
- Proposes 10 categories of post-AGI human purpose: curator, explorer, athlete, creator/amateur, relationship-builder, philosopher, patron/philanthropist, storyteller, parent/educator, healer — each built around things AI can simulate/produce but not authentically "live" (presence, taste, lived experience, connection).
