# 🔮 The market misread Google’s AI exodus

## Insights

- Jeff Dean, Sanjay Ghemawat (25+ year Google veterans) and DeepMind founder Demis Hassabis (persuaded into a chair role) are all exiting or stepping back from core roles at Google/Alphabet around the same time — read by markets as a talent crisis, dropping Alphabet's share price 4% in a day.
- The piece reframes this not as a verdict on talent or morale but as a signal about capital and compute allocation: the departures may reflect how Google is now deploying its TPU/compute resources rather than a loss of confidence in AI.
- Two competing explanations are posed as opposite ends of the AI capital cycle: researchers leaving because they've lost faith in Alphabet's AI prospects, versus leaving because serving today's profitable production models now earns a higher return on compute than open-ended research — squeezing out moonshot-style work.
- Frames Google's historical strength as an unusually deliberate corporate system for protecting speculative research from the pressures of its cash-generating core (cites 20% time, X moonshot factory, the Alphabet holding structure, aggressive acquisitions) — and suggests that system may now be breaking down or shifting.
- The full (paywalled) piece promises seven supporting charts covering: Google's model performance on the Pareto frontier, the reallocation of compute away from research, Google Cloud growth/economics as a demand signal, and four proposed signals for identifying when the AI infrastructure cycle has turned.

## Quote

> If even Google now allows the engineers who built its very foundations to leave, something about the way it allocates capital has changed.
