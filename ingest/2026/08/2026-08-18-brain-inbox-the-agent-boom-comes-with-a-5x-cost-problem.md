# ⚙️ The agent boom comes with a 5x cost problem

## Insights

- Gartner predicts AI inference costs per agentic workflow will rise more than fivefold through 2028, driven by an "inference paradox": even as per-unit AI costs improve, total spending keeps climbing because agentic workflows consume far more tokens than chatbots.
- Enterprises are pushed toward agentic AI over simpler chatbot tools because agents are seen as more likely to deliver step-change returns — even though those returns aren't currently predictable or guaranteed.
- Info-Tech's Scott Bickley describes a top-down "mandate" forcing enterprises into aggressive, often under-analyzed AI adoption, frequently skipping total-cost-of-ownership assessment and organizational readiness/culture considerations.
- Demand for inference is now a primary revenue driver for major labs (OpenAI, Anthropic), fueling massive compute investment — e.g., Nvidia's reported $105B financing commitment for OpenAI's Ohio data center — as compute scarcity becomes a real constraint on growth.
- Unlike typical tech innovation cycles where efficiency gains compound benefits, AI's compute and inference costs are bounded by finite physical resources, prompting some experts to argue a deliberate slowdown may be the more rational path.
- Separately, in enterprise coding: AI coding startups (CodeRabbit, Lovable, Cognition) are hitting multi-billion valuations amid heavy experimentation by companies testing multiple tools simultaneously; the piece speculates this crowded field may consolidate once buyers narrow down favorites, similar to streaming service shakeouts.

## Quote

> "This blind foray into the AI abyss often lacks the in-depth understanding of the total cost of ownership." — Scott Bickley, Info-Tech Research Group
