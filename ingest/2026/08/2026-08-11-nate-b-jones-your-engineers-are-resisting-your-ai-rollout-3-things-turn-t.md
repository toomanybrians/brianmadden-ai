# Your Engineers Are Resisting Your AI Rollout. 3 Things Turn That Around.

## Insights

- Frames engineer resistance to AI rollouts as a leadership honesty problem, not a training gap — teams withhold effort when leaders won't address job risk directly.
- Recommends a time-bound, public employment commitment specific enough that leadership can actually be held to it, rather than vague reassurance.
- Suggests anchoring pilot selection to a narrow, high-value use case tied to bottom-line impact, arguing that team-level managers (not executives) determine whether adoption actually sticks.
- Notes a structural tension (illustrated via an "Uber token budget" example) between centrally mandated AI usage targets and the practical constraints teams face implementing them.
- Argues that as agents absorb more first-draft work, leaders must explicitly define what human work remains and protect it, framing this as the third pillar after commitment and pilot design.
- References Jensen Huang's framing of AI-and-jobs messaging as a model that resonates with technical teams, without detailing the framing itself.

## Quote

> Leaders can require the change, and the rollout only holds if they can also tell people where that change leads.
