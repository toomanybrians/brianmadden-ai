# Base Power Company: Chapter 3

## Insights

- Base frames its growth as compounding a "Capability Cannon" — a portfolio of learned capabilities (manufacturing, deployment, supply chain, software, brand, policy) that it points at successive hard problems rather than treating any single product as the end goal.
- The company's culture is described via a "web" metaphor: scrappy, ad hoc experimentation happens at the organizational edge, while the center's job is to take proven ad hoc solutions and standardize them into repeatable processes and software.
- Leadership deliberately reversed a major hardware decision (canceling a semi-outsourced "Gen 2" battery program) after employees converged independently on the same conclusion, prioritizing long-term capability-building and vertical integration over sunk-cost momentum.
- Executives explicitly named intelligence/AI as a future target for their capability stack, alongside physical infrastructure buildout tied to data-center power demand — treating "electrons" and "intelligence" as parallel commodities to be made, moved, stored, and sold.
- Internal tooling (custom manufacturing execution system, inventory tracking, quality management software) was built in-house once off-the-shelf systems designed for different scale/use cases proved inadequate, reflecting a pattern of encoding operational learning into software as fast as possible.
- Company leaders describe deliberately limiting automation and formal process early on to preserve flexibility during rapid design iteration, only standardizing/automating once a solution has been proven.

> "A good solution to an engineer might not be a good solution to the person that has to use it." — Andy Ross, Base Head of Manufacturing
