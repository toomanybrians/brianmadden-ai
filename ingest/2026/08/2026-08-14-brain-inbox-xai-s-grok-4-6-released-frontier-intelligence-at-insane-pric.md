# 🛰️ xAI's Grok 4.6 Released: Frontier Intelligence At Insane Pricing

## Insights

- Grok 4.6 matches GPT-5.6 Sol on Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index (61 vs 61) while charging far less: $2/million input tokens and $6/million output vs Sol's $5/$30 — signaling that frontier capability and price are decoupling rather than moving together.
- xAI's jump from Grok 4.5 (56) to 4.6 (61) in five weeks was reportedly fueled by SpaceX's $60bn all-stock acquisition of Anysphere (maker of Cursor), feeding Cursor's coding data into training — a case of buying data/tooling access as a faster path to frontier performance than pure R&D.
- Grok 4.6 is architected for long-running agentic work: stronger one-shot output on visual/interactive tasks and more built-in self-checking mid-task, which the piece frames as the difference between an agent completing a job versus derailing partway through.
- On knowledge-work benchmarking (AA-Briefcase), Grok 4.6 ranks second behind Claude Opus 5 but completes tasks in ~53 turns/0.5B tokens versus Opus 5's ~103 turns/2.0B tokens — an efficiency gap as notable as the raw score.
- DeepSeek's V4-Pro appeared with no announcement, paper, or model card yet already scores near-frontier on coding benchmarks at a tiny fraction of Claude's cost — evidence that model releases are becoming untethered from traditional launch/disclosure norms, with leaderboards now the primary discovery mechanism.
- Separately, Sergey Brin (no formal Google title) is reportedly pushing the company informally toward recursive self-improving AI systems, with internal disputes over compute and coding resourcing cited as reasons Google fell behind after Gemini 3.
- Anthropic investors are reportedly modeling a ~$2tn IPO valuation based on projected 800% revenue growth (to $100-120bn annualized by end of 2026), despite charging over 2.5x OpenAI's pricing — raising questions about whether growth assumptions justify premium pricing as competitors undercut on cost.
