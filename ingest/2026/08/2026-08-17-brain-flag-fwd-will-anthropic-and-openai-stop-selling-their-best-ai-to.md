# Fwd: Will Anthropic and OpenAI Stop Selling Their Best AI to Businesses?

## Insights

- Anthropic and OpenAI are increasingly building vertical, industry-specific apps and features that put them in direct competition with customers built on their APIs (e.g., Canva); the piece frames this as an escalating risk pattern, not an isolated case.
- Multiple pressures could push both labs to degrade or restrict API access to their most capable models: government-driven staggered release processes (Trump administration case-by-case approval, proposed pre-release testing regime), safety concerns about catastrophic misuse (cyber, bioweapons), and anti-distillation motives aimed at blocking Chinese firms from training on frontier outputs.
- Anthropic has already selectively degraded model performance on certain tasks for safety reasons (cited example: Fable's cybersecurity-related tasks), suggesting this isn't hypothetical but an active practice.
- A more cynical read, sourced to investors, is that Anthropic could deliberately withhold its best models from API customers to advantage its own competing applications — which the article notes would likely trigger antitrust scrutiny given Anthropic's API market-leader position.
- In response to this uncertainty, some AI-app companies (Harvey, Cursor) have started training their own in-house models rather than remain dependent on OpenAI/Anthropic APIs — a hedging strategy other developers are expected to copy.
- Context data points: Anthropic Q2 revenue rose ~14x YoY to $11.5B and was profitable on adjusted operating income; open-source/Chinese models (e.g., Z.ai's GLM-5.3) are claiming cybersecurity capability parity with Anthropic's frontier model.

> Some investors have been warning developers that Anthropic could hold back its best technology so it can develop its own competing applications.
