# CPUs and the rise of neurosymbolic AI

## Insights

- Frames a hardware-level distinction: pure neural nets are GPU-bound (parallel matrix math), while classical/symbolic computation leans on CPUs, and neurosymbolic systems need both — a proxy for tracking where AI architecture is actually heading.
- Dates the "pure neural network" era as roughly 2012–mid 2023, ending when frontier labs began quietly folding in symbolic components like code interpreters — a claim worth flagging since it implies today's leading systems are already neurosymbolic in practice, not just in theory.
- Positions the rise of neurosymbolic AI as a response to limits of pure scaling — consistent with Marcus's long-standing (and consensus-contrarian) argument that scaling alone won't get to robust reasoning, so this piece is best read as reinforcing his prior thesis rather than a neutral technical observation.
- The CPU/GPU angle is a useful lens for enterprise infra and cost conversations — if neurosymbolic approaches gain share, it could shift compute procurement and vendor dynamics away from pure GPU-scaling narratives, though the post itself is largely a paywalled teaser and doesn't substantiate this with data.
