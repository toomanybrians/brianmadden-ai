# It’s about capabilities, not technology

## Insights

- Argues most enterprise AI transformation programs fail by framing AI as a subject to teach (training/courses) rather than a capability to build through organizational mechanisms.
- Proposes that firm-level "skill infrastructure" requires systems that apply AI to real business problems, then capture and diffuse each discovery so learning compounds instead of staying siloed in individual workflows.
- Draws an analogy to national-level skill infrastructure (engineering education, standardized best practice, university-industry links) as a model for what companies need internally.
- Claims a company's best source of applied AI R&D is its existing employees/customers experimenting in their daily work — not a separate specialized function — referencing a prior piece ("The End of Programming as We Know It") arguing AI expands who can build rather than replacing builders.
- States the core organizational task is making grassroots AI experimentation visible, shareable, and rewarded.
- Notes this framework underlies O'Reilly's enterprise AI transformation programs, developed drawing on ideas from Ethan Mollick and Dan Guido (CEO, Trail of Bits).

## Quote

> The problem with most enterprise AI transformation programs is that they treat AI as a subject to be taught rather than a capability to be built.
