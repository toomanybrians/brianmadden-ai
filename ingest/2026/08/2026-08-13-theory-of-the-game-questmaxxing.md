# QUESTMAXXING

## Insights

- Hoffman frames "theory of the game" as an explicit model of how an environment converts effort into outcomes, plus a self-defined claim about what counts as success — since most work environments (unlike chess) don't hand you a fixed win condition. He argues most people in organizations run on an inherited, unexamined theory (e.g. "work hard and you'll be noticed") that hasn't been updated despite changed conditions.
- Argues "missionary" companies outperform "mercenary" ones operationally, not just ethically — a well-operationalized mission functions as coordination infrastructure that makes decisions across product, hiring, and strategy more coherent, because it gives everyone the same reference point for judgment calls.
- Describes LinkedIn's practice of asking candidates "what's the job you want after LinkedIn?" as a deliberate mission-alignment device — treating employees as having their own trajectories rather than as retained assets, consistent with the company's mission of enabling individual economic mobility.
- Uses a LinkedIn case study (2007 "Red Team" project to try to destroy LinkedIn using Facebook's new platform) as a model for gamified organizational learning with a defined, high-stakes quest — contrasted with a second case (LinkedIn Groups) where lack of a clear mission led to ambiguous, resource-draining outcomes rather than fast kill/learn cycles.
- On AI specifically: argues AI agents optimize around fixed objective functions and can drift from broader intent when given autonomy, but that same autonomy enables adaptive problem-solving beyond human anticipation — leadership shifts from "conducting a score" to "leading jazz improvisation," setting direction rather than dictating steps.
- Claims that as intelligence/compute becomes commoditized and AI systems learn faster than humans, the scarce and decisive factor becomes the ability to learn faster than the environment changes and convert that learning into coherent, fast-moving strategy — plus the human capacity to supply subjective purpose, since AI "cannot want an outcome."

## Quote

> A theory of the game is also the prerequisite for everything that follows.
