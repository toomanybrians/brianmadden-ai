# 🌻 no data centers in my backyard

## Insights

- Data center backlash has become a rare bipartisan issue, polling roughly 70% opposed across both major parties — framed by one activist as "the most bipartisan issue since beer."
- Opposition clusters around distinct grievances: secretive NDA-based negotiations with developers, uneven risk-sharing (towns bear consequences if projects fail), and fears of repeating past over-reliance on single large employers (echoing deindustrialization trauma, e.g., Janesville's GM plant closure).
- Local governments often lack the legal and financial capacity to negotiate favorable terms with sophisticated developers, leaving small communities vulnerable to lopsided deals despite promised tax revenue.
- Siting patterns raise equity concerns — data centers are frequently proposed for economically distressed or already-industrialized areas rather than wealthy tech hubs, prompting "not in Zuckerberg's backyard" framing.
- Environmental objections (water use, aquifer impacts, land contamination cleanup) mix with procedural distrust, suggesting the backlash is as much about governance transparency as about AI or energy policy per se.
- The controversy is reshaping local politics directly — triggering resignations, referendum campaigns, and new city council candidacies tied specifically to data center fights.

## Quote

> If you look at where data centers are being built, it's not in Mark Zuckerberg's backyard. It is in hardworking Janesvillians'.
