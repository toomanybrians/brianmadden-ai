# The Other Wage Puzzle

## Insights

- Wage growth for non-professional occupations (blue-collar, service, sales, support) has fallen from over 6% in 2022 to about 3%, now below wage growth for management/professional workers — the opposite of consensus expectations.
- Two labor-market tailwinds that should be boosting these wages — AI's presumed threat concentrated in knowledge work, and immigration enforcement tightening labor supply in these occupations — are both present, yet wages are decelerating anyway.
- One explanation: the 2021-23 low-wage surge was a tightness-driven anomaly now reverting to trend, not a durable structural shift, though this is getting harder to sustain as an explanation three years out.
- A second, more provocative explanation: the "non-professional = AI-safe" assumption is wrong, since this category includes office/administrative support, sales, and customer service — routine information work — plus warehousing and manufacturing exposed to advancing automation.
- The piece frames this as a challenge to the dominant AI-labor narrative, suggesting less-educated occupations may be more exposed to AI/automation displacement than commonly assumed.

## Quote

> The consensus quietly equates "non-professional" with "AI-safe." Much of this group is neither.
