# Executive Briefing: The $350K Job Has Three Parts and You Already Do One

## Insights

- Forward-deployed engineer roles are being posted at wildly inconsistent pay bands (OpenAI: $162K–$280K; Handshake senior version: $250K–$350K), suggesting employers themselves disagree on what the job actually entails.
- The role is framed as three distinct jobs bundled into one title: choosing the right problem to solve, building the solution, and owning the outcome after deployment — work typically split across four separate roles at most companies.
- Most candidates arrive with only one of the three components already mastered (engineers, operators, or domain experts each own a different piece), and standard career advice to "learn to code" discards the domain-expertise component people already possess.
- Anthropic's analysis of 400,000 Claude Code sessions found people from non-software occupations finishing within a few points of software engineers on tasks that produced code — used as evidence that coding ability is becoming less of a differentiator.
- The argument is that industry/domain background (e.g., a claims adjuster's or finance operator's specific knowledge) is the scarce, non-bootcamp-able asset that determines whether resulting code/systems actually do the right job — and that this is what these high salaries are implicitly paying for, even though listings don't say so explicitly.

## Quote

> Your background isn't the thing holding you back. It's the one part of this job that can't be picked up in a bootcamp.
