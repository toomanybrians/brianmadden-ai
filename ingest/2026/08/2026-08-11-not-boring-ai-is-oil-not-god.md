# AI is Oil, Not God

## Insights

- McCormick frames the AI safety debate as a choice between two mental models: "Oil" (a scalable, refinable commodity that multiplies productivity) versus "God" (a potentially superintelligent entity that could subjugate humanity) — and argues the "Oil" frame is correct.
- The open-weight letter signed by Nadella, Huang, and others is read through Joel Spolsky's "commoditize your complements" strategy: nearly every signatory's business benefits when models become cheaper, more numerous, and more open (chipmakers, cloud platforms, VCs, enterprise software firms, security vendors).
- McCormick argues the signatories' self-interest (avoiding an OpenAI/Anthropic frontier duopoly) happens to align with broader consumer and enterprise interests — a case that competitive dynamics, not altruism, drive beneficial diffusion of AI capability.
- He infers that because Nadella and Huang are closest to frontier capabilities yet still advocate open diffusion, their revealed belief is that models are not on a trajectory toward uncontrollable, conscious superintelligence.
- A counterargument is surfaced via Matt Kaufman (Collaborative Fund): open-weight models could be fine-tuned to strip bio/cyber safeguards, and since AI often favors offense over defense in many domains, unregulated proliferation could become genuinely dangerous as capability grows and reaches actors outside the tech industry.
- The piece positions this as a live fault line in enterprise AI strategy: whether organizations should bet on open, disintermediated model access (Palantir's Karp is cited as pushing this in the enterprise) versus more locked-down, safety-gated frontier models.

## Quote

> AI is more Oil or God... an economically useful commodity... [or] a supremely intelligent and powerful being that's going to wake up and make humanity subservient.
