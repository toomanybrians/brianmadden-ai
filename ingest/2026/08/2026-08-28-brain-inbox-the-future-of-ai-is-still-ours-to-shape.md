# The Future of AI Is Still Ours to Shape

## Insights

- Frames the current AI backlash as a predictable response to years of messaging telling workers that jobs, career ladders, and human performance would be displaced by machines.
- References Geoffrey Hinton's warning that AI could surpass human intelligence, using his "apex intelligence"/chicken analogy to describe losing control to a smarter power.
- Argues uncertainty about AI's future is distinct from inevitability, and that individuals and leaders retain agency in shaping outcomes rather than passively accepting predicted futures.
- Proposes reframing the core question from "What is AI going to do to my job?" to "What can I do now that I couldn't before?" and "What can AI do that it couldn't do without me?"
- Distinguishes automation (making existing work faster/cheaper) from augmentation (creating possibilities that didn't previously exist), positioning augmentation as requiring curiosity, judgment, creativity, and a "beginner's mind."
- Introduces a personal heuristic, "WWAID" (What Would AI Do?), as a prompt to surface and challenge one's own assumptions before engaging AI — explicitly not to offload thinking or decision-making to it.
- Positions leadership's key task as helping people believe they have a role in shaping AI's trajectory, rather than treating the future as already determined.

## Quote

> If you want to understand what it feels like to no longer be the apex intelligence, consider the chicken.
