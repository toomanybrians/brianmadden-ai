# 5 useful things you'll learn in my new post-training textbook (shipping now!)

## Insights

- Nathan Lambert's new book "Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback: Aligning and Post-training LLMs" (Manning) frames post-training as a systems-and-intuition problem rather than pure math — core RL techniques (PPO, GRPO, GSPO, CISPO) have converged and stayed roughly stable for several years despite rapid model progress.
- Modern RL post-training is increasingly a systems/infrastructure challenge — managing off-policy data, training-inference mismatch, and throughput via asynchronous setups (separate GPUs for learning vs. rollout generation) — more than a novel-algorithm problem.
- The book traces post-training's lineage through three eras: general RL-on-preferences research (pre-2018), early application to language models (2019–2022), and the post-ChatGPT explosion (2023–present), arguing the field's founders deserve more recognition.
- A chapter is devoted to demystifying "distillation," positioning it as a well-understood, broadly-used technique (not a nefarious geopolitical tool) — tracing its evolution from 2015-era knowledge distillation to modern multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) used in models like Xiaomi MiMo-V2-Flash and DeepSeek V4.
- Signals that foundational post-training knowledge is becoming standardized/curriculum-ready — the book targets people with a CS bachelor's background, suggesting the field is maturing past pure research-lab tacit knowledge into teachable, institutionalized expertise.
