# The “We Must Act Now” Letter

## Insights

- The "We Must Act Now" letter (200+ economists/AI researchers, 16 Nobel laureates, now 2,000+ signatories) frames potential AI-driven economic transformation as larger than the Industrial Revolution but compressed into a much shorter timeframe.
- Notable signatories include former AI skeptics — e.g., Daron Acemoglu, whose 2023 estimate put AI's decade-long TFP impact at just 0.5–0.7% and GDP impact at 0.9–1.2% under conservative assumptions.
- The author argues the shift among economists isn't driven by AI's popularity (which has declined) but by accumulating evidence: visible labor displacement, productivity acceleration, and rapid capability jumps (e.g., models moving from text generation to executing research workflows).
- Central thesis: each new capability generation has converted a fresh cohort of skeptics who previously dismissed AI's economic significance — a pattern the author says has repeated at every stage since 2023.
- Implication for forecasting: judging AI's trajectory by current-generation capability ("the snapshot") has been a consistently losing bet; the correct frame is extrapolating the rate of change.

## Quote

> Judge the trajectory, not the snapshot.
