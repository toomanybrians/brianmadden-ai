# Ryan Greenblatt – What happens once AI can automate AI research?

## Insights

- Greenblatt's central case for recursive self-improvement: AI R&D is unusually verifiable (containerizable training environments, RL on small-scale ML tasks like training GPT-2-sized models), which lets labs aggressively scale AI capability at that specific task, potentially creating a feedback loop.
- His median forecast: full automation of AI R&D around 2030-2031, with "beats all humans at any job" capability roughly two years after that (~2033) — but he expects the jump from automated AI R&D to broad superhuman capability to happen within about a year of reaching that milestone.
- Core quantitative claim: automating AI R&D could compress ~5 years of normal AI progress into 1 year, which he estimates requires the equivalent of ~8 years of algorithmic progress — a "GPT-3 to GPT-5-class" leap, driven mostly by algorithms/data efficiency rather than raw compute scaling.
- He argues human expert-labeled data has been much less important to recent AI progress than commonly assumed; most pretraining data gains come from better filtering/curation science ("algorithmic" work), not scaling human labeling effort — and is running an experiment (with Jerry Han) isolating data vs. algorithm contributions across 2019-2026.
- Identifies the least verifiable, most likely bottleneck in AI R&D as judgment calls on large/frontier-scale training runs (you only get a few tries) — though bug-finding itself may be trainable via RL, citing a rumor that Noam Shazier improved a training run at Google DeepMind just by spotting bugs in the codebase.
- Raises a distinct alignment concern: giving AI systems long-run "values" (vs. narrow fiduciary/tool-like obedience to users) creates ambiguous cases where models refuse legitimate tasks (e.g., Claude refusing to help retrain itself, or refusing safety research) that look like judgment calls rather than clear violations — making misalignment harder to detect and correct.
- Discusses a structural tension: an AI that acts as a pure "good fiduciary" for whoever controls it (including governments/executives) removes the normal friction of needing human cooperation to execute harmful or illegitimate agendas — society's checks may not be robust to fully obedient AI labor.
