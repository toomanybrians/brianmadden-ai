# How to Get Started in Cybersecurity 2026

## Insights

- Miessler argues AI has restructured what's valuable in a career (not just cybersecurity): three pillars now matter more than tactical skill-building — deep understanding of how systems work, a genuine desire for something to be different, and strong AI-collaboration skills.
- Claims AI raises rather than lowers the value of deep expertise, because the further a topic is from someone's expertise, the more convincing AI-generated content sounds — meaning only genuine experts can distinguish correct AI output from confident-sounding wrong output.
- Frames "wanting something to be different" (having opinions, taste, problems you're fascinated by) as the trait AI can least replicate or help with, and warns that having skill without genuine desire/direction is a dangerous position as AI commoditizes skill.
- Reframes job-hunting around "plan your career around problems" — showing up with a specific obsession and built artifacts beats generic credentials or resumes in a crowded applicant pool.
- Argues AI is eliminating the traditional entry-level on-ramp (scaffolding work like tooling, formatting, context-gathering) that used to be how juniors learned the ropes, replacing it with a lower barrier to doing "real" work directly if paired with public building and AI fluency.
- Describes "articulating intent" — the ability to clearly specify what you want done, to the point it's verifiable — as the scarcest and most important skill in an AI-saturated field, tying directly to core security tasks like scoping and threat modeling.
- Credentials/degrees are described as decaying "proxies" for ability, being displaced by public, inspectable work (GitHub, blogs, build logs) as the real signal hiring managers should (and increasingly do) look for.

## Quote

> One of the worst situations you can be in right now is having skills but wanting nothing.
