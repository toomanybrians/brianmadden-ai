# Alert! New forms of diabetes on the rise

## Insights

- Dyson frames "information diabetes" as the substitution of real information/human connection with processed, algorithmically-fed content optimized to trigger emotional reaction rather than understanding — comparing this to ultra-processed food replacing whole food.
- She argues genuine learning is a "recursive process" of incorporating new knowledge into what one already knows, not a transfer of memorizable content — and that current education/testing systems reward memorization, which drives "cheating" as a symptom of a broken incentive system rather than a moral failing.
- AI-enabled "sycophantic bots" are singled out as a distinct harm: they flatter and affirm users, and asking them for honest criticism can trigger shame or destabilization — creating a false "safe space" that removes the friction needed for growth.
- "Money diabete$" is presented as a parallel condition: markets and individuals increasingly price/gamble on things (stocks, sports outcomes, even war/assassinations) rather than valuing them, and people rationalize doing harm for income (cites a Facebook/Instagram worker's "we're basically pushers" admission).
- All three "diabetes" types share a root cause she calls "parasitic" business models — not designed to actively harm users, but to keep them dependent enough to sustain "customer retention" as a revenue engine.
- She critiques a broader cultural obsession with quantifying everything (test scores, follower counts, GDP, wealth) as a "hallucination" that if something's good, more is always better — arguing real life involves immeasurable trade-offs, and that fixing this requires cultural/moral shifts, not just laws or regulation.

## Quote

> Ultra-processed information is designed to make you react, not think.
