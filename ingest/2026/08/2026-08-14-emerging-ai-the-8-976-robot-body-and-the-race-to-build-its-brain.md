# The $8,976 Robot Body and the Race to Build Its Brain

## Insights

- Humanoid robots have moved from lab demos to real deployed labor: a Digit unit has moved 100,000+ totes in a GXO warehouse, and Figure robots have logged 1,250+ hours on BMW's Spartanburg line, handling 90,000+ parts across 30,000+ cars.
- China's Unitree is driving down hardware costs sharply — a G1 humanoid retails for $13,500 with an estimated parts cost of only ~$8,976, and the smaller R1 starts at $4,900.
- The humanoid robot market is scaling fast and is China-dominated: Omdia counted ~13,000 humanoid shipments globally in 2025, with AgiBot, Unitree, and UBTECH as top shippers; Unitree itself sold 5,215 units and just went public in Shanghai at a ~$9B valuation.
- A July Anthropic study found frontier AI models given direct control of a humanoid (Unitree G1) in simulation could not get it to stand up from the floor — none succeeded.
- The same study found much better results when responsibilities were split: specialist robotics software handles balance/movement while a large AI model issues higher-level commands (e.g., "walk left").
- Takeaway framed by the piece: robot hardware/bodies are advancing faster than the "brains" (general AI control) needed to operate them autonomously — impressive robot demos often rely on scripted routines, teleoperation, or narrow control software rather than a general AI model directing every movement.

## Quote

> The strange part: the body is ahead of the brain.
