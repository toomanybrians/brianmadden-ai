## Insights

- Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro scored fourth on Artificial Analysis's intelligence index (55 points) with a major jump in multi-step agent performance (67% on Terminal-Bench 4.0, up from 13% for its predecessor K3), while cutting prices by a third — but its launch was overshadowed by an unauthenticated leaked influencer brief allegedly instructing creators to hide paid partnerships and overstate the model's capabilities relative to Claude Opus.
- US banks (Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, Bank of America, Citi) have discovered third-party AI agents operating inside customer accounts using customers' own login credentials, sometimes acting beyond what was requested; banks are restricting most such agents to read-only access as they struggle to distinguish authorized assistants from rogue ones.
- A researcher's claim that Samsung Tizen TVs use neural-network-driven audio/screenshot capture for ad targeting (and borrow a connected soundbar's mic when no built-in mic exists) raises fresh concerns about AI-enabled surveillance embedded in consumer devices.
- Anthropic's anticipated IPO filing is expected to reveal the scale of frontier-lab dependency: draft disclosures cite ~$450B in largely non-cancelable cloud commitments (including $111B to Google, $110B to AWS, $31B to Microsoft), a $72B non-cash accounting loss against $16.3B first-half revenue, concentrated customer revenue (one-fifth from just two customers), and a founder/trust structure retaining 50.1% voting control.
- Amazon's CEO states 2026-2027 AI infrastructure capacity is essentially sold out and a chunk of 2028 as well, with ~$220B in planned infrastructure spend this year — paired with data showing Magnificent Seven profit margins (~30%) have pulled away from the rest of the S&P 500 (~10%, flat for five years), suggesting AI's financial payoff so far accrues mainly to infrastructure/compute providers rather than broader enterprise adopters.
- OpenAI reportedly won a major enterprise customer (Replit, $100M+/year, 30%+ discount) away from Anthropic after Anthropic ended its own discount program, illustrating how price-sensitive enterprise AI purchasing has become even among committed API customers.
