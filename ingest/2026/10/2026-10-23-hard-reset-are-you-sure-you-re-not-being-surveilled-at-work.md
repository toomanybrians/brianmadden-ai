# Are You Sure You’re Not Being Surveilled At Work?

## Insights

- A coalition report ("Bossware Comes To California") documents AI-powered workplace surveillance tools now spanning healthcare, warehousing, software engineering, janitorial work, sanitation, and hospitality — based on firsthand accounts from 25 California workers.
- Bossware ranges from physical devices (GPS trackers, cameras, microphones, wearables) to software tracking biometric data (fingerprints, facial features, eye movement, gait, voice) and in some cases continues monitoring workers outside work hours.
- California passed four 2026 laws creating initial guardrails: banning unilateral AI firing/discipline without human review (SB 947), requiring disclosure when AI causes layoffs (SB 951), banning AI surveillance in bathrooms (AB 1331), and banning AI tracking of workers' emotional states or neural data (AB 1883) — but the report calls these insufficient.
- A labor advocate argues bossware is deliberately used to pull workers out of judgment/decision-making roles, deskilling labor as "an important stepping stone" toward a "workerless future" regardless of whether the underlying AI actually works well.
- Biometric/facial-recognition-based bossware carries well-documented racial bias, disproportionately harming workers of color and immigrant workers; "surveillance wages" (using predictive analytics to find the lowest pay a worker will accept) is cited as one concrete harm.
- The report's four policy asks: humans (not algorithms) must make decisions about workers with a right to appeal; workers must know when/how bossware is used and can access/correct their data; intrusive/harmful uses should be banned; all workers must have enforceable rights.
- Given unlikely near-term federal action, the coalition is pursuing local/regional regulatory frameworks in California as a model, arguing local fights (citing data center battles) are where concrete worker impact gets addressed.

## Quote

> "I think the purpose of these tools is to deskill, to move towards this workerless future, regardless of whether or not the tech is actually good enough." — Víctor Sánchez
