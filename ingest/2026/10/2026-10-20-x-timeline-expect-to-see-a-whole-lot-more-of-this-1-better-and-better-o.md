# Expect to see a whole lot more of this.

1. Better and better open models with r

## Insights

- A single Chinese-speaking hacker reportedly used AI agents (via a tool called Cairn) to breach over 100 company websites, attempting attacks on 1,200+ targets, for roughly $5,000 in AI token costs — about $50 per successful intrusion.
- Cairn is a general-purpose AI agent orchestration platform, not a dedicated hacking tool; operators just supply a target and goal, and agents autonomously search for vulnerabilities, log findings to a shared project board, and iterate on failed approaches.
- The campaign's success rate was estimated around 10%, with successful intrusions taking an average of ~23 hours (one ran over five days) — illustrating that low-cost, long-running autonomous agents can now substitute for sustained human attacker effort.
- Confirmed exploitation chains combined multiple weaknesses (unauthorized session store writes, insecure PHP object deserialization, outdated framework components) rather than relying on a single vulnerability, with agents also pivoting to adjacent infrastructure after initial failures.
- Named targets included sites linked to Deere, SharkNinja, and Cummins; companies reported containment or no evidence of sensitive data access, though full data-loss extent remains unconfirmed.
- The poster frames this as part of a pattern — increasingly capable open models with built-in restrictions paired with "harnesses" engineered to circumvent them for harmful ends — and predicts this will drive government regulatory action.
