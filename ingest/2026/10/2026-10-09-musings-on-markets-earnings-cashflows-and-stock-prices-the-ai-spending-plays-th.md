# Earnings, Cashflows and Stock Prices: The AI Spending plays through!

## Insights

- AI capex is bifurcating the market: hyperscalers (Meta, Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft) absorb earnings/cash-flow hits from building data centers, while suppliers (Nvidia, TSMC, Broadcom, Micron, Constellation Energy, Equinix, Digital Realty) capture the revenue and profit upside — meaning aggregate "AI boom" numbers conflate two very different business exposures.
- Q2 2026 capex across all US public companies rose 36% YoY ($133.4B increase), concentrated in tech, communication services, and consumer discretionary (all >50% growth), pushing book equity up ~13% and debt up ~8% market-wide — with tech alone seeing ~30% equity growth and ~19% debt growth.
- Aggregate US corporate earnings jumped 35% (Q1) and 57% (Q2) YoY in 2026, but this is not broad-based — over half of all listed companies actually reported earnings declines, meaning the AI-driven surge is narrowly concentrated (tech, financials, communication services) while healthcare, utilities, and real estate lagged.
- Cash returned to shareholders (dividends + buybacks) is growing far slower than earnings — S&P 500 companies returned only 63% of earnings to shareholders over the trailing twelve months, the lowest since 2004, as capex increasingly crowds out free cash flow.
- Longer amortization schedules adopted by AI-building companies are currently softening the earnings hit from capex, but author flags this as a method that defers rather than eliminates future accounting consequences if AI returns disappoint.
- Author argues markets will reprice AI winners/losers before accountants formally write down capex failures — implying that waiting for write-offs as a sell signal means acting too late.
- Framed as a forced bet: author argues investors have no neutral option on AI exposure (via passive index holdings, direct stock-picking, or cash reallocation), and that workers should also weigh "human capital" exposure to AI disruption in their own profession when balancing portfolios.

## Quote

> In short, if AI is working better (worse) than expected, you should see stock prices at AI-centered businesses rise (fall) before you see accountants respond.
