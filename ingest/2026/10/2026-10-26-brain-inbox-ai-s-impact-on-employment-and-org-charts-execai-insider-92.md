# AI’s Impact on Employment and Org Charts (ExecAI Insider #92)

## Insights

- Frames a core tension: the traditional novice-to-expert career ladder relied on junior employees doing tactical work to build judgment, but AI is now absorbing that foundational work, breaking the mechanism by which future managers and experts are developed.
- Poses a specific set of organizational questions as unresolved: how current roles evolve, what new roles emerge, which skills/traits stay uniquely human, how AI agents reshape workflows and org charts, and how middle management and entry-level roles are affected.
- Argues AI's organizational effect goes beyond task automation — it is actively redefining what expertise means and how professionals learn, contribute, and advance within companies.
- Stakes out a normative position: AI transformation should be optimized to "maximize the benefit to people, not just generate greater profits," explicitly framing this as a goal of growing careers alongside companies.
- Calls for leaders to take an active, human-centered stance on AI transformation rather than a wait-and-see approach, tying this to a broader question of what kind of organization and economy to build.

## Quote

> If AI does the work people once learned from, how will organizations develop future managers, experts, and executives?
