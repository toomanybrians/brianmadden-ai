# ChinAI #376: Around the Horn (27th episode)

## Insights

- Chinese AI labs (described as "BAT giants") have reportedly reworked pre-training pipelines over the past six months to address "dirty data" — junk corpus content, ambiguous labeling rules, and flawed evaluation sets.
- A technical trend is emerging in Chinese LLM architecture design: models combining Kimi's linear attention approach in some layers with DeepSeek's sparse attention approach in others, within the same configuration.
- An essay argues U.S. advantage in the AI race isn't compute/chips per se, but hyperscalers' (Google, Microsoft) unique ability to aggregate and build large-scale infrastructure — compute, land, energy, cooling — including internationally (e.g. Netherlands).
- Tencent convened a roundtable on "AI employees" transitioning from pilot deployments to large-scale rollout, drawing on its own WorkBuddy agent as a case study.
- New Chinese draft regulations on minors' internet use would further restrict AI companion/virtual-partner services, banning AI-human "romantic relationships," building on earlier companion-AI rules and citing prior investigations into weak child-protection measures in AI apps.
- Real-world AI assistant performance is drawing consumer disappointment: Chinese travelers using AI travel-planning assistants during the National Day holiday reported being let down by the results.
