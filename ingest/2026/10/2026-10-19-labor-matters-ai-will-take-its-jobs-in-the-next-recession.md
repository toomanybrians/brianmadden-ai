# AI Will Take Its Jobs in the Next Recession.

## Insights

- The argument draws a historical analogy to manufacturing job losses from 1999–2010: despite a strong economy and low unemployment in 1999-2000, BLS projected manufacturing employment would *grow* through 2010 — instead it fell by a third, one of the worst labor forecasts on record.
- Key pattern: ~88% of routine-job losses (not just manufacturing, but clerical/administrative work too) occurred during and immediately after recessions, not during the preceding boom — firms held a "stock" of labor-saving tech/offshoring options but didn't use them while profits were good; recessions gave them "license to cut."
- The piece argues this maps directly onto AI today: firms are sitting on a large, growing gap between what AI models can do and what firms actually deploy, and the absence of major job losses so far reflects forgiving conditions (no recession since ChatGPT, record corporate profit share, low layoff rates) rather than evidence AI isn't a threat.
- Cites early warning signs: a white-collar jobs group (finance, insurance, information, professional/business services — "FIIPB") peaked in April 2023 and has been declining outside of a recession for the first time in decades, now ~4% below its 2005–2019 trend.
- Estimates 1.5–2% of knowledge workers hold AI-created jobs, plus unspecified but "significant" physical-buildout employment (data centers, power, chip fabs) — framed as the job-creation counterweight to watch alongside losses.
- Notes the prior manufacturing shock left lasting regional damage (no employment recovery even 9 years later in exposed commuting zones) and elevated deaths of despair/suicide — raised as the stakes of displacement, not just job counts.
- Prediction: the next recession will do to AI-exposed office jobs what 2001 did to manufacturing — cuts continuing through the recovery that don't come back — and current struggles of young college graduates are flagged as an early warning sign.

## Quote

> My prediction is that the next recession does to AI-exposed office jobs what 2001 did to manufacturing.
