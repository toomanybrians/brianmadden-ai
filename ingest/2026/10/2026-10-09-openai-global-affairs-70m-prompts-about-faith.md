# 70M Prompts About Faith

## Insights

- OpenAI reports more than 70 million ChatGPT requests per week relate to faith and religion: 22M/week on studying religious texts, 16M/week on religious practices/holidays, and 16M/week on beliefs, meaning, and spiritual guidance.
- Usage patterns track religious calendars — Islam-related queries spike around Ramadan, Christianity around Easter, Judaism around Passover — suggesting AI use is woven into the rhythms of practiced faith rather than abstract inquiry.
- Case examples (a pastor, a rabbi, a Hindu educator) show AI being used as a research/preparation aid — exploring interpretations, navigating commentary, examining how doctrine applies to personal decisions — explicitly not as a replacement for clergy, sermons, or spiritual authority.
- OpenAI's framing: AI should expand human judgment and agency rather than displace it, with Chris Lehane outlining three principles — alignment with human intent, transparency about capabilities/limits, and human control over use — and arguing AI governance should involve public/democratic institutions, not just tech companies.
- Separately, OpenAI's AI Sentiment Tracker shows net negative public opinion of AI in the US: a three-month rolling average of -24 (60.3% negative vs. 36.3% positive), indicating sentiment remains persistently skeptical despite capability gains.
