# Last week, we brought together #FSI technology leaders for a roundtable to discu

## Insights

- Financial services IT leaders cite endpoint rebuild time (hours to a full day) as a recurring resilience gap, turning device recovery into a business continuity risk rather than just an IT issue.
- Leaders frame modernization as justified primarily through its resilience payoff: the business case strengthens when simplifying legacy systems reduces dependencies and speeds recovery, not just when it cuts cost.
- AI governance is discussed as inseparable from cloud/platform control — participants want AI investment matched with visibility into where workloads run, who can access them, and how usage is governed in regulated workflows.
- One bank collapsed its access model from ~190 personas to 14, prompting discussion of moving from static, overly granular access control toward more dynamic, role-adaptive application delivery.
- A persistent "measurement gap" was named: organizations struggle to quantify how complexity and operational fragility translate into business impact (productivity, continuity, customer experience), limiting their ability to justify simplification investments.
- Celent research (cited in the discussion) found a link between system availability and customer satisfaction scores, offered as a model for connecting technical performance metrics to business outcomes.

## Quote

> Institutions need visibility into their environments, governance over access and usage, and clear accountability to meet their resilience, security, and regulatory expectations.
