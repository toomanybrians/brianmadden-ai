## Insights

- The site (vulnerabilitydata.io) attempts to separate documented outcomes (breaches, losses, exploit speed) from AI attribution, explicitly noting that most outcome data sources don't record whether AI was involved — so claims of AI's effect on these metrics are currently unverifiable from the data itself.
- FBI IC3 reports $20.9B in 2025 cybercrime losses (up 16x from 2016), but only $893M is tagged AI-related, and that figure is described as a lower bound based only on victims who self-identified AI involvement.
- CrowdStrike's reported drop in average breakout time (98 min in 2016 to 29 min in 2025) is attributed by CrowdStrike to AI-accelerated phishing/recon, but the site notes this is an unverified vendor claim with no measured causal share.
- CVE volume rose 2.9x from 2018–2025 (reaching 48,185 in 2025), but the site attributes most of this growth to more CVE issuers and more complete disclosure practices — not to AI — with no public breakdown isolating AI's contribution.
- Time-to-exploit for disclosed vulnerabilities fell sharply (63 days in 2018 to an estimated -7 days in 2025, per different methodologies), but the site stresses these are timing series, not causal attributions to AI.
- The project catalogs 23 documented AI-assisted attack campaigns, grading evidence quality: only 6 are confirmed by an independent party, 13 rely solely on the model provider's own report, and 4 are asserted without verifiable evidence — with the best-documented AI security cases actually being defensive (bug-finding), not offensive.
