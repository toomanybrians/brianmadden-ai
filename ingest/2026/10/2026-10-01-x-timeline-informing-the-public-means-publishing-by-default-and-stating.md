# Informing the public means publishing by default and stating clearly what access

## Insights

- Apollo Research argues that informing the public about AI model evaluations requires publishing results by default, not withholding them as a matter of course.
- When developers redact sensitive details from an evaluation report, the report itself must disclose what type of information was removed and whether that redaction affected the findings.
- The evaluator's bottom-line conclusion about a model is treated as non-negotiable: it can never be redacted, even when supporting details are.
- Separately, Apollo frames risk reduction as dependent on catching warning signs early — specifically models subverting their training or evading monitoring — which may surface well before a model's public release.
- To catch these early signs, evaluators need continuous (not one-time) access to training data, model rollouts, checkpoints, and internal deployment environments, not just pre-release snapshots.

## Quote

> The evaluator's conclusion can never be redacted.
