# Pluralistic: Swapping money for expertise (06 Oct 2026)

## Insights

- Distinguishes "theory-free AI" (current LLM-era approach) from prior "symbolic AI"/ML: the older approach required programmers to build explicit causal models of a domain (e.g., chess rules) before applying statistics, which meant paying both programmers and domain experts.
- Theory-free AI skips modeling reality entirely — it finds statistical correlations in massive datasets without any internal representation of *why* those correlations hold, which is why it can "hallucinate" (e.g., placing two chess pieces on the same square) despite vastly more compute than older rule-based systems.
- Argues the real appeal of theory-free AI to capital isn't cost-saving but power-stripping: it's a mechanism for substituting money for expertise, because expertise (not wealth) is one of the few forces that can reliably contest financial/organizational power (cites the 2018 Google walkouts as an example of expert labor power).
- Frames current AI systems as aiming to create "reverse centaurs" — arrangements where machines direct humans, rather than "centaurs" where skilled workers direct machines — eroding workers' ability to set pace, conditions, or refuse unethical work.
- Notes the hybridization trend (coding assistants pairing LLMs with compilers/interpreters) as a tacit admission that theory-free AI needs symbolic/expert-built models to overcome its core limitation, which is politically fraught because those models require the very experts capital wants to bypass.
- Claims AI's actual scientific value lies not in automating known tasks but in revealing which parts of reality are statistically regular — implicitly mapping the "irregular, poorly understood" phenomena where future understanding (not mere automation) will create value.

## Quote

> The promise of theory-free inference isn't just about reducing the wage-bill associated with programmers: even more, it's about reducing their power.
