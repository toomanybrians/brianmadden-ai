# Pete Florence: When Robots Improvise

## Insights

- Generalist's CEO frames dexterity (not a specific industry like manufacturing) as the horizontal capability analogous to "coding" for robotics — the general-purpose skill that unlocks broad applicability across use cases.
- Gen 1.5 shows one-shot and few-shot physical task learning (e.g., learning to take money from a wallet from a single demonstration), with modest success rates but notable because they exceed zero at all — described as a "GPT-3 moment" for robotics, analogous to in-context learning but for physical tasks.
- The mechanism behind this emergent one-shot learning may mirror GPT-3's: training data has "burstiness" (similar examples cluster together in sequence), which the model exploits without being explicitly trained for one-shot learning.
- Task difficulty in robotics is multi-dimensional: motion precision, force precision, and bimanual/degrees-of-freedom coordination all compound to determine how hard a task is — not simply a function of data volume.
- Florence argues that once dexterity is cracked, robots become capable of building successor-generation robots, creating a manufacturing scaling loop tied to the machines' own capability rather than human labor.
- Raw data collection for robotics is described as extremely physical/logistically primitive — e.g., flying with backpacks of hard drives because of poor site connectivity, and building custom infrastructure to bulk-upload from memory cards; an hour of training data can vary enormously (even near-infinitely) in value depending on task/context, versus being "fungible" the way more mature datasets are.
- The company explicitly prioritizes simplicity and iteration speed over vertical integration — buying existing arms/cameras where adequate, building custom hands, and resisting "nerd-sniping" distractions — treating this focus as itself a competitive strategy under "The Bitter Lesson" logic (compute/data/scale matter more than bespoke engineering elegance).

## Quote

> If we can really crack dexterity, then the robots can build the robots and the robots can start building next-generation robots.
