# This is not going to be the only area in which AIs beat humans...

Paper: https:

## Insights

- A study (Gary King and Steven Worthington, Harvard) found AI-generated peer reviews outperformed human expert reviews "by every measure" they constructed, using 124 Harvard social science papers spanning statistical analysis, formal models, and qualitative work.
- The human reviews used as the comparison baseline weren't average — they were well-compensated experts whose reviews were independently judged high-quality by journal editors and authors, raising the bar for what AI beat.
- The model used was Opus 4.8, evaluated across a deliberately broad range of methodological traditions rather than a single narrow discipline or method type.
- The authors frame this as forcing a policy question for academia: journals need to decide how to adapt the practice of peer review and science itself to AI capability, and note both concerning downsides and potential upsides (faster publication, fewer adversarial interactions, better experience for editors/reviewers/authors/readers).
- The poster (Ethan Mollick) frames this result as one instance of a broader pattern — AI exceeding human performance in domains beyond this one.

## Quote

> By every measure, Opus 4.8 beats human experts as peer reviewers in a study of 124 Harvard social science papers.
