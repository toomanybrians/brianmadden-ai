# OpenAI’s enterprise AI problem: The hard part starts after the model

## Insights

- OpenAI, Anthropic, AWS, and Microsoft have all converged on the same strategy: embedding specialized "forward deployed engineers" (FDEs) inside customer organizations to drive agentic AI adoption, treating this as a billion-dollar bet.
- OpenAI's stated intent is explicitly not to create long-term dependency — the FDE goal is to transfer capability so customers can build future use cases themselves, rather than retaining engineers as permanent fixtures.
- Model capability is estimated to account for only "about 20% or less" of the enterprise deployment gap; the bulk of the challenge lies in organizational factors — data management, permissions, evaluation practices, and how engineering teams are structured.
- Example deployments cited include John Deere (turning machine data into farmer guidance), an oil and gas company (building agents with result-verification mechanisms), a semiconductor firm (shifting from siloed AI applications to a shared agent architecture), and a Japan-based COBOL-to-Java migration project with SoftBank.
- The framing positions FDE work as distinct from traditional consulting, suggesting a new category of hands-on technical deployment labor is emerging specifically around agentic AI rollout.
- A core thesis of the piece: better underlying models will not eliminate the need for this deployment-layer work, since the bottleneck is organizational readiness, not raw model capability.

## Quote

> We want to help our customers build the capabilities so that they can build the next use cases themselves.
