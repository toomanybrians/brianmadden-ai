# 💸 Anthropic's First Step Toward Universal High Income

## Insights

- Anthropic will commit 5% of its shares (up to ~$100B at its expected ~$2T IPO valuation) to a new Public Benefit Fund, with the board able to add up to 2% more for every 50% rise in company value above a baseline.
- Counting co-founders' pledge to give away 80% of their equity plus an employee donation match, Anthropic says roughly 20% of the company is now earmarked for public benefit.
- The fund may make direct distributions to specific populations, such as workers whose jobs are disrupted by AI, but Anthropic explicitly frames this as not a universal basic income.
- The fund is structured as a wholly-owned LLC (not a charity), so Anthropic retains control; its board, recipients, and valuation baseline are still unannounced ("coming months").
- Critics (e.g., David Sacks) note the pledge is real but governance is opaque — no outside party yet knows who decides where the money goes; Elon Musk countered that shares should go directly to the public, coining "universal high income."
- Context for the move: co-founder Jack Clark told IMF/World Bank officials evidence points to a "singularity," citing an internal Anthropic model projecting US GDP reaching $44 trillion by 2030 (from ~$30T today).
