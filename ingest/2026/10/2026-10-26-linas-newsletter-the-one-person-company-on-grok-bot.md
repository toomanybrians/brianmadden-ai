# The One-Person Company on Grok Bot 🤖

## Insights

- A three-person team from SpaceXSI used Grok Bot to build and ship a game (Thursday Arena) from scratch in 72 hours, generating 400+ merged pull requests, ~2,000 day-one players, and a top Google ranking — but $0 in revenue, even though a bot was tasked all day with making money.
- The experiment's main finding: bots can build, test, ship, support, and market a product quickly, but they can't decide what's worth building, can't make people care, and can't close a sale — described by team member Roshan Sadanani as the hardest problem even with unlimited AI.
- A proposed "one-person company" model: every recurring business function (customers, product, support, launch) is owned by a dedicated bot operating from a defined source of truth, with a hard boundary it can't cross without founder approval.
- Under this model, the founder's irreducible jobs are three: deciding what to build, selling it, and saying no.
- Recent Grok Bot upgrades cited as accelerants: Team Bots, a proactive "Primary Bot," read-only bank data access, purchases with approval, a dedicated email address, and an October underlying-model switch to Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 — each reducing the need for early hires while raising the stakes of misconfiguration.
- The piece frames two uses of AI agents: most founders will use bots to do the same work faster, while a smaller group will use them to eliminate whole functions and redirect human time to higher-value work.

## Quote

> A bot that had been told to spend the whole day making money also made nothing.
