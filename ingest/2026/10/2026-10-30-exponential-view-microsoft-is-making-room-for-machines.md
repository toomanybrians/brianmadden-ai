# 🤖 Microsoft is making room for machines

## Insights

- Microsoft offered a voluntary exit to ~60,000 employees (27% of 223,000 staff) across US, UK, Canada, and India, open even to vice presidents and spanning tenure from the Ballmer era to recent hires.
- Azhar rules out the three common explanations: capex pressure (spending headed to $170B from $23B five years ago), AI directly replacing headcount, or Covid-era hiring bloat — arguing the facts don't fit any of them.
- Covid hiring (58,000 added in two years to mid-2022) was real, but Microsoft has since cut 25,000 jobs and held headcount flat for four years, suggesting that bloat was already absorbed before this offer.
- Since 2022, revenue growth has outpaced headcount growth every year, with the gap hitting 20% (the widest on record) in the year to June; revenue per employee is up two-thirds to $1.5 million.
- Azhar's inference: the exit offer isn't about cutting dead weight but reflects confidence that an already highly productive company can run even leaner going forward — a forward-looking bet rather than a cleanup of past excess.
- The piece frames the real question as what Microsoft anticipates about future productivity/operations that justifies paying real severance costs to shed a quarter of its workforce.

## Quote

> A firm with its healthiest margins in history is not carrying 60,000 unproductive passengers.
