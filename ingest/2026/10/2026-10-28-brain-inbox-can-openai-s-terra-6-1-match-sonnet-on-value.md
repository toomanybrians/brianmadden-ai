# ⚙️ Can OpenAI's Terra 6.1 match Sonnet on value?

## Insights

- Anthropic's IPO filing shows enterprise concentration: 11,900 customers generate $100K+ in trailing revenue, ~1,900 generate $1M+, and five customers account for 25% of total revenue — enterprise dependency with real customer-concentration risk.
- Net revenue retention of 900% indicates existing enterprise customers are expanding Claude usage roughly ninefold year-over-year, suggesting deep account penetration rather than just new-logo growth.
- Anthropic's revenue growth (15x YoY to $16.3B in H1) sits alongside an $80.7B reported loss, though most of that is non-cash (convertible notes/SAFE fair-value changes) — actual operating loss is $693M, a more modest figure.
- OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Terra is explicitly positioned as a mid-tier, cost-optimized model for agentic/computer-use tasks at enterprise scale, undercutting Anthropic's Sonnet and OpenAI's own higher-tier models on price while improving accuracy and reducing error rates (53.6% → 4.3% in internal factuality tests at max effort).
- The piece frames mid-tier/cost-efficient models as the "sweet spot" for enterprise agentic adoption — the compute/cost tradeoff is treated as the key gating factor for scaling agents into high-volume business workflows, not raw capability.
- Separately, Brilliant Labs' Halo glasses push local, privacy-preserving on-device processing (no cloud retention of raw audio/video) as a differentiator, including an enterprise mode letting companies plug in their own agents — signaling enterprise interest in on-device/controlled AI deployment outside the major cloud labs.

## Quote

> "Anthropic is an enterprise machine, with the vast majority of its revenues being tied up with some of the world's largest enterprises."
