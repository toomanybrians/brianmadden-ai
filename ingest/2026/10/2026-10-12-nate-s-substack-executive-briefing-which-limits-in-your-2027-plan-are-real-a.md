# Executive Briefing: Which Limits in Your 2027 Plan Are Real, and Which Are From the Last Time You Tried?

## Insights

- Personal software (people building small custom apps/tools for specific work needs) is becoming widespread, and the arrival of Meta's "Muse" agent (described as carrying out work across browser and connected apps) has accelerated this activity further.
- A shift is underway where AI agents are now handling tasks that people previously solved by "vibe coding" small personal apps themselves.
- The piece argues capability expansion from AI has outpaced people's willingness to attempt new things — the bottleneck is psychological/behavioral, not technical access.
- Proposes five interlocking skills — taste, agency, focus, resourcefulness, follow-through — that develop together through doing real work: taste motivates action, agency applies judgment before full information exists, focus exposes hard parts, resourcefulness builds learning through difficulty, and follow-through reveals what actually serves the original intent.
- Argues people's sense of "what's possible for me" is shaped by past experience and tends to persist as outdated assumption even after circumstances (e.g., new AI tools) have changed — a core framing for reassessing self-imposed limits.
- References supporting case studies (teased, not detailed in this excerpt): a Reuters journalist building an AI tutor for her son, and Stardew Valley's solo developer cutting a working system — both used to illustrate how first attempts and sustained focus change judgment and capacity.

## Quote

> AI has expanded what we can do faster than many of us have changed what we're willing to attempt.
