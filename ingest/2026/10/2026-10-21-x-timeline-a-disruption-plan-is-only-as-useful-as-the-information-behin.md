# A disruption plan is only as useful as the information behind it.

When data is

## Insights

- Frames supply chain resilience as fundamentally an information problem: disruption plans are only as good as the connected, real-time data available to execute them.
- Distinguishes scenario planning (identifying risks, assigning ownership) from execution-readiness (having timely, connected information to act) — argues most organizations have the former but not the latter.
- Cites survey data showing the scale of the visibility gap: only 20% of shippers report full visibility across regions/transport modes, and only 13% of businesses have full visibility into sourcing networks.
- Argues fragmentation across teams, systems, and partners (suppliers, logistics, regulators, customers) means disruptions aren't detected or connected to downstream impact until they've already spread.
- Introduces "observability" as a CIO-level concern distinct from data/app access — monitoring whether the applications, networks, and workflows underlying supply chain operations are actually functioning, since a slow app can delay decisions even with accurate underlying data.
- Positions this as a vendor pitch (Citrix) for centralized visibility/observability tooling as the mechanism connecting planning to execution.

## Quote

> Resilience depends on more than having a plan. It depends on knowing what is happening, understanding what it means, and coordinating the right response quickly.
