# The Attention Bottleneck 🧠

## Insights

- Anthropic's October 2026 report ("How Employment is Changing at Anthropic") claims AI agents now log 6 hours of work for every 1 hour logged by employees, based on internal telemetry (agent logs, commits, calendars, Slack networks, hiring scorecards).
- Engineering productivity reportedly rose sharply: code commits per engineer up 5.1x over six months, with no corresponding increase in reverted or deleted code.
- Junior technical hiring at Anthropic dropped significantly, from 27% to 9% of technical hires, suggesting a shrinking entry-level pipeline.
- Despite heavy AI agent usage, agents reportedly sit idle roughly 80% of session time waiting on human input — framed as evidence that human attention/judgment, not raw labor, is the new bottleneck.
- Hiring managers are now reportedly weighting judgment and autonomy above domain expertise when evaluating candidates.
- The newsletter positions Anthropic as a "leading indicator" for the wider economy since it operates ahead of other companies on frontier model workflows, and notes its own deeper analysis cross-checks this data against Sequoia, a16z, and Boris Cherny's prior commentary — including a claim that it contradicts the popular narrative that AI companies are hiring more juniors.

## Quote

> AI agents spend about 80% of their session time idle, waiting on a human.
