# Bank of America just put the first number on what defending against AI costs a bank 🤖💸; OpenAI’s London staff cost £2.3M each 😳🇬🇧; Citi joins JPMorgan in blocking AI Agents 🤖🏦

## Insights

- Bank of America hired roughly 1,000 contractors to patch security flaws that Anthropic's "Mythos" AI model exposed in decades-old bank and vendor code, with patching reportedly lagging behind the pace attackers can exploit.
- These contractors reportedly don't appear in BofA's officially falling headcount numbers, meaning the human cost of AI-driven security remediation is being obscured in standard workforce metrics.
- BofA's defense spending (described as "a couple hundred million plus" for Q3) is blended together with anti-money-laundering (AML) fixes, making the true standalone cost of AI-related defense difficult to isolate from investor-facing figures.
- The piece frames this as complicating the bank's own narrative about AI-driven efficiency savings, since defense costs are rising alongside touted AI productivity gains.
- OpenAI's UK subsidiary reported a £20.3M "profit" for 2025, characterized as essentially a fee set by the US parent company rather than organic UK earnings, with UK turnover said to move in tandem with OpenAI's overall valuation.
- OpenAI's London staff reportedly cost approximately £2.3M each, offered as a data point on the real price of frontier AI talent.
- Citibank is now following JPMorgan in blocking AI agents from accessing customer accounts; the piece suggests banks and regulators are aligned on this stance, though the full reasoning is reserved for the full article.
