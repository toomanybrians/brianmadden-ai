# Different Pathways to the Same End

## Insights

- Sparked by Jacob Coxon (27-year-old Anthropic researcher) publicly walking away from unvested equity after four months, alleging the company's safety promises were "decorative text on a slide deck"; Evan Hubinger (ex-OpenAI/Google) backed his claims, prompting rival AI CEOs to jointly call for "slowing down" development.
- Argues "slowing down" is performative cover, not real change — model releases continue at the same pace, meaning the same trajectory toward risk just plays out over a longer timeline ("five years to drive us into the impact zone instead of three").
- Claims the debate over AI-extinction probability functions as a distraction/smokescreen that lets both tech companies and individuals avoid addressing the actual broken systems in front of them, while feeling like accountability is happening.
- Draws an organizational-culture parallel: companies claim to want principled innovators who challenge the status quo, but often quietly reward compliant "mercenaries" for speed and ego-stroking, which produces fragile cultures that lose their best people under pressure.
- Suggests fixation on macro-level AI doom scenarios serves as an emotional escape hatch, letting people externalize anxiety onto an uncontrollable hypothetical instead of doing the "unglamorous work" of fixing immediate, manageable problems (e.g., personal habits, broken systems, hard conversations).
- Frames the real near-term risk for leaders as organizational "drift" (referenced from a prior article) rather than a sudden catastrophic AI event.
