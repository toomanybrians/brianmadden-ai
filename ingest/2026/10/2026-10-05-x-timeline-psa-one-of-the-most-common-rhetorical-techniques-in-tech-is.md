# PSA: one of the most common rhetorical techniques in tech is to start with somet

## Insights

- Identifies a specific rhetorical pattern in tech discourse: pairing a plausible near-term claim with an implausible long-term claim, then treating both as equally credible.
- Uses a concrete example: AI capex rising to $4 trillion (called plausible) versus AI doubling global GDP within a decade (called "almost zero chance").
- The critique is aimed at the framing/presentation technique itself, not just this one instance — positioned as a general pattern to watch for ("one of the most common rhetorical techniques in tech").
- Referenced claim originates from Anthropic's Sholto Douglas, who projected AI capex growth from $1T to $4T by 2028 and GDP-doubling effects by the early 2030s, citing recent historical compute scaling (2-3x per year).
