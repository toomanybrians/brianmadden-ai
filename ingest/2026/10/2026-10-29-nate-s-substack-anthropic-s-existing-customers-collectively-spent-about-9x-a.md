# Anthropic's existing customers collectively spent about 9x as much as a year earlier. Why your first AI pilot is a poor guide to your next budget.

## Insights

- Anthropic's S-1 shows existing customers' spend grew ~9x year-over-year (quoted as ~900% net revenue retention), even though average spend remains under 1% of total expenses among publicly traded qualifying customers — suggesting large headroom for budget growth rather than saturation.
- Customer count at the $100K+ revenue tier grew from ~950 to more than 11,900 year-over-year; growth is broadening rather than concentrating, with non-top-five customers rising from ~64% to ~79% of quarterly revenue.
- Because ~80% of first-half revenue is consumption-based, spend tracks actual usage closely — demonstrated by a ~20% two-week revenue drop during the 2025–2026 holiday period attributed to code freezes and reduced activity.
- Case study: Novo Nordisk's NovoScribe system (Claude 3 + other models) cut clinical study report writing time by 90% and has since expanded to device protocols and patient materials; expansion speed varied heavily depending on how unstructured/legacy the underlying data was.
- Anthropic is moving beyond being a tools vendor into direct competition with customers' industries — acquiring a drug-development startup (Mithril Medicine) and hiring for life-sciences corporate development — raising the question of which capabilities a customer should build in-house versus buy, given a supplier that may later compete with them.
- Efficiency gains cut both ways: Opus 5.5 reportedly reduced customer cost-per-task ~40% versus Opus 5, while Anthropic's own revenue-per-dollar-of-compute more than tripled year-over-year — model efficiency can expand usage (more affordable tasks) even as it compresses per-task price in both directions of the value chain.

## Quote

> Anthropic reports roughly 900% net revenue retention: existing customers collectively generated about nine times as much revenue in the September quarter as they did a year earlier.
