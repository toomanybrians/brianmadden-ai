# 💊 Anthropic Is Now in the Drug Business

## Insights

- Anthropic's Claude is being used across the drug-development pipeline (target selection, molecule design, manufacturing QA, clinical-trial planning), with six external partners and one acquired biotech now running Claude-managed programs; its first in-house drug candidate is targeted for human trials as early as late 2028.
- Concrete productivity claims: Claude redesigned a CAR T-cell therapy component (70% shorter, ~10x more receptor output), generated ~35,000 antibody candidates in a month for a food-allergy drug, and at Enveda cut manufacturing-document review time by 89% (one regulatory draft went from ~3 months to 4 hours) while catching errors human reviewers missed.
- Apple is embedding an AI assistant (built on its own models plus OpenAI's) into Pages, Keynote, and Numbers for drafting, slide design, and chart/table generation, and adding Model Context Protocol support so third-party AI agents can directly read/edit office files — a shift toward agent-native document formats (e.g., Keynote's new .keyx).
- A new benchmark study ("Memory Tax") found that dedicated AI memory systems often perform worse than simply searching raw chat history — the best memory system hit 84% accuracy and the worst 63%, versus 90% for plain two-pass search — while costing far more compute (hours vs. minutes) to build, because summarization/compression destroys retrievable detail.
- Infrastructure economics are becoming a visible lever: Nebius found that splitting a GPU server into eight independent model copies yielded 42.5% more usable video output per hour than one large distributed copy, at ~30% lower cost per second, but with much higher per-clip latency — a throughput-vs-responsiveness tradeoff relevant to scaling AI-generated media/training data.
- Updated independent benchmarking (Artificial Analysis v5) now weights 60% of its intelligence index toward private, untrainable test sets, and shows top closed models (Claude, GPT-6 variants) pulling further ahead of best open-weight models (gap grew from 3 to 12 points) — suggesting a widening frontier/open-source capability split.
