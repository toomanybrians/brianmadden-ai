# We see this as a starting point and expect the methodology to evolve. We welcome

## Insights

- Apollo Research proposes "embedded evaluators" — third parties given employee-like access inside frontier AI companies to training, evaluation, and deployment processes, as a mechanism to catch loss-of-control/scheming risks early.
- The framework shifts from overall subjective verdicts to claim-based assessments: specific, pre-agreed claims (e.g., "models never attempted to disable monitoring") are each given one of five ranked verdicts (Verified, Unrefuted, Self-reported, Evaluator-found, Blocked).
- Evaluators would get standard claims (common across all developers, enabling comparison) plus developer-specific recommended claims drawn from safety frameworks, past incidents, or emerging risks.
- Key fairness/incentive design: developers can rebut findings publicly but cannot veto an evaluator's verdict; redactions are allowed only for security/legal/commercial reasons and must be flagged via "meta-transparency," never to hide unfavorable findings.
- Apollo argues voluntary commitments are likely insufficient long-term and advocates embedded evaluation eventually becoming a legal requirement, with interim leverage from insurers, boards, and public scrutiny.
- The model draws explicitly on auditing practices from other high-risk industries (e.g., nuclear power), including staff rotation across companies to prevent evaluator-developer collusion.

## Quote

> We believe purely voluntary commitments are unlikely to be sufficient to ensure these incentives.
