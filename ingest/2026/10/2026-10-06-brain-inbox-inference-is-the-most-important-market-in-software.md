# Inference Is the Most Important Market in Software

## Insights

- AI inference spending is projected to grow from ~$25B (2025) to ~$130B (2026) to ~$350B (2027), overtaking the database market (forecast at $161B in 2026, $190B by 2027) — making inference the largest software-infrastructure category.
- This shift turns applications into "inference resellers": usage-based inference consumption costs are poised to exceed platform/seat-license fees, disrupting sales comp plans, customer budgeting, and revenue forecasting.
- Classic SaaS gross margins (~72% blended) are expected to compress unless companies build proprietary "harnesses" to cut token overhead, or model-price competition outpaces constrained GPU supply.
- Bring-your-own-key (BYOK) arrangements let vendors retain higher software margin but shrink total contract value, since customers supply their own compute/model credentials.
- Capability-adjusted token costs fall ~47% per quarter, yet frontier model list prices have still spiked (e.g., $0.96 to $11.25 over 18 months before a new model reset pricing to $4.00) — price pressure and price escalation are happening simultaneously.
- Infrastructure/inference cost is becoming a dominant share of COGS for software companies, potentially over half of revenue, pushing firms toward smaller models, better harnesses, and smarter routing to manage costs.

## Quote

> That crossover makes inference the most important market in software: a structural transformation mutating every application into an inference reseller.
