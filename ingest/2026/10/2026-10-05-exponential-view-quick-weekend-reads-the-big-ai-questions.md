# 🔮 Quick weekend reads: the big AI questions

## Insights

- The Economist frames effective altruism (EA) as "this century's biggest idea" but warns that some Silicon Valley EAs believe only they can be trusted to handle powerful AI — a concentration-of-trust problem the piece calls alarming.
- The EA argument for AI risk centers on fear that powerful AI systems could optimize for a single, catastrophically wrong objective — but the same single-objective trap can afflict the small group of people controlling the technology.
- Alexander Berger, CEO of Coefficient Giving (an EA-aligned philanthropy), published a brief response to the Economist's critique, suggesting the EA framing is contested even within the movement.
- Anthropic has reportedly consulted religious scholars and theologians on questions of AI consciousness and personhood, prioritizing this question more than other AI labs.
- Anthropic's team reportedly considered withdrawing from a Vatican event ahead of a papal encyclical ("Magnifica Humanitas") that explicitly denies AI systems have experiences, bodies, feelings, or moral conscience.
- Anil Seth's commentary on AI consciousness drew public pushback, indicating the topic remains contested among researchers.

## Quote

> AIs "do not undergo experiences, do not possess a body, do not feel joy or pain… nor do they have a moral conscience."
