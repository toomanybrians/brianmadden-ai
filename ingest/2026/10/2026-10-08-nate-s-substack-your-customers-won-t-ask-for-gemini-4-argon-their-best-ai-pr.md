# Your customers won't ask for Gemini 4 Argon. Their best AI product idea probably isn't in their feature requests either. Four questions surface the work they gave up on.

## Insights

- The piece frames "customer obsession" as a more useful benchmark for AI products than model-level benchmarks (e.g., Gemini 4 Argon's Vals Index results), arguing that headline model performance doesn't tell you what a specific customer will actually be able to do with it.
- Proposes watching where customers delegate tasks to AI and tracking the failures in that delegation — each failure point reveals where the customer is still doing manual work themselves, which is a signal for product opportunity.
- Suggests looking specifically for work people have "given up on" — tasks customers stopped expecting any tool to handle — as a distinct and underused source of product ideas, separate from stated feature requests.
- Argues that the best product ideas don't show up in direct customer feature requests; customers generally ask for incremental versions of what already exists (the "Gemini 4 Argon" framing), not for the thing they've abandoned hope of getting.
- Advocates letting a customer's actual life and context (not just a text-box interface) shape product design, using a "forest walk" analogy to argue that products should extend beyond conventional AI chat interfaces.
- Offers three evaluative questions for builders to apply to their own work: whether they understand whose problem they're solving, and what they've changed in the product after directly observing a customer attempt to use it.

## Quote

> There is useful intelligence available today that we have barely begun to turn into thoughtful products.
