# Manhattan’s Office Jobs Stayed. Its Lunch Crowd Didn’t.

## Insights

- Manhattan office attendance has plateaued at 72-76% of pre-pandemic levels (spring 2024 to March 2025), and the broader recovery of commuter-dependent businesses stalled in 2023 rather than continuing to close the gap.
- Office-sector jobs themselves didn't leave Manhattan — finance/insurance share is up 8% vs. 2019, computer systems design up 7%, legal and information up 3-4%, with these four industries growing from ~612,000 to ~666,000 jobs (+9%). The disruption is in attendance frequency, not job location.
- Businesses tied to daily in-person presence near offices (lunch spots, dry cleaning, gyms, pharmacies) show sustained share losses: dry cleaning -25%, lunch/limited-service restaurants -18%, gyms -16%, pharmacies -18% versus 2019 share, with lunch-spot losses still deepening since 2024.
- Author frames this as hybrid work (people in office 3-4 days/week) producing a structurally smaller weekday service economy rather than a temporary dip still recovering — spending likely shifted to home boroughs/suburbs on remote-work days instead of disappearing.
- Residential-facing businesses (salons, retail, grocery, bars) are still gradually recovering share, suggesting a split between "commuter economy" (flat/declining) and "resident economy" (slowly rebounding) within the same geography.
- Some lunch-spot job loss may reflect delivery apps/ghost kitchens relocating food-prep jobs to cheaper boroughs even when the customer is in Manhattan — a confound the author flags but can't isolate.

## Quote

> The office jobs are still in Manhattan. The big change is how many days a week the people holding them come in.
