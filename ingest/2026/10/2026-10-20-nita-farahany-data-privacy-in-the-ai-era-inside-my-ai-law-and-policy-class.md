# Data Privacy in the AI Era (Inside my AI Law and Policy Class # 15)

## Insights

- An OpenAI claim about AI agents solving Navier-Stokes sparked accusations from mathematicians that their private chat data (fed into Codex/Claude) may have been used for training; OpenAI's response ("the model doesn't look up user data") dodged the actual training-data question — illustrating that companies can't or won't confirm what personal data entered training.
- Legal/regulatory analysis (EU Opinion 28/2024) concludes AI models can contain personally identifiable information, but remedies are impractical: you can't cleanly delete a person's data from a trained model, and deleting the whole model isn't realistic either — the practical fix is cleaning/de-identifying data before training, especially for narrow domain models (e.g., a hospital-system-specific model) rather than large general-purpose ones.
- OpenAI launched ChatGPT ads (Feb 2026) and can target users based on prompts, email, phone number, or device identifiers — chats people assume are private are usable for ad targeting.
- Senate testimony (Ryan Calo, July 2024) frames AI as intensifying three existing privacy harms: insatiable data demand, AI's ability to infer intimate details from mundane/available data, and worsened information/power asymmetry enabling commercial exploitation.
- Research point raised in class: targeted manipulation is much more effective at reinforcing behavior aligned with a person's existing values/goals than at reversing them — so the larger AI-persuasion risk may be disengagement/demobilization (e.g., suppressing voting) rather than flipping core beliefs.
- Policy options discussed: shifting from the GDPR's source/category-based privacy model toward a harms-based model (analogous to GINA for genetic data); Stanford HAI proposals for opt-in (not opt-out) data reuse, mandatory training-data transparency, and privacy-by-design system architecture; and a "data fiduciary/intermediary" model where a trusted party must approve secondary uses of a person's data, with open questions about who oversees and funds such fiduciaries.
- Court history example: in NYT v. OpenAI, a federal magistrate ordered OpenAI (May 2025) to preserve all output log data it otherwise would have deleted; that blanket preservation order was terminated by joint motion in October 2025 — underscoring uncertainty about what chat data is actually retained, deleted, or used after the fact.

## Quote

> AI fuels an insatiable demand for consumer data. — Ryan Calo, testimony to U.S. Senate Committee on Commerce, Science, & Transportation
