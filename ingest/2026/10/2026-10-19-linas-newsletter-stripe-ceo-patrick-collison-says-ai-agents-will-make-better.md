# Stripe CEO Patrick Collison says AI agents will make better products win 🚀

## Insights

- Patrick Collison (Stripe CEO) argues companies have historically acted as "the original superintelligence," exploiting an "amortized cognitive surplus" against inattentive, underinformed buyers.
- His thesis: personal AI agents close that information/attention gap, which shrinks revenue models built on inattention (checkout upsells, forgotten subscriptions, paid placement) and increases the payoff for genuinely better products.
- Supporting evidence cited: ChatGPT reaches 1.2B+ weekly users with ad revenue hitting a $1B annualized run rate; AI-referred shoppers convert 60% better than other visitors on US retail sites; a federal appeals court lifted an injunction blocking Perplexity's shopping agent from buying on Amazon.
- The newsletter identifies three limits to Collison's thesis: (1) agents can only reward quality that's legible in structured data, verified reviews, and outcome records; (2) agents carry their own biases, notably a strong preference for the first offer presented; (3) an agent's loyalty may depend on who pays for it — e.g., ChatGPT shows ads only on its Free and Go tiers.
- Additional evidence referenced (not detailed in excerpt): experiments showing AI agents booking the same Manhattan hotel in up to 100% of sessions, a Netflix-scale experiment on whether agents converge on the same choices for everyone, and Amazon continuing to block rival shopping agents post-ruling.
- Framed as relevant to anyone whose revenue depends on customer inattention or whose growth depends on being discovered by AI shopping agents.

## Quote

> Companies are "the original superintelligence," turning "amortized cognitive surplus" against buyers.
