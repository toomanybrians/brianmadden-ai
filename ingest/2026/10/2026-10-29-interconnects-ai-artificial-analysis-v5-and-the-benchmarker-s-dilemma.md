# Artificial Analysis v5 and the benchmarker's dilemma

## Insights

- Independent AI benchmarking faces a structural paradox: reducing model capability to a single aggregate score is necessary for usability but inherently distorts a highly "jagged" and fast-moving capability landscape.
- Artificial Analysis's v5 Intelligence Index reweights categories (knowledge work, coding, science, general) and shows 8 closed models beating the best open-weight model (Kimi K3.1 Pro), widening the open-vs-closed gap compared to prior versions — a sign the previous index may have overrated open models.
- Open models lag most on "science" evaluations (e.g., terminal-bench-science, CritPt v2) but were never designed to compete at the absolute frontier; their value lies in commoditizing high-demand inference and enabling specialization/ownership of intelligence inside companies.
- A pattern across model cycles: top open models benchmax modestly and are narrower in capability coverage, while weaker open models benchmax heavily but get little attention — a gap partly explained by less sophisticated RL stacks and distribution-matched training environments.
- AAv5's shift toward private/hidden-prompt benchmarks is defensible as an anti-benchmaxxing measure but sacrifices community scrutiny that normally surfaces and fixes benchmark flaws.
- Frontier labs like OpenAI and Anthropic have largely stopped optimizing for public indices, focusing instead on internal R&D automation and large paying customers, while smaller/mid-size labs depend existentially on looking competitive on these indices.

## Quote

> The benchmarking industry comes down to trust.
