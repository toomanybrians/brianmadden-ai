# ⚙️ How US open models are closing the gap with China

## Insights

- Anthropic is losing three major revenue sources simultaneously: the Pentagon formally ceased using Claude after a supply-chain-risk blacklisting, while Microsoft cut its expected ~$1B internal Anthropic spend by over a third and Meta halved its Claude Code seats (60,000 to ~30,000), partly due to layoffs and partly a deliberate shift to internal tools.
- The article frames the real competitive threat to frontier labs (Anthropic, OpenAI) as not each other but the rising attractiveness of open models and efficiency-focused neocloud architectures — once large enterprises wean off a frontier provider, switching costs make them unlikely to return.
- Controllability/data trust is cited as a second driver of enterprise pullback: companies like Meta and Microsoft don't want to route deeply integrated workflows and proprietary context through an outside vendor, especially a competitor.
- Mistral's new 1-trillion-parameter open-weight model (ML4, "le Chonk") is pitched explicitly as a hedge against vendor lock-in — enterprises get a full copy of model weights so they aren't exposed if a provider deprecates or limits a model, which Mistral argues matters most for latency/token-heavy use cases like cybersecurity.
- Google (EmbeddingGemma 2, a small on-device multimodal embedding model) and Reflection (Beam, a 500B+ parameter open coding/agentic model claimed to be far cheaper than comparable open models) both launched to grow the US open-model ecosystem, positioned against the fact that most open-source AI development is currently concentrated in China.
- Enterprises are increasingly weighing three tradeoffs when picking models: cost/efficiency, control/data sovereignty, and security — with open-weight models gaining ground specifically because enterprises can inspect, retain, and run them independent of a provider's continued support.

> "You cannot afford to be vulnerable to the fact that the model you are using to protect yourself might disappear one day." — Guillaume Lample, Mistral
