# ⚙️ Why we make music matters more than how it sounds

## Insights

- Multiple experts (Cella, Prey, Das) argue AI can make people "good enough" at creative output but not virtuosic — there's a claimed gap between AI-assisted competence and genuine mastery that comes from sustained practice.
- A composer source frames AI parity with human creative output as undermining the "specialness" of human creation — likened to "playing against a gray entity" you can't beat, which saps motivation to create at all.
- Argument that the democratization framing around generative AI tools (lowering skill barriers) may conflict with the value of struggle/failure in developing mastery — raises the question of whether frictionless creation blunts drive toward expertise.
- Distinguishes technical/output quality of art from its emotional function — the claim is that people value music for the feeling and personal meaning it carries, not its technical polish, which is framed as a distinct thing from what AI replicates.
- Cites a Management Science study (May, US/China universities) finding LLMs trained on AI-generated data perform worse than those trained on human-created data, even with mitigation techniques (multi-model outputs, quality filtering) — framed as evidence AI creative tools need continuous human-made input to avoid degrading.
- A Google DeepMind product lead (working on Lyria) states the design intent for AI music tools is to generate "emotional resonance" rather than regurgitate past work, suggesting an industry framing of AI as augmentation rather than replacement.

## Quote

> "It's like playing against a gray entity that I will never be able to win against." — Carmine-Emanuele Cella
