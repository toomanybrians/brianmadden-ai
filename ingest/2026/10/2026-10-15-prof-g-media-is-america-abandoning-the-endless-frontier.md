# Is America Abandoning the Endless Frontier?

## Insights

- The Trump administration has cut, delayed, or disrupted nearly $200 billion in federal research funding across 17 policy sectors and 21 agencies, with NSF grants down 45% and NIH awards down 13% from five-year averages.
- Federal research funding is being reallocated away from basic/long-shot science toward AI, quantum computing, biotech, and advanced materials — fields already receiving trillions in private investment, raising concerns about an overconcentrated national research portfolio (echoing stock-market-style concentration risk).
- In 2025 alone, health research funding dropped $1 billion while machine learning/AI funding rose $468 million and networking/IT funding rose $368 million; the White House has announced plans to redirect $200 billion toward AI initiatives.
- New political review processes — including an LLM-based "computational text tool" screening grant applications for 235 banned terms — have slowed or blocked research, including work related to minority health disparities (e.g., $800M in NIH grants cut over mentions of minorities).
- A researcher source warns the deeper damage is a "break in trust": universities grow hesitant to invest in research areas due to funding uncertainty, eroding long-term national scientific capacity beyond any single grant cut.
- Historical examples (GLP-1 drugs, histotripsy/HistoSonics, GPS, stealth tech) illustrate how federally funded "undirected" curiosity research — not guaranteed near-term ROI — has historically produced outsized economic and medical breakthroughs over decades.

## Quote

> "In the big picture, we're undermining the United States' broad scientific capacity." — Noam Ross, Grant Witness
