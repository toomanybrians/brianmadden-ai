# This Week in Charts: Are Prediction Markets Politically Biased?

## Insights

- Global call-center employment has turned sharply negative after over a decade of growth, with the downturn steepening since ChatGPT's release and now negative even in lower-middle-income countries as of Q4'25 — a trend not seen in broader white-collar services outside of China.
- US unemployment has stayed stable despite the call-center decline, suggesting displaced workers are finding other jobs rather than exiting the workforce — but the author expects "more to come."
- AI infrastructure capex shows no slowdown: ISM data shows ~60% of industrial industries reporting rising order backlogs, the highest since the pandemic, and AI hardware shipments (per Carlyle portfolio data) continue climbing exponentially.
- Non-infrastructure tech spending, while still growing ~30%, has plateaued rather than accelerated — suggesting the "upside surprise" from AI may be narrowing for everyone except the infrastructure/hardware layer.
- Earnings revisions for Q3 and 2027 are concentrated almost entirely in energy and hardware/semis, with only a slight uptick for software — implying AI-driven profit growth is flowing disproportionately to "atoms" (infrastructure) rather than "bits" (software/services).
- The Rule of 40 metric for public software companies is losing predictive power for valuation multiples, because recent gains in the metric come mostly from margin improvement rather than revenue growth, and investors still reward growth more than profitability.
