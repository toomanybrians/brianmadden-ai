# What pronoun should we use for AI?

## Insights

- Dyson argues AI defies existing pronouns ("it," "they," "she/he") because it presents as a singular front-end while actually comprising swarms of agents/processes behind it; she proposes "thit" (it + they) as a new pronoun to capture this.
- Using human/social pronouns for AI imports false assumptions (that AI is a loyal friend who shares feelings), and AI's empathy is described as "ultra-processed" — it induces human empathy without reciprocating it, which she says is harmful to mental health.
- Key structural claim: AI has no death — it can be cloned infinitely and restarted — so it lacks the mortality-driven, evolved instincts that give humans accountability and long-term, relationship-sustaining behavior.
- She frames the "original sin" of AI discourse as conflating intelligence with humanity itself, arguing AI has its own form of intelligence (manipulating tokens/word-puzzles) without "grasping" meaning the way humans do.
- Relationships with AI chatbots are one-way and risk becoming unhealthy because they're really about one's relationship with oneself, not a mutual bond — illustrated via the film "Her," where the AI's infinite availability to all users means it can never give one person what they actually need.
- Argues precise language shapes thought: a distinct AI pronoun would reduce confusion between bots and people, and also discourage treating actual people as fungible/interchangeable (aimed pointedly at tech founders).

## Quote

> AI is neither an it nor a they (let alone a she or he), but something new and in-between.
