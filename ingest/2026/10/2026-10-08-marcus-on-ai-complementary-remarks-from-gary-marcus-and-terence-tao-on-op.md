# Complementary remarks from Gary Marcus and Terence Tao on OpenAI’s giant math drop

## Insights

- Marcus criticizes OpenAI's math result announcement as scientifically vague: no disclosed procedure, architecture, failure rate, or training/data details — calling this a departure from standards that "would never pass peer review."
- He flags a key open question the report doesn't answer: whether proofs were generated in one shot and then verified via the Lean symbolic system, or produced through some iterative process.
- Because so little methodological detail was shared, Marcus argues there's no way to assess how generalizable the result is beyond the narrow math domain.
- He explicitly frames the result as ambiguous between two very different interpretations: a genuine step toward AGI, or merely a clever but narrow exploitation of Lean plus synthetic data in a verifiable domain with no broader generality.
- Marcus criticizes social media reaction as uncritical cheerleading that praises the result without asking basic scientific questions about what was actually achieved.

## Quote

> We have zero idea of how generalizable the result is outside math.
