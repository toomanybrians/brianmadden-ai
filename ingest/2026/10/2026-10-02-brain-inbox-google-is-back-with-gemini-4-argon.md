# 💎 Google Is Back With Gemini 4 Argon

## Insights

- Google released Gemini 4 Argon, its first model above the budget "Flash" tier in over seven months (after reportedly scrapping a planned Gemini 3.5 Pro), with output limit raised from 64K to 1M tokens to support long, multi-step reasoning tasks.
- On Google's internal benchmarks Argon leads or ties on 14 of 19 tests against GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable 5.1, and Claude Opus 5.5; independent tester Artificial Analysis scores it 53 — level with GPT-6 Astra but behind Anthropic's Opus 5.5 (58) and Sonnet 5.5 (56).
- Argon has the lowest hallucination rate among leading models on Artificial Analysis's knowledge test (15% vs. 51% for Astra), though its raw answer accuracy on that same test is lower than Astra's at standard pricing.
- Some Google employees with hands-on access reportedly say Argon underperforms its benchmark scores in real work — citing uneven coding ability, weak front-end design, and "benchmaxxing" (tuning for test scores over real tasks); Google disputes the characterization.
- Access is tightly restricted at launch — limited to trusted cyber-defense testers and select partners, with paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers next in line (introductory pricing $2/$10 per million input/output tokens).
- Separately, OpenAI says it disrupted a coordinated campaign (tied to people associated with Kimi-maker Moonshot AI) that tried to extract and decrypt its models' hidden chain-of-reasoning to train rival models — illustrating how contested "frontier" technical advantages are.
