# ⚙️ Anthropic’s audit push tests AI transparency

## Insights

- Anthropic will give a six-person METR team "employee-like access" to its hardware, office space, and internal tools to independently assess frontier model risks, with results to be published by METR.
- Anthropic defines four requirements for credible third-party evaluators: frontier model expertise, financial/operational independence, "enough access" to models and training methods, and freedom to publish findings.
- A concurrent Reuters report claims Anthropic and OpenAI are simultaneously seeking to control what evaluators can publish publicly, to prevent IP exposure — potentially undercutting the transparency the audits are meant to provide.
- Current AI safety commitments (evaluator access, "morally binding" pledges, White House Accord on Super Intelligence) remain voluntary and honor-system based, with no mechanism forcing labs to act on findings.
- Separately, Meta and Nvidia released new open-weight models (Muse Spark Open, Nemotron 3.5 Super VL) aimed at enterprises seeking cheaper, on-premise alternatives to proprietary APIs from OpenAI/Anthropic.
- Amazon entered the AI wearables market with Bee Frontier/Eclipse, undercutting rival Plaud on price and leaning on Alexa ecosystem integration for always-on conversation capture and summarization.

## Quote

> "We want to be publicly accountable for the decisions we're making... But we can't publicly share all the information relevant to our safety work." — Anthropic
