# Pluralistic: Critique and writing (23 Oct 2026)

## Insights

- Doctorow argues the core value of a peer writing workshop isn't getting feedback on your own work — it's the discipline of critiquing *other* people's unfinished, imperfect work, which builds critical instincts that transfer to one's own writing.
- He reports that some beginning writers now use LLMs as editorial feedback generators on finished drafts, valuing the suggestions for revision.
- His counterargument: LLM feedback only ever plays the "get notes on your own work" role, which he considers the less valuable half of the workshop process — it can't replicate the skill-building that comes from analyzing someone else's flawed material.
- He frames a writing career as building a body of work through repeated practice ("write the next thing"), not perfecting single pieces — implying tools that optimize single-draft revision address the wrong bottleneck.
- Notes that even the author himself wouldn't use a chatbot for this kind of feedback, and if used at all, favors local/offline models over "foundation models" he associates with environmental and political harms.
- Broader point: writing is unusually opaque among creative disciplines (unlike music, visual art, dance) — practitioners rarely observe each other's actual process, which shapes why peer critique substitutes for that missing visibility in ways an AI tool doesn't.

## Quote

> Having an editor-on-demand of highly variable (and often dubious) quality might help you improve your writing, but it's no substitute for participating in a peer workshop.
