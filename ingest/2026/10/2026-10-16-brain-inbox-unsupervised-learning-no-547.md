# Unsupervised Learning NO. 547

## Insights

- The author argues AGI has effectively already arrived in 2026 via "Claude Tag," defined not by abstract reasoning benchmarks but by a persistent, organization-wide AI memory that can read all emails, meetings, Slack, and customer interactions, then offer to take over an average knowledge worker's entire output (reports, code, documentation) at a fraction of the time — and scale that offer to an entire team.
- This AGI framing is explicitly tied to replacing "average" knowledge workers (data/business analysts, generalist coders) rather than top performers, whose tacit, hard-to-articulate skills are seen as harder to replicate; the piece predicts resulting layoffs or reduced rehiring after attrition, concentrated where management/company quality is already weak.
- A key feature noted: these agents work invisibly inside existing tools (Slack/Teams via "@Claude") rather than as a separate "AI app," removing the friction of context-switching to a chatbot interface.
- Separately, the newsletter describes AI-driven content/software replication becoming near-costless — citing a hypothetical where a AAA-scale game could be cloned or remixed in days for a tiny fraction of cost — with human attention (visibility, marketing, distribution) proposed as the remaining scarce constraint once production costs collapse.
- Anthropic's internal code-merge volume reportedly jumped from ~2,500/day to over 30,000/day as agents took on engineering work, forcing them to build a custom version-control system because git couldn't handle agent-scale throughput — offered as a concrete sign of AI-driven labor/tooling shifts inside a real engineering org.
- Other voices cited: Nikita Bier argues technical moats are largely gone, leaving proprietary data, network effects, or physical assets as durable advantages; Alexandr Wang frames the new scarcity (once intelligence/building are cheap) as clarity about what you want the world to look like in 5–10 years.
