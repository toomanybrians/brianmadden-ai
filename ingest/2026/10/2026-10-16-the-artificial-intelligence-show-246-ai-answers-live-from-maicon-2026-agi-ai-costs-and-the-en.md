## Insights

### Adoption and governance
- A speaker argues capable AI stalls in "ordinary organizations" not on capability but on permissions, usage limits, and organizational trust — change management (IT, legal, procurement, HR) has to be deliberately brought to the table, not bypassed.
- Example given: connecting Claude to the company's CRM system reportedly unlocked "thousands" of new use cases across marketing, sales, and customer success that were blocked while the tool operated in isolation.
- The speaker says scaling bottom-up ideas in a small organization still runs through the CEO: a proposed idea gets executive buy-in, then legal is told to make it work within 48 hours — leadership override, not pure employee autonomy, is what unblocks adoption.
- A separate thread describes "AGI horizon teams" (a concept the speaker says they pitched to SaaS companies in 2023): a standing team whose job is to track 12-18 months of model advancement and map the staffing/product implications, as the best available substitute for a fixed roadmap.

### Model volatility and cost
- A speaker claims Opus/Astra-class frontier models will cost "10x less in 12 months," and that organizations should expect to settle on a "good enough" model and build around it rather than chasing frontier releases.
- The same speaker says a four-day span surfaced two unplanned product changes (Google's Gemini agent-harness rebrand and the Argon model going public) that could have invalidated material already built for a live workshop — offered as evidence that vendor volatility, not model capability, is the practical planning risk.
- GPTs (OpenAI's custom-instruction product) are being deprecated with roughly two months' notice; one organization built an entire marketing team's workflows and ran hackathons around GPTs and now has to migrate everything.
- Recommended migration path: audit existing GPTs by actual usage, then route each into whichever current OpenAI construct fits — a repeatable task becomes a "skill," ad hoc personal use becomes a "project," with no guarantee either survives the next naming/product change.

### AGI definition and unemployment
- A speaker's working definition of AGI: parity with the "B player" on a team — a model given the same 20 tasks as an average staffer and judged on whether output is comparable. Under that definition, the speaker claims this threshold was already crossed roughly a year ago for most marketing tasks.
- The speaker predicts that within 12 months, models will reach "A player" level across most departmental tasks given the right data and skilled use — and argues this already satisfies what AGI originally meant, even though industry debate continues.
- On whether AI can solve the unemployment it creates: the speaker says AI labs are betting that future superintelligence will retroactively fix problems created along the way (drawing a parallel to data-center emissions promises), but there's no equivalent to a provable math benchmark for economic policy — you can't just "throw compute at it."
- The speaker claims the current U.S. administration is unwilling to acknowledge AI's job impact for political reasons, citing a White House-adjacent figure's public pushback against job-loss narratives as evidence the policy conversation (UBI, universal basic services) isn't being seriously engaged.
