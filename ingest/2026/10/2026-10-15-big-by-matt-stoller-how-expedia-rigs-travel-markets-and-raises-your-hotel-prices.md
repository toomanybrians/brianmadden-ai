# How Expedia Rigs Travel Markets and Raises Your Hotel Prices

## Insights

- Major hotel chains face lawsuits alleging algorithmic price-fixing via third-party AI/analytics tools (e.g., SAS Institute's G3 Revenue Management System, Cendyn's "Rainmaker"), which automate pricing decisions framed as driving "incremental revenue."
- Hotels using these pricing algorithms reportedly accept the system's recommended price roughly 90% of the time, effectively delegating core pricing decisions to a shared third-party system across competitors.
- This pattern is described as a shift from traditional collusion ("smoky back rooms") to pricing coordination "concealed in code," which makes anticompetitive behavior harder to detect and prosecute.
- Legal doctrine (the Twombly standard) makes it difficult for courts to scrutinize how these algorithms actually function, since plaintiffs can't easily obtain details pre-discovery — one major case (Gibson v. Cendyn) was dismissed on this basis while a similar case against Caesars was allowed to proceed.
- The piece frames the proliferation of these algorithmic tools as part of a broader "multi-level enshittification" dynamic (consolidation, platform-enforced price parity, algorithmic coordination, and junk-fee deception) across industries, not unique to hotels — drawing parallels to Amazon's and Expedia's price-parity enforcement mechanisms.
- Antitrust scholar Herb Hovenkamp is noted as "scrambling to make sense of" the rise of what the piece calls "cartel management services" — a term for these algorithmic coordination tools.

## Quote

> Price fixing has always been patently illegal... but today, price fixing conspiracies are concealed in code.
