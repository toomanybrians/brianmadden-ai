# AI Experts Largely Agree on Which Policies Most Reduce Catastrophic AI Risk

## Insights

- Across four distinct forecaster groups (general AI experts, superforecasters, AI risk experts, and the general public), there is wide disagreement on baseline catastrophic AI risk by 2050 (estimates range from 1.6% to 5.2%), yet strong convergence on which policies would help — suggesting policy consensus doesn't require risk-estimate consensus.
- The policies forecasters believe would most reduce catastrophic risk (international pre-release authorization body, US-China bilateral slowdown, strict liability) are consistently rated as least likely to actually be implemented by 2030 or 2050.
- Federal preemption of state AI laws is the only policy all groups believe would increase catastrophic risk, yet it's rated as the single most likely policy to pass (26% by 2030, 40% by 2050) — largely because it would remove state-level "floor" standards companies currently comply with.
- Bilateral/multilateral (US-China) versions of policies are consistently forecast to reduce risk more than unilateral US-only versions, but are also seen as less politically feasible.
- A bundled policy package (US-China slowdown + international pre-release authorization + strict liability) is forecast to cut catastrophic risk by over 70% among experts, but is given only a 1% chance of being implemented.
- Splitting respondents by EA-affiliation showed virtually identical policy preferences and risk-impact beliefs, indicating the findings aren't an artifact of effective-altruism-aligned thinking.

## Quote

> The policies most expected to reduce risk were also the ones that respondents thought were least likely to be implemented.
