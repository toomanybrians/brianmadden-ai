# Monopoly Round-Up: Goodbye AI Bubble, Hello Real Economy

## Insights

- Three linked signals point to the AI bubble deflating: falling "neocloud" stocks (e.g., CoreWeave down 24% in a week, bonds in distressed territory), hot wholesale inflation (6.3%), and new Chinese export controls on key tech/materials (solar, biotech, chips, AI) that could de-industrialize parts of US manufacturing and pharma.
- Neoclouds are characterized as shell companies leasing servers rather than real businesses — many pivoted from bitcoin mining — with a circular financing scheme where firms like CoreWeave buy chips from investor Nvidia, then raise capital at inflated valuations.
- CoreWeave's three co-founders cashed out $3.3B in stock since IPO (over $2.4B in 2026 alone), framed as a "rug pull" where insiders profit while public investors absorb losses.
- Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's public optimism about CoreWeave was followed by an additional 11% stock slide, suggesting investor confidence in AI buildout returns is cracking even at the systemic level.
- The "ex-AI index" (S&P 500 minus AI companies) outperformed the full S&P 500 by 4.8% in one week — the largest spread since 2024 — signaling a broader rotation away from AI investment.
- Structural economic exposure to a potential AI-driven crash is historically high: 62% of Americans own stock, and equities make up 48.2% of individual/non-profit financial assets (vs. 22.5% in 1945, an all-time low of 10.7% in 1984), making a bubble pop more dangerous to ordinary households than in past eras.
- Argues the AI bubble's biggest cost is opportunity cost: political and media attention is consumed by financier concerns (stock valuations) while more consequential developments — like China's export controls threatening real industrial/manufacturing capacity — go largely unnoticed.
