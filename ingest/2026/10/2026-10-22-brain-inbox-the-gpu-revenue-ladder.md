# The GPU Revenue Ladder

## Insights

- A "GPU revenue ladder" compares per-card-hour economics: DeepSeek's theoretical max ($38, best-case single cluster), spot market price ($8.77), SpaceX's negotiated contract price ($5.27), and SpaceX's actual realized revenue ($2.44) — each rung roughly 40-77% below the one above.
- SpaceX's CFO claims new compute deployments are paying back in under a year; modeling in the footnotes shows this depends heavily on assumptions (after-tax basis, bonus depreciation timing benefits that shorten payback without changing lifetime economics).
- Two opposing forces are in tension: GPU rental prices doubled in six months ($4.40 to $8.08/card-hour) while inference token prices collapsed much faster (one benchmark cost fell 377x in 18 months) — meaning margin per card-hour depends on throughput/efficiency gains outrunning price cuts.
- A structural demand-side shift is emerging: Qualcomm reports AI companies want 100B-parameter models running locally on phones by 2028, which would route inference away from cloud GPUs entirely.
- Despite deflationary pressure on token prices, supply constraints (idle GPUs awaiting power, >60% of planned 2027 datacenter capacity not yet under construction per JPMorgan) suggest demand may outstrip capacity, keeping the inference business model healthy.
- All per-card-hour figures in the piece are explicitly flagged as estimates/derivations — no company publicly discloses this metric directly.

## Quote

> At current pricing, new compute deployments are generating a payback of less than a year.
