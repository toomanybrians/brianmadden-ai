# TG-AI-F: Meta and Nvidia’s deep pockets push US open models forward

## Insights

- Meta released Muse Spark Open, described as "the most capable US open-weight model to date," designed to run entirely on two Apple M5 Ultra Mac Studios — enabling local deployment without cloud infrastructure.
- The Meta/Apple pairing is mutually commercial: Apple gains a selling point for high-end Macs, while Meta gets distribution of its models into developer workflows — illustrating how open models monetize indirectly through hardware/ecosystem sales rather than direct licensing.
- Nvidia's new Nemotron model targets business agents, with smaller "Bolt" models built to run locally on Nvidia-powered machines — reinforcing Nvidia's strategy of using open models to lock customers into its hardware/software ecosystem.
- Both companies' open-model moves are framed as core business strategy, not altruism — open weights function as a demand driver for chips, computers, and services rather than as a product in themselves.
- These releases partially address a concern raised at the "Open Frontier" gathering (July): that if closed US labs dominate the frontier, China could become the default supplier of open models globally — a dynamic researchers there called a democratic and national security risk.
- Sustaining a US open-model ecosystem is described as still dependent on "deep pockets" — i.e., only well-capitalized corporations can currently afford to subsidize open releases at scale.
- A related podcast (Matt White, Open Intelligence Foundation / ex-Linux Foundation AI CTO) reportedly covers lessons from China's compute-constrained researchers, calls for universities to get a bigger share of "GPU wealth," and Washington's understanding of open AI — noted as adjacent content, not detailed in this piece.

## Quote

> What happens if the US frontier is dominated by closed labs while China supplies the open models everyone else builds on?
