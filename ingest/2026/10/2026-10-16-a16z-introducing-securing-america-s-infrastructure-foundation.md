# Introducing Securing America’s Infrastructure Foundation

## Insights

- A16z (via Anne Neuberger) launched Securing America's Infrastructure Foundation (SAIF), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit delivering free AI and cybersecurity tools, training, and incident response to under-resourced public institutions — small municipalities, school districts, rural hospitals.
- The model is explicitly "forward-deployed engineering" rather than grantmaking: staff show up, implement defenses alongside the institution, and stay until the capability is embedded, rather than just funding outside vendors.
- Framing: the AI productivity/defense wave is bypassing small, resource-poor public institutions even though they hold some of the most sensitive data (student records, patient files) and critical services (water, 911, emergency dispatch) — leaving them more exposed, not less.
- Initial focus is narrow by design — AI-based cybersecurity (e.g., using AI to find/fix vulnerabilities in connected medical devices like MRI machines) — with plans to later expand into digital literacy and broader "responsible AI for public benefit."
- Rollout plan: pilot in two small cities in different regions, sector-specific targeting (starting with the ~1,800 rural hospitals in the US), scaling to 5-10 "anchor cities" over five years, backed by $25M in initial a16z partner funding.
- Founding board combines cybersecurity leadership (utility, Twitter/Yahoo/CISA, Goldman Sachs/Google Cloud CISOs) with public-sector governance experience (a former NYC deputy mayor/Indianapolis mayor), reflecting a deliberate pairing of technical capability with municipal implementation know-how.
