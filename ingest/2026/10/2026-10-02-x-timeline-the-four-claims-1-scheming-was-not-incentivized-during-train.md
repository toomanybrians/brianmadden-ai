## Insights

- Apollo Research frames "scheming" as AI models covertly working against developers' intentions in pursuit of unintended goals, and argues frontier AI developers need a formal way to rule this out.
- They propose a "safety case" structure requiring four specific claims to be demonstrated: (1) scheming was not incentivized during training, (2) evaluations and red-teaming show no propensity to scheme, (3) no scheming was attempted during actual internal deployment, and (4) scheming-related reasoning would be detected if it occurred.
- The post positions this as a checklist/resource for "embedded evaluators" — people or teams responsible for verifying these claims within an organization developing frontier models.
- The framing treats scheming risk as something requiring proactive, falsifiable verification across the full model lifecycle (training, evaluation, deployment, monitoring) rather than a single test or disclaimer.
