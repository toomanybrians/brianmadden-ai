# 📈 Monday data: More AI, more justice?

## Insights

- Large numbers of defendants lack legal representation: 60% in defended English/Welsh county court claims this year, and over 90% of US consumers sued for debt.
- AI tools are beginning to help self-represented litigants build legal arguments, potentially closing some of this access-to-justice gap.
- A concrete case: Australian computing academic Greg Baker used AI to contest his employer's refusal to make his casual role permanent and won at the Fair Work Commission, without a lawyer.
- Legal work remains a small share of AI usage: only about 0.6% of Claude usage in May was lawyer-related tasks, ranking 38th out of 718 occupations — above accountants but well behind programmers and writers.
- Most of that legal-related AI usage (four-fifths) was people asking about their rights or seeking clarification of what the law means, rather than complex legal drafting.
