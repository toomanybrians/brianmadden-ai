## Insights

- Gary Marcus testified before the NYC City Council on AI policy, framing it as cities and states stepping in where federal regulation has stalled (referencing the Trump administration's reluctance to regulate AI due to ties with tech companies).
- He cited concrete harms attributed to current AI systems: tens of thousands of hacking incidents enabled by general-purpose agents with internet access, a surge of deepfake-based propaganda and nonconsensual pornography, and chatbots implicated in suicides and a mass murder.
- His central policy ask: an "FDA-like" independent regulatory review regime for AI, where developers must demonstrate benefits outweigh risks before market access — a position he says he's held since 2023 Senate testimony with no federal action resulting.
- He frames NYC's proposed "third-party validation" bill as a chance for the city to become a regulatory leader akin to California's role in clean energy standards, compensating for federal inaction.
- He also pushed for whistleblower protections, referencing recent reports that OpenAI may have fired employees for whistleblowing.
- Notably, he downplays extinction-risk narratives ("I rather doubt that human extinction is imminent") while still asserting AI carries serious, demonstrable risks — a middle position between doomerism and dismissal.
