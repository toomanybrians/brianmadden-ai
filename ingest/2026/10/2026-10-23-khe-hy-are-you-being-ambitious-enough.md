# Are you being ambitious enough?

## Insights

- Felix Rieseberg (Claude Cowork PM) frames AI ambition as four dimensions: Speed (same work faster), Range (drawing on multiple fields/expertise), Depth (adopting expert personas and working from raw data/methods rather than pre-digested material), and Altitude (operating at a higher level of abstraction toward the "goal beyond the goal").
- Altitude is positioned as the highest-leverage dimension: instead of asking AI to complete a narrow task, ask what outcome the task ultimately serves (e.g., not "fix crashes" but "keep the app crash-free"), using a "so that" chain to find the real goal.
- A proposed diagnostic for fit: if a task will generate recurring meetings or feed a decision/process, it's strong-fit for altitude-climbing; purely creative or one-off work benefits less and may favor Depth/Range instead.
- For Depth, a suggested prompting technique asks the model to identify what a top professional would do unprompted, what raw/undigested source material they'd start from, what specific tools/methods they'd use, and what independent evidence (not the model itself) would validate the result.
- The piece includes a full worked example: a consulting firm asked to train clients on building "Claude skills" could escalate scope by screening for skill opportunities, mining chat history for repeatable workflows, auto-generating skill templates, building rubrics, and setting up evaluations — described as "5x more value" from the same mandate.
- Durable AI workflows (meant to replace repeated re-tasking) require explicit data access, a feedback loop for verifying success, a trigger for starting runs, defined human checkpoints, and changes to existing work processes — outside verification (never self-graded by the model) is treated as essential at every level.
