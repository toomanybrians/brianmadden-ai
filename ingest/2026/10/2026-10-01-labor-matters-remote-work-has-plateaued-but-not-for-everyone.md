# Remote Work Has Plateaued. But not for Everyone.

## Insights

- National work-from-home rates have been essentially flat for two years (22.3% of workers doing any WFH, 15.4% of hours worked at home as of Aug 2026), masking sharp divergence within subgroups.
- Federal workers saw the steepest decline — any-WFH share fell from 42.5% to 22.4% between Jan 2025 and Aug 2026 — tracking a January 2025 federal return-to-office directive; state/local government workers showed no comparable shift.
- STEM occupations (computer/math, science, engineering) are losing remote work fastest among private-sector groups, with hours worked at home dropping from 39% to 35.5%, even though computer/math workers still do the most remote work of any occupation group.
- Young college graduates (22-24) lost remote work access much faster than older graduates — a 4.2-point drop in at-home hours vs. 2.6 points for 25-34 year-olds and no change for 35-44 — widening the age-based remote-work gap.
- The authors' hypothesis: remote work functions as an employer concession tied to labor-market leverage, not mere policy preference — tight-labor-market groups (managers, lawyers) retain it while slack-market groups (new grads, tech workers) lose it.
- Framing shift: remote work isn't disappearing overall, but is becoming stratified by career stage, occupation, and employer type rather than declining uniformly.

## Quote

> Remote work isn't going away. What has changed is who gets to do it.
