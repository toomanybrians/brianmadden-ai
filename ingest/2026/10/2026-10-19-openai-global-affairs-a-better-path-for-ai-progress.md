# A Better Path for AI Progress

## Insights

- OpenAI frames its policy stance around three principles articulated by Sam Altman at the UN Security Council: AI must remain under human control (alignment, correctability, pace-matched safeguards), progress should be judged by what people can actually do with it, and the technology's purpose is to expand human agency rather than position AI companies as the protagonists.
- The piece explicitly rejects a binary framing of the AI debate ("push ahead without guardrails" vs. "stop altogether"), positioning OpenAI's approach as a deliberately harder middle path.
- Cities Deliver, a $50M joint initiative with The Rockefeller Foundation, funds AI-driven public-service projects (public health, economic mobility, environmental resilience, civic engagement) via $500K–$2M grants, aiming to reach up to 100 million residents; existing examples include dengue forecasting in Cali, government communication tools in Cape Town, and flood-alert/emergency-response integration in Rio de Janeiro.
- OpenAI reports over 1 million public-sector ChatGPT seats, driven partly by a $1-per-agency-per-year offer to federal agencies, now expanded to waive license fees and discount usage for federal, state, local, and tribal governments.
- Pennsylvania's year-long ChatGPT pilot reported employees saving an average of 95 minutes/day on tasks like policy navigation, drafting, and paperwork, while the pilot's report stressed that human judgment and experience remained essential to the work.
- Other cited data points: 92% of educators in OpenAI's AI Skills Jams left with usable material (lesson adaptations, simulations); weekly AI agent use among EU small/medium businesses in OpenAI's sample rose over 700% since May.
