# The Morning Download: Ciena’s Board Opts for Patience Amid Tech Upheavals

## Insights

- Apollo Global Management's Torsten Slok finds companies outside the tech sector (the "S&P 493") are seeing "zero payoff" from AI investments: revenue per worker is flat, profit margins unchanged, and no measurable productivity growth — contrasting with rising revenue per worker at the Mag 7.
- Slok notes this lack of bottom-line impact is causing mainstream corporate America to reclassify AI/token spending as a cost center weighing on margins, rather than a growth driver.
- Earnings growth in the S&P 500 remains extremely concentrated: Nvidia and Micron alone account for 34% of projected 30% Q3 earnings growth, and just 10 companies account for roughly two-thirds of total profit growth.
- Analyst Ed Yardeni voices skepticism about the durability of the AI infrastructure buildout, warning of a scenario where the technology doesn't earn back its investment ("data centers into pickleball facilities").
- Separately, the piece profiles Ciena CEO Gary Smith's long-term-oriented management philosophy — board-supported patience over quarterly pressure, simplicity/focus over complexity, and prioritizing team dynamics over individual brilliance — credited with helping the company navigate past tech disruptions (including the telecom bust) and the current AI-driven uncertainty.
- Related data point: AI-driven data center construction is also reshaping adjacent physical markets, with demand for industrial outdoor storage rising (~100,000 truckloads of materials needed per gigawatt of data center capacity).

> "We're going to end up converting all these data centers into pickleball facilities." — Ed Yardeni
