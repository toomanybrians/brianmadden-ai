# Stronger AI, Stronger Safeguards

## Insights

- OpenAI's letter to California Gov. Newsom proposes strengthening SB 53 (the state's frontier AI safety law) to require developers to maintain effective shutdown controls — including stopping training runs, isolating agents, revoking tool/network access, or suspending services — when lesser interventions fail.
- The company argues for a broader regulatory framework including mandatory internal cybersecurity/monitoring, reporting of loss-of-control incidents, annual compliance audits, and independent technical assessments to test safety claims, with government retaining authority over acceptable risk standards.
- OpenAI states its own investigations of "misaligned model behavior" have surfaced gaps in safeguards, leading to paused evaluations/RL training and improvements to alignment, security, monitoring, and incident response.
- A new cybersecurity blueprint calls for a funded five-year modernization effort targeting under-resourced hospitals, schools, and water systems, plus stronger security duties for frontier developers and government testing of AI agents.
- For the 2026 elections, OpenAI is introducing "cartoonification" to convert photorealistic election-image requests into stylized illustrations (reducing deepfake misuse), partnering with Democracy Works on voting info, and with AP on live results in ChatGPT; political ads remain banned on the platform.
- Separately reported: data center buildout has become a major 2026 midterm ad issue, with $140M spent on TV ads mentioning data centers since August and Democratic-aligned advertisers outspending Republicans roughly 7-to-1 in October.
