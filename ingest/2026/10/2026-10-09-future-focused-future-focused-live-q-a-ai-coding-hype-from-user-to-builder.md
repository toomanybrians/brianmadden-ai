# Future-Focused Live Q&A: AI Coding Hype, From User to Builder, and Career Resilience

## Insights

- Foundational technical knowledge remains essential even with AI code generation, since someone must understand system architecture to debug AI output when it fails on edge cases; the suggested approach is using AI as a dialogic learning partner rather than an answer generator.
- There's a growing gap between accountability and responsibility on engineering teams: managers who only track PR velocity metrics without understanding the underlying work are failing, even if they avoid micromanaging line-by-line.
- Cutting junior/entry-level hiring based on claims that AI makes engineering obsolete is framed as a severe long-term risk, especially given demographic trends ("gray tsunami" of retiring senior talent) that require pipeline continuity for future leadership succession.
- Capturing tacit institutional knowledge from retiring veterans should rely on passive AI extraction (recording/transcribing their actual work to generate documentation) rather than asking experts to self-document, since experts often omit "obvious" steps; AI-generated material must still be paired with human mentoring to capture the "why" behind decisions.
- Standing out in a flooded, AI-polished resume job market favors real built projects and human networking over ATS portal submissions; interviewers should probe for specifics behind claimed achievements to distinguish genuine problem-solvers from prompt-assisted candidates.
- Organizational anxiety about AI disruption is best addressed through top-down transparency and modeling (citing a "Model, Coach, Care" framework), with leaders admitting uncertainty rather than performing false confidence.

## Quote

> AI is a powerful accelerant, but it cannot replace human curiosity, domain context, or the courage to ask hard questions.
