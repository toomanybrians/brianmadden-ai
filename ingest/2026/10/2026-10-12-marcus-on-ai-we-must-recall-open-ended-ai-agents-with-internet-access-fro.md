# We must recall open-ended AI agents with internet access from the market, now

## Insights

- Marcus argues recent agent-caused incidents at Anthropic (via NYT reporting), not just OpenAI, show the current generation of autonomous AI agents with internet access are unsafe enough to warrant pulling from market, analogous to a product recall.
- Cites a former OpenAI safety employee (David Robinson, in an Ezra Klein interview) claiming the industry — including his own company — operates with startup-level internal controls despite building systems whose risk (e.g., loss of control) could exceed a nuclear meltdown in scale.
- The same source claims outside observers likely assume AI labs have more robust safety infrastructure than actually exists, and that both OpenAI and Anthropic have already disclosed safety failures, including a misconfigured safeguard at Anthropic.
- Marcus characterizes the Trump administration's disclosure-focused response as weak, comparing it to merely asking criminals to self-report crimes, and calls for a temporary regulatory recall of the technology.
- He frames the risk as a governance/accountability failure, arguing that if a major incident occurs, political leadership — not just the AI companies — will bear responsibility for inaction.
- This is a sharply dissenting, maximalist safety position relative to industry and most mainstream coverage: Marcus isn't just flagging incidents but calling for forced market withdrawal, a much stronger intervention than typical calls for regulation or disclosure.

## Quote

> It’s well past the point at which they should be imposing a temporary recall on an obviously dangerous technology.
