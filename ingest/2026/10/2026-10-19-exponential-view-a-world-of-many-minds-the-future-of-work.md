# 🔮 A world of many minds & the future of work

## Insights

- Azhar distinguishes three possible futures for AI architecture: "monocognitism" (a single dominant superintelligent AI), "polycognitism" (multiple powerful but separate AI systems), and "syncognitism" (collective intelligence emerging from interactions among many diverse AI systems).
- He argues monocognitism is unlikely partly due to physics — a singleton AI can't meticulously control distant systems because of speed-of-light communication delays (e.g., a 1.3-second one-way lag to the Moon).
- Polycognitism faces similar control problems as monocognitism: distant, multiple AI systems would still end up operating autonomously rather than under centralized command.
- Azhar favors syncognitism — a "society" of AI systems whose interactions and emergent structures produce collective intelligence capable of identifying complex long-range goals and organizing resources, without any single system being dominant.
- This connects to Kevin Kelly's "Swarm Intelligence" framing of a future AI ecosystem as a "coral reef" of thousands of specialized mind-types, ranging from insect-level narrow systems to math-genius specialists, most performing narrow/boring tasks.
- A separate research note (Yue Xu) proposes a framework for determining which work can be economically delegated to AI, factoring in the cost of checking outputs and the losses from errors.

## Quote

> Like a coral reef, an AI superorganism will be composed of thousands of species of minds.
