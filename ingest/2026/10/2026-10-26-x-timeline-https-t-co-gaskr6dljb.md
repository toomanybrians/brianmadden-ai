# https://t.co/GASKR6DljB

## Insights

- Argues that as AI execution quality improves, most failures attributed to "bad AI" are actually articulation failures — the AI did what was asked, but what was asked didn't match what was meant.
- Frames this as "Writer's Blindness" (aka curse of knowledge): once an idea is in your head, it's hard to tell when your written description of it is incomplete or ambiguous to someone (or something) else.
- Proposes code is an abstraction/representation of intent, not the source of truth — directly disputing a developer's position that "the code, not the spec, should be the center of your universe."
- Introduces the "Ideal State Artifact" (ISA): a living document combining prose intent, constraints, decisions log, and atomic testable "Ideal State Criteria" (each with its own eval) — meant to be checked against instead of iterating on the code/output itself.
- Uses an accuracy-vs-precision framing (archery metaphor): writing has more accuracy (closer to true intent) while code has more precision, and when building with AI, accuracy matters more.
- Ties the argument to Sutton's "Bitter Lesson" — as models get smarter, effort should shift from micromanaging implementation steps toward clearly articulating desired outcomes.
- Illustrates with three examples (drawing an owl, a yoga-studio booking app, a kitchen remodel) where skipping written articulation and treating the first implementation as "truth" locks in unintended defaults.

## Quote

> When an AI builds the wrong thing, check what you told it before you blame the model.
