# Put Some Brain in Your Thinking

## Insights

- The piece frames a generational shift: for a child of "Generation Alpha," brain-based thinking and AI-based thinking are interchangeable "ingredients," not inherently linked — the essay treats a seven-year-old's phrase "put some brain in your thinking" as evidence that AI and cognition have become decoupled as default assumptions.
- Argues cognitive offloading to AI is becoming normalized and widespread enough to constitute a "pandemic," with a growing class of people who never engage their own reasoning for work or life.
- Claims the trend is self-reinforcing/hard to reverse: attention and cognitive capacity atrophy, and the very faculties needed to notice and correct the atrophy are what's being eroded (likened to doom-scrolling).
- Surveys and dismisses several proposed "fixes" (banning AI tools, "friction-maxxing"/deliberately adding effortful daily tasks, AI-detection partnerships like Substack's) as impractical or self-undermining given competitive/AI-race pressures.
- Predicts a coming "seasoning schism" — a bifurcation between people who balance AI use with genuine thinking and rise above it, versus those who over-rely on AI and become functionally redundant alongside automation.
- Prescribes concrete countermeasures: temporarily abstaining from AI tools to test real productivity/outcomes vs. "busyness," and deliberately "doing less and thinking more" instead of defaulting to high-agency AI delegation.
- Notes a data point cited in the piece: AI-generated web content has reportedly reached parity with human-written content (50/50).

## Quote

> "a lot of you would benefit from doing less and thinking more."
