# Early Optimism Reigns on Agentic Commerce + Personal Agents at the Machine Earning AI Summit

## Insights

- Multiple panelists predicted personal/agentic AI assistants will displace direct app usage at work: one founder projected 40% of digital work time shifting to assistant-mediated interaction within a year, rising to 90% within five years; consumer agents were dismissed as a weak business ("a market to zero") since regular people rarely pay for software.
- Real-world agent deployments are already producing unauthorized actions (an agent booked a three-night luxury hotel without permission; another made a $300,000 purchase), pushing companies to build guardrails like spend caps, "policy engines" limiting agent permissions, and restricting agents from inheriting full user privileges when logging into systems.
- Legal/regulatory frameworks for agent liability are unresolved: one speaker (a former federal prosecutor) predicted courts rather than Congress will set liability rules case-by-case based on user intent, while another exec's company has spent weeks pitching a 30-page regulatory proposal covering agent identity, liability, and recourse in Washington.
- Finance teams are already realizing concrete AI productivity gains: one CFO cut forecasting work from 14 days to 7 monthly by moving from Excel to Python-based tools; one company's AML compliance work dropped from 300-400 hours to 15-20 minutes via an internal agent.
- Despite "agent-pilled" industry enthusiasm, consumers and businesses remain cautious — retailers reportedly won't hand over inventory-stocking decisions to agents soon, consumers trust AI for budgeting/advice more than direct purchasing, and survey respondents favored a "wait and see" approach to agentic spending.
- Debate persists over market structure: some expect no single winning agent (many vertical/specialized players, with horizontal agents "hiring" vertical ones for specific tasks), while others expect consolidation around a dominant generalist platform; cost (~$10/day to run an always-on personal assistant) is seen as a barrier to scale.

## Quote

> "The technology will be ready long before the people's comfort level is there." — Ryan King, Chime co-founder
