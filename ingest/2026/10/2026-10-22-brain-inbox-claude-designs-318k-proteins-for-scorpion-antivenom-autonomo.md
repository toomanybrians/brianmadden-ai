# 🧬 Claude designs 318K proteins for scorpion antivenom autonomously

## Insights

- Anthropic had Claude autonomously run a drug-discovery workflow: it chose target binding sites on five scorpion toxins, set its own scoring thresholds, generated 318,484 candidate proteins using 12 design tools, and selected 180 for lab testing — all from one initial prompt plus four follow-ups, with minimal human direction.
- Lab validation showed real but partial success: of 211 designs tested, 17 met strict binding criteria, with the strongest binding the Mexican Cn2 toxin at single-digit nanomolar affinity; none bound more than one toxin and none neutralized the Brazilian Ts1 toxin. None of the 17 have yet shown actual venom-neutralizing effect in cells or animals.
- Anthropic published all 211 designs and the full lab dataset publicly, signaling a move toward open, reproducible AI-driven bioscience rather than proprietary black-box results.
- Separately, Anthropic released a free library of 400+ Claude agents/skills/plugins, including 11 role-based "knowledge-work-plugins" (sales, legal, finance, data, marketing, product) that connect Claude to tools like Slack, Jira, HubSpot, and Snowflake — positioned as ready-to-use, no-code task automation for enterprise functions.
- DeepSeek open-sourced its actual production inference stack (DeepInfer) under Apache 2.0, including speculative decoding, distributed inference, and structured-output support — a signal that cost/infrastructure barriers to deploying frontier-grade AI are dropping as leading labs commoditize their serving layers.
- A separate RL technique (GRAFT) addresses reward hacking by letting models exploit a disposable adapter freely, then discarding it — part of a broader trend toward AI systems that self-correct rather than just self-direct.
