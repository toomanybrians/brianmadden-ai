# Pluralistic: Scrutinized (05 Oct 2026)

## Insights

- Meta's AI-enabled camera glasses ("pervert glasses") are marketed on the premise of solving face-blindness/social awkwardness (reminding users of names), but their actual dominant use case is covert recording of strangers for shaming and viral content.
- Once these glasses are paired with facial recognition, the piece argues they become "doxing factories," converting mundane public mistakes (tripping, nose-picking, arguments) into instantly identifiable, distributable content — democratizing the worst aspect of celebrity (constant exposure) without its benefits.
- A UK case is cited where British Transport Police ran facial recognition on millions of train travelers and caught zero suspects, offered as evidence that facial-recognition surveillance dragnets perform poorly despite their scale.
- The piece distinguishes "surveillance" (being watched) from "scrutiny" (being watched by many and specifically judged), framing the latter as the newer, content-creator-driven harm enabled by always-on AI cameras plus facial recognition.
- Several tech/business figures (Larry Ellison, Mark Zuckerberg, Eric Schmidt, Peter Thiel) are cited as publicly advocating surveillance or "supervision" for the general public while aggressively guarding their own personal privacy — framed as evidence that constant scrutiny suppresses people's "authentic selves" rather than improving behavior.
- Argument: environments of continuous recording/judgment (e.g., a phone appearing at a social gathering, a parent watching a child too closely) kill spontaneity and the ability to fail privately, which the piece treats as necessary for growth and authenticity.

## Quote

> Citizens will be on their best behavior, because we are constantly recording and reporting everything that's going on. — Larry Ellison
