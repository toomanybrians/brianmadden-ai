# What 100 CEOs Told Me About AI When the Room Got Honest

## Insights

- In closed-door workshops with ~100 CEOs/COOs, many privately admitted uncertainty about AI — "I know this matters... but I'm not sure where to start" — a candor they don't share with their own organizations.
- Most leaders default to narrow AI applications (chatbots, productivity, automation, content) and frame it as "bolt-on" optimization of existing workflows, rather than asking how the business should work differently because AI exists.
- The real bottleneck is described as imaginative, not technical — leadership teams often lack a shared vision of AI's possibilities and jump to tools/use-cases before forming a point of view; cited Microsoft data shows only 26% of AI users feel leadership is clearly aligned on AI.
- AI fluency can't be delegated to IT or power users; without senior-level fluency, whichever voice sounds most confident (vendor, consultant, enthusiast) becomes the de facto expert, regardless of actual judgment.
- Unmanaged employee AI adoption is producing an "AI tax" — more volume (drafts, decks, emails) but not more value, creating rework, generic output, and quality-control burdens on managers.
- Proposes a leadership agenda: start with work (not use cases), define a clear AI ambition, build executive fluency continuously, treat governance as an enabler not a brake, confront data fragmentation, redesign work before cutting headcount, and reinvest freed capacity into growth rather than removing it.
- Frames the human/change dimension as trust and narrative-setting, not classic "change management" — if leaders don't define AI's intent (eliminate vs. empower), employees will default to a fear-based narrative.
