# Human-Pilled

## Insights

- O'Reilly argues human value in an AI economy isn't just "judgment" or "taste" but the focusing function of human attention: an LLM contains a "sea of possibility" and nothing sets it in motion except a human directing it toward a specific purpose.
- Illustrates this with an internal case: an O'Reilly research agent reasoned generically that an IT team needed a new hire, but when told to answer "as O'Reilly experts would" (filtered through a specific book, Site Reliability Engineering), it reversed course and recommended a toil audit and restructuring instead — same underlying knowledge, different framing/expertise lens, opposite conclusion.
- Describes "magic words" (e.g., naming "GTD") as compressed context-triggers, and frames context engineering/prompting as "the future of programming" — distinct from traditional coding skill.
- Criticizes the AI industry for optimizing capability and monetization while neglecting "mechanism design": the incentive structures/standards needed to let humans and their expertise get discovered, valued, and paid (contrasts with YouTube's Content ID, which turned piracy conflict into a creator economy).
- Points to MCP (Model Context Protocol) as an accidental "architecture of participation" analogous to Unix's protocol-centric design — enabling a decentralized ecosystem of interoperating models/agents rather than one dominant centralized AI platform.
- Warns that the dominant AI business game (superintelligence race, capital-intensive, closed APIs, locked-in memory/context) is shaped by investors' need for returns, and argues for an alternative path via open source/open protocols/open memory and "sovereign AI" that keeps agents working for individuals rather than labs.

## Quote

> We don't study what mechanisms we could introduce to create new economic opportunities for humans.
