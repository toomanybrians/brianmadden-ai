# I realize this is because thinking traces have gotten too long (and too propriet

## Insights

- AI labs (implied: Claude, ChatGPT) have moved toward hiding detailed reasoning/thinking traces from users, showing only final outputs rather than step-by-step process.
- This shift is attributed to two drivers: thinking traces becoming too long to usefully display, and concerns about proprietary reasoning being exposed (anti-distillation, i.e., preventing competitors from training on exposed reasoning chains) alongside UX simplification motives.
- Hiding the reasoning process removes the ability for a human to review and weigh in on AI's in-process work, not just its final answer.
- This is framed as pushing the human-AI relationship toward automation (AI acts autonomously, human only sees results) rather than augmentation (human collaborates with and steers AI mid-process).
- The move toward opaque "magic" outputs is characterized as a default trend toward disintermediating humans from AI work — one the author argues is worth actively resisting rather than accepting as inevitable.

## Quote

> The solution of hiding them almost entirely so the AI just does "magic" feels like a failure of imagination.
