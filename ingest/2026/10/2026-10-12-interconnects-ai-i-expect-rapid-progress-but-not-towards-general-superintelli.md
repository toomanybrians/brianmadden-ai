# I expect rapid progress but not towards general superintelligence

## Insights

- Argues the near-term AI progress wave is primarily an engineering/infrastructure acceleration (training speed, inference cost, GPU utilization) rather than a fundamental jump in model research capability toward general superintelligence.
- Describes a historical pendulum in AI between "research-driven" and "engineering-driven" eras; coding agents are now easing research work itself, shifting value back toward good ideas over good execution — echoing Ilya Sutskever's "era of research" framing.
- Predicts pretraining research (architecture, data selection for current model classes) could become largely automated within 2-3 years, while GPU flexibility remains valuable as an exploration space during this period.
- Expects near-exponential decline in effective cost of model intelligence, driven by compounding gains in inference efficiency (citing past 10-30% cost reductions post-launch) and eventual accelerator/model co-design for further orders-of-magnitude gains.
- Frames this efficiency wave as triggering Jevons paradox-style demand growth for agentic models, with the bottleneck shifting to figuring out how to orient and deliver agents effectively (citing Meta's Muse as an early example).
- Flags RL training data/environments as a current low-quality bottleneck despite large commercial revenue in that sector — "fixable" but currently poor, even as labs see ROI from buying it.
- Notes a parallel acceleration possible in scientific fields (biology, chemistry) where AI's ability to crawl literature and connect sparse, siloed research communities could drive either genuine new discovery or just accelerate existing trends.

## Quote

> We're about to go through a massive acceleration over a few years where the bottleneck is less and less engineering again.
