# You delegated the work to agents and ended up with a new job: reading their updates. My guide to a Chief of Staff that brings you decisions with the evidence.

## Insights

- Delegating tasks to AI agents can create a new form of hidden labor: monitoring, reading, and triaging the stream of status updates, alerts, and follow-up suggestions the agents generate.
- Each individual agent update feels reasonable in isolation, but the aggregate volume of notifications (completed tasks, "nothing changed" watches, suggested next steps) becomes a de facto second job.
- A proposed fix is a "Chief of Staff" layer that filters agent output down to decisions with supporting evidence, rather than raw status reports requiring the human to reconstruct context.
- Specific failure modes named: agents that report "nothing happened" instead of staying silent, finished tasks that spawn unsolicited new work, and recurring jobs/alerts that don't actually confirm they've stopped.
- The suggested design principles include: closing out threads with a single finishing instruction (no spawned side-projects), silent watches that only alert on real changes, and a "truth-teller" prompt that explicitly recommends what to stop or finish.

## Quote

> This is how you end up managing more work after supposedly delegating it.
