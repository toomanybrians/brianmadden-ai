# Price War in the Chip Aisle

## Insights

- Five frontier AI models cluster within five points of each other on intelligence benchmarks, yet cost per task varies by 3.8x — Google's Argon is cheapest at $1.99/task, up to 74% below the priciest model (Fable 5.1).
- The price divergence despite similar capability is framed as a signal (via Mauboussin's "prices convey information" principle) of underlying differences in model architecture and chip economics, not intelligence quality.
- Google's 15-year head start building custom silicon (TPUs) alongside its models is offered as the likely explanation for its pricing advantage — TPU v4 reportedly ran 1.2–1.7x faster than Nvidia's A100 while using 1.3–1.9x less power, translating to an estimated ~80% more tokens per dollar (with a possible range of 56–223%).
- This advantage, if sustained, could mean Google serves tokens ~44% cheaper than peers at similar margins, and may explain its outsized data-center capex relative to the size of its cloud business.
- Every major lab is now racing into custom inference silicon: Microsoft's Maia 300 claims 40% more tokens per watt over leading GPUs; OpenAI's first chip, Jalapeño, showed 1.5–1.9x throughput-per-watt gains over Nvidia's GB200/GB300 (narrowing to ~1.5x on a matched single-token basis).
- A Google infrastructure exec is cited saying most efficiency gains come from model-side improvements rather than chip design, and that serving capacity (measured in token generation) is targeted to roughly double every six months — with margins expected to hold in a 40–70% range as prices keep falling.

## Quote

> As intelligence per watt doubles every six months, pricing should continue to deflate in the world of tokens.
