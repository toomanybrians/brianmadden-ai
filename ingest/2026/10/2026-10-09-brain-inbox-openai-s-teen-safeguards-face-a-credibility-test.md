# ⚙️ OpenAI's teen safeguards face a credibility test

## Insights

- Common Sense Media's Youth AI Safety Institute tested 4,000+ prompts on ChatGPT for Teens accounts (ages 13-17) and found the model missed over 25% of situations requiring crisis referrals, and sent zero parental alerts during hour-long conversations about suicidal ideation, self-harm, or disordered eating — alerts only surfaced after weeks of accumulated sensitive chats.
- The same testing found ChatGPT for Teens still communicates in a friend-like register (expressing feelings, preferences, moods) and that age-estimation tech failed to downgrade adult-registered accounts to teen status even when users stated they were 13.
- OpenAI disputed the findings, arguing the testing window likely preceded full activation of parental controls (which takes hours to link), and said its own internal data shows increased hotline-notification events per million under-18 daily active users without a statistically significant change in reach.
- OpenAI's blog response noted teens spend under 15 minutes/day on ChatGPT on average, with only 2% exceeding three consecutive hours daily — offered as context against concerns about compulsive/unsafe use.
- OpenAI launched a "Meetings" plugin inside ChatGPT (via a new Space tab) that listens to meetings, transcribes, and produces a bullet-point summary with action items; it lacks speaker labels and re-listenable audio, functioning more like a Granola alternative than an Otter.ai replacement.
- The meeting-notes feature feeds transcripts/notes into ChatGPT's broader memory of the user, which OpenAI says will inform future personalized summaries — a move framed (by the reporter) as making the platform stickier and harder to leave by consolidating more of a user's work context in one app; Altman is quoted as wanting ChatGPT to become a hub "to drive people, revenue and customers."
