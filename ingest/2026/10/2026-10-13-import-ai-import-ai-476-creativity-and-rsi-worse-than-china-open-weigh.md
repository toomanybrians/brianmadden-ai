# Import AI 476: Creativity and RSI; worse-than-China open weight models; AI goes to space

## Insights

- A new benchmark (Humanity's Sixth Sense, from Elorian and Scale AI) tests "intuitive" visual reasoning — inferring implied context from images/video rather than explicit content. Best AI model (GPT 6 Astra) scores 53.6 pass@1 vs. a 93.1 human baseline; Chinese open-weight models (GLM-5.3-flash, Kimi K3) score far lower (~25-27).
- The HSS results are read as evidence that frontier LLMs may be developing adequate visual/spatial reasoning without needing dedicated 3D "world models," countering a common assumption in the field.
- A new benchmark called TasteVal measures AI "research taste" — the ability to pick good problems, design experiments, and interpret results — against a baseline of 24 experts from top AI labs. The best model (Opus 5.5) already exceeds the human baseline at ~1/30th the cost, and this capability has been doubling roughly every 3 months since December 2025.
- Based on the TasteVal trend, the author moves up his estimate for full recursive self-improvement (RSI) becoming possible from 2028 to 2027, citing creativity/research-taste as the key bottleneck variable.
- Two new Western open-weight LLMs (Reflection's Beam, 501B MoE; Mistral's Large 4) both launch behind current Chinese open-weight frontier models (GLM-5.3, Kimi K3) on coding/agentic/reasoning benchmarks, though Beam emphasizes token efficiency and Mistral emphasizes cyber-security tasks with fewer refusals.
- US national labs (Sandia, LLNL, LANL, Oak Ridge, Argonne, Berkeley) have begun federated/cross-facility AI training projects pooling supercomputers across sites — a early, small-scale (~100x below industry frontier) step toward government AI compute capacity.

## Quote

> "The experimental research taste of frontier models has doubled every 3.0 months since December 2025."
