# The Jobs Report Is a Productivity Report

## Insights

- Productivity growth has roughly doubled versus the pre-pandemic baseline: 2013–2019 averaged 1.2% annual productivity growth (2.5% GDP, 1.8% hours), versus 2.4% since early 2023 (2.7% GDP, only 0.4% hours) — similar output growth achieved with far fewer added hours.
- The author's nowcast puts Q3 productivity growth around 4% annualized, driven by flat nonfarm business hours (after two months of downward payroll revisions totaling 60,000) against GDPNow's 3.7% GDP growth estimate.
- In FIIPB (finance, insurance, information, professional/business services), real value added is up 16.1% since end-2022 while employment in those industries is down ~2% over the same period, with Q3 2026 the weakest stretch yet (job losses in July, August, September).
- Productivity gains are concentrated, not broad-based: FIIPB ~4%/year, advanced manufacturing 3.4%, retail 2.2%, everything else only 0.3% — a pattern the author attributes (without proof) to AI and related technologies rather than general cyclical recovery.
- FIIPB employment is 757,000 below its April 2023 peak, with a modeled shortfall (based on pre-pandemic GDP-to-employment relationships) of 2.6 million jobs — up from a prior estimate of 2.3 million due to upward GDP revisions.
- Health care has been the dominant job-growth engine (520,000 jobs added over the past year, versus 496,000 for all of nonfarm combined) but is decelerating, now growing at its slowest six-month pace since the data series began in 2023.

## Quote

> Payrolls are weak, output is not, and the gap between them is productivity.
