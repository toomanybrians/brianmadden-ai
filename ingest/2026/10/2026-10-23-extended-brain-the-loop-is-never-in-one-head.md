# The Loop Is Never in One Head

## Insights

- The piece introduces a "Write-Read-Rewrite (WRRw) loop with Stakes" framework, extending Howard Pattee's concept of the "epistemic cut" (symbols vs. dynamics) from biology to agentic systems generally, both individual and collective.
- Core distinction: a system only "interprets" rather than merely reacting if it has genuine Stakes — defined as a bearer whose own continuation is affected by a Read's correctness, with no way to offload that cost (a thermostat has no stakes; a cell does).
- The "Rewrite principle": a loop is aligned to the extent that those who pay the cost of a failed Read can actually trigger a Rewrite of the governing mark; when the two groups diverge, "meaning follows the Rewrite" and stakes become displaced from those who hold revision power.
- Illustrative cases span a spectrum: a tick's instinct-driven behavior (no rewrite mechanism at all), Viktor Frankl's personal/collective meaning-making (peaceful because stakeholders bear their own stakes), a startup founder torn between conflicting charters (stakes misaligned by design across founder/employees/investors), English common law (stable because the rewrite channel stays open to those bearing stakes), and the U.S. Constitution (vicarious stakes, three rewrite channels — amendment, interpretation, and extra-channel rupture like the Civil War).
- General claim: almost no real-world interpretive loop is purely individual — most are distributed across multiple agents and across time, such that no single party ever holds the full "closure."
- Meaning is framed not as a static state but as a cascade — one agent's Write becomes material for another's Write/Rewrite, producing an approximation of shared meaning without any central point of control.

## Quote

> A loop is aligned to the degree that those who bear the cost of a failed Read can cause a Rewrite that answers it.
