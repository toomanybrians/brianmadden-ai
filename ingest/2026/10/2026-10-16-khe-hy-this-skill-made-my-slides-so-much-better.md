# This skill made my slides so much better

## Insights

- The author built a reusable AI "skill" (via Claude's skill-creator) that encodes McKinsey-style slide-construction principles — Pyramid Principle, SCQA, MECE — adapted for lighter, teaching-oriented (not heavy research) presentations.
- Key visual/structural heuristics surfaced: match the message to the type of comparison, declutter then direct attention, treat concepts as the "data" in teaching decks, and ensure a viewer can guess the slide's title within 5 seconds.
- The workflow is interview-style: the AI asks three high-leverage questions per slide before generating it, builds one slide at a time, and outputs HTML rather than PowerPoint, which the author sees as faster and cleaner to work in than the PowerPoint ecosystem.
- The skill was used to produce a slide illustrating the "faster horse" trap in AI adoption — getting anchored to optimizing existing workflows (faster/cheaper) instead of reimagining work, training, hiring, and architecture from scratch given cheap intelligence.
- Since deploying the skill, the author generated 35+ slides with it, calling out that output still visibly "looks AI-generated" but considers that acceptable since content and presentation hold up on their own.
- Despite the HTML/AI workflow, slides are still often converted back to PowerPoint for practical editing, and the author is withholding the actual skill from public sharing.

## Quote

> If someone can't guess the title within 5 seconds of looking at the visual, the visual isn't doing its job.
