# The Morning Download: AI Is Almost the Easy Part. Everything Else Is Hard

## Insights

- Serving/selling AI infrastructure is proving to be the "easy" problem for big tech — demand consistently outstrips supply (Google Cloud +103% YoY, Azure +48%) — but monetizing AI and restructuring the business around it is the much harder, unsolved problem.
- Google's core search/ads business shows AI cannibalization pressure: AI Mode is growing query volume at its fastest rate in five years, but monetizes at a lower rate than traditional search, slowing search revenue growth and spooking investors (shares fell ~10%).
- ServiceNow's stock dropped 14% despite meeting expectations, purely on fear that AI will structurally disrupt its SaaS business model — echoing a broader "SaaSpocalypse" anxiety among software incumbents.
- Microsoft offered buyouts to ~60,000 employees (about a quarter of its workforce) while simultaneously publishing an internal playbook on navigating AI-driven reorganization.
- Microsoft's Kathleen Hogan says the company's biggest mistake was leading with technology instead of people and process, noting "a bad process with AI is still a bad process."
- Anthropic's IPO filing pegs the economic opportunity for AI in knowledge work at an estimated $40 trillion, surpassing SpaceX's earlier $28.5 trillion valuation claim — signaling how AI's addressable value is explicitly being framed around displacing/augmenting knowledge work.

## Quote

> "A bad process with AI is still a bad process." — Kathleen Hogan, Microsoft chief strategy and transformation officer
