# America’s Seed Fund Should Back AI Resilience Startups

## Insights

- Monthly disclosures of serious cyber vulnerabilities at major tech companies have surged sharply since new AI models with enhanced cyber capabilities emerged this spring; one model reportedly found over 10,000 critical software flaws in a single month.
- Proposal: NSF's "America's Seed Fund" (SBIR/STTR) should award $100M over two years via a new "AI Resilience Pilot" solicitation, funding ~25-company cohorts twice yearly with Phase I ($500K) and Phase II ($2M) nondilutive grants, modeled on an existing scientific-instrumentation pilot.
- Core market failure identified: resilience technology (cyberdefense, agent security, biosecurity, cognitive/infrastructure security) is systematically underfunded by private VC because the "risk becomes legible" — and paying customers materialize — only after an attack or outbreak occurs, by which point it's too late to build defenses.
- Externalities compound the problem: a hospital or utility investing in cybersecurity is partly protecting neighbors, insurers, and national security, not just itself, so individual buyers underinvest relative to social benefit (cited: 2021 ransomware attack forcing a San Diego hospital offline, causing stroke cases at neighboring ERs to more than double).
- Historical precedent cited: NSF's ~$250,000 in 1979 seed awards to a four-person company (Machine Intelligence Corporation) eventually became Symantec, illustrating how early government bets can seed companies that are ready when a threat (the computer virus) later materializes.
- Specific resilience technologies highlighted: Far-UVC (222nm) lighting shown to reduce airborne bacteria by 98% in a room-sized test as a pathogen-agnostic biodefense; DNA-synthesis screening (e.g., Aclid); automated vulnerability discovery/patching (e.g., Trail of Bits in DARPA's AI Cyber Challenge).
- Mechanism details: SBIR "Phase III" status gives awardees an expedited, non-competitive federal procurement pathway, which the authors frame as particularly valuable since many resilience startups' main customer is the government itself.

## Quote

> If we wait until the market for defensive technologies fully materializes, we will have waited too long to build those technologies.
