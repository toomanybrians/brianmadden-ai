# Will Generative AI do more harm than good?

## Insights

- Marcus reframes the generative AI debate around a cost-benefit ledger: he catalogs concrete harms (nonconsensual deepfake porn, phishing scams, disinformation, "cognitive surrender," government influence over LLM outputs, surveillance uses, IP theft from artists/writers) and argues the negative case is currently "quite serious."
- He explicitly notes labor-market disruption has been "more modest than some people expected" — a concession that cuts against his own skeptic framing and is worth flagging as an area where he departs from the doom consensus.
- Cites Torsten Slok (Apollo Global Management chief economist, described as "at times fairly positive" on AI) making a case that many anticipated positive effects of generative AI simply haven't materialized yet — an outside data point Marcus leans on rather than his own analysis.
- References a wave of AI-related security incidents in October 2025/2026 (dubbed "Black October" / "The Hackening" by others, called a "Code red" by Salesforce's Marc Benioff amplifying Anthropic's Jason Clinton) as evidence the harm side of the ledger is escalating, not stabilizing.
- Acknowledges real personal upside anecdotally (friends benefiting from AI, "mainly for coding") before concluding it's now plausible to argue generative AI has been "on the whole... a net detriment to society" — framed as an argument that can be made, not a settled verdict.
- Flags macro tail risk: raises the possibility that AI-related excess could "pull down the entire economy," though this is asserted rather than substantiated in the piece.

## Quote

> At this point it's not hard to make the argument that it has on the whole been a net detriment to society.
