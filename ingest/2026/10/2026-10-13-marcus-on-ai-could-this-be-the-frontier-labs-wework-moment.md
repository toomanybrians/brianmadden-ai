# Could this be the Frontier Labs’ WeWork moment?

## Insights

- Marcus argues the extreme valuations floated in the hype peak (OpenAI near $1T, Anthropic near $2T) are becoming harder to justify given subsequent developments, not necessarily impossible but increasingly disconnected from fundamentals.
- He cites the collapse of "tokenmaxxing" (the assumption that scaling token usage/compute indefinitely drives value) as a key narrative shift, compounded by Chinese open-weight models triggering price wars that compress margins.
- He flags unresolved questions about the accuracy of OpenAI's revenue projections as a credibility concern feeding valuation skepticism.
- He treats poorly controlled AI agents causing real-world harm (which he frames as "bad software" mislabeled as "rogue AI") as a emerging liability risk for frontier labs, coining "Large Liability Models" as a play on LLMs.
- A U.S. appeals court copyright ruling is noted as a new legal headwind specifically undermining generative AI companies' position.
- He reads OpenAI's reported IPO delay to 2027 and Anthropic's rumored IPO without a public S-1 filing as corroborating signals that the financial story is less solid than the hype suggested.

## Quote

> LLM's might well someday be renamed Large Liability Modules.
