# Databricks & the Future of the Firm

## Insights

- Databricks' technical evolution (Spark → Lakehouse → Lakebase → agents) is framed as the database infrastructure now underpinning agentic work: 17M+ databases are launched by agents daily, and agent queries already exceed human queries on the platform.
- The piece argues AI's core limitation isn't intelligence but "context" — most of a firm's ontology (what things mean, who decides what, what worked) lives tacitly in employees' heads and must be externalized into a formal "system of record for agents" (SORA) for agents to act autonomously.
- Draws on Coase (transaction costs) and Herbert Simon (centralized record enables decentralized action) to argue each computing era (mainframe, database, internet) reshaped the firm's boundary and structure; AI is framed as the next such shift, raising the "complexity ceiling" for both large and small firms.
- Claims the 20th-century firm was pyramid-shaped because hierarchy functioned as its information-processing system; natural-language interfaces (e.g., Databricks' Genie) let non-technical employees query/act on data directly, reducing the need for managers as information routers.
- Frames the shift not as "everyone becomes an engineer" but "everyone becomes a manager" — knowledge workers increasingly scope, delegate to agents, and judge/review output rather than execute directly, mirroring how software engineering is already changing.
- Introduces "sovereign firm" concept: competitive advantage will come from firms owning their accumulated data/decision loop (definitions, overrides, outcomes) rather than just renting a frontier model, since models become commoditized across competitors.
- Cites Burnham's "managerial revolution" thesis to argue professional managerial class dominance (born from organizational complexity exceeding individual oversight) is now reversing as AI automates coordination/information-routing work, returning authority toward "front-line" practitioners.
