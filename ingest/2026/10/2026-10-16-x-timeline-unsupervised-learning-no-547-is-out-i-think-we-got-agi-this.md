## Insights

- Miessler argues AGI effectively arrived in 2026 via "Claude Tag," a persistent organizational-memory AI integrated into Slack/Teams that can read every email, meeting, Slack message, and customer interaction an employee has ever produced.
- His operational definition of AGI is the ability to replace an "average knowledge worker": the system can audit an employee's full work history, summarize their output, then offer to simply take over all of it — not just one task, but the entirety of a role, faster and cheaper.
- He expects this to disproportionately affect average/below-average performers rather than top performers, whose work involves more tacit, hard-to-articulate skill — but still predicts meaningful layoffs or reduced rehiring after attrition.
- A key feature he highlights: the AI isn't a separate "AI app" users tab out to — it's embedded as an agent inside existing workflows (Slack/Teams), which he frames as central to its transformative power.
- He frames uneven adoption/visibility as the reason AGI doesn't feel like it has arrived yet — "it's just not evenly distributed."
- Elsewhere in the same piece: AI-driven content/game generation could let anyone customize or clone hit games/shows in days, shifting competitive advantage from production capability to audience attention and marketing.

## Quote

> AGI already happened; it's just not—as they say—evenly distributed.
