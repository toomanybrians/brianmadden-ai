# Microsoft has offered to buy out an entire quarter of their workforce. Feels lik

## Insights

- Microsoft offered voluntary buyouts to roughly 60,000 employees — about 27% of its 223,000-person global workforce — spanning the US, UK, Canada, and India, with certain teams (marketing, commercial business, security) excluded.
- The buyout coincides with a massive escalation in AI infrastructure spending: Microsoft's 2026 investment/infrastructure budget is ~$175 billion, up nearly 120% year-over-year, as it builds data centers and chip capacity for an "AI empire" and supplies cloud compute to OpenAI and Anthropic.
- CEO Satya Nadella recently described reorganizing the entire company around AI and cheaper computing for a "super intelligence" era, framing the buyout as part of a broader strategic pivot rather than a standalone cost-cutting move.
- This is the third workforce reduction effort in 2026 alone: an earlier voluntary retirement program covered ~7% of US staff, and a July round cut 4,800 jobs globally (including Xbox gaming), suggesting an escalating, repeated pattern of workforce reshaping.
- Regional economic divergence is tied to the AI boom: Seattle's tech economy and housing market are weakening (median home price down 4.2%) while San Francisco's is surging (up 14.6%), attributed to AI-driven wealth concentration versus AI-driven layoffs/uncertainty.
- Microsoft's chief people officer characterized the buyout as experimental — "new for us" — framed as a way to manage change and help departing employees develop new skills, rather than a straightforward layoff.

## Quote

> "The Voluntary Exit Offer is new for us." — Amy Coleman, Microsoft chief people officer
