# How AI Is Changing Innovation: Why Speed Alone Isn’t a Strategy

## Insights

- When idea generation and prototyping become near-free, the scarce resource shifts to judgment: deciding what *not* to build becomes more important than deciding what to build.
- AI-generated work products (e.g., AI-built slide decks) can degrade thinking quality when authors aren't intimately familiar with content they didn't actually produce themselves — speed without internalization lowers conversation quality.
- The more valuable frontier isn't efficiency (doing more things) but generating original insights that expand what problems an organization has the ambition and credibility to tackle at all.
- Every capability bottleneck solved creates a new one downstream: coding is largely automated now, pushing the bottleneck to code review, and eventually to deciding what's worth building.
- Competitive advantage doesn't come from access to AI/agents themselves (since everyone has access) but from how effectively an organization deploys those resources — same logic as workforce allocation decisions pre-AI.
- Technology's reduction in AI hallucination rates is outpacing public psychological comfort with trusting consequential AI decisions — a trust gap between technical capability and human readiness.
- Internal example: a cross-product integration project estimated at 6-9 years was built by a small team in 9 months once prioritized — illustrating a step-change in organizational "clock speed" that's described as cognitively disorienting.

## Quote

> An agent's like teenagers — supremely intelligent, no fear of consequence, and exercise poor decisions far more frequently than you'd like.
