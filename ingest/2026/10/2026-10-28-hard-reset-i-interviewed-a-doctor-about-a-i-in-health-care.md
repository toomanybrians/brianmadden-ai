# I Interviewed A Doctor About A.I. In Health Care

## Insights

- A practicing dermatologist and ML scientist argues AI will not replace doctors, calling that belief "deluding," but sees healthcare's deeper problems (clinician burnout, consolidation, volume-over-care incentives) as the real crisis AI gets layered onto.
- He fears AI will be used by incumbent power structures to further consolidate control over medicine rather than fix systemic problems — and distrusts AI himself partly because patients don't trust how their data will be used.
- Credible near-term AI use cases he identifies are narrow and low-risk: automated blood cell counts, ambient scribes for notes, and possibly automating prescription of low-risk acne topicals — not general diagnosis or care decisions.
- Reviewing a Utah state AI pilot program, he found the approving documentation cited apparently nonexistent research papers, suspected to be AI hallucinations — raising concerns about rigor in regulatory AI vetting.
- Real-world deployments cited show harm: an automated hospital scheduling system (Palantir, at HCA) reportedly causes unsafe staffing patterns and nurse burnout, and AI-drafted patient messages have been called "completely absurd" by the reviewing doctor.
- A separate source (Astra Taylor) claims hospitals and insurers are using AI against each other to overbill and contest bills, adding over $1 billion in excess U.S. healthcare spending — AI increasing costs rather than reducing them.
- Research cited suggests patients disclose less sensitive information (e.g., substance abuse history) when they know an AI is processing the conversation, a trust/data concern distinct from accuracy.

## Quote

> "Patients don't trust the quality of the A.I.—or what companies are going to do with the data they put into A.I. applications."
