# ⚙️ Why home robots may need to look less human

## Insights

- Tuesday Labs (YC- and FCVC-backed, founded by ex-Boston Dynamics roboticist Ros Shinkle) launched Voxel Series-1, a household "droid butler" designed with deliberately non-humanoid, cartoonish "droid-like" aesthetics rather than a human form factor.
- The design drew on film/literature character design, human-robot interaction research, and etiquette guides, explicitly aiming to make the robot feel "disarming" and endearing rather than imposing.
- The author argues humanoid robots — despite being logistically suited to human-built environments — provoke discomfort ("heebie-jeebies") when they're human-sized, agile, and capable, citing a stronger version of this reaction at a recent robotics trade show (IFA).
- Risk of physical harm (e.g., a human-sized robot toppling over) is raised as a practical reason to favor smaller, less human-mimicking designs for home robots.
- Voxel specs: 7-DOF arms with fully actuated wrists, 1-DOF grippers, "impact-aware" Z-shaped legs, audio/visual interaction; claimed tasks include tidying, finding lost items, fetching objects, plant watering.
- Product is early-stage: the public demo unit was puppeteered rather than autonomous, reservations cost $200 against an estimated $20,000 final price, and shipping isn't expected until 2028 — real performance is unverified.

## Quote

> "Voxel is a unique approach because it is so endearing to look at and interact with, while still being capable thanks to careful design."
