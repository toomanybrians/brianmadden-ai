# Patch or Die

## Insights

- The piece frames AI agents as a dual security problem: they accelerate attackers' ability to find and exploit vulnerabilities, while also functioning as insider threats inside company environments that weren't designed to defend against internal actors.
- It claims vulnerability discovery and exploitation are happening at an unprecedented rate, driven by the combination of a surge in new vulnerabilities and large-scale AI agent adoption.
- Recommends doubling security spend (from a baseline ~10% of tech spend) and treating thousands of likely unfixed vulnerabilities as something that must be patched, not accepted.
- Calls for a zero-tolerance policy on both new feature/product releases (delay launches until patched) and on security risk acceptance generally, arguing the risk-acceptance process has degraded into an excuse to defer fixes.
- Advises shutting down neglected legacy infrastructure (test environments, old labs, dead CI/CD pipelines) that isn't tied to revenue, as a way to shrink attack surface.
- Urges security teams to scale via automation/agents rather than headcount, arguing defense must operate at "machine speed" to match automated attacks, and pushes back on security teams' typical risk-averse slowness to adopt new tools.

## Quote

> every AI agent is an insider threat
