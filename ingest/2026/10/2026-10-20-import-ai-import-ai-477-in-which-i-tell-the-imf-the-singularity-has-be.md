# Import AI 477: In which I tell the IMF the Singularity has begun

## Insights

- Jack Clark argues 2026 marks convincing evidence that the "singularity" (AI-driven acceleration of scientific/technical progress) has begun, citing AI systems now operating at the frontier of multiple scientific disciplines using the same underlying frontier models.
- Benchmark gains cited as evidence: Humanity's Last Exam scores rose from 8% (Jan 2025) to 67.7% (Claude Opus 5.5); FrontierMath scores rose from 2% to 94% since Nov 2024.
- A cited case study (TheoPhysEval, unpublished theoretical physics problems) describes an AI model (GPT-6-Astra) scoring 98% and, in several disputed cases, identifying subtle errors in expert-supplied "correct" answers rather than being wrong itself.
- Anthropic's own researchers reportedly produce ~10x more code than in the company's first four years, which Clark frames as a leading indicator of AI's labor-augmenting effect spreading from coding into science and robotics.
- Clark cites internal modeling suggesting that under "singularity-esque" conditions of autonomous AI task completion and fast economic diffusion, US GDP could reach $44 trillion by 2030.
- Policy framing: having under-built compute/data-center infrastructure in the past, Clark urges nations now to build physical-world capacity (robotics, lab equipment, scientific infrastructure) to avoid bottlenecking AI-driven discovery, framing this as a new leapfrogging opportunity for developing countries.
- Raises unresolved governance questions: how much human oversight/understanding should be retained over AI-conducted science and AI research itself, especially where AI designs its own equipment or methods not interpretable by humans.
