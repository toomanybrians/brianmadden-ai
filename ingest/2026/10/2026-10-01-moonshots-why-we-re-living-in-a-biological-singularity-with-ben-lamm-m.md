# Why We're Living in a Biological Singularity With Ben Lamm | MOONSHOTS Live #297

## Insights

- Ben Lamm (Colossal co-founder) argues generalist foundation models aren't yet suited to specialized scientific domains: early-era LLMs were good at "writing term papers" but failed at tasks like ancestral state reconstruction or comparative genomics; frontier models are only now starting to help with simulation/experiment design.
- He frames the near-term AI value-add in biology as a "communication layer" problem — AI bridging inconsistent nomenclature and classification across published research (he cites 40-60% failure rates trying to replicate published mouse-study experiments) rather than generating novel biological insight outright.
- His company is building "inflection models" meant to sit between raw genomic/sequence data and foundation models — domain-specific models trained on evolutionary/genomic datasets that could act as a "traffic control" layer feeding large-scale biological foundation models (e.g., hypothetical "Mythos"-type systems).
- Claims gene-editing capability is scaling exponentially: from ~20 simultaneous edits as a milestone a few years ago to 300+ edits at 90%+ efficiency now, with 1,000-edit experiments underway — though he cautions this is unproven to continue.
- Predicts DNA synthesis (not just multiplex editing) will eventually replace current editing approaches, and that a "CAD software for biology" — prompting a full designed organism — is less than 10 years away; fully ex-utero mammalian gestation claimed as a ~24-month target.
- Advocates for large-scale, government-funded "BioVault" genomic backup infrastructure as a moonshot nobody is sufficiently funding, framing species/genetic preservation as scientific infrastructure analogous to data backup.
- On ethics of de-extinction/engineering decisions, describes an ad hoc internal weighting of factors (ecological role, indigenous spiritual connection, educational/cultural value) rather than a fixed framework, acknowledging society "will get it wrong" at times.

## Quote

> I think that world is less than 10 years out. — Ben Lamm, on a prompt-based "CAD software for biology"
