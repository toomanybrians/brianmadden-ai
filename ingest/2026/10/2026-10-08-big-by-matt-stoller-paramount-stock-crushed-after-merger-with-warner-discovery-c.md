# Paramount Stock CRUSHED After Merger with Warner Discovery Closes

## Insights

- Skydance (merged Paramount-Warner) closed with $79B in debt, relying mainly on declining linear cable cash flow to fund a transition to streaming competitiveness.
- Immediately after closing, CEO David Ellison awarded himself a $150M bonus and gave large payouts to his executive team ($25M, $38M, $46.5M to various executives, plus $550M to the outgoing Warner CEO) while simultaneously organizing significant layoffs.
- The layoff memo's "difficult decisions" framing is described as explicitly signaling "we're not all in it together," which is characterized as corrosive to employee morale and incentivizing self-interested, mercenary behavior among remaining staff.
- The piece frames successful execution of a risky, highly-leveraged merger as requiring broad-based goodwill and sacrifice (remaining employees absorbing larger workloads, partners deferring cash flow) — but argues that visible executive self-enrichment amid layoffs undermines the very cooperation needed to make the deal work.
- Combining two large streaming platforms (Paramount+ and HBO Max) to compete with Netflix faces a structural problem: subscriber overlap and brand-loyalty cancellations mean the combined service may need price increases or more ad-supported/pay-per-view monetization just to maintain current revenue — a dynamic described as "enshittification" of the product.
- Firing the Warner leadership team (described as effective despite being distribution-focused "sharks") in favor of Ellison's own team, with Ellison personally overseeing "creative vision," is flagged as a major execution risk given his mixed track record.

## Quote

> Every employee at Skydance has essentially been told to act like a mercenary and to extract as much as he or she can while he or she can.
