# My book comes out tomorrow, so this is the last day to qualify for the pre-order

## Insights

- The book's central framework, the "jagged frontier," argues AI capability doesn't map cleanly onto task difficulty for humans: some easy-for-humans tasks (counting objects, staying consistent across a long conversation, saying "I don't know") fall outside what AI does well, while some hard-for-humans tasks (Math Olympiad-level problems, polished legal briefs, psychologically tailored persuasion) fall inside it.
- A second framework, "the exponential," contends AI capability has been doubling every few months, and each doubling is larger than all cumulative progress before it — meaning plans built around today's AI tools quickly become obsolete as the curve continues.
- The book frames the current period as "a time before true AGI," positioning itself as a guide to the practical, messy interim phase rather than a speculative end-state.
- Framed questions the book claims to address: how to work alongside AI that is sometimes smarter than you, what this means for jobs/companies/schools, how to distinguish genuine AI capability from hype, and how to retain independent thinking when AI can finish your sentences.
- The author's prior book ("Co-Intelligence") is described as having shaped how companies, schools, and policymakers think about AI capability via the same "jagged frontier" concept, suggesting continuity between the two works' core arguments.

## Quote

> How do we live and work with machines that are sometimes—but not always—smarter than we are?
