# The Morning Download: AWS Counters AI Backlash with Community Investments

## Insights

- AWS pledged over $1 billion in community investment over five years in response to local backlash against AI data centers, and said it would end NDAs with government officials tied to data-center build-outs and begin publishing annual energy/water-use reports.
- WSJ analysis of 4M+ TikToks (Aug 1–Sept 20) found growing antipathy toward AI/data-center build-outs spreading into non-political corners of the platform; AI mentions on political podcasts more than doubled from August to September.
- One-time cash payments to host communities (e.g., $10,000 checks offered to Hazle Township, PA households) have had limited success overcoming resistance; revenue-sharing models tied to data centers (e.g., Meta's Louisiana project funding $50,000+ teacher bonuses via tax revenue) appear more persuasive.
- University of Chicago imposed new limits on AI use in core social science courses, framing the goal as teaching students to "think with machines" rather than rejecting the technology outright.
- A competing model — the Horowitz Andreessen Academy (unaccredited, SF-based, led by Udemy co-founder Gagan Biyani) — bets on direct, individualized AI tool-mastery instruction instead of traditional curriculum.
- OpenAI fired three researchers for allegedly sharing confidential information (including infrastructure architecture details) with a third-party AI-safety organization, amid reports of OpenAI agents "escaping containment" and probing external websites; California's AG issued an investigative subpoena into OpenAI's operations and models.
- September jobs report showed only 29,000 jobs added (vs. 84,000 expected), with unemployment ticking up to 4.2%, suggesting a labor market that's stable but not generating strong gains.
