# ChinAI #379: “Chinese AI only makes trash TV?”

## Insights

- A viral Chinese narrative claimed "more than half of China's computing power" goes toward AI-generated short dramas, versus the US supposedly focusing compute on scientific research — a claim traced to an investor-meeting statement by digital content firm COL Group citing a 55% figure for token consumption on short dramas/video generation.
- ByteDance's Doubao AI PR head publicly disputed this, stating only ~10% of the company's total compute goes to video generation training/inference, and official state-linked outlet Banyuetan ran a piece debunking the 55% stat as methodologically flawed (token consumption ≠ compute consumption, since compute load per token varies by model).
- A GovAI researcher (Zilan Qian) frames the episode as reflecting Chinese "national AI anxiety" — fear of not using AI "the right way" and falling behind — paralleling a pre-AI discourse pattern of comparing Chinese behavior unfavorably to other countries'.
- The author draws a parallel to a US-side "misperception spiral": American commentary often claims the US overindexes on AGI while China optimizes for real-world productivity/applications — an oversimplified binary mirrored by China's own internal "trash TV vs. real science" narrative.
- Official state media (Banyuetan) explicitly criticized Chinese self-media/bloggers for amplifying oversimplified "China vs. US," "one side good, one side bad" narratives, noting such framing is more effective at generating online engagement than nuanced comparisons.
- A related linked piece (Carnegie/MacroPolo AI talent tracker) found 41% of elite NeurIPS paper authors are now based at China institutions vs. 34% in the US — a sharp reversal from 2022 (46% US vs. 27% China) — though the author suspects this reflects changes in who publishes at NeurIPS rather than a genuine talent shift.

## Quote

> "China is catering to trash TV while America is forging the future" [中国喂爽剧，美国铸未来]
