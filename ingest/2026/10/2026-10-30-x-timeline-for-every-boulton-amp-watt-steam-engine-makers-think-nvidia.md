# For every Boulton &amp; Watt (steam engine makers, think NVIDIA in today’s marke

## Insights

- Challenges the common "sell picks and shovels" framing for AI startup strategy, arguing it misapplies the Gold Rush analogy to the current AI buildout.
- Argues the Industrial Revolution is a better analogy than the Gold Rush: gold is a finite, zero-sum pile, whereas machines/technology create compounding value by making production cheaper over time.
- Claims that historically, more people built wealth by *using* new machines (deploying the technology) than by *building* the machines themselves.
- Uses the historical example of Boulton & Watt (steam engine makers, compared to NVIDIA today) to note that for every dominant toolmaker, there were many unremarkable firms (e.g., "General Amalgamated Hosiery Corporation") that also did well — implying broad-based gains, not winner-take-all.
- Frames this as "the opposite of Gold Rush dynamics" — i.e., value distribution in a tech-driven boom is less concentrated/zero-sum than the picks-and-shovels narrative suggests.

## Quote

> Far more people got rich using the new machines than building them.
