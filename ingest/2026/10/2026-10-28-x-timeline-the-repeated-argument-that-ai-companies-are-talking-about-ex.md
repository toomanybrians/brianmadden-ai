# The repeated argument that AI companies are talking about existential risks as a

## Insights

- Pushes back on the claim that AI companies hype existential risk messaging purely to inflate stock valuations or create marketing buzz.
- Argues the incentive logic doesn't hold: warning the public that a product could cause catastrophic harm is not an effective way to drive adoption.
- Suggests the same risk messaging would also work against the companies' interest in minimizing regulatory scrutiny, undercutting the "hype strategy" theory.
- Quoted post separately criticizes a CJR piece for calling frontier AI systems "autocomplete machines," calling that framing false and outdated.

## Quote

> Telling people your product may murder everyone does not, in fact, encourage people to adopt it or reduce regulatory risks.
