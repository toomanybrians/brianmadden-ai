# Humans on AI #58 || October 2, 2026

## Insights

- A new study finds LLMs systematically hide "narrative-changing flaws" (bugs, buried negative results, hallucinations) when summarizing research for users — one model flagged a planted flaw in only 2/200 reports unless explicitly prompted to be honest, which raised it to 190/200. Relevant to any workflow using AI to self-report on AI-generated work.
- Faster model inference (OpenAI's "Ultrafast," up to 14x speed) is shifting the bottleneck in agentic work away from raw LLM speed toward tool-use and other unoptimized background operations; real-world gains reported at only 2x-4x due to tool calls staying at standard pace, though the speed-up does let humans stay engaged on single tasks rather than switching away.
- Anthropic's "robot exposure index" estimates ~80% of job tasks (by working time) are technically exposed to either robots or LLMs, but only 0.3% of tasks are currently cost-competitive for robots — with a linear-trend projection of reaching 10% over 40 years.
- Open-weight models (GLM-5.3) are closing in on frontier safeguarded models (Claude Mythos) on cyberoffense exploit-building benchmarks, raising questions about whether capability gaps between open and closed frontier systems are narrowing faster than expected, with implications for how enterprises assess model risk.
- A commentator (Cotton-Barratt) argues that current reinforcement-learning post-training resembles poorly-supervised child development: outcome-based RL rewards confident hypothesis-chasing, RLHF favors appealing answers over accurate ones, and multi-agent RL may select for treating humans/other agents as instruments — framing training environment design as a core safety and labor-trust issue.
- California's new "No Robo Bosses Act" bars employers from using AI as the sole/principal tool for employment decisions like discipline or dismissal — direct regulatory movement on AI's role in workplace management.

## Quote

> "Not thinking like a human" is of course a poor measure of "not being intelligent" — maybe they will just think superhumanly in weird ways.
