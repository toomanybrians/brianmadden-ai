# Executive Briefing: 10 Hours Saved by AI Don't Become 10 Useful Hours on Their Own. How to Commit That Capacity to Revenue Your Company Couldn't Fund Before.

## Insights

- Frames AI access itself as a new, discrete economic input — something companies now "buy" similarly to labor or capital — but one that comes with no built-in guidance on where to apply it.
- Argues the hard problem isn't acquiring AI capability but allocating it: deciding which customer problem to solve, which systems to connect, and how to make the resulting output useful enough that customers adopt it.
- Suggests this allocation gap is why finance/procurement struggle to fit AI into standard contracts — ROI can't be established before the useful applications are even discovered.
- Claims individuals are being pushed into de facto "managers of intelligence" roles without a management title, becoming responsible for outcomes/work scope far beyond what they could personally execute.
- Notes that AI's economic ambiguity can justify companies paying for more than model access — e.g., deeper partnerships, outcome-based pricing, or forward-deployed engineers — to supply the missing judgment layer.
- Previews a structural breakdown for evaluating AI deals: separating what's billed vs. who does the work vs. who owns the upside, and distinguishing value a company creates from value it actually keeps.

## Quote

> We're becoming managers of intelligence before anyone gives us a management title.
