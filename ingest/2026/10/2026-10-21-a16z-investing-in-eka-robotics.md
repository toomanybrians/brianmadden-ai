# Investing in Eka Robotics

## Insights

- Eka Robotics (a16z-led Series A) frames dexterous manipulation — not perception or planning — as the key unsolved bottleneck in robotics, since most physical work involves variable, deformable, misaligned real-world conditions that rigid industrial automation can't handle.
- Eka's core technical approach is a "Vision-Force-Action" model that co-designs sensing, control, model, and hardware together, using force/contact feedback (not just vision) so robots can adjust grip, rotation, or pressure mid-task rather than failing or erroring out.
- Rather than training via human demonstration (which is slow and can't capture rare failure modes), Eka trains manipulation policies through reinforcement learning in physics simulation, aiming to let robot "experience" scale with compute rather than human labor.
- The historically hard "sim-to-real" gap (simulated contact dynamics not matching real-world friction/compliance/actuation) is presented as the central technical risk the founders are positioned to address, given backgrounds in sensorimotor learning (MIT) and foundational RL algorithms (Berkeley/DeepMind).
- Reported performance: up to 1,500 picks/hour in lab bin-picking/induction tasks, ~2.5x human speed, with demonstrated handling of fragile, slippery, and contact-rich insertion tasks — though sustained performance over full production shifts is noted as still unproven.
- Economic argument: if adaptable dexterity becomes a general capability rather than requiring per-task engineering/tuning, it could make automation viable for tasks and volumes that previously didn't justify a dedicated integration project — expanding the addressable scope of "automatable" physical work.
