# Google X's Astro Teller: The $1B Bet No CEO Will Back, Moonshots 3x Cheaper in 16 Yrs, and Clean Water at 1¢/L| EP #300

## Insights

- Teller's "moonshot" definition requires three components: a named huge world problem, a science-fiction-sounding product/service that would solve it, and a breakthrough technology giving at least a sliver of a chance of building it.
- X deliberately keeps graduation teams tiny (~18 people) even inside a half-trillion-dollar parent company, arguing moonshot success is a mindset/culture problem, not a resourcing problem — a claim worth weighing skeptically given how much Alphabet-specific slack that argument assumes.
- Teller claims AI is cutting the cost-per-graduated-moonshot at X by roughly 10-20% year over year (down ~3x over 16 years), attributing this partly to AI-accelerated de-risking, not just organizational learning.
- He expects AI to raise the "audacity ceiling" — letting teams credibly pursue more far-fetched ideas — while keeping the overall structure (large funnel, small graduation rate, ~2%) the same.
- Core organizational claim: disruptive bets must be structurally sequestered from core-business incentives (10% profit mandates kill 10x thinking), reporting directly to the CEO/parent rather than being embedded in normal P&L review — framed as the main reason other companies fail to replicate X's model.
- Emphasizes killing ideas fast and cheaply ("train the monkey before building the pedestal") as a deliberate counter to institutional incentives that reward visible progress over honest-but-early failure.
