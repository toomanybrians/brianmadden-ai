# How to foster health: Redistribute AI wealth!

## Insights

- Two healthcare conferences showed divergent AI postures: clinical-trial operators (DPHARM) treat AI as a straightforward efficiency tool for data collection, patient recruitment, and drug approval, with over half of exhibitors having "AI" in their names; healthcare-delivery leaders (HE Connect) were far more ambivalent, focused on AI's effects on jobs, patient care, and fund allocation rather than efficiency.
- In drug development, the "scarce resource" is time to assemble trial data, which AI can genuinely compress — whereas in care delivery, the scarce resources are trust, money, and human attention, which AI cannot manufacture.
- Much of healthcare's dysfunction is attributed to billing/revenue-cycle infrastructure (e.g., EPIC's dominance) rather than clinical need, meaning AI applied to "connecting silos" still operates inside an extraction-heavy financial system rather than fixing it.
- A sourced example (Lumeris/"Tom") frames incumbent healthcare AI as competing against subsidized, ad-funded general chatbots (Gemini, Claude) that lack patient-protecting guardrails; over half of patients who consult generic chatbots about health never follow up with a human provider.
- Core argument: AI-driven profits/savings should be redistributed as higher wages for healthcare and adjacent care/education workers, and invested via government-paid (not government-run) health and social infrastructure (child care, schools, community programs) to build long-term population resilience — because the biggest returns on health lie outside the healthcare system and on a multi-year horizon no single company will underwrite.
- AI's best future role is cast as modeling/predicting the impact of upstream interventions (citing Nurse-Family Partnership, Head Start, ACEs research) rather than optimizing treatment after illness occurs.

## Quote

> If we want to fix health using AI, maybe it's simpler to start by fixing income disparities.
