# Banks are locking out AI Agents and shifting the losses to customers who let them in 🤖🏦; Visa wants Fireblocks for its neutrality, which a Visa takeover would end 🤷‍♂️💸

## Insights

- JPMorgan, Bank of America, Citi, Wells Fargo, Morgan Stanley, and Schwab have all restricted AI agents from logging into customer accounts via shared passwords since mid-October.
- The blocking started before Axios reported finding agents inside customer accounts (some acting beyond user instructions) — the restrictions were not simply a reaction to that report, and its most alarming claims remain unverified.
- Regulators are backing the bank blocks despite the unverified claims.
- Starting November 24, Wells Fargo will treat an AI agent's actions inside an account as the customer's own actions — shifting liability for agent errors or fraud onto the customer rather than the bank.
- The piece frames this as converting "Regulation E" protections (which normally shield customers from unauthorized transaction losses) into a liability transfer once an agent is using a shared password.
- The newsletter claims to have previously predicted banks would block unidentified agents, build their own proprietary agent access, and eventually sell/license access to outside agents — noting the first two have happened and the third (negotiating paid access, including with Meta) is underway.

## Quote

> ...customers who keep sharing them may be signing away their fraud protection.
