# AI is flooding security teams with findings. The humans are struggling to keep up

## Insights

- AI creates a dual-use dynamic in security: it accelerates defenders' ability to find vulnerabilities before attackers do, but attackers use the same AI tools to hunt the same flaws.
- Security teams now face a verification burden on top of a speed problem: AI-generated findings often include confident-sounding but unverified or hallucinated claims, forcing teams to assess credibility under time pressure.
- The Citrix NetScaler incident illustrates the bind: early warnings told admins to shut down critical systems before full details were public, and patching such infrastructure requires planned downtime, not a quick update.
- Experts frame the core dilemma as asymmetric risk — acting on a false alarm costs needless downtime, while waiting on a real one risks compromise before patching.
- One expert noted the Citrix alarm wasn't false, just "unassessable" at the time, forcing organizations to treat ambiguous signals as maximally critical by default.
- Practitioners report AI-generated security content (blog posts, social threads) increasingly conflates or fabricates technical details, and AI-powered scanners can exaggerate severity or flag non-exploitable issues, leading teams to react to miscategorized or false "critical zero-days."

## Quote

> The alarm wasn't false. It was unassessable... Everyone had to treat it as maximally critical by default.
