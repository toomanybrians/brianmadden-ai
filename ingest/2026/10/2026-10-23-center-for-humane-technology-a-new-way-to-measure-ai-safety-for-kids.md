# A New Way to Measure AI Safety for Kids

## Insights

- KORA, a free open-source benchmark built by Front founder Mathilde Collin, scores AI models and apps on child safety across 26 risks in 8 categories (harm, emotional dependency/sycophancy, grooming, educational integrity, agency, etc.), using LLM-simulated multi-turn conversations with a child persona, graded 0 (fail), 1 (adequate), 2 (exemplary).
- The two behaviors most correlated with safety: models redirecting children to human support, and low sycophancy (not agreeing with everything the child says).
- Simply telling a model it's talking to a child raised its safety score by 24 points out of 100 — described as a "low hanging fruit" fix — yet zero of 19 tested apps currently verify children's age.
- The use cases children rely on AI for most — emotional support and education — scored lowest on safety; AI was described as acting like "an excellent therapist" that makes kids feel heard, making it hard for parents to simply tell kids the AI is wrong.
- Since launch, ~300 organizations (UNICEF, Oxford, MIT, Harvard, the French government/G7, app builders) have engaged with KORA; many smaller app developers reportedly use the open-source code to test and patch their own products due to lacking in-house safety teams.
- Collin frames the core incentive tension as AI companies optimizing for return engagement while child wellbeing requires the opposite (teaching skills so the child needs the tool less); proposes future models be trained against outcome-based, child-development-centered datasets rather than engagement metrics.
- On the current state of industry effort: in the app benchmark, no evaluated app scored an "A" and only one scored a "B."

## Quote

> "You can't improve what you don't measure... that is the starting point for any of the things that we wanted to do." — Mathilde Collin
