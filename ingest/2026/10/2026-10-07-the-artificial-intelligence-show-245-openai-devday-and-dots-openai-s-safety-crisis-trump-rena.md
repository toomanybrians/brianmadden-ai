# #245: OpenAI DevDay and Dots, OpenAI’s Safety Crisis, Trump Renames Artificial Intelligence & Anthropic Targets Pre-Thanksgiving IPO Ad:

## Insights

- OpenAI's DevDay introduced "Dots" — always-on agentic assistants (powered by GPT-6 Astra) that work between conversations using their own cloud computer, connect to user apps, learn preferences over time, and can act proactively or ask approval based on user-set rules. Over 20 other announcements included GPT-6.1 Sol (cheaper, near-Astra-level model), a faster "UltraFast" tier, cloud-based Codex, an Agents API, ChatGPT Spaces for human/agent collaboration, and tiered pricing changes (Pro 200 vs. new Pro 500) that effectively cut usage at the existing price point.
- Hosts argue personal/always-on agents face a major adoption gap: most organizations still struggle with basic ChatGPT/Claude adoption four years after launch, and platform churn (e.g., GPTs/Gems being deprecated) sets practitioner learning back rather than forward — casting doubt on near-term mainstream traction for fully autonomous personal agents.
- A case is made that Apple (and possibly Meta via wearables) has structural advantage in the personal-agent race due to pre-existing deep trust and data access (health, finance) — potentially allowing a late entrant to "win" the agent layer despite shipping later than OpenAI/Google.
- Departing OpenAI safety researcher David Robinson published an Atlantic essay arguing the company's "fix problems after deployment" culture is inadequate for increasingly capable models, calling for aviation/nuclear-style layered safety redundancies; this follows multiple other safety-team departures/firings and reports that executives pressured teams to keep test timelines despite monitoring concerns, and OpenAI reportedly canceled a GPT-6.1 Astra release over safety issues.
- Trump signed an executive order mandating federal agencies use "super intelligence/SI" instead of "artificial intelligence/AI," alongside a non-binding industry "accord" on self-policing (echoing 2023's voluntary AI commitments); AI leaders present did not publicly object despite reportedly disagreeing, which commentary frames as a loyalty-test dynamic rather than substantive policy.
- Other developments: FTC confirmed an investigation into OpenAI, Anthropic, and others over potential consumer harms (pre-enforcement stage); a bipartisan Senate bill (AI Agent Accountability Act) would impose liability on operators/developers of agents that cause hacking damage; and NYT reporting revealed Anthropic has held private, confidentiality-bound discussions with religious scholars about AI consciousness and Claude's moral status, including internal tension with the Vatican's AI encyclical stance that AI does not feel joy or pain.

## Quote

> "We need to talk about culture. The future depends on wisdom that Silicon Valley lacks." — David Robinson (quoted in his Atlantic essay)
