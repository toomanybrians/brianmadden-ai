# Pluralistic: Agents vs ecommerce (28 Oct 2026)

## Insights

- Doctorow argues "agentic AI" repeats the core fallacy of the early-2000s Semantic Web: assuming the rest of the world will reorganize itself around a new technology's needs rather than its own interests.
- Single-chatbot agents (e.g., one bot booking flights) failed due to task complexity, leading to "swarms" of specialized bots — but chaining specialized bots compounds error rates multiplicatively, so more specialization increases overall system unreliability even as it improves individual components.
- Agentic AI push has a cost dimension too: Microsoft's plan to make Windows agent-ready involves streaming continuous screenshots of user activity to the cloud, raising privacy/confidentiality concerns.
- Ecommerce sites' profitability increasingly depends on opacity — surveillance pricing, drip pricing, junk fees, price-fixing, "most favored nation" deals — all of which are undermined by agents capable of automated comparison-shopping.
- These pricing tactics are often illegal, but enforcement is weak enough that companies find it cheaper to pay fines/lobby than to stop; this incentivizes continued resistance to legible, bot-friendly commerce.
- AI companies themselves reportedly plan to monetize this opacity (e.g., Google reportedly selling merchants tools combining surveillance data with AI for price-gouging), while ecommerce platforms are now actively mass-blocking AI shopping agents.

## Quote

> It's easy to come up with amazing applications for technology if you're allowed to assume that you can get everyone else in the world to set aside their priorities.
