# Public reports come out on a fixed schedule. They list every claim and verdict,

## Insights

- Proposes that evaluation reports on AI model safety be published on a fixed schedule, listing every claim, verdict, and the access level the evaluator requested versus received.
- Developers may append a statement disputing findings but cannot veto or overturn the evaluator's verdict.
- Verdicts are structured on a ranked scale designed to incentivize transparency: insufficient access to the model automatically yields the worst possible verdict.
- Self-disclosure is rewarded — a safety problem the developer reports on its own scores better than the same problem being discovered independently by the evaluator.
- The underlying design goal is to align incentives so that improving actual safety is the easiest way for a developer to achieve a better verdict, rather than gaming the process.

## Quote

> Insufficient access gives the worst verdict. A problem the developer reports itself scores better than one the evaluator finds.
