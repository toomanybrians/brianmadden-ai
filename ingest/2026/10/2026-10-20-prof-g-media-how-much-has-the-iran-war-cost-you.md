# How Much Has the Iran War Cost You?

## Insights

- CoreWeave's bonds dropped to distressed levels (below 80 cents on the dollar), signaling investor doubt about repayment of ~$50 billion borrowed to build AI data centers, and triggered a selloff wiping out nearly $1 trillion in market value across AI-linked stocks (Micron, Nebius, SK Hynix, Oracle, Nvidia).
- Analysts are openly questioning the AI trade's fundamentals: Jefferies noted credit risk now reflects doubt that large language models will ever be profitable; a Barclays analyst said no one has presented a credible framework for where AI infrastructure financing returns will come from.
- Gartner estimates 80% of "neocloud" AI infrastructure providers will cease independent operations by 2030.
- Ramp data shows top business spenders cut AI spending per employee by 3% month-over-month, suggesting enterprise AI demand growth may be softening.
- Open-weight models are rapidly gaining share of actual usage — 60% of tokens consumed by U.S. users on OpenRouter in August were open-weight (up from 26% a year earlier), mostly from Chinese models — reducing reliance on paying per-request to major AI labs.
- In response, U.S. players are pushing their own open-weight efforts: Nvidia released updated open models, and Meta/Apple announced Meta's next open-weight model will run locally on Apple's Mac Studio, letting developers avoid cloud/API costs entirely.

## Quote

> The reason credit risk has become more of an issue is that it can no longer be assumed … that large language models will ever be profitable.
