# The Morning Download: Quantum Research Races Ahead

## Insights

- Quantum computing startup Oratomic raised a $475M Series B just three months after its Series A, lifting its valuation from $1.5B to $5.4B and total funding to $775M — signaling accelerating investor enthusiasm even as some AI model developers discuss slowing down.
- Oratomic's CEO claims the company can build a commercially meaningful quantum computer with ~10,000 qubits, far below the prior industry consensus of ~1 million, with a target of a working machine by 2030; the differentiator is its error-correction approach.
- Investors (Vinod Khosla, Index Ventures) are explicitly drawing parallels between early quantum bets and early OpenAI investments, suggesting a shift in perception of quantum from "sci-fi" to commercially viable on normal VC timelines.
- Oratomic's CEO admits the business models and applications for quantum computing remain unknown; current funding is aimed purely at crossing the technical threshold to a "cheap and manufacturable" machine.
- Separately, US venture dealmaking hit $515.8B through Q3 2026 with AI capturing ~83% of deal value, while IPO exits remain stalled (only 18 companies went public in Q3) — creating a growing backlog of 992 active unicorns and liquidity pressure across the broader tech market.
- Report flags rising uncertainty in AI, including public pushback on AI/data centers and industry voices calling for a development slowdown — a risk factor given how concentrated 2026 deal value is in AI-dependent startups.

## Quote

> "We have no idea what the business models are going to be... but we need to cross this tipping point where we can build a working computer that is cheap and manufacturable." — Dolev Bluvstein, Oratomic CEO
