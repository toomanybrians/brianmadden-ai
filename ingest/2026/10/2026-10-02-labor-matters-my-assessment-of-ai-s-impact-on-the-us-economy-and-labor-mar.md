# My Assessment of AI’s Impact on the US Economy and Labor Market

## Insights

- Author estimates underlying US GDP growth near 3%, driven largely by AI-related business investment (data centers, chips, power); official AI-investment figures are described as a floor since supporting infrastructure (plants, grid) isn't labeled "AI" in national accounts.
- Productivity growth has roughly doubled vs. the 2010s (~2.5%/year) and is heavily concentrated in finance, insurance, information, and professional/business services (FIIPB, ~4%/year) plus advanced manufacturing and retail — while most of the rest of the economy shows near-zero productivity growth, a pattern read as inconsistent with a broad cyclical rebound.
- FIIPB employment fell for 36 consecutive months (Sept 2023–Aug 2026) even as output rose ~13% and hours fell ~2% — a combination the author says has historically only occurred during/after recessions, estimating 1.5–2.6 million "missing" white-collar jobs; clerical roles are hit hardest while judgment-heavy professional roles hold up.
- Young bachelor's-degree holders (22-27) show unemployment up ~1.5 points since late 2022 — three times the rise for non-degree young workers — with a shrinking bachelor's/associate wage premium, attributed to AI absorbing entry-level research/drafting/coding/analysis tasks plus anticipatory hiring caution.
- Argues AI-exposure-score studies finding "no labor market effect" are measuring the wrong thing — exposure ≠ actual adoption/displacement — and cites his own comparison of 29 occupation groups where exposure scores failed to predict employment outcomes (one had no relationship, another pointed the wrong direction).
- Estimates 1.5–2% of knowledge workers now hold jobs that wouldn't exist without AI, with job creation concentrated in a few tech hubs (Bay Area's share of US market cap has roughly doubled since 2009); argues major job losses/restructuring will be delayed until the next recession, drawing an analogy to manufacturing automation's delayed visibility after 2000.
