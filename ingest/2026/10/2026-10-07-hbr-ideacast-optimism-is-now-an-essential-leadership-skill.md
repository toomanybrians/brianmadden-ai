# Optimism Is Now an Essential Leadership Skill

## Insights

- "Resilient optimism" is defined as optimism that persists through repeated setbacks and prolonged change, distinct from fleeting situational optimism at the start of a project or role.
- Data from an 11-year study of 25,000+ business professionals links high optimism to 3x higher engagement, 5x lower burnout, 31% higher productivity, 37% higher sales, and 40% greater promotion likelihood.
- Rational/resilient optimism is distinguished from naive positivity: it requires clear-eyed acknowledgment of real risks and problems while maintaining belief that one's own behavior can still change outcomes.
- Pessimism is reframed not as accurate risk assessment but as the belief that one's actions won't matter — which drives disengagement and passive "head down" behavior in organizations.
- A "commitment triage" tool (core-impact / not-to-do / secondary-work lists) is offered as a way to reclaim time and reinforce a sense that effort is producing visible progress.
- Leaders are described as "broadcasters" whose nonverbal affect and meeting openers ("power lead" — starting with a positive data point) measurably shift team mood and have been linked to major performance gains (e.g., a sales team's revenue up 50%/$300M after adopting optimism-focused morning meetings).
- Reanalysis of longitudinal data shows most people still self-identify as optimists, but expression of optimism (speaking up, encouraging others) has declined sharply over time, framed as a culture-level problem since optimism functions as transmittable "information," not just private belief.

## Quote

> Optimism fuels nearly every single business, health, and educational outcome that we know how to track.
