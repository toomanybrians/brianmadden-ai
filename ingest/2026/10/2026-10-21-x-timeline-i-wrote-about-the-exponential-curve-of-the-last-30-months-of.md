# I wrote about the exponential curve of the last 30 months of AI development: htt

## Insights

- Since GPT-4's release in early 2023, twenty different models (from Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI only) have held the "best in the world" title at various points, and 180 models now surpass GPT-4's original capability — 19 of which can run on a smartphone.
- On the METR Long Horizon Benchmark (amount of human work an AI can complete with 50%+ success from one prompt), capability doubled eight times between GPT-4's release and April 2026, going from a 4-minute task to 17 hours of work — at which point METR said it could no longer accurately measure further gains; other estimates suggest current capability may be around 17 weeks of work.
- Cost for solving GPQA-level questions (graduate-level problems) dropped from 30 cents to four-hundredths of a cent per correct answer over the same period.
- AI capability remains "jagged" — long-form writing stayed weak while math went from terrible to superhuman in the same 30-month window; even in strong domains, small gaps still require human involvement.
- A third mode of human-AI collaboration is emerging beyond "centaur" (divided work) and "cyborg" (blended work) from Co-Intelligence: the "conjurer," who summons agents, delegates tasks, and evaluates results without staying in the loop on execution — more powerful but with reduced visibility into how results are produced.
- Demonstrated via Claude (Opus 5.5) in "Projects" mode acting as an orchestrator that spins off sub-agents: author delegated chart-making and a full image-to-video creative project (otter-on-a-plane concept, script, visuals, music) with only high-level, informal feedback like "make it better."

## Quote

> AI is improving exponentially but unevenly, and the way we use it is shifting from chatbots to agents that do real work.
