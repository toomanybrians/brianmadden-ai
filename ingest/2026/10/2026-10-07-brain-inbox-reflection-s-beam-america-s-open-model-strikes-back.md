# 🔦 Reflection's Beam: America's Open Model Strikes Back

## Insights

- Reflection AI released Beam, a 501B-parameter open-weight mixture-of-experts model (only 23B active per token) aimed at coding/agent work, claiming parity with Zhipu's larger GLM-5.2 using 3-4x less inference compute — though it trails on the HLE reasoning benchmark and lags China's Kimi K3 and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash on coding-agent scores (44.4% vs 68-74%).
- Beam was trained fast: pretraining began in July on 23.8T tokens, followed by a four-week RL run on 10,500 Nvidia GB300 chips with 100M+ practice attempts — described by its creator as the largest openly documented RL run; weights ship under Apache 2.0 later this month, unverified by outsiders so far.
- A commentator (Nathan Lambert, Ai2) groups Reflection with Nvidia and Thinking Machines as American labs whose best open models still trail Chinese counterparts, framing Beam as a bid to give Western buyers a credible home-run open-weight alternative rather than a benchmark leader.
- SemiAnalysis pricing analysis finds Claude's $200/month Max plan delivers ~5x more API-equivalent value than OpenAI's equivalent ChatGPT Pro plan for agentic coding workloads, a gap that widened after OpenAI recently halved what its $200 plan includes; caveat is the math assumes full usage of monthly limits.
- Separately, The Information reports Microsoft has cut internal Claude spending by about a third and Meta is pushing staff toward its own tools as AI costs rise — a signal of enterprise customers pulling back from paying for frontier model usage even as consumer pricing looks favorable for Anthropic.
- Other items: Cloudflare open-sourced small, fast "decision models" (Clef/Clef-flash) for structured agent sub-tasks like ticket routing, positioned as cheaper alternatives to full chatbot calls; Ghost launched a $3,499 always-on home AI computer with no subscription; OpenAI will begin invisibly watermarking ChatGPT/Codex text for EU users to comply with the EU AI Act, though its own tests show watermark detection drops sharply (92%→17%) under light paraphrasing.
