# Ten months ago, I visited the Arizona desert on the front lines of the AI data center boom. Here’s what’s happened since

## Insights

- A 2,000-acre Arizona site (Hassayampa Ranch), backed by investors including Chamath Palihapitiya, won unanimous rezoning approval in April after initial approval in December, clearing the way for a proposed $25 billion AI data center development.
- Local opposition shifted substantially: previously vocal opponents Kathy and Ron Fletcher signed a letter of support after months of negotiated concessions from the developer.
- Concessions used to convert opponents into supporters included larger setbacks, placing office buildings (not data center structures) nearest to neighboring homes, rerouting roads away from residences, and preserving a desert landscaping buffer.
- The case illustrates a negotiation playbook hyperscale data center developers are using to overcome rural community resistance — trading physical design concessions for political/social license to build.
- The project is part of a broader wave of gigawatt-scale AI data center developments (comparable to the Oracle-OpenAI Stargate site in Texas) reshaping rural land use in Arizona, Michigan, Texas, and Louisiana.
