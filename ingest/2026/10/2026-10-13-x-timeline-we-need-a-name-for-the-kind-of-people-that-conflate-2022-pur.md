# We need a name for the kind of people that conflate 2022 pure LLMs that were in

## Insights

- Gary Marcus argues there's a recurring rhetorical conflation between early (2022) "pure" LLMs — which he says had some resemblance to stochastic parrots — and 2026-era systems that combine LLMs with symbolic harnesses, code interpreters, and other symbolic components.
- He suggests this conflation is common enough to warrant a specific name/label for people who make this error, implying it's a frequent and sloppy argumentative move in AI discourse.
- He notes he did not coin the "stochastic parrots" phrase and does not personally endorse it as a characterization.
- The quoted post (Emad Mostaque, via @rohanpaul_ai) claims 10,000 AI agents working 88 hours solved a proof of Navier-Stokes, framed as equivalent to 100 years of top-level mathematician labor, and poses the rhetorical question of whether humans can cognitively do anything the AI can't.

## Quote

> We need a name for the kind of people that conflate 2022 pure LLMs...with 2026 systems that couple those LLMs together with symbolic harnesses.
