# 'This is absolutely not being overhyped': Sherrod DeGrippo on the PHP flaws and AI's cybersecurity reckoning

## Insights

- Two long-standing PHP vulnerabilities (affecting an estimated 60-70% of websites) were found with AI assistance and disclosed via coordinated effort between OpenAI, Wiz, PHP maintainers, and security firms; Palo Alto Networks alone identified 22 million exposed instances.
- AI is described as dramatically accelerating vulnerability discovery — finding high-severity flaws faster and cheaper than before — which is shifting cybersecurity into a higher-frequency, higher-severity "new normal" rather than a one-time event.
- The vulnerability disclosure process itself (coordinated, bug-bounty-based) is viewed as mature and shouldn't be fundamentally restructured despite AI's acceleration effect — the concern is organizations misusing early warnings, not the process design.
- Traditional vulnerability management is framed as insufficient going forward; organizations need resilience planning (backups, network segmentation, response plans) as a baseline requirement, not a nice-to-have, since patching fast enough is no longer assumed possible.
- A distinct emerging risk flagged: "non-human identities" (machine-to-machine logins, service principals, certificates, cookies) which can't use multifactor authentication and are often unin­ventoried within organizations.
- The interviewee frames AI's vulnerability-finding capability as double-edged — a "flashlight" exposing long-existing flaws in old, unglamorous infrastructure (like PHP) that underpins the internet, enabling faster industry-wide remediation despite the alarm it also triggers.

## Quote

> AI is that flashlight under the subway car, showing us the little flaky, rusted pieces and the broken axle.
