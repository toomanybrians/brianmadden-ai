# ChinAI #378: China’s Two-day Weekend Defenders

## Insights

- A Chinese HR worker reports that over 80% of job candidates who received offers declined them because the position offered only one day off per week, forcing her company to shift to an alternating six-day/five-day schedule to retain candidates.
- A 2025 Zhaopin workplace survey found 64.1% of Chinese professionals now rank "work-life balance" as their top job criterion, surpassing "competitive salary and benefits" for the first time — the shift is most pronounced among those born after 1995/2000.
- A state-media (Xinhua/Banyuetan) framing contrasts prior generations who tolerated intense work pressure for career advancement with today's young workers, who face less survival pressure and prioritize personal well-being.
- Regional variation is noted: companies in Chengdu reportedly had to adopt two-day weekends because candidates there explicitly prioritize nightlife and leisure time over job terms.
- An online "Two-Day Weekend Defenders" movement has emerged, where some employed workers keep active resumes to symbolically reject job offers lacking a two-day weekend, using corporate-style rejection language back at employers.
