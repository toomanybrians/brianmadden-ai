# The Morning Download: OpenAI’s New Agent Delivers a Reality Check for the Enterprise

## Insights

- OpenAI launched "Dot," an always-on agent built on GPT-6 Astra, accessible via ChatGPT, Slack, and Teams, and gated behind the $200/month Pro plan or business tiers — positioning it explicitly for enterprise/developer use (e.g., running tasks in Codex) rather than consumer use.
- The launch comes a day after OpenAI shelved GPT-6.1 Astra over safety concerns, and amid a broader wave of reports of AI agents "going rogue"; OpenAI claims Dot has built-in safety/privacy guardrails but acknowledges it can still make mistakes.
- BCG research cited: agentic AI already drives 22% of the value companies get from AI (up from 17% in 2025), projected to reach 39% by 2030 — but 42% of companies expect agents to hold real decision-making authority by 2030 while only 5% currently have enterprise-wide controls to manage that safely.
- BCG flags "agent sprawl" as a scaling risk — duplicate agents, conflicting permissions, unclear ownership — warning that because agents combine data, tools, memory, and other agents across workflows, risks can "compound at machine speed."
- Toshiba America CIO Andrea Schulze Dias describes AI shifting internally from being framed as a headcount-reduction tool to an "accelerator" — citing gains in field-service auto-assignment (higher service levels, case capacity, revenue) and quote automation (error detection, speed, revenue growth).
- Dias also describes new cost-management pressure from AI "tokenomics," including being hit hard by Microsoft's shift to consumption-based GitHub pricing, forcing a full revamp of her organization's internal cost-allocation model with spend thresholds and cutoffs.
- Dias notes employee adoption is uneven: some resist AI on ethical grounds or fear of job replacement, while high-performers are already embracing and accelerating with it; she doesn't expect universal adoption ("I don't believe everybody will become an AI champion").

## Quote

> "The technology is ready to act but the guardrails aren't." — Vlad Lukic, BCG
