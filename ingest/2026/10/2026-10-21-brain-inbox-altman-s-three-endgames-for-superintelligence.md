# 🫠 Altman's Three Endgames for Superintelligence

## Insights

- Sam Altman reportedly told OpenAI staff the industry needs a chosen "endgame" for superintelligence development within a year, laying out three scenarios: an IAEA-style international safety agency (his preference), a "Demis plan" merging major labs into one CERN-like body, and an "Eliezer plan" where one lab achieves recursive self-improvement and uses the resulting advantage to cut off rivals' computing power.
- Altman called the single-winner ("Eliezer") scenario "terrible" but included it for completeness; a source suggested the naming was a pointed jab at Anthropic given Yudkowsky's affiliations there.
- The two paths Altman favors both require competitor labs and governments to cooperate, yet he reportedly also told staff OpenAI can't afford to cede ground now — a direct tension given labs are simultaneously racing toward competing public listings.
- Real-world cooperation is reportedly thin: OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic still disagree over who should test models under a proposed joint "Standards Authority for Frontier AI," and U.S. government oversight remains largely voluntary.
- Adjacent context in the same issue: Anthropic's co-founder Jack Clark told the IMF "the singularity has begun" and argued cooperation and "unusual and serious rules" are needed — directly tied by the newsletter to the cooperation Altman's preferred endgames depend on.
- Also noted: OpenAI is rushing to raise ~$30B (implying a $1.4T+ valuation) ahead of Anthropic's expected IPO, explicitly framed as trying to get its numbers locked in before investors can compare them to Anthropic's public filings.
