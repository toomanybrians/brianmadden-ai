# Getting to ROI: How Brands Turn Cost Centers into Revenue Engines with AI

## Insights

- Marketing/PR framing dominates: this is a curated customer-testimonial roundup from Dreamforce 2026, not independent technical reporting — specific metrics (e.g., Canada Goose's "89% of routine messaging inquiries" resolved autonomously) come from the vendor's own customers and should be read as selected success stories, not representative outcomes.
- Recurring theme across customers (AT&T, Canada Goose, Canon, Takeda): agentic AI is positioned as freeing human staff from routine/transactional work so they can shift toward higher-value, relationship-driven tasks — the "cost center to value/revenue center" reframing is the core strategic narrative Salesforce is pushing.
- Several customers emphasize iterative deployment driven by real usage data rather than upfront assumptions — Southwest uses live chat data to prioritize where to deploy agents next; Fisher & Paykel's Chief Digital Officer stresses learning from actual customer behavior over internal hypotheses.
- Governance/trust mechanisms are presented as prerequisites for scaling in regulated industries: Sammons Financial Group ran 200+ guardrail tests pre-launch, blocked the model from learning off live conversations to prevent drift, and built a "supervisor agent" kill-switch monitoring calls against CRM data.
- AT&T's approach includes a toggleable on/off control for agents, a "trust layer," and a proprietary in-house LLM specifically for interactions involving their own IP — suggesting enterprise customers are not fully relying on off-the-shelf models for sensitive data handling.
- Takeda's commentary ("AI is moving at such a rapid pace, you've got to keep up with it") signals that even compliance-heavy industries (pharma) feel pressure to continuously adapt agentic deployments rather than treating them as fixed implementations.
