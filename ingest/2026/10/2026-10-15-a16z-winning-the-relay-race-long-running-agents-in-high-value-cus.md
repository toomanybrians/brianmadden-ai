# Winning the Relay Race: Long-Running Agents in High-Value Customer Journeys

## Insights

- New "long-running" AI agents can pursue goals across dozens of steps and extended time horizons (weeks/months), moving beyond bounded tasks (one email, one call) to owning entire customer journeys end-to-end — e.g., insurance claim to payout, mortgage pre-approval to closing, lead to signed contract.
- Framed via a plaintiff law case study: a single agent could own a case from intake through settlement, spawning sub-agents to chase witnesses indefinitely, reviewing every document in detail (catching things like a missed brain-injury indicator), and maintaining a complete unified case record — addressing "value leakage" that occurs at each human handoff in a multi-step process.
- Because long-running agents own a greater share of the final work product, outcomes become more directly attributable to the agent, enabling vendors to shift pricing from cost-reduction/labor-replacement to outcome-based or revenue-share models — expanding the addressable market from "share of labor budgets" to "share of revenue."
- Red Ventures is cited as a pre-AI precedent for owning a full customer journey (search-to-close) and getting paid per outcome; its EBITDA decline (~$370M in 2022 to ~$160M est. in 2025) is used as early evidence that AI agents and AI-native discovery (e.g., personal AI assistants replacing search/comparison sites) are disrupting even sophisticated human-run funnel businesses.
- Three go-to-market models are proposed for long-running agent vendors: (1) sell as software/platform to existing enterprise teams, (2) sell as a service replacing an existing outsourced function (shorter sales cycle, existing budget lines), (3) go full-stack/vertically integrated by owning the underlying asset or business itself (e.g., buying debt portfolios rather than selling collection software) to fully capture value and avoid attribution disputes.
- Noted frictions for outcome-based pricing: legal restrictions (e.g., non-lawyers can't share in legal fees), lack of procurement precedent for outcome-based contracts, and unresolved attribution questions when agents and humans jointly produce an outcome.
