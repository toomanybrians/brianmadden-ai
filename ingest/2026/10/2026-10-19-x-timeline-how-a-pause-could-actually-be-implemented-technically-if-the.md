# How a pause could actually be implemented, technically, if there was a political

## Insights

- A 26-researcher multidisciplinary study (UC Berkeley, Princeton, Stanford, Harvard, Oxford) proposes a technically feasible, mutually verified international pause of frontier AI training lasting at least 10 years.
- The core mechanism: halt production of AI training chips and phase out existing stock, replacing them with "inference-only" chips that are hardwired to be unusable for training new frontier models even if stolen.
- The plan leverages the AI chip supply chain's concentrated chokepoints, arguing this makes enforcement and verification more tractable than other proposed AI governance mechanisms.
- The pause is designed to still permit use of existing AI products/inference, continued economic benefits (cheaper inference, reduced environmental/data-center friction), and AI-powered scientific/medical research, making it more politically palatable.
- The framework explicitly addresses two failure modes: "covert evasion" (secret training while appearing compliant) and "overt breakout" (openly abandoning the pause to race ahead).
- Authors draw analogies to historical dual-use coordination successes (nuclear energy control, ozone layer protection) as precedent that global coordination on AI is achievable given sufficient will and monitoring.

## Quote

> Coordination is possible if the decision makers care enough about the future, not just immediate payoffs.
