# Meta shakes up the frontiers, open and closed

## Insights

- Meta's Muse Spark Open (816B total/42B active params, 1M context window) is now the strongest American open-weight model, roughly cutting the US-China open model gap in half; previously 20+ Chinese open models beat the best Western ones.
- Muse Spark Open's custom license carries heavy restrictions (branding requirement, revenue-gating above $20M/month requiring separate licensing, acceptable-use policy, ambiguity around using outputs/derivatives), making it not truly open-source despite open weights; the author argues such restrictive licensing is an unnecessary strategic risk given licenses haven't historically determined adoption.
- Meta is now the only American company fielding both frontier open (Muse Spark Open) and frontier closed (Muse Wonder, Muse Wonder Full) models, with the closed models ranking just behind Anthropic's Opus 5.5 and comparable to GPT-6-Astra/Gemini-4-Argon.
- The author attributes Meta's turnaround to organizational structure — a flat org (Alex Wang reportedly has 200+ direct reports) avoiding the bureaucracy/politics blamed for the earlier Llama failures — contrasted with Gemini's reputedly harder-to-navigate org and xAI's talent turnover/organizational instability.
- Author's current lab ranking: Anthropic > OpenAI > Meta ≈ Gemini > xAI ≈ Chinese Open Weights > US Open Weights; frontier-capable competition is narrowing to five US labs (Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta, Gemini, xAI), with Microsoft a potential sixth.
- Raises a financial/strategic tension: frontier model-building now costs billions per model, squeezing "tier 3" players (Mistral, Nemotron, Reflection, Arcee); meanwhile Meta's ad business profitability versus Anthropic's IPO valuation (both near $2T) suggests one valuation is likely mispriced, with real-world model success still hard to predict from benchmarks alone.

## Quote

> We're at a weird equilibrium where this license is complex and unproven in court, but many players will likely ignore it.
