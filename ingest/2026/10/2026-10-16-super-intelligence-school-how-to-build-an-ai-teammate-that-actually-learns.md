# How to Build an AI Teammate That Actually Learns

## Insights

- Grok Bot's new "Team Bots" feature lets a shared AI teammate's skills, plugins, credentials, and memory be distributed across a team via Grok Bot and Slack, so one person's lesson to the bot becomes everyone's.
- Much workplace knowledge is informal and context-bound (e.g., "this client needs special handling") — it normally stays tacit or spreads unevenly between people, which is both a strength (team cohesion) and a weakness (lost knowledge when people leave).
- The core risk identified: instructions given for a specific situation get stored as general rules, and the bot applies them indiscriminately later, because it retains "only the words that were saved" without the surrounding context of who, why, or for which specific case.
- Shared AI memory raises a new organizational responsibility: deciding what should be shared vs. kept personal, and which instructions need verification before being trusted or propagated.
- The piece distinguishes "remembering" (recalling past interaction info) from "learning" (using correction to actually improve future task handling) as fundamentally different capabilities often conflated in AI teammate design.
- Frames the central challenge not as expanding AI memory capacity, but as curating what deserves to be remembered in the first place.

## Quote

> A team can survive an employee forgetting an instruction. It may have a harder time when a shared Bot remembers it too well.
