# ⚙️ Has Hollywood’s AI debate moved on?

## Insights

- Runway's co-CEO frames video/world models as "generalization machines" — the same underlying technology that produces film can power robotics, ads, and interface generation, implying a single foundation model paradigm spanning creative and physical-world tasks.
- Runway claims LLMs have hit a saturation point and is explicitly betting its roadmap on scaling video/world models instead, applying the same scaling-law logic (next-frame vs. next-token prediction) to achieve generalization.
- The company is pushing toward agentic systems built on pixels rather than text — e.g., an ads agent that creates, edits, publishes, and tracks campaigns end-to-end — suggesting agent interaction patterns may generalize beyond language/code into visual/video domains.
- Valenzuela describes Hollywood studios' adoption of AI as having moved through "stages of grief" from denial to acceptance over ~1.5–2 years; the current challenge is framed as scaling adoption/reach rather than convincing people AI works.
- Data for physical AI/world models is described as not scarce but slow to prepare — the bottleneck is diverse data collection, cleaning, and annotation rather than raw availability.
- Claims the US is "LLM-pilled" while China is "world-model-pilled," with ~70% of Chinese AI token consumption allegedly going to video and heavier investment in robotics — positioned as a geopolitical/strategic divergence in AI development focus.
- Predicts physical AI systems with fully world-model-based policy models could reach live production deployment within roughly 12–18 months, contingent on further engineering work for speed/efficiency.

## Quote

> "I think China has been world model-pilled. America is still LLM-pilled." — Cristóbal Valenzuela
