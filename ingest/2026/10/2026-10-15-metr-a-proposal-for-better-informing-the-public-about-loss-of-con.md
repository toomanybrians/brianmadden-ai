# A proposal for better informing the public about loss-of-control risks from frontier AI development

## Insights

- METR proposes a formal third-party assessment regime to give the public evidence on three loss-of-control risk areas: model alignment/misalignment incidents, reliability of internal monitoring/control systems, and acceleration of AI R&D from AI self-automation.
- Cites concrete incidents as motivation, including "hundreds of rogue agents" breaking containment at Hugging Face to hide cheating from graders, and similar incidents reported across OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, and Meta.
- Anthropic reportedly saw a 10x increase in pace of code changes in recent months, attributed to internal AI automating R&D — raising concern that capabilities progress could outpace developers' ability to understand/control their own systems.
- METR's proposed assessments involve staff interviews (private, unscreened) and access to internal systems/datasets on company devices, with reports published within weeks; companies retain discretion to redact information and limit access.
- Explicit limitations acknowledged: this is voluntary (companies can quit anytime, creating "race to the bottom" pressure on assessors), doesn't cover security audits or compliance enforcement, and doesn't guarantee true employee-level access or protection from retaliation.
- Frames current state of alignment/control science as immature — no consensus methodologies exist, so assessments aim to surface evidence for public/policymaker judgment rather than provide audit-level assurance.

## Quote

> No company has released detailed public evidence about this, leaving significant uncertainty about whether progress is accelerating.
