# ⚙️ White House AI safety plan skips new regulations

## Insights

- OpenAI launched "Dots" — always-on AI agents with dedicated cloud computers that work across apps like Slack, Messages, and Teams, acting autonomously once a goal is set; initially limited to one Dot per user, with multi-agent teams planned.
- Dots run on GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI's most capable and most "aligned" model) and include permission controls: Custom Rules define autonomous vs. approval-required vs. forbidden actions, with explicit consent required for sensitive actions like password changes or data deletion.
- OpenAI's DevDay strategy (Dots, GPT-6.1 Sol, Ultrafast tier, Private Intelligence, Codex-in-cloud, a new Marketplace) is read as targeting Anthropic and power users/enterprises rather than competing with Meta's consumer-focused Muse agent.
- Pricing shifts accompanied the launch: new $500/month tier with "Ultrafast" speed, while existing $200/month Pro plan usage allowances were cut (20x→10x ChatGPT Work/Codex usage, GPT-6 Pro weekly messages halved).
- Trump and six AI industry leaders (Amodei, Pichai, Zuckerberg, Brockman, Musk, Huang) signed a voluntary "White House Accord on Super Intelligence," pledging self-regulation (internal controls, internal oversight team, external audits, board-level safety committee) with no new binding laws.
- The accord explicitly leaves open that these steps "may" be codified into law later, with the article's commentary noting industry self-policing creates inherent conflict since the same companies are racing to push capability frontiers.

## Quote

> "over time, it may make sense to codify these steps into laws and regulations."
