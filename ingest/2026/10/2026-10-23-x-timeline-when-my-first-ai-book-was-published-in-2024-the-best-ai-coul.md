# When my first AI book was published in 2024, the best AI could do 4 minutes of h

## Insights

- Using the METR Long Horizon Benchmark (time-horizon of tasks an AI can complete with 50% success), AI capability went from 4 minutes of human-equivalent work (early 2024, GPT-4 era) to 17 hours (April 2026) to an estimated 17 weeks (October 2026) — the benchmark itself stopped being able to measure progress accurately beyond that point.
- Model leadership has fragmented: a single dominant model (GPT-4) defined 2023-2024, but twenty different models have since held "best in the world" status across Anthropic, Google, and OpenAI, with 180 models now surpassing original GPT-4 capability (19 of which run on smartphones).
- Cost collapse paired with capability growth: a benchmark exam (GPQA) that cost 30 cents per correct answer in AI usage now costs four hundredths of a cent.
- Despite overall gains, capability remains "jagged" — AI stayed weak at long-form writing while going from terrible to superhuman at math in the same period, and even where AI excels, small remaining gaps still require human involvement.
- Proposes a third mode of human-AI collaboration beyond "centaur" (divided work) and "cyborg" (blended work): the "conjurer," who summons agents, assigns them work, and evaluates results rather than staying continuously in the loop — described as more powerful but riskier due to loss of visibility into how results are produced.
- Illustrates the shift concretely: an author produced research, graphics, citations, and even a full concept video ("otter on an airplane using wifi") via a single orchestrator agent spinning up sub-agents, with minimal prompting effort compared to the chatbot-era back-and-forth of 2024.

## Quote

> AI is improving exponentially but unevenly, and the way we use it is shifting from chatbots to agents that do real work.
