# A Perfect Storm Popping The A.I. Bubble

## Insights

- This is a promotional opinion piece. It names no sources for its central claims, closes with subscribe and follow-us calls, and its demo anecdotes are unattributed and unverified. Treat it as one publication's thesis, not as evidence.
- Thesis: the AI data-center bubble is no longer a prediction, it is unwinding now. The author points to half-built data centers with no tenants, neocloud borrowing costs approaching 13%, Oracle as the likeliest first big failure, and delayed or doubtful IPOs for the two leading labs. He says the exposure runs through insurers' data-center portfolios and the mutual funds in ordinary retirement accounts.
- Shape of the collapse: he expects an 18-month slow deterioration of the narrative, not a single crash, and says the money already sunk into land, power rights and construction cannot be recovered.
- The pivot is the real argument: he says the unwind clears the way for "distributed computing at invariant scale" (a term he promises to define in later posts), and that centralized data centers are unnecessary, too expensive, widely opposed and a single point of failure. He cites the Iran war and cheap drones as exposing the risk of concentrating compute, and says the military is being pushed toward distributed compute.
- Demo claims, all unverified: an AI given only an ERP system's user interface and data rebuilt a parallel system in about 24 hours that ran reports roughly 1,000 times faster at a tenth of the cost on a shoebox-sized machine; a hospital revenue-recovery system moved from concept to production in a quarter and recovers 10% to 17% of lost revenue; a grid-failure predictor ran without a data center. No vendors, customers or benchmarks are named.
- The enterprise-software claim he draws from them: any ERP can be replicated as a digital twin in a weekend, so SAP, Oracle, Salesforce and the wider SaaS family lose their subscription and consulting economics, and hedge-fund observers now attend such demos.
- The piece also carries a political charge (blame for the administration and the AI-boosting press, and local opposition to data centers). That is outside the scope of this note.
