# The Cost of Convenience

## Insights

- Agentic AI (e.g., Meta's Muse, OpenAI's Dots, xAI's Grokbot) marks a qualitative shift from prompt-response LLMs to autonomous systems that take a goal and pursue it independently, requiring users to hand over decision-making, system access, and sometimes hardware control.
- Argues engineered "friendliness" (cutesy mascots, free tiers) in agentic AI products functions like historical tobacco marketing (Joe Camel) — designed to lower psychological guardrails toward adoption; pairs this with the argument that believing oneself immune to manipulation is itself a major vulnerability.
- Claims organizations are deploying autonomous agents into unmapped, messy processes without having mastered basic LLM literacy first, citing his own "AI Effectiveness Rating" data showing "Impact" as the weakest of six measured disciplines.
- Warns that unsupervised AI cleaning up disorganized organizational processes will discard what it doesn't understand and bury the rest, making fixes more costly than the hoped-for savings.
- Challenges the idea of "AI standards," arguing most people's stated rules are just personal rationalizations ("AI should never be used for X" except how I use it) rather than genuinely collaborative, evolving standards.
- Pushes back on narratives of technological inevitability, arguing that external pressure (hype, social proof, marketing) doesn't remove personal responsibility for choosing to adopt agentic tools — individuals remain accountable for "opening the gate."

## Quote

> Most people's definition of 'acceptable AI use' is simply whatever they have personally rationalized doing themselves.
