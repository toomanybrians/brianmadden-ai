# Charts of the Week: Attention is All You Need

## Insights

- GPU rental rates for every model in a tracked series (including aging A100s) are climbing and now sit 9–143% above what straight-line 6-year depreciation would predict — rental pricing behavior inconsistent with "GPUs are becoming obsolete" narratives.
- Token consumption (per OpenRouter data) has been in a steep exponential climb since spring 2026, with proprietary/closed models even gaining share recently, reinforcing that compute demand is still accelerating rather than cooling.
- The domestic AI-capex manufacturing buildout (semis, communications, electrical equipment) is mostly being met by squeezing more utilization out of existing capacity and leaning on imports, rather than building new domestic capacity — electrical equipment/switchgear (needed for grid buildout) stands out as the sector where capacity has actually declined despite surging prices.
- Output gains in AI-buildout manufacturing sectors are coming disproportionately from existing workers logging more hours rather than net new hires (per ADP data), alongside rising gross pay and a ~5pp wage premium for job-switchers — evidence pointing to a skilled-labor shortage as a bottleneck to the compute buildout, not job displacement.
- Cybersecurity stocks, despite strong YTD gains tied to AI-driven risk narratives, have sold off sharply since October; revenue growth at major cyber firms has only modestly accelerated (and in some cases reversed) even as valuations ran up ~125%, suggesting the market is now demanding proof of AI-driven revenue rather than rewarding narrative.
- A few cyber vendors report early AI-linked traction (e.g., one citing >800% growth in agentic traffic in 9 months, others ~200% ARR growth for AI security products), but these are described as too small yet to move overall company growth numbers.
