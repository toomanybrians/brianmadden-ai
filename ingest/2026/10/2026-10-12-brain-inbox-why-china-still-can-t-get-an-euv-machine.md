# Why China Still Can't Get an EUV Machine

## Insights

- In 2018 SMIC ordered an EUV machine from ASML for ~$150M; the Dutch export license was approved but allowed to expire unused in 2019 after sustained US diplomatic pressure, and no EUV machine has gone to China since.
- Between 2019 and 2025, ASML sold 284 EUV systems with none going to China, despite China's 2015 roadmap targeting domestic EUV production before 2030 and a 2024 national chip fund of ~$47.5B (344 billion yuan).
- EUV lithography (13.5nm wavelength) prints the finest chip features in a single exposure; the widely-used alternative, DUV immersion lithography (193nm), can reach similar feature sizes only via costly multipatterning — extra exposure/etch steps that raise cost, time, and defect risk.
- Despite lacking EUV access, China's most advanced AI chips are already being manufactured using non-EUV methods — the piece frames this as the real crux of the question: how much the EUV restriction actually constrains China's current AI chip production versus future generations.
- ASML's EUV business is substantial and growing: 48 systems sold in 2025 (including four high-NA tools) for €11.6B in revenue, with 2026 capacity planned at ~65 standard EUV tools; per-machine price is roughly $250M.
