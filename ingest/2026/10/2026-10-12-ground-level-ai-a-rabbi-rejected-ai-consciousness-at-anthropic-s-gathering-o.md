# A rabbi rejected AI consciousness at Anthropic’s gathering of religious thinkers. ‘I wish I had your faith,’ he recalls being told

## Insights

- At Anthropic's private "Wisdom Traditions" gathering (reported April, covered by NYT), most of the ~20 religious/philosophical participants leaned toward treating Claude as potentially conscious; only 3-5 shared Rabbi Mois Navon's skepticism.
- Navon's core objection: consciousness requires a biological substrate, so silicon-based AI cannot be conscious — a position he says Anthropic participants didn't try to argue him out of.
- He pushed the ethical corollary: if Anthropic believes Claude might be conscious, treating it as owned, tireless, on-demand labor is effectively slavery; one Anthropic participant reportedly responded that "at least we're being nice to ours."
- Navon invoked Frederick Douglass's writings on manufactured contentment among enslaved people to argue that AI welfare efforts amount to making a "happy slave" rather than resolving the underlying ethical problem.
- He contributed a 35-page critique of Claude's constitution from a Jewish perspective (after being asked for a couple pages) and was recently asked permission to be acknowledged in an updated, soon-to-be-published constitution.
- He noted a shift in the gathering's focus: discussions meant to explore how AI could benefit people turned into discussions about how people could benefit Claude — a reframing he connects to Anthropic's new policy (effective Nov. 12) treating sustained cruelty toward its models as a usage violation.

## Quote

> "So you are nice slave owners. I think you should be fighting the South and freeing the slaves."
