# Closing the Gap in Cancer Care

## Insights

- CureWise is an AI medical agent/navigator built by Steve Brown that aggregates a patient's full medical record from thousands of US health systems, then runs multiple AI agents modeled as a "virtual tumor board" that mimic different specialists and look for consensus.
- The tool's founder claims it flagged diagnostic leads and an off-label drug combination that human doctors missed, based on genetic variant analysis of his own cancer — illustrating AI synthesizing scattered data/literature faster than individual clinicians can.
- Underlying problem cited: medical knowledge is growing exponentially (1.3M new PubMed papers/year, ~3,500/day) while clinical practice lags, often relying on guidelines built around decades-old drugs and "average patient" trial data rather than individualized treatment.
- Swiss Rockets is pursuing a parallel but separate track: driving genome/transcriptome sequencing costs toward $100 (and eventually $10) to enable routine, repeated molecular monitoring for early cancer detection, alongside "theranostic" radioligand ("torpedo") molecules that both image and kill tumor cells.
- Framing: cost collapse in sequencing (~$100M in 2001 to a targeted $100 now) is presented as outpacing Moore's Law, with the argument that sufficiently cheap diagnostics shift from elite/rare use to routine testing — a pattern the piece says mirrors computing, solar, and telecom.
- Broader structural point: precision/personalized treatment is bottlenecked less by scientific discovery than by fragmented patient data and clinician bandwidth — the stated gap AI tools and cheap diagnostics aim to close.
