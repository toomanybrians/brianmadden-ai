# My tips for meeting AI safety people in the Bay (without moving there)

## Insights

- AI safety hiring is geographically concentrated in the Bay Area, and informal, in-person familiarity often precedes or substitutes for formal applications — especially for roles not yet well-defined enough to be officially posted.
- Proximity-based networking (short visits, conferences, coworking spaces, shared meals) can substitute for relocation: the author got short-term jobs because people she'd met in person later thought of her when a role came up.
- Effective networking in this world is framed as "being known" rather than pitching for a job in any single conversation — building low-stakes social familiarity over time is described as more valuable than direct asks.
- A practical tactic: when someone mentions a problem matching your skills, offer a small-scale (2-10 hour) trial task rather than a general pitch, as a low-commitment way to demonstrate fit.
- The AI safety professional culture is described as blending personal and professional life unusually closely ("the meeting is at the party"), with people also more blunt/skeptical in feedback than typical business environments.
- The piece treats this geographic/social clustering of AI safety talent as a structural fact with real trade-offs for the field, though it doesn't elaborate on what those trade-offs are.

## Quote

> People may also be more frank about their opinions of your work and plans than in other places.
