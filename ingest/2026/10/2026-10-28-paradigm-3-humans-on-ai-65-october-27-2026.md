# Humans on AI #65 || October 27, 2026

## Insights

- Microsoft is offering voluntary redundancy to 27% of its workforce (60,000 people), following an earlier small voluntary retirement program and summer layoffs; the newsletter frames this less as direct AI-driven job replacement and more as a capital reallocation move — trading headcount for GPU/compute spend, especially given Microsoft's relatively weak position in the underlying model market.
- Apollo's chief economist argues AI has delivered zero measurable financial payoff for most S&P 500 companies (ex-major AI players), citing flat profit margins and revenue-per-worker, and attributes this to AI's inability to grasp tacit organizational elements like trust, culture, and ethics — a claim the newsletter disputes as premature given how recently most firms have actually adopted AI at scale.
- Anthropic reports over 90% of contributions to its own internal codebase in September 2026 were written by Claude, with average code output per engineer running 45x higher than in 2024 — offered as a concrete internal metric of AI-driven productivity change inside a frontier lab.
- A gap in AI-driven productivity gains is emerging by job type: Anthropic researchers self-report ~4x output increases from AI use, while a separate survey of third-party AI safety researchers found only a 1.6x speedup, suggesting uneven returns depending on the nature of the knowledge work involved.
