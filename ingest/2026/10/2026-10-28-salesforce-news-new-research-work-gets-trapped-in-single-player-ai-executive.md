# New Research: Work Gets Trapped in Single-Player AI. Executives Are Ready for Multiplayer

## Insights

- Survey of 250 U.S. executives (director+ with AI investment authority, collected Sept 14–22, 2026) finds AI use at work is largely siloed: 68% see insights go unshared weekly, 66% see colleagues unknowingly duplicate AI-solved work, 59% see work stall because it's trapped in one person's tool.
- Interface fragmentation has a measurable cost: executives estimate 3.6 hours/week (~22 business days/year per employee) lost toggling between AI tools and other systems; only 11% report AI sitting in one integrated ecosystem with no time cost.
- Deployment has outpaced integration — 49% of orgs run AI agents with task/workflow autonomy, but only 15% have agents embedded seamlessly across multiple systems/interfaces.
- Siloed AI problems scale with company size: 73% of enterprise executives (2,500+ employees) report weekly work stalls from isolated AI use vs. 37% at SMBs (under 250 employees).
- 72% of executives say their ideal AI model is one with shared team context where value compounds, vs. 26% preferring individual, private AI use — framed by Salesforce/Slack as a shift from "single-player" to "multiplayer" AI.
- Anecdotal pain points cited include duplicated engineering work from separate AI chat histories and debugging time lost to lack of visibility into AI-generated code.

## Quote

> "The single-player era of AI made people faster. The multiplayer era is what makes a whole company smarter, together." — Rob Seaman, EVP/GM of Slack
