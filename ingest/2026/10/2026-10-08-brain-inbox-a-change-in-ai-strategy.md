# A Change in AI Strategy

## Insights

- Multiple data points suggest frontier AI models are commoditizing: OpenAI cut prices over 80% on one model to win share, frontier models' share of token usage dropped from 53% (August) into the mid-40s, and open-source demand has shifted toward medium/small-tier models rather than frontier ones.
- Enterprise AI spend among top adopters fell 9.7% in August (to $7,205/employee/month) even as overall token usage surged 50% since July and prices fell 41% — volume is compounding while per-dollar spend cools.
- Specialized "decision models" (e.g., TypeSafe's Jev) are capturing routing/classification/gating tasks at roughly 1/75th the cost of standard LLMs, suggesting task-specific small models are displacing general-purpose ones for narrow functions.
- Labs are responding with three strategic moves: (1) partnering/reselling other labs' models for high-margin toll revenue without bearing serving costs (e.g., OpenAI-Baseten, Grok routing to "best" model regardless of origin); (2) building sticky user-facing interfaces/"harnesses" to own distribution, potentially monetized via ads and commerce; (3) aggregating user data across routed models for training and intelligent routing advantages.
- The argument concludes that in a commoditizing model market, value accrues not to whoever has the best benchmark score but to whichever platform aggregates the most usage/distribution — illustrated by OpenAI's rise toward a ~$70B run rate via price cuts and marketplace aggregation, closing the gap with Anthropic.

## Quote

> In a commoditizing market, the spoils do not accrue to the lab with a marginal benchmark lead, but to the platform that aggregates the volume.
