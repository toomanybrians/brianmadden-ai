# 🔮 The internet under attack, AGI compression & the next oil shock that never will be... #607

## Insights

- Azhar characterizes the current wave of cyberattacks and software vulnerabilities as the internet's "Wuhan moment" — a crisis-level inflection point comparable to January 2020, where AI is turning minor bugs into serious vulnerabilities.
- He argues openness will likely have to be sacrificed in the short term to restore trust and security in cyberspace, but believes a long-term path to a more trustworthy internet and software ecosystem exists.
- He draws a historical analogy: major technological/social transitions (animal domestication, urbanization) have historically intensified a form of "pollution" or risk (zoonotic disease, disease spread) that was eventually met with a structural response (public health, sanitation) — implying a similar structural response may emerge for internet/software security.
- Demis Hassabis has repeatedly shortened his AGI timeline estimate: ~5-10 years at Davos in January, then 3-5 years, now 2-3 years per a WSJ interview.
- Azhar notes Hassabis's track record as a "measured" voice gives this accelerating estimate added weight, calibrated against what Hassabis observes inside his own lab.
- Even granting that "AGI" as a term is contested or ill-defined, Azhar frames Hassabis's estimate as marking a real capability threshold the world will need to contend with.

## Quote

> The pandemic gave us mRNA, and this crisis could give us software and an internet we can trust.
