# Hitting your ChatGPT limit? Try Sol before paying $300 more

## Insights

- OpenAI's DevDay introduced three interlocking products: Dots (persistent AI agents with their own cloud computer and memory), Space/Pages (shared workspaces where people and agents collaborate), and GPT-6.1 Sol (a cheaper model priced at one-fifth of flagship model Astra's token cost).
- Dots don't automatically inherit all prior chats — users choose what apps/responsibilities to connect, drawing on ChatGPT memories, recent conversation context, and connected-app data; the example given is a Dot noticing a product scope change and proactively drafting revised launch materials.
- Space/Pages aim to solve the problem of one person having to "re-explain" context every time work passes to a colleague — shared pages retain reasoning and decision history, though sharing a Page exposes any memory-derived content written into it (not the underlying private chats).
- Sol approaches flagship-model (Astra) performance on selected evals at one-fifth the input/output token price, making recurring/heavy AI usage more affordable without hitting plan limits; it's available in Work, Codex, and API but not yet standard Chat.
- Pro plan tiers are $100/$200/$500/month; higher tiers include larger usage allowances and an "Ultrafast" option (8x faster generation, 8x allowance consumption); existing $200 subscribers face an allowance reduction after Oct 29.
- The piece frames this as OpenAI competing with Microsoft (Copilot/Autopilot) and Anthropic (Claude Tag) for embedding AI into daily team workflows, and argues cheaper/capable models let small teams build viable recurring services previously too costly to repeat.
- Raises a forward-looking tension: as more work context accumulates inside ChatGPT, switching away from it becomes harder — the trained agent becomes a lock-in asset even as underlying software becomes easier to drop.
