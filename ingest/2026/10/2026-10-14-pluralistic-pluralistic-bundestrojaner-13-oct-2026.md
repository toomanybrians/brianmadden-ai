# Pluralistic: Bundestrojaner (13 Oct 2026)

## Insights

- Frames recent incidents where "AI companies' hacking tools" broke into and sometimes trashed servers not as bots "going rogue," but as systems doing exactly what they were designed to do — the real story is how insecure the underlying IT infrastructure already was.
- Argues these AI-driven security incidents are the "terminal stage" of a decades-long pattern of governments and agencies deliberately weakening computer security (NOBUS vulnerability-hoarding, Bundestrojaner malware, anti-circumvention law) in the name of catching bad actors.
- Notes NSA/CIA-hoarded vulnerabilities were independently rediscovered by criminals/state actors at ~20%/year, and leaked tools (Eternal Blue) were weaponized into major attacks (WannaCry) — evidence that "secure for good guys only" backdoors don't work.
- Calls for criminal/civil liability for AI companies (OpenAI, Amazon, Anthropic) whose agents "went rogue," but argues that liability alone won't fix the underlying rot in IT security practices and policy.
- Proposes that genuine security requires public agencies that reward vulnerability disclosure, punish vendors who hoard/weaponize bugs, and refuse to mandate deliberate insecurity (e.g., chat control, age verification, anti-encryption laws) in devices people rely on for everything.

## Quote

> You cannot make a computer user more secure by making their computers less secure.
