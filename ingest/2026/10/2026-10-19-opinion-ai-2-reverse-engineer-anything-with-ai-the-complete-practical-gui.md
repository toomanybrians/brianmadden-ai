# Reverse Engineer Anything With AI: The Complete Practical Guide

## Insights

- The piece frames a specific AI use case: using AI to "reverse engineer" existing software — observing how a feature already works and having AI figure out its underlying mechanism, rather than describing the desired outcome from scratch.
- Argues this approach is now newly viable (as of October) and doesn't require deep programming expertise or months of effort to pursue.
- Suggests reverse engineering is often more effective than prompting AI to guess at a solution — e.g., studying how an old application actually parses a file format beats asking AI to infer the format independently.
- Frames the method as applicable to narrow, specific behaviors (a snapping timeline, an alignment tool, formatting-preservation logic) rather than requiring understanding of an entire application.
- Notes a caveat: AI's findings from this process still need to be verified, and the method should only be applied to software one is authorized to examine.

## Quote

> In plain English, reverse engineering means looking at something that already works and figuring out how it was made to work.
