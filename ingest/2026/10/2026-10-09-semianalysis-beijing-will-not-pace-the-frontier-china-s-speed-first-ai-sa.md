# Beijing Will Not Pace the Frontier: China’s Speed-First AI Safety Regime

## Insights

- SemiAnalysis built an original dataset of 857 model releases from China's 9 leading AI developers (2021–Sept 2026) and found only 3.6% ever shipped with any published safety evaluation, and just 1.1% had results available at launch; 94.9% carry no safety disclosure at all.
- China's regulatory approach is "speed-based, not safety-based": its AI Safety Governance Framework 3.0 uses striking loss-of-control vocabulary (self-aware models, recursive self-improvement, shutdown-script disabling) but opens by naming innovation "first priority," and a comprehensive binding AI law was shelved in 2025 shortly after DeepSeek's debut.
- China's rules cluster entirely at the application/content layer (minors' protections, companion-AI rules, labeling, cybersecurity) with zero frontier-risk duties tied to training compute or model capability — stricter than the US/EU on content, looser on frontier thresholds (vs. EU's 10²⁵ FLOP or California SB 53's 10²⁶).
- Analysis of 65 public statements by lab founders/CEOs found only 15 engage with frontier safety at all; Zhipu (Z.ai) accounts for 6 of the 9 that express genuine concern, while DeepSeek's Liang Wenfeng has made zero public safety statements in four years and Alibaba's CEO gave a 6,000-word superintelligence speech never mentioning "safety" or "risk."
- A coded analysis of 102 expert/official texts found Chinese technical scientists (86% cite frontier risk) and legal/policy scholars (universally development-first, using the Xi-derived line "failing to develop is the greatest insecurity") talk past each other — and of 13 texts demanding binding frontier obligations, none achieved adoption; those with policy seats who made such demands were ignored.
- Supply-side safety institutions (SAIL, CAICT, CnAISDA, TC260) exist and produce frameworks/evaluations, but none have enforcement power, budget, or mandate — compliance is voluntary and no developer is obliged to submit models for testing.
- The US-China AI race has reframed Chinese safety discourse toward "security" (e.g., export controls as supply-chain risk) and prompted some Chinese engineers/scholars to frame Western safety rhetoric as geopolitical "cognitive-domain shaping" or fear-based marketing rather than genuine concern.
