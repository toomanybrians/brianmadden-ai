# Steering AI Beyond Automation

## Insights

- Argues AI development isn't a technologically determined path toward total automation; it's a contested design space, and "augmentation" (human-AI collaboration) is a viable alternative direction currently under-resourced relative to automation.
- Identifies the "black box" problem: most AI agents don't reveal intermediate process steps because training data (e.g., open-source code repos) rarely captures process traces — proposes "white-box agents" that expose sources, methods, assumptions, and uncertainty as a path to genuine collaboration.
- Names four concrete technical research gaps the automation agenda neglects: white-box/legible agents, low-latency real-time interaction (to preserve "flow" rather than cause burnout), continuous/non-turn-based human-agent collaboration, and evaluation methods for human-AI teams rather than solo-agent benchmarks.
- Frames a "trilemma" for industrial AI deployment: fully autonomous, reliable in high-stakes settings, and truly general-purpose — pick two — suggesting automation-first strategy is economically strained even on its own terms.
- Traces automation's dominance to economic incentives (labor is the biggest cost, capital favored over labor in tax policy) plus CS culture itself (clean benchmarks favor autonomous systems; HCI's human-collaboration tradition has been marginalized since the 1960s).
- Proposes "automate and compensate" (automation + UBI) is politically unrealistic in the current US political economy and unwanted by most workers, who want real work, not welfare.
- Sketches other complementarity models beyond direct collaboration: AI mediating team coordination ("collective intelligence"), enabling wholly new tasks, upskilling workers long-term, and "ambient agents" that offer help without being explicitly invoked.

## Quote

> Automation cuts costs but does little to raise the ceiling... we are committed to building bicycles for the mind.
