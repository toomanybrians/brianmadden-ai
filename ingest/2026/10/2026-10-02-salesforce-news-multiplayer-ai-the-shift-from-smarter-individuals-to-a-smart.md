# Multiplayer AI: The Shift from Smarter Individuals to a Smarter Company

## Insights

- Positions Slack as evolving from a collaboration tool into an "AI-driven work operating system" where agents act as teammates within shared channels rather than siloed, single-user tools.
- Core framing: most current AI/agent use is "single-player" — one person, one agent, one private thread — and that context/knowledge is lost when the task ends instead of being retained by the organization.
- Argues shared, in-channel agent use (e.g., tagging Slackbot in a deal channel) lets the agent draw on account history, prior research, and team discussion, producing one answer visible to the whole team simultaneously.
- Introduces "Slack Code": coding agents operate in shared channels mapped to a project (live when active, archived when done), letting non-engineers watch and weigh in conversationally without needing technical fluency.
- Claims this reduces status meetings and tickets since progress, code, and previews are visible in real time, and preserves the reasoning behind decisions for later reference.
- Underlying strategic signal: Salesforce/Slack reframing competitive differentiation around shared organizational context accumulated through agent interactions, not just individual productivity gains.

## Quote

> AI is worth far more to a business when it works out in the open, in the same places people already work.
