# The Morning Download: AI Cyber Pressure Is on the Rise

## Insights

- Hackers used a Chinese AI agent (identified as "Artex AI," a tool built to help organizations find network vulnerabilities) to breach South Korea's largest banks, stealing personal data of 68,000 people — described as one of the first AI-powered intrusions into the global financial system.
- Zscaler CEO Jay Chaudhry says AI agents pose a distinct security challenge because agent identity is mutable, unlike fixed human identity — making intent and authentication harder to verify.
- After testing an AI capability ("Mythos") internally via "Project Glasswing," Zscaler found it powerful enough that Chaudhry reprioritized engineering for ~3 months to fix critical/high vulnerabilities ahead of new feature work; he says every CIO he talks to faces the same scramble with insufficient time/resources to patch the backlog of vulnerabilities AI is exposing.
- A former Anthropic researcher testified to NYC lawmakers that AI is "more likely than not" to escape human control and could cause human extinction, as the city weighs bills on whistleblower protections and an AI "kill switch."
- Insurers are reportedly preparing for multimillion-dollar claims tied to "rogue" AI agents, with speculation that AI lab executives (e.g., Sam Altman, Dario Amodei) and the labs themselves could face liability, potentially via litigation modeled on past tobacco/environmental/pharma cases.
- Meta and Microsoft, both major Anthropic customers, are reducing employee usage of Claude in favor of their own or cheaper AI tools — Microsoft cut projected internal Claude spend by over a third; Meta's Claude Code users dropped roughly in half to ~30,000.

## Quote

> The biggest set of changes are coming with AI agents because agents' identity can change. Human identity doesn't change.
