# Higher Ed’s Existential Crisis

## Insights

- Frames AI disruption of higher ed as a distraction: the sector was already collapsing for structural, non-AI reasons before chatbots entered the conversation.
- U.S. degree-granting institutions have dropped from 4,720+ in 2013 to at least 800 fewer today, with projections of ~80 more closures by 2029 and over a quarter of private colleges at risk within a decade.
- The 18-year-old applicant pool peaked in 2025 (3.9M graduates) and will shrink to 3.4M by 2041 — a 13% decline driven by falling U.S. birth rates since 2007.
- Demand is eroding independently of demographics: immediate college enrollment among high school grads fell from 70% to ~63% in a decade, and adult (25+) enrollment is down roughly 25% since 2011.
- Elite schools with low acceptance rates (e.g., 96% rejection) are insulated from the shrinking applicant pool, while regional private colleges with high acceptance rates are already feeling the squeeze.
- Tuition has risen roughly 40x since 1963 and about 4x faster than housing/gas costs since 1983, pointing to a pricing model unmatched by other industries.

## Quote

> For now, the question isn't whether AI will kill college. It's why college was already dying in the first place.
