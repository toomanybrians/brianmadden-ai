# Pluralistic: History is something you do (27 Oct 2026)

## Insights

- Doctorow argues against "inevitabilism" — the tech-bro/policymaker claim that technological trajectories (including AI) are predetermined and must be accommodated rather than shaped by human choice.
- He specifically frames current AI deployment as a choice, not a necessity: AI could have been built as a climate-modeling tool (an IPCC/Sandia supercomputing project with modest open-source spinoffs) rather than "the most climate-destroying, economy-wrecking, job-killing" version we got.
- He characterizes current commercial AI ("a word-guessing sentence-extruder") as a deliberate, non-inevitable design and deployment path chosen by specific actors (he names Sam Altman and Dario Amodei), not an inherent property of the technology.
- Argues inevitabilism serves oligarchic interests: framing figures like AI company leaders as "avatars of the Great Forces of History" removes accountability for how AI is built and who it serves/harms.
- Connects this to a broader critique of "Great Man" narratives and anti-collectivism (echoing Thatcher's "no such thing as society"), which he says erases the possibility of organized resistance or alternative design choices for technologies like AI.
- Frames science fiction's value not as prediction but as a tool for imagining alternative, non-inevitable futures for technology and society — explicitly positioning this against the idea that AI's current trajectory is fixed.
