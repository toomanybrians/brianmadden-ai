# ⚙️ AI's philanthropy push is also about power

## Insights

- Inception's Mercury 3 uses a diffusion-based architecture (parallel token generation/refinement vs. autoregressive one-token-at-a-time) that delivers ~1,000 tokens/sec and claims 4x-8x speed over comparable models (Haiku 5.5, GPT-6 Luna, GLM-5.3 Flash) while beating them on agentic benchmarks.
- This challenges the dominant AI scaling thesis (bigger LLMs + more/faster GPU hardware) by instead extracting more efficiency from existing GPUs via parallelized inference — suggesting a possible alternative path to performance gains that doesn't require ever-larger compute spend.
- Google DeepMind's quiet entry into diffusion text models (DiffusionGemma, 26B parameters) alongside Inception's Mercury line suggests multiple major labs are hedging on this architecture as a potential challenger to frontier-scale LLMs, though it's unproven whether it stays a niche (real-time/low-latency) use case or becomes a broader competitor.
- Anthropic's new Public Benefit Fund (5%+ of equity, potentially $75-100B in value) and related giving commitments are framed as both genuine public-good efforts and a strategic necessity: AI labs need public/political goodwill to secure support for massive data center buildouts and to manage perception ahead of possible IPOs.
- Elon Musk's pledged SpaceX share giveaway (~$10.5B to US children) is flagged as unverified and inconsistent with his philanthropic track record, contrasted against Anthropic's structured, larger-scale commitment.
- Chinese labs (MiniMax, DeepSeek) are rapidly closing the performance gap with US frontier models while undercutting them sharply on price (e.g., MiniMax M3.1 Flash at $0.15/$0.60 per million tokens), intensifying pressure on US AI pricing power and highlighting that most open-model innovation is now concentrated in Chinese firms — with trade-offs in safety/security rigor and distillation concerns.
