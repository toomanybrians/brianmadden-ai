# Investing in the public benefit

## Insights

- Anthropic is committing 5% of its equity to a newly created "Anthropic Public Benefit Fund," with an additional 2% addable at board discretion for every 50% increase in company value above a set baseline — meaning the fund's stake scales with growth.
- The Fund is structured as a wholly-owned LLC (not a 501(c)(3)) specifically to allow flexibility across different giving formats, including large nonprofit donations or direct distributions to specific populations.
- Explicit prohibition: none of the Fund's Anthropic equity can be used for lobbying or political giving, domestically or abroad.
- Labor market disruption is named as a priority use case — Anthropic cites a prior $200M commitment to studying/addressing AI's workforce impact, with the Fund positioned to enable "bolder approaches" if needed.
- The company frames this as redirecting equity that would typically go to escalating executive compensation toward public benefit instead.
- Combined with existing co-founder pledges (80% of equity) and an employee equity-donation match program (~50% participation), Anthropic states roughly 20% of total shares will now be dedicated to public good, with more possible at future valuation milestones.
- Core safety/interpretability research, biomedical research, and nonprofit/scientific Claude discounts remain housed inside the company itself (not the Fund) since they have dual commercial and public value.

## Quote

> Because of the way the Fund is structured, the more Anthropic grows, the larger the share of the company that can be dedicated expressly to the public good.
