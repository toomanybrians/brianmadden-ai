# I think “sell picks and shovels” is the wrong lesson for AI startups.

The Gold

## Insights

- Argues the common "sell picks and shovels" framing for AI startups is misapplied, and that the Gold Rush analogy underlying it is flawed.
- Proposes the Industrial Revolution as a better analogy than the Gold Rush: gold is a finite resource that depletes, whereas machines continuously drive down the cost of production.
- Claims that historically, far more wealth was created by people who used new machines productively than by those who manufactured the machines themselves.
- Implication: the bigger opportunity in AI may lie with those who apply the technology to do things cheaper/better, not primarily with the toolmakers/infrastructure providers.

## Quote

> Far more people got rich using the new machines than building them.
