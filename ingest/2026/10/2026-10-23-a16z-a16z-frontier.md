# a16z Frontier

## Insights

- a16z is renaming its crypto fund "a16z Frontier," broadening its mandate from crypto specifically to "frontier" startups building new technologies and creating markets that take years to mature — crypto investing continues but is no longer the exclusive focus.
- The author argues the crypto/non-crypto distinction is dissolving as blockchain infrastructure becomes invisible background plumbing (e.g., Wall Street and fintech firms building on blockchains without being "crypto companies"); he argues companies should be defined by what they do, not the tech stack they use.
- AI is framed as the current "major tech wave," analogous to prior waves (mobile devices, crypto) — current AI use is mostly "old things better" (faster/cheaper knowledge work, coding), but the more consequential shift will be "native use cases": entirely new activities not previously possible, often first appearing as niche or toy-like experiments.
- Predicts a coming proliferation of new hardware/devices (autonomous machines, biology/medicine-linked devices, human-machine hybrids) alongside software, paralleling how smartphones catalyzed the prior computing cycle — expects more "full-stack" startups combining software, hardware, and services for defensibility.
- Anticipates AI agents becoming increasingly autonomous actors, potentially changing what it means to be a "founder" and how industries are organized; expects new "machine-age networks" connecting people and AI agents that learn, coordinate, and transact — distinct in structure from human-designed networks like today's social platforms.
- Positions blockchains/stablecoins as infrastructure for this AI-driven shift, providing programmable, machine-to-machine payment rails to bootstrap new agent-driven economic networks.

## Quote

> It makes more sense to define companies by what they do rather than by the technologies they use.
