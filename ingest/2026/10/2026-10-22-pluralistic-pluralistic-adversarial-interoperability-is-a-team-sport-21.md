# Pluralistic: Adversarial interoperability is a team sport (21 Oct 2026)

## Insights

- AI-driven "vibe-coding" is enabling non-technical users to write jailbreak/interoperability software that restores stripped features or blocks unwanted ones in commercial devices (smart TVs, speakers, tractors, laptops).
- The author frames LLMs as functionally replacing the "fiddly, labor-intensive" integration/UX work that previously required well-capitalized firms (analogy: Canonical's Ubuntu installer vs. 1990s manual Linux installs) — AI collapses the cost of making complex technical workarounds usable.
- LLMs are described as effective at research-and-synthesis tasks: parsing forums/logfiles, finding prior hobbyist fixes, and generating obscure configuration solutions a human would otherwise spend days on through trial and error.
- Some jailbreaking tools can run fully locally without "frontier" models from major AI companies, suggesting local/smaller models are sufficient for this class of technical troubleshooting work.
- Vibe-coded software carries real risk (security vulnerabilities, defects) distinct from the legal/ethical question of a user's right to modify their own property — the quality of AI-generated code and the legitimacy of circumvention are framed as separate issues.
- Argues individual AI-enabled jailbreaks don't scale into systemic market change (e.g., alternative app stores, generic cartridge manufacturing) without legal reform — AI lowers technical barriers but doesn't substitute for policy/legal shifts enabling businesses to form around the capability.

## Quote

> Once we fix our policy, then we can fix our code.
