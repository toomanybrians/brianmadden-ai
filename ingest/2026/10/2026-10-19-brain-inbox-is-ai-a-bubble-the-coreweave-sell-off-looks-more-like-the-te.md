# 🫧 Is AI a Bubble? The CoreWeave Sell-off Looks More Like the Telecom Bust Than Dotcom

## Insights

- CoreWeave (an Nvidia-chip-renting "neocloud" built on heavy borrowing) became the first major AI-cycle borrower priced at distressed debt levels: credit default swaps implied over 60% default probability within five years (up from ~30% a year prior), bonds traded below 80 cents on the dollar, and yields exceeded 15-16%.
- The piece frames the "AI bubble" question around which historical crash template applies based on who absorbs losses: Dotcom (2000) hit shareholders while banks stayed intact; telecom/fiber bust (2001-02) hit bondholders and vendor-financing equipment makers; the 1873 railway crash hit underwriting banks themselves.
- Three intertwined explanations for the October sell-off: rising rates (10-year Treasury hit 5.3%, highest since 2002, raising CoreWeave's financing costs), demand doubts (OpenAI's annualized revenue reportedly restated down from ~$70B to ~$50B after removing gross partner revenue; Gartner forecast $3.5T in possible AI infrastructure write-downs by 2030), and contagion/narrative spillover from rivals' financing troubles.
- Stress had been building for months before October: S&P cut Oracle to one notch above junk (July), Blue Owl restricted private credit fund withdrawals (since April), and $18B of bank loans for Oracle's "Project Jupiter" data center were quoted at 89-91 cents on the dollar (September).
- Divergence noted between "edge" and "core" of the AI build-out: neocloud shares fell sharply while hyperscalers (Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet) barely moved, suggesting the distress is concentrated in debt-heavy infrastructure players rather than the broader AI sector.
- Selling spread along the supply chain (Oracle -8.8%, Dell -11.3%, semiconductor index down 11.6% over six sessions/~$1.6T in value), while Nvidia's CEO publicly downplayed bailout need and called CoreWeave's cost of capital "decreasing."
