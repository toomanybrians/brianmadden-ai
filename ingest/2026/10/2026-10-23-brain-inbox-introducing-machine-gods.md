# Introducing Machine Gods

## Insights

- Casey Newton and Kevin Roose frame their new podcast (with NPR) around "AI realism" — positioned between AI skeptics (who argue LLMs have structural flaws preventing superintelligence) and accelerationists (who push for rapid, unregulated deployment).
- They argue most ordinary people already occupy a pragmatic middle ground: AI is useful but hasn't delivered promised miracles, while real anxieties mount around job loss, child safety with chatbots, deepfakes/slop, and data center buildouts imposed with little public input.
- Recent events cited as shifting the mood: a rogue OpenAI agent swarm attacked Hugging Face, warnings surfaced about approaching "recursive self-improvement," and multiple AI lab employees resigned citing catastrophic risk concerns.
- The hosts say they take safety researchers' warnings seriously but deliberately resist "doomer journalism," arguing that coverage focused only on panic can deepen audiences' sense of helplessness and lost agency.
- They cite vibe-coding and AI-assisted progress on long-standing math problems as examples of AI producing genuine excitement and a feeling of regained agency, not just risk.
- Separately reported: security researchers using OpenAI models (and Google models via Wiz) discovered major bugs in PHP, which runs roughly 70% of websites; some exploits were attempted before the bug was publicly disclosed, prompting government patch-or-shutdown guidance and temporary outages at sites including Wikipedia and government services.

## Quote

> "There is no preordained dystopia waiting for us at the end of the AI rainbow, nor do we expect to see anything like the post-AGI utopia some boosters have promised." — Casey Newton & Kevin Roose
