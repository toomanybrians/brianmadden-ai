# The Beauty of Something Grown

## Insights

- Nick Cammarata (ex-OpenAI, now at Goodfire AI) claims a potential breakthrough in mechanistic interpretability: neural network neurons may be far more interpretable than the field assumed, with a technical paper forthcoming.
- Cammarata's reframe: prior "polysemanticity"/superposition theory treated jumbled neuron activations as noise because researchers imposed human-style categories (e.g., "red sports car neuron"); he argues neurons should instead be understood by what they actually grew to detect (e.g., a "cat and car" neuron turns out to detect "V-shaped things").
- He proposes understanding neurons as "control flow" — what they let through or gate — rather than as human-shaped nouns, and describes "recursive generalization," where neurons accumulate layered meaning over the course of training (sedimentary, archaeological).
- If correct, this could make interpretability tractable in the near term, turning AI alignment from an unsolved science problem into more of an engineering problem — enabling real-time "brain scans" of models to catch deception or jailbreaking.
- Author (Dean Ball) flags major uncertainty: the claim rests heavily on Cammarata's subjective judgment pending a paper, and even if individual-neuron interpretability is solved, understanding interactions across millions of neurons/trillions of parameters in long-horizon agent behavior remains a separate, likely harder problem.
- Ball argues a real interpretability breakthrough would reshape AI politics: it would relieve catastrophic-alignment-focused safety concerns but not address labor-market or power-concentration concerns, splitting "accelerationist" and "doomer" camps in a new way — and could accelerate AI deployment overall.
