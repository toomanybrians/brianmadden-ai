# When Anyone Can Fake Anything (Inside My AI Law & Policy Class #11)

## Insights

- Deepfakes (AI/ML-generated synthetic media) are distinct from "cheapfakes" (basic edits like slowed/sped video with no AI involved) — regulatory definitions conflating the two risk either criminalizing ordinary editing tools or missing real harms.
- 90-95% of deepfake videos created since 2018 are non-consensual pornography, with roughly 90% of that targeting women; Telegram ecosystems are documented as major distribution/generation hubs, including content targeting minors.
- Three core deepfake techniques are explained: face-swapping (training on footage of two people to transpose one face onto another's movements), lip-syncing (tools like Wav2Lip can make any face "say" new words without training on that specific person), and GAN-based "puppet master" full-body control.
- Contrary to widespread fear, reporting cited (POLITICO, Meta's Nick Clegg, Aspen Institute's Josh Lawson) found no demonstrated case of AI disinformation changing a nationwide election outcome as of 2024 (Pakistan, Indonesia cited) — the realized harm is concentrated elsewhere, especially non-consensual intimate imagery and fraud/social-engineering scams.
- Detection is fundamentally failing: in a live classroom exercise testing human ability to spot deepfakes, participants scored only 2-for-4 (no better than chance) despite high subjective confidence; detection also faces an inherent arms-race dynamic (publishing detection methods teaches creators how to evade them) plus unresolved attribution problems (VPNs, hacking claims, tool-maker liability disclaimers).
- Even failed or debunked deepfakes cause lasting harm, and realistic fakes enable the "liar's dividend" (Chesney/Citron) — real evidence can be dismissed as fake, meaning the deeper risk may be the erosion of society's ability to establish any truth at all, not just belief in falsehoods.

## Quote

> The threat driver is human credulity, and the content itself doesn't need to be advanced to be effective.
