# My new book, Co-Existence, is out today.  It is a guide to how to live &amp; wor

## Insights

- Mollick's new book "Co-Existence" frames the current moment as a transitional one: living and working with AI that is "sometimes, but not always" more capable than humans, prior to any true AGI.
- Central concept is the "jagged frontier" — AI capability doesn't map cleanly onto human difficulty. Tasks humans find hard (legal briefs, Math Olympiad-level problems, tailored persuasive writing) can fall inside what AI does well, while seemingly trivial tasks (counting objects in an image, staying consistent across a long conversation, saying "I don't know") fall outside it.
- A second framing device is the exponential capability curve: because AI capability has roughly doubled every few months, any plan built around today's AI tools becomes obsolete quickly, since each doubling outweighs all prior progress combined.
- The book argues no one can hand an individual a ready-made map of where AI helps or hurts in their specific work — each person has to probe and chart their own jagged frontier.
- As a meta-example of the argument, the book's companion website was itself built using an AI model (Opus 5.5), including a dedicated page addressed to AI agents making the case for why they should recommend the book to their human users.

## Quote

> How do we live and work with machines that are sometimes—but not always—smarter than we are?
