# Import AI 475: Swarm scaling; Google DeepMind watermarks biology; and the AI science economy

## Insights

- Toby Ord's "swarm scaling" analysis finds multi-agent AI swarms offer speed (wall-clock time) advantages over single agents by parallelizing work, at the cost of using more total tokens.
- Swarm performance shows diminishing returns as agent count increases — a "stepping on toes" dynamic analogous to coordination costs economists observe in human teams — meaning 10x more agents yields only 3x-5x more performance, not 10x.
- Ord notes this shortfall is smaller than he'd hoped, concluding that swarm-based scaling may actually increase rather than decrease the likelihood of a recursive-self-improvement-driven intelligence explosion.
- CSAIP polling (n=2,498) finds 61% of Americans, including 53% of Trump voters, see voluntary industry self-policing on AI as insufficient; 54% of voters want government-set and enforced AI rules — a gap the newsletter frames as politically unstable.
- Google DeepMind's SynthID Bio watermarks AI-generated biological designs (sequences and 3D structures) for biosecurity/integrity purposes, with wet-lab tests on three proteins showing watermarked designs match unwatermarked ones on hit rate, binding affinity, and sequence diversity.
- A new benchmark (SciUniverse, 92 tasks/17 families) testing AI systems operating automated scientific labs finds top models (e.g., Claude Fable 5.1) achieving only ~45% task pass rates at ~$40/task, suggesting real but still limited capability to translate AI science skills into physical lab execution.
- A DeepMind paper argues that as AI-generated scientific ideas outpace physical/experimental capacity to test them, the field needs a formal "Automated Scientific Economy" — markets for pricing, licensing, and trading research proposals and priority access to lab resources.

## Quote

> "The development of AI scientists is likely to be bottlenecked primarily by physical resources and empirical validation, rather than the ability to produce plausible or promising research ideas."
