# Inside a new data factory training AI for the physical world

## Insights

- Innodata, a 38-year-old data company, has pivoted into collecting physical-world training data for humanoid robots, running a motion-capture lab in a New Jersey warehouse.
- The lab uses a Unitree G1 humanoid robot ("IRA") that is still in early, clumsy stages of operation — described as walking like "a drunk toddler" guided by remote control.
- Innodata's approach: capture detailed human motion (full body, individual fingers, and the tools being used, e.g. a broom) via motion-capture suits and sensors, then convert that into simulated training data.
- The simulated data is fed into NVIDIA's Isaac Sim so robots can practice movements virtually before attempting them in the real world, rather than learning from direct human demonstration every time.
- At the time of the visit, the robot was still failing to successfully perform a basic task (sweeping) despite the motion-capture data pipeline being in place.
- This reflects a broader industry pattern: data companies repositioning themselves from text/content data work toward "physical AI" data collection for robotics.
