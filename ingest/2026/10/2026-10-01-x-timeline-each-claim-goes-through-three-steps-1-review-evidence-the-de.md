# Each claim goes through three steps:

## Insights

- Describes a three-step verification process for safety claims made by AI developers: (1) the developer presents existing evidence for a claim, (2) an evaluator actively searches for counterexamples, (3) the evaluator assesses whether the developer's own methods were rigorous enough to have caught a counterexample if one existed.
- The third step shifts evaluation from just checking the claim's truth to checking the adequacy of the developer's detection methodology itself.
- Claims subject to this process come from two categories: "standard claims," which apply universally and represent baseline properties any safe developer should be able to demonstrate, and "recommended claims," which are developer-specific and derived from that developer's own commitments, safety case, past incidents, or evaluator observations.
- This framework implies a two-tier accountability structure: a common baseline all AI developers must meet, plus individualized scrutiny based on each developer's specific track record and stated commitments.
