# 30 Months on the Exponential (in Charts)

## Insights

- AI model leadership has changed hands roughly 20 times since GPT-4 (across Anthropic, Google, OpenAI), and 180 models now outperform GPT-4's original release, with 19 able to run on a smartphone — illustrating both pace and diffusion of capability.
- On the METR Long Horizon Benchmark (human task-time an AI can complete with 50% success from one prompt), capability doubled eight times: from a 4-minute task in 2024 to 17 hours by April 2026, at which point METR said it could no longer reliably measure further gains; other estimates suggest current capability may be around 17 weeks of work.
- Cost for a correct answer on the GPQA benchmark fell from 30 cents to roughly four-hundredths of a cent over the same period.
- Capability gains remain uneven ("jagged frontier"): long-form writing is still weak, while math went from poor to superhuman in the same stretch; small capability gaps still require human involvement.
- Mollick adds a third mode of human-AI collaboration beyond his earlier "centaur" (divided work) and "cyborg" (blended work) concepts: the "conjurer," who summons and directs agents and evaluates results without staying in a continuous loop — more powerful but with reduced control/visibility over what the AI actually does.
- He demonstrates agentic delegation directly: using Opus 5.5 in Claude Projects as an orchestrator spinning off sub-agents, he produced research, charts, layouts, and even a full "otter on an airplane" video from a few casual sentences of guidance, work that previously required manual back-and-forth with a chat window.
- His forthcoming book argues human advantage going forward rests on deep knowledge, wide knowledge, taste, and agency, and focuses on deciding when to hand work to AI versus when to avoid it.

## Quote

> AI is improving exponentially but unevenly, and the way we use it is shifting from chatbots to agents that do real work.
