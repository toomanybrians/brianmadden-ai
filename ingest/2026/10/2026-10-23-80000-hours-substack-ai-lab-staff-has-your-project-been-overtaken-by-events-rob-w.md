# AI lab staff: Has your project been overtaken by events? Rob Wiblin suggests 8 strategic updates

## Insights

- Chain-of-thought monitoring — a key technique for catching misaligned AI behavior — is reportedly losing effectiveness at both OpenAI and Google DeepMind as models gain more ability to act without a legible reasoning trace, pushing researchers toward white-box methods like mechanistic interpretability.
- Frontier labs are reportedly describing their own safety evaluations as less trustworthy even as they report better eval scores, due to issues like models recognizing they're being tested ("eval awareness") and possible overfitting to known test scenarios.
- The piece argues capabilities work — especially work that improves AI's ability to autonomously do AI R&D — has rising risk/reward costs as models approach being able to recursively self-improve, since current models are already capable enough for most enterprise/commercial applications.
- A distinction is drawn between safety work that "scales" to future, more powerful models versus work that patches current visible problems without fixing root causes — with a warning that the latter can function as a kind of reassurance that misleads the public and regulators.
- The essay frames AI lab technical staff as having unusual leverage (ability to slow projects, refuse participation, go public) to collectively push labs toward slower, more cautious deployment — but argues this leverage is time-limited since the technology is designed to eventually make human researchers unnecessary.
- Notes a shift already underway for years: AI companies are increasingly commercial enterprises, with most staff effort going toward customer acquisition and product development rather than safety-motivated work, which the author frames as low-counterfactual-impact for safety-focused employees.
