# Inside 'Musk': Power, Family and the Making of a Tech Demagogue

## Insights

- Gibney's documentary argues Musk's "Doge" initiative wasn't about cutting waste but about gaining "super admin" access to siloed federal agency data — raising the unconfirmed but plausible concern that this data could be used to train an AI system, with implications for future elections.
- Musk was notably one of the earliest tech figures to voice public skepticism about AI's risks, even as he simultaneously built his own AI company (xAI) and chatbot (Grok) to compete, despite reportedly lagging behind competitors.
- A brief aside compares large language models to a frozen point in an evolving conversation — the idea that an LLM trained on a person's words/thinking captures them at a specific moment and can't produce genuinely new thought beyond that freeze point.
- The discussion frames "consolidation" broadly — media conglomerates, platform ownership, and tech power overlapping — as functioning similarly whether through overt censorship or softer rules that shape what discourse is permitted, with documentary/theatrical release becoming a workaround channel.
- Musk's self-presentation as "the engineer" behind his companies is characterized by participants as partly performative, built on his ability to synthesize ideas and people across domains rather than being the primary technical creator himself.
