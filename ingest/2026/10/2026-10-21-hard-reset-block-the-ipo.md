# Block the IPO

## Insights

- NYC Comptroller Mark Levine asked Anthropic to pause its planned IPO (expected next month), citing the company's own public statements that frontier-model development may be outpacing society's ability to understand or control it.
- The author argues the stronger legal basis for SEC intervention is financial opacity, not AI-safety rhetoric: private companies can selectively leak unaudited metrics like "revenue run rate," which extrapolates a short period's revenue over a full year and can be manipulated by cherry-picking a strong period.
- Anthropic reported a $9B run rate at end of 2025, but court filings showed actual 2023–2025 combined revenue was closer to $5B — illustrating the gap between run-rate messaging and audited reality.
- A discrepancy between reported OpenAI run-rate figures ($70B vs. $50B) reportedly traced to Anthropic counting gross revenue from cloud partners (e.g., Amazon) while OpenAI counts only its own revenue share — exposing inconsistent, non-standardized accounting across the industry.
- Anthropic reported Q3 revenue of $16.8B (~$67.2B annualized) and claimed "adjusted" profitability, but the Wall Street Journal reported the company lost $81B in the first half of 2026; analysts also noted Q3 revenue was only 3% above the July run-rate, suggesting growth had nearly stalled despite positive spin.
- The piece argues the SEC has legal authority to block a registration statement that is "incomplete or inaccurate in any material respect," and calls for the SEC to require two consecutive quarters of audited financials before approving Anthropic's IPO.
- The author warns that letting Anthropic (and soon OpenAI) go public now would add unprofitable, opaque mega-cap tech giants to already overconcentrated, overvalued markets, raising systemic contagion risk if the AI trade falters.
- The piece also notes a dual bind: if Anthropic's extinction-risk statements are sincere, the company may be too dangerous to access public capital; if they're a publicity stunt, that deception further undermines trust in its disclosures.

## Quote

> There is, at this point, little reason to trust and every reason to doubt any financial information that Anthropic provides to the public.
