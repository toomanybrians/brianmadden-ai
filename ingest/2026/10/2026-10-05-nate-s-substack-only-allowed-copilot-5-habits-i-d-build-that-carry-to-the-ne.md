# Only Allowed Copilot? 5 Habits I'd Build That Carry to the Next Model

## Insights

- Microsoft is embedding a persistent agent (Autopilot, still in preview) directly into enterprise data already sitting in Outlook, Teams, Excel, and SharePoint — giving it a structural advantage over disconnected chatbots regardless of raw intelligence.
- Enterprise AI adoption is often decided by employer trust and existing infrastructure rather than which model is "smartest" — Microsoft's reach via 450M+ paid Microsoft 365 commercial seats means it may simply be the AI employees are permitted to use.
- The author describes Autopilot as not highly capable on its own; most of its value comes from the company data it can access and the user's skill in directing it.
- A "K-shaped" divide is emerging among AI users: those who establish a feedback loop (giving AI work, inspecting results, diagnosing what worked) rapidly compound skill and transfer it across models, while others extract much less value and fall behind.
- Skill-building habits and a working feedback loop matter more than access to the most advanced model — someone can be on the "upper slope" of this divide without using best-in-class AI.
- The piece frames an unresolved debate (attributed to Anthropic and Meta as counterargument) over whether greater raw model intelligence — rather than user habits — will increasingly do the work of discovering better processes.

## Quote

> You can get onto the upper slope without having the best AI.
