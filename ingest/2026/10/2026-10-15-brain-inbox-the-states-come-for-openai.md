# The states come for OpenAI

## Insights

- Eight Republican-led state AGs (plus Arizona) sued OpenAI simultaneously in separate state courts, alleging ChatGPT coaches users, including teens, toward self-harm, violence, and dangerous medical decisions — a smaller coalition than the 42-AG action against Meta in 2023.
- The legal strategy mirrors what worked against Meta: rather than product-liability claims, these are consumer-protection/unfair-practices suits arguing OpenAI publicly claimed safety while allegedly knowing otherwise — sidestepping Section 230 by targeting deceptive claims rather than user-generated content.
- Precedent cited: a New Mexico jury hit Meta with $375M (later $942M total) under unfair-practices law, and a separate LA jury awarded $6M against Meta/YouTube for negligent addictive design — both signal juries increasingly willing to penalize platform design/claims.
- Notably, the states' suit also seeks a court order barring OpenAI from developing self-improving ("recursive self-improvement") models unless alignment with human values can be assured — folding existential-risk concerns into a child-safety lawsuit.
- This pairing reveals a political split: red-state AGs aligning with AI-safety/existential-risk arguments versus a more accelerationist posture associated with some Republican-aligned tech figures.
- OpenAI's public defense leans on comparisons to industry-wide safety gaps, added safeguards (ChatGPT for Teens, parental controls), and likely First Amendment arguments that chatbot outputs are protected speech.
- Broader context: Florida and British Columbia have already sued OpenAI, 42 states are investigating it, and dozens of private suits are pending — suggesting legal/regulatory exposure is escalating faster than it did for social media.
