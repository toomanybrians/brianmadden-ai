# 📈 Monday data: For science the bottleneck isn’t intelligence

## Insights

- Anthropic's Jack Clark argues the "singularity" — AI-driven acceleration of scientific/technical progress beyond human tracking speed — has effectively begun, citing 2026 evidence as convincing.
- Concrete data points: Anthropic says 26% of its AI R&D in August was led by Claude (with human supervision); OpenAI's internal model produced 719 math manuscripts in October.
- The "self-improvement loop" closes most easily in math and coding, where work can be verified without leaving a desk — it's unclear whether it closes in other scientific fields.
- Across cited experiments (Sakana AI/UBC's self-rewriting agents, Google's AlphaEvolve improving its own training kernel, MiniMax's M2.7 iterating its own harness), the gains came from AI rewriting the scaffolding around a model — harness, memory, sampling — not the model's core weights directly.
- A September test where an AI-built agent matched a human-built one at improving other agents in fewer steps was statistically inconclusive, since run-to-run variance exceeded the AI-vs-human gap.
- Recursive self-improvement (RSI) talk is rising (Pichai references "RSI loops" in model development), but one framework breaks self-improvement into five types; only the lower-autonomy types 1-2 have actually been observed, and greater autonomy doesn't by itself guarantee an accelerating "takeoff" — each improvement round still needs to outperform the last.

> I believe that this year we have gained convincing evidence that it has now begun.
