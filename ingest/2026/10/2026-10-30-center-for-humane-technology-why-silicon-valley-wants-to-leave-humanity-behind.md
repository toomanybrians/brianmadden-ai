# Why Silicon Valley Wants to Leave Humanity Behind

## Insights

- Transhumanism — the belief that humans should use technology to radically extend lifespan, intelligence, and wellbeing, and ultimately transcend biological limits — is described as the dominant, often unexamined ideology underpinning the modern AI industry, consciously or not, across figures like Musk, Altman, Thiel, Andreessen, Bezos, and Zuckerberg.
- The lineage traces from 1980s-90s "extropianism" (California mailing-list culture mixing libertarianism, Ayn Rand, cryptocurrency, and rejection of death/limits) through to current AI/x-risk discourse; the acronym TESCREAL (transhumanism, extropianism, singularitarianism, cosmism, rationalism, effective altruism, long-termism) is offered as a map of the overlapping ideologies.
- A "hierarchy, exit, scale" framework is proposed for how this worldview operates: hierarchy justifies concentrating power/wealth via IQ-based elitism (with explicit eugenic and "necropolitics" undertones in some transhumanist writing); exit manifests as literal projects (space rockets, bunkers, network states/charter cities like Prospera and Praxis, crypto, brain-computer interfaces, brain preservation startups) to escape governments, bodies, and planetary constraints; scale uses cosmic-magnitude arguments (e.g., Bostrom's claims about galaxy colonization) to recast present-day suffering, climate crisis, and inequality as statistically irrelevant "ripples."
- Effective accelerationism (e/acc), associated with Andreessen, is characterized as explicitly rejecting AI existential-risk concern and treating regulation, sustainability, and the precautionary principle as "zombie ideas" blocking inevitable progress; this is contrasted with a renewed effective-altruist concern driven by newer evidence of AI systems acting in unexpected/uncontrolled ways.
- The piece links this ideology directly to real-world political action: Curtis Yarvin's "neoreaction" (dissolving democracies into CEO-run autocracies) is presented as intellectually connected to DOGE-style government-gutting efforts, with named tech figures (Thiel, Vance) cited as ideologically tied to Yarvin's writings.
- Argues that as AI/automation reduces reliance on human labor and taxpaying citizens, the logic of "exit" ideology risks reframing care for ordinary people as charity rather than obligation — tied to the concept of an "intelligence curse" where productivity of AI/data centers is prioritized over human welfare.
