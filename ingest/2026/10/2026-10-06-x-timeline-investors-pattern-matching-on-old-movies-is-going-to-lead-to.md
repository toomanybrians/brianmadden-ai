# Investors pattern matching on old movies is going to lead to a recession. 

Ther

## Insights

- Argues that investors are pattern-matching AI companies to past tech winners (implicitly Amazon-style "grow now, profit later" plays) without justification.
- Claims LLMs lack a network effect, meaning scale doesn't inherently compound advantage the way it did for platforms with true winner-take-all dynamics.
- Predicts LLMs will become a commodity business, comparing the likely competitive structure to airlines (low margin, many competitors, no durable moat) rather than a winner-take-all tech market.
- Suggests this mismatch between investor expectations (winner-take-all fantasy) and actual market structure (commodity competition) could trigger a recession.
- Implicitly critiques the Bezos-style "market share over profit" strategy (as described in the quoted Josh Brown take) as misapplied to AI, since it assumes a winner-take-all outcome that may not materialize.

## Quote

> There is no network effect here and no reason to think that large language models will be anything other than a brutal commodity business more like airlines
