# Investors Turn on Data Center Builders as Rising Interest Rates Dampen the AI Trade

## Insights

- Rising interest rates, political backlash, and market jitters are undermining the "neocloud" business model — highly leveraged companies (CoreWeave, Nebius, Nscale, Firmus) that borrowed heavily against Nvidia chips/equity and private credit to build AI data center capacity.
- CoreWeave's bonds traded at distress levels (15% yield vs. the investment-grade ~6% its financial model requires) before partially recovering; its CEO blamed "irrational markets" for the selloff.
- Some neocloud firms show large gaps between promised capacity and delivered capacity — e.g., Firmus Grid sought a $30B IPO valuation with only $59M revenue and 46MW delivered against a 912MW pipeline; its IPO was pulled after investor pushback.
- Anthropic's disclosed Q3 revenue (~$17B, up 46% quarter-over-quarter) showed decelerating growth compared to prior 100% quarter-over-quarter growth, and 5-7% of revenue reportedly came from "distillation" (rivals training on its model outputs) — denting confidence in its ~$2T IPO valuation target, with some investors reportedly discussing ~$1.5T instead.
- Anthropic projects over $500B in data center spending over the next decade, a projection that cloud providers' expensive debt financing is implicitly betting on.
- Separately, Ramp data shows enterprise AI token spending growth has flattened for two months, attributed to falling frontier model prices and improving model efficiency, alongside a slowdown in new AI customer growth since summer.
