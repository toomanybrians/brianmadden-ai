# Unsupervised Learning NO. 546

## Insights

- Miessler proposes a new metric called "Deeds" to measure developer output in the AI era, arguing pull requests no longer reflect real work since individual developers (aided by AI) now do much more solo, and PRs don't necessarily map to actual improvements.
- Andrej Karpathy argues AI outputs are shifting from text toward custom-generated media (diagrams, interactive pages, explainer videos) tailored to whatever format best conveys an idea — a shift Miessler ties to a 2016 prediction that software's display, interaction, and payment layers would unbundle into separate specialized companies.
- An article on enterprise AI adoption reports that while most companies use AI, only 7% have scaled it across the business; the proposed fix is redesigning entire workflows before automating, not bolting AI onto broken processes — citing a study of 5,179 support agents showing 14% productivity gains while human understanding of the work remained necessary.
- Cloudflare's new agent-facing tools (a CLI covering 3,000+ API operations, and automated issue-routing to coding agents) reportedly already account for 48% of recent usage of one of its developer tools, suggesting agents are becoming primary "users" of enterprise infrastructure rather than humans.
- A commentator (Justin Welsh, referenced) argues that fast learners are becoming more valuable than experienced workers as tools and expectations change rapidly, framing adaptability as increasingly more important than tenure.
- Separately, a piece on college preparedness claims the "students are getting worse" narrative masks growing bifurcation — a widening gap between struggling and unusually advanced students, rather than uniform decline.
