# Are You Overprompting Claude? STOP. DON’T. PLEASE.

## Insights

- Anthropic removed more than 80% of Claude Code's system prompt for newer models, with no measurable drop in coding test performance — suggesting much of the elaborate instruction-writing practice was unnecessary overhead even by the builder's own measure.
- Newer models (cited: Opus 5.5) show gains in speed, long-task handling, and autonomous problem-solving, which the piece argues reduces the need for prescriptive prompting (step-by-step instructions, self-checking loops, explicit reasoning cues).
- The piece frames most users' prompt-engineering effort as misdirected: the most heavily-refined parts of a "prized" prompt are likely the least necessary with current models.
- Implies a shift in prompting philosophy — moving from micromanaging how a model thinks/checks its work toward simpler prompts, better high-level instructions, and settings that let the model work more independently on larger tasks with less back-and-forth.
- Suggests capability gains in frontier models can make earlier prompt-engineering practices (and the skill/effort invested in them) obsolete quickly.
