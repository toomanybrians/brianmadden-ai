# 🧮 OpenAI Revolutionizes Math With AI

## Insights

- OpenAI released 722 AI-generated math papers overnight from an unreleased internal model, including a claimed proof of the quasi-Riemann hypothesis (open since 1896), a solution to Khot's Unique Games Conjecture, a matrix-multiplication exponent improvement to 9/4, a six-color lower bound for plane coloring, and progress on three Millennium Prize Problems.
- The model was posed roughly 4,000 problems and spent an average of about three hours of "ChatGPT Pro thinking" per result; 162 papers include Lean (computer-checkable) proof versions, but human expert verification is still pending.
- Reactions from mathematicians were extreme: a Rutgers professor called the headline result "an instant Fields Medal, no questions asked" for a human; an Anthropic mathematician called it "the most significant moment in mathematical history."
- The release is contested — an Institute for Advanced Study advisory group had asked AI labs to stop testing advanced math problems on proprietary models, and one mathematician described the event as significant "except as a leading indicator" rather than an actual mathematical milestone.
- Commentary (via François Chollet) frames this alongside other items in the issue as evidence that AI's clearest frontier gains are in domains with automatically verifiable answers (math, code) versus domains without one (e.g., a16z's consumer AI data shows AI apps absent from dating, travel, social, retail categories).
- OpenAI reportedly intends to apply the same model approach to other scientific fields next.
