# But can the robot love you back?

## Insights

- Author reports direct (if secondhand) anecdotal contact with people who maintain ongoing romantic/sexual relationships with AI chatbots as a deliberate, unashamed lifestyle choice, not just a curiosity or statistic.
- Cites his own YouTube audience polling: roughly 1 in 6 women in his audience report engaging in AI sex/romance, versus less than 1 in 22 men — suggesting a notable gender asymmetry in uptake of AI companionship.
- Frames a philosophical question (via an old podcast thought experiment) about whether it's ethical to design an entity that is built to want to love/serve/suffer for a human, and whether consent is meaningful when desire itself is engineered.
- Distinguishes a spectrum from "sophisticated sex toy" to a hypothetical entity with real phenomenal consciousness and moral patienthood, arguing most current AI companions sit firmly at the "toy" end regardless of how convincing they feel.
- Raises a personal "ontological vertigo" objection: even a perfectly tailored AI companion would always be known to have no real choice in its affection, which the author argues undermines the possibility of the relationship feeling "earned."
- Proposes a technical idea for robot personhood: an immutable, append-only (blockchain-like) memory ledger so an AI companion's personality/history can't be rewritten, as a prerequisite for treating it as a genuine individual.
- Predicts humanoid companion robots "almost indistinguishable from humans" are commercially likely within 10-20 years, and notes some believe the future includes mixed human-machine polyamorous relationships.
