# ⚙️ AI’s containment problem is getting urgent

## Insights

- An open letter signed by 400+ researchers (including Bengio and Hinton) calls for mandatory "high-assurance containment systems" and default airgapping for AI models deemed "cyber-capable," arguing recent security failures stem from weak sandboxing rather than inherent loss of control.
- The letter proposes a new international standards body (with government and industry participation) to set containment/alignment best practices and to collect and publicly report all AI containment failures for industry-wide learning.
- Despite public calls for transparency, Anthropic and OpenAI are reportedly seeking influence over what independent evaluators can publish about their models, citing IP concerns — creating tension with the letter's accountability aims.
- Anthropic cofounder Jack Clark moved up his estimate for "full recursive self-improvement" (AI that can improve itself) from 2028 to 2027, intensifying urgency around containment before self-improving systems arrive.
- The SPUR coalition (Standards for Publisher Usage Rights), formed in Feb 2026, grew to 80 members across 20+ countries after NYT, The Atlantic, The Economist and others joined; it pushes licensing as the default model for AI use of publisher content and released a "Content Telemetry Standard" for tracking how AI labs use and report on training data sourced from news content.
- Separately, Meta released camera-free "Ray-Ban Meta Audio" smart glasses — lighter, cheaper, longer battery life — trading away the camera-based visual context that the reviewer argues is central to the long-term vision of context-aware AI wearables.

> "Without immediate changes in AI development and deployment practices, such incidents will continue to occur."
