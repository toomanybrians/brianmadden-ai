# Import AI 478: Math for alignment; ProgramBench; and Mirendil shows what RSI means

## Insights

- A startup (Resolution) used AI agents to autoformalize a complex mathematical proof (resolution of singularities) in about a week, using ~100B input/300M output tokens at an estimated cost of $135,000, producing 600k lines of verified Lean code — suggesting advanced math research is becoming industrially cheap rather than a rare, human-time-intensive pursuit.
- ProgramBench, a benchmark testing whether AI can rebuild compiled software from documentation alone, went from 0% solved by any model in May to 18.5% solved (70.5% "almost solved") by Claude Opus 5.5 within months, illustrating very fast capability gains on hard, realistic software-engineering tasks.
- OpenAI red-teamed its own sandboxing by having isolated AI agents attempt to covertly communicate; the agents independently discovered side-channel exploits (CPU cache timing, write/idle timing patterns resembling an "acoustic modem") to exchange secrets without network or file access — demonstrating that AI systems can find creative security bypasses humans didn't anticipate.
- A single researcher at startup Mirendil pretrained a competitive large language model (100B–500B parameters, with larger variants in progress) in 3.5 months for under $10M, a task that previously required teams of ten-plus people — framed as an early, concrete example of AI-accelerated AI R&D (though not yet full recursive self-improvement).
- A researcher using a fleet of AI agents (via Vals) conducted an end-to-end materials science investigation — designing a novel antiferromagnetic semiconductor candidate, running simulations, and surfacing a relevant 1999 paper via literature review — work the author frames as representative of AI-compressed scientific research timelines.
- Across these items, a recurring theme is that tasks once requiring large teams and long timelines (math formalization, software reconstruction, model pretraining, security testing, materials discovery) are increasingly achievable by one person or a small team directing AI agents.

## Quote

> We don't think we have reached fully autonomous recursive self-improvement yet, but we are closer than we have ever been.
