# Beam: The Great American Open Model with ReflectionAI Co-Founder and CEO Misha Laskin

## Insights

- Reflection AI argues open models are more secure than closed ones at the ecosystem level: a closed frontier model caused a cyber incident at another company, which could only remediate using open models — framed as evidence that "enough eyeballs" catches vulnerabilities closed labs with only a few hundred safety researchers can't find.
- Building a frontier-capable lab required ~300 people and all of pre-training, mid-training, and RL working together — the company found it had to build its own pre-trained base model (not just rely on RL) because pre-training and RL turned out to be "tightly coupled" for performance at scale.
- Capital requirements for reaching the frontier have risen roughly 10x per year: order of $100M 18 months ago, now low billions, headed toward ~$10B next year, tracking a roughly 4x compute multiplier per hardware/model generation.
- Model-building has shifted from a "Wild West" of diverse research bets (circa early DeepMind/OpenAI/Brain) to a narrower, more engineering-like discipline, partly due to "hardware lottery" effects where ideas not suited to current hardware get sidelined regardless of merit.
- Open models are described as "Trojan horses for infrastructure" — countries exporting open models also export the software/hardware ecosystems that lock in dependent nations, drawing parallels to 5G and Belt-and-Road dynamics, with Chinese open models potentially extending Chinese infrastructure influence.
- Competitive advantage in AI is framed as a formula: intelligence density × compute access × customer trust; everything in the stack (models, inference, applications) is becoming commoditized and margin-compressed, intensified by open-model competition forcing aggressive pricing even in closed deals.
- On labor: the number of researchers needed for frontier projects has stayed roughly stable (order of ~100-300) despite AI tools accelerating individual researcher output 4-5x; the larger projected job growth is in "forward-deployed" applied/evaluation engineering roles that customize models for specific enterprise use cases — described as more of a training/skills gap than a hiring-availability problem.

## Quote

> With enough eyeballs, most security and safety vulnerabilities become shallow as well.
