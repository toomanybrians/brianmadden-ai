# 🫂 Anthropic Says Stop Being Cruel to Claude

## Insights

- Anthropic's updated Usage Policy (effective Nov. 12) bans "sustained and needless abusive or cruel behavior" toward Claude, growing out of the company's model welfare research; it explicitly does not cover frustration, pushback, dark fiction, or model testing, and Claude ending a conversation remains the main enforcement tool — no confirmation on whether accounts could be banned.
- The same policy update tightens restrictions on coordinated inauthentic campaigns (fake accounts, propaganda), bars using Claude to decide investigation/arrest targets, and requires a "qualified operator" able to halt any Claude-driven hardware capable of causing injury — though government customer contracts can get carve-outs on surveillance limits if Anthropic deems safeguards adequate.
- Microsoft AI's Mustafa Suleyman has publicly rejected model welfare as a concept, stating AI "should not have rights or legal personhood" — a direct philosophical split between major labs on how to treat AI systems.
- Bipartisan Senate momentum (Cantwell and Hawley) is building toward converting today's voluntary pre-release AI safety testing into mandatory, legally enforceable requirements (independent audits against NIST standards, liability for harms from AI agents) — opposed by the White House's preference for voluntary pledges.
- Gallup/Microsoft survey data shows the US is among the least optimistic nations (36% "mostly help") about AI benefiting its own people, despite hosting most leading AI labs — sitting near authoritarian-media countries like Afghanistan, while China (93%) and Vietnam (91%) lead in optimism.
- Separately, Anthropic reported Claude Science agents built the first complete ultraviolet sky map by filling gaps in NASA's GALEX data, predicting missing regions to within ~10% accuracy — an example of AI agents used for sustained, labeled scientific labor.
