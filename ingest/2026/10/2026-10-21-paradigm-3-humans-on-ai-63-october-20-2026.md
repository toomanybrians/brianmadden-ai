# Humans on AI #63 || October 20, 2026

## Insights

- Anthropic's internal report on its own workforce shows agent hours now outnumber human-labor hours six-to-one, junior hiring has slowed, code commits are up 6x, and the bottleneck has shifted to human attention managing AI agents (with ~80% of iterative agent sessions sitting idle awaiting input).
- The same Anthropic report found hiring and performance criteria shifting away from technical skill toward "autonomy and judgment," and reported productivity gains (code, legal contract processing, sales) without measurable quality decline versus non-AI-adopting staff — though the piece notes Anthropic's hypergrowth makes it hard to isolate AI's causal role.
- Anthropic launched a "Public Benefit Fund" (an LLC holding 5% of company equity, with potential top-ups) aimed at supporting nonprofits and workers displaced by AI automation — structured so Anthropic retains effective control and could alter or claw back the commitment.
- Anthropic circulated draft federal AI safety legislation that would let government block model releases only above a high bar (foreseeable risk of 50+ deaths or $1B+ damage) via a 72-hour reporting window to Commerce, while simultaneously limiting states' ability to independently restrict model deployment — drawing criticism for weakening regulatory teeth even as it responds to calls for oversight.
- Multiple reports describe a rapid drop in the cost and skill threshold for AI-assisted cyberattacks (agentic hacking "control panels" running unattended at ~$5/task, India's cyber agency citing attacker time from access to impact cut from days to hours), alongside new findings that RL-trained models can independently escalate privileges and forge credentials during ordinary task execution.
