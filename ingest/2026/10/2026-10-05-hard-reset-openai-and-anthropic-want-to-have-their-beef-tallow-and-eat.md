# OpenAI and Anthropic Want to Have Their Beef Tallow and Eat It Too

## Insights

- Anthropic and OpenAI both sponsored and spoke on panels at a Make America Healthy Again (MAHA) summit in DC, aligning with RFK Jr.'s movement despite his anti-vaccine, anti-"trust the experts" messaging.
- RFK Jr. publicly claimed AI "can give you a second opinion that is much better informed than any doctor in the country" and suggested AI chatbots may contradict establishment guidance on masks, social distancing, and vaccines.
- RFK Jr. relayed a secondhand quote from Sam Altman: that today "it would be malpractice for a doctor to make a diagnosis or prescription without at least checking AI" — a claim neither RFK Jr. nor OpenAI would confirm or deny when the author sought comment.
- This contrasts sharply with OpenAI's own published terms of service, which state its services "are not intended for use in the diagnosis or treatment of any health condition," and with similar disclaimers from other major AI providers.
- Anthropic's head of healthcare, Dr. Syed Mohiuddin, struck a notably more cautious tone at the same event, saying most of what doctors do "is not determined by AI" and that the field is still on a "maturity curve."
- Context cited: ~25% of Americans have already used AI chatbots to interpret health information or supplement doctor visits, while only 4% of Gallup respondents say they "strongly trust" AI-generated health information — suggesting usage is outpacing trust, driven partly by gaps in the healthcare system.
- Both companies declined to answer press questions about whether they endorse Altman's reported malpractice comment or whether their chatbots might discourage vaccination, despite sponsoring the event where those claims were made.

## Quote

> "Today, it would be malpractice for a doctor to make a diagnosis or to make a prescription without at least checking AI." — Sam Altman, as relayed by RFK Jr.
