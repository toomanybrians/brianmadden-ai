# 🛑 Nadella Wants an Emergency Brake for AI

## Insights

- Nadella argues frontier AI models (closed and open-weight) should be treated as "insider risks" — capable actors with privileged access that can err or be compromised, not trusted systems — because, unlike traditional software, their behavior can't be traced to a specific code path.
- His proposed controls sit outside the model itself (so the model can't tamper with them): model diversity (no model checks its own work), tamper-proof human-readable logs of every meaningful action, incident disclosure requirements, and readable chain-of-thought reasoning (while acknowledging no one yet knows how to make such reasoning reliably faithful).
- Centerpiece proposal is an "emergency brake" — a human must always be able to pause or shut down a model mid-task — framed as something companies can implement now, with eventual industry standardization where existing norms fall short.
- At Goldman Sachs, new junior bankers reportedly now start their careers directly supervising teams of AI agents rather than doing traditional entry-level grunt work, shifting the coordination function away from middle management and flattening the traditional banking hierarchy.
- a16z data (via Revelio Labs) shows global call-center employment growth, which ran ~4-5%/year through the 2010s and into 2021, has turned negative (~-4%) by 2026 — including in lower-middle-income countries that had held up through 2024 — though the decline began before ChatGPT's launch alongside rising interest rates.
- Separately, utilities (Entergy in Mississippi, also Texas, Duke Energy, PJM) are gaining contractual/regulatory authority to curtail or switch large AI data centers (including Amazon's) to backup power during grid stress, without needing operator sign-off.

> "We simply can't outsource responsibility for what intelligence does on our behalf." — Satya Nadella
