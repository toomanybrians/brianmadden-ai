# SCOOP: Benchmark Backs Early-Stage Startup Tendrils Compute as Chip Momentum Builds

## Insights

- Benchmark is leading a new early-stage funding round for Tendrils Compute, a Cambridge, UK-based chip startup led by Nils Cremer, with a fast-follow round reportedly already being discussed that could value the company above $1 billion.
- Tendrils is building general-purpose chips based on "interaction nets," an obscure graph-based computing model, betting this avoids the coordination and cost problems of multi-core CPUs — relevant as multi-step AI agent inference and tool calls may make CPU speed a new bottleneck.
- The deal is framed as part of a broader revival of VC interest in chip startups, reversing years of investor focus on software/networking, with bets that specialized (rather than general-purpose) chips will matter for AI infrastructure.
- Comparable deals cited: Etched doubled to a $21B valuation on a $700M raise; Fractile in talks at $6.5B after an Anthropic supply deal; OLIX raised $312M at $3.3B; Euclyd, Delos Data, and Volantis Semiconductors also raised nine-figure/eight-figure rounds recently.
- Cerebras is cited as the proof point for chip-sector returns (Benchmark's Series A stake saw a 20.5x multiple at IPO), though its stock has since fallen back near its offering price, underscoring the sector's capital intensity and cyclicality.
- VCs are using novel deal structures to share chip development risk/cost, e.g., Coatue and MatX reportedly discussing a joint venture worth billions to help finance memory purchases and manufacturing capacity.
