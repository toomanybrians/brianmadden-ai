# The full post, with notes and related reading, is on my blog: https://t.co/gtjVQ

## Insights

- Proposes a "General Hill-Climbing" loop as a universal model for working with AI: articulate desired state, measure progress via evals, do the work, iterate — applicable to software, business, writing, or any task.
- Argues most AI failures today stem not from execution but from humans failing to clearly or consistently articulate what they want; AI increasingly does exactly what's asked, so the bottleneck shifts to specification quality.
- Describes a three-layer abstraction problem: the idea in one's head (ground truth) → its capture in an artifact (text/voice) → the AI's build based only on that artifact, never the original idea — making thorough capture (e.g., long dictation sessions) essential.
- Treats "Evals" as the measurement layer, originating in software but becoming general-purpose across any work (reports, sales, design, etc.), with examples ranging from simple spelling checks to AI-judged rubrics and tournaments.
- Suggests ideal-state articulation and evals are in a feedback loop: if output passes all evals but still feels wrong, that signals the evals themselves need updating to better reflect true intent.
- Frames this loop as bigger than AI itself — a general-purpose problem-solving system — with implications for personal productivity and enterprise use of AI alike.

## Quote

> The primary limitation for AI today is not its ability to build what you ask for. It's your ability to clearly articulate what you want.
