# This Week in Charts: Agents Denied

## Insights

- Cloudflare data shows the share of 403 "Forbidden" responses to agents/bots has roughly doubled since summer 2025, suggesting etailers (e.g., Amazon) are actively blocking AI shopping agents rather than embracing agentic commerce.
- Etailers' resistance to agentic commerce is driven by multiple incentives: avoiding disintermediation of customer relationships, protecting ad revenue tied to customer eyeballs, and pre-existing fraud/returns problems (NRF data shows return rates and costs roughly doubled and tripled since 2019) that agents could worsen.
- Consumer survey data shows limited trust in AI purchasing: ~25% comfortable letting AI buy various goods, but 42% say they wouldn't trust AI at all to make purchases on their behalf — openness to AI for search/price-finding doesn't extend to the final transaction.
- Similarweb clickstream data (through June) indicates AI is used mostly in the "middle phase" of shopping journeys (research/comparison) rather than for discovery or final purchase, complicating both agentic commerce's near-term disruption potential and AI-driven ad attribution.
- TikTok Shop's extreme sales concentration (top 1% of sellers drive ~60% of sales, top 10% drive ~93%) is tied to high-volume video content creation — a "personal," human-driven dynamic the author suggests may be hard for shopping agents to replicate.
- AI infrastructure buildout is generating broader economic ripple effects: semiconductor/component prices for computer manufacturers are up over 25% YoY (BLS), coinciding with falling PC shipments (IDC) and rising iPhone prices, and tech-sector bond yields have diverged upward alongside a steep drop-off in AI-linked debt issuance since June.

> It's fair to take this data with a grain of salt... but the implications are otherwise pretty clear: for now, consumers seem to be using AI predominantly to do their homework.
