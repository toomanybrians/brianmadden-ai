# The Morning Download: AI Business Use Rises Despite Growing Concern

## Insights

- Wharton/GBK Collective's fourth annual enterprise AI study (1,416 US leaders and employees) finds daily AI usage among managers-and-above rose 16 points year-over-year to 62%, even as sentiment cools and caution grows, especially among heaviest users.
- 82% of VP-and-above respondents say their focus has shifted to managing AI output rather than producing it themselves; 48% of that tier worry about their own skills/thinking declining, vs. 32% of managers/directors.
- A perception gap persists between leadership and staff: 78% of managers-and-above believe employees are adequately trained on AI, but only 56% of entry/mid-level employees agree; executives also believe AI is transforming work faster than employees perceive.
- ROI is nearly universal but rarely strong — 80% of managers+ report positive returns, only 28% "strongly positive." Measuring success by hours-saved is most common, but departments measuring by growth report stronger returns; researchers argue growth-oriented use requires imagining new tasks, not just automating existing ones.
- Two-thirds of departments now use AI agents in some form, 17% let agents act without per-step human approval; agent adoption correlates with stronger ROI, but accountability for agent errors remains unresolved.
- High-ROI departments are distinguished not by headcount cuts but by structural change: 64% changed management structure, 84% significantly changed workflows, 92% monitor AI use (vs. 47%/69%/74% for others). Companies with a dedicated Chief AI Officer report notably higher strongly-positive ROI (58%) than those folding the role into an existing exec's duties (24%) or without the role (8%).
