# Does intelligence need a hard cap?

## Insights

- At The Curve conference, multiple speakers (unnamed under Chatham House Rule) floated capping AI "intelligence" itself as a safety measure, potentially amounting to a de-facto ban on systems reaching superhuman capability.
- The urgency behind this idea is tied to two developments: fallout from an OpenAI-Hugging Face incident, and recent OpenAI/Anthropic posts describing progress toward recursive self-improvement — AI systems researching and training their successors, accelerating release cycles and raising loss-of-control risk.
- Proposed mechanisms for limiting intelligence remain vague but could include: restricting frontier models from doing AI research themselves, capping compute or the number of self-copies a system can run, or barring deployment past a defined capability threshold.
- Any such restriction would require enforcement mechanisms that don't currently exist — individual labs or even single countries can't unilaterally impose them, and the current US administration opposes such limits, instead oscillating between a de facto licensing regime and urging companies to move faster.
- Existing safety mechanisms (Anthropic's "responsible scaling policy," embedded evaluators now adopted by OpenAI too, proposed antitrust waivers for cross-lab safety collaboration) were all described by speakers as insufficient to meet their own safety bar.
- A core unresolved disagreement: AI lab leaders reportedly believe catastrophic risk could arrive as soon as next year, while government officials show little urgency or clear position on the timeline.

## Quote

> We may want to limit how intelligent a future system is allowed to become.
