# DoorDash's $17B Acquisition of Instacart Points to a World Without Employees

## Insights

- DoorDash's proposed $17B acquisition of Instacart would combine the two dominant platforms in restaurant and grocery delivery, raising antitrust concerns that center on labor/supplier power rather than just consumer pricing.
- The piece frames gig platforms as the leading edge of dismantling the 20th-century employer-employee "social contract" (wages/benefits in exchange for employer control), replacing it with a model where companies control work without taking on worker obligations.
- Cites data that 81% of California app-based gig workers treat it as their main job (not supplemental), and one estimate that over half the US workforce could be gig workers by 2027 — spreading beyond delivery/rideshare into nursing, software engineering, etc.
- Describes "wage discrimination algorithms" and a viral example of a "Desperation Score" used to individually price gig labor — an instance of algorithmic, surveillance-based compensation-setting.
- Argues labor law and antitrust law, historically separate, are converging as large firms use misclassification and platform structures to exert monopsony-like power over workers who are nominally "independent contractors."
- Notes potential legal exposure: if independent contractors are treated as independent businesses, platform-set "wages" could constitute unlawful price-fixing among competitors under laws like California's AB 325.
