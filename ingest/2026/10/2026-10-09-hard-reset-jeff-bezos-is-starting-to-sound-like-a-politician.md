# Jeff Bezos Is Starting to Sound Like a Politician

## Insights

- Bezos claims AI-driven productivity gains (he cites "double-digit") will create a labor shortage rather than job losses, letting workers choose shorter hours (e.g., supporting a family on three days of work) instead of eliminating jobs.
- He explicitly rejects the view of "smart people" who predict AI-caused job losses, repeating this dismissal across three separate interviews (CNBC x2, Fox News) and a Paris panel.
- His AI startup Prometheus is pitched as a tool to collapse the distance between "having an idea" and "manufacturing/building it," aiming to make AI tools so simple that "anyone will be able to be an engineer" — but no concrete product has been articulated.
- On AI regulation, he argues for "light," targeted rules that govern harmful applications rather than the underlying technology, using a knives-vs-murder analogy (regulate the act, not the tool).
- He favors rebranding AI as "SI" (superintelligence), framing "artificial" as an unflattering term — aligning with a political push for that terminology.
- Despite record Amazon corporate layoffs (30,000 roles cited) under his prior leadership, he frames AI-era labor disruption as empowering rather than destructive, a framing the author flags as self-serving.

## Quote

> "I do like SI, because artificial is not a very flattering thing." — Jeff Bezos
