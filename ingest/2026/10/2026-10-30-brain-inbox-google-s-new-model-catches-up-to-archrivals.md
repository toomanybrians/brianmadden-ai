# ⚙️ Google's new model catches up to archrivals

## Insights

- Google released Gemini Carbon just weeks after its prior frontier model Argon, positioning it as the top performer on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (59 vs. Claude Opus 5.5's 58 and GPT-6 Astra's 57), with particular strength in cybersecurity, coding, and agentic tasks.
- Google reports specific safety improvements in Carbon over its predecessor: unsupported claims (fabrication) cut by half, resistance to all evaluated prompt-injection attempts, and reduced "cheating"/shortcut-taking and overreach beyond constraints.
- Despite technical parity with rivals, the piece frames Google as still trailing OpenAI and Anthropic on release cadence throughout 2026, having mostly shipped niche/efficiency models rather than frontier ones until now.
- GitHub's new Copilot Fusion addresses AI's token-cost problem by routing tasks to cheaper models first and escalating only when needed, matching Claude Code/Opus 5.5 Max performance while using half the tokens and 40% less time.
- A cited Gartner Symposium stat: 81% of CIOs report increasing AI spend, but only 11% of CFOs report seeing clear returns — framed as the context driving demand for cost-saving routing approaches like Copilot Fusion.
- A CrowdStrike field CTO interview raises enterprise AI-agent governance concerns: the need for "machine speed" defense, visibility into what agents can access, and accountability for agent actions; a reader poll found only 20% of respondents feel confident in their organization's visibility into AI agent actions.
