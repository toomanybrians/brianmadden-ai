# Pluralistic: The death of history's most successful publisher (20 Oct 2026)

## Insights

- Doctorow defines publishing functionally as "the act of making something public" — identifying a work, an audience, and connecting the two — and argues Google was, by this definition, the most successful publisher in history for most of its existence.
- He argues Google's AI products (AI summaries, Gemini) represent a structural break from that function: instead of connecting publishers' works to audiences, Google now extracts and recombines web content to intercept and retain audiences itself, starving the sites it once sent traffic to.
- Frames this as an escalation of "enshittification" — but distinct from earlier tactics (e.g., deliberately degraded search results to sell more ads), which were at least profitable/sustainable; AI, by contrast, is framed as enormously money-losing for Google even as it extracts value from publishers.
- Cites Tim Wu's "Age of Extraction" concept of intermediaries developing "main character syndrome" — abandoning their facilitator role to become the primary beneficiary of transactions they were meant to enable — as the lens for Google's AI pivot.
- Suggests the endgame isn't just worse service for users/publishers but potential outright collapse of Google itself, likening the dynamic to a "Mafia bust-out" rather than classic enshittification.

## Quote

> Google has a scorching case of main character syndrome.
