# The Morning Download: Mining Unwritten Data for AI’s Edge

## Insights

- AWS report (via Tom Godden) argues that since most organizations now have access to the same AI models, competitive advantage shifts to unwritten knowledge — what employees know but never recorded anywhere a system could read.
- Nestlé's chief data and AI officer notes agentic AI exposed a blind spot: companies focused on structured data but neglected SOPs, factory documentation, and process knowledge — which agentic AI now makes equally or more important than structured data.
- Addepar's CTO describes the hardest integration challenge as capturing "between the systems, between the people" knowledge — specifically how employees make decisions and value judgments, not just what tasks they do.
- Addepar uses a "data breadcrumb trail" approach: aggregating a user's actions across an entire journey/decision sequence over time, then combining that with ML/AI, producing a "massive uplift" in AI accuracy and ROI.
- Despite productivity gains from AI, Addepar continued hiring engineers and maintains $150M+ annual R&D spend — AI uplift didn't translate into headcount reduction in this case.
- Separately noted: only 11% of ~400 surveyed businesses can accurately forecast AI spending, partly because AI agents behave more like autonomous workers (taking actions, making mistakes) than traditional predictable software.

## Quote

> Every organization has access to the same AI models, so competitive advantage sits in their own data. — Tom Godden, AWS
