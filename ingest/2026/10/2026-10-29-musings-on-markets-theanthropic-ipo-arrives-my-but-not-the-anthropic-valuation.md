# TheAnthropic IPO Arrives: My (but not the) Anthropic Valuation!

## Insights

- Anthropic's prospectus shows revenue growing ~14x year-over-year in H1 2026, but growth decelerated in Q3 as OpenAI gained share, compute capacity constrained output, and some enterprises shifted to cheaper open-source models.
- Enterprise adoption is concentrating at the high end: customers spending $1M+/year on Claude rose from under 100 to 1,900, though the top five customers still account for 25% of revenue.
- Compute costs are falling sharply as a share of revenue (from over 250% to 48% quarterly), suggesting AI providers may be following a path toward enterprise-software-like margins as they scale.
- Valuing AI companies requires a view on market size driven by four variables: whether AI is a productivity tool or outright labor replacement, whether displacement hits top-tier wages or spreads broadly, how many industries are affected, and whether disruption is US-centric or global.
- Anthropic is explicitly positioning itself not just as a model/tool vendor but as a potential direct competitor in industries its agents disrupt (e.g., pharma), aiming to capture revenue from the work itself rather than just licensing the AI.
- Anthropic's corporate structure — five share classes with founders retaining 50.1% voting control regardless of future dilution, plus a public-benefit-corporation framing — is flagged as significantly limiting shareholder power to challenge management decisions, even ones framed as "safety."
