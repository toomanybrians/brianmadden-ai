# Frontier Chips for Frontier AI Labs, with Walter Goodwin, Founder/CEO of Fractile

## Insights

- Fractile is a full-stack AI chip startup (founded 2022, ~150 people) betting on memory bandwidth rather than raw flops, aiming to run frontier models much faster via better inference speed, since memory bandwidth has scaled only ~40x in 20 years versus a millionfold rise in flops.
- Most AI ASICs (Google TPU, Meta MTIA, Microsoft Maya, OpenAI's Jalapeno) are architecturally similar despite being built by different hyperscalers, because they're mostly designed in partnership with the same small set of "ASIC house" back-end delivery firms (e.g., Broadcom) and share common components like HBM memory and tensor cores.
- The chip design cycle has structural latencies that AI can't fully compress: foundry turnaround is 3-5 months even in rush scenarios, and chips need a 3-5 year amortization window to be financially viable — so faster front-end design doesn't eliminate the overall bottleneck (an "Amdahl's law" dynamic).
- The strategic goal for chip companies is building a "rolling frontier of bets" — staying ready to trigger production ramps the moment a workload shift is confirmed — because a structural 3-6 month lead time advantage can translate into winning most frontier-lab deployment deals.
- Frontier AI labs are judged to be irrational to go all-in on proprietary chip architectures, because a competing lab's compute-efficiency breakthrough tied to a different chip could leave them unable to respond for months — creating convergence pressure toward shared chip platforms despite model-level competition.
- A semiconductor CEO predicted full automation from chip-architecture intent to a finished layout file (GDS2) is roughly 10 years away; the interviewee suggested applying a "divide by four" heuristic to AI-driven timeline predictions, implying much faster automation of chip design workflows.

## Quote

> If you can just find a way to structurally carve out a three to six months advantage, you will be winning all of those deployments.
