# The Actual Reason Why Meta Got Back in the AI Race Changes Everything

## Insights

- In early 2026, Meta was so far behind in AI that up to 60,000 employees were using Claude Code, with the company spending on the order of 60–70 trillion tokens/month on Claude and other external models.
- Meta internally tracked an employee "token-maxxing" leaderboard (dubbed "Claudeonomics" by critics) that was mocked publicly and shut down after backlash.
- Meta projected its Anthropic spending could reach $10 billion/year; over the same period Anthropic's annualized run-rate revenue jumped from ~$19B (March) to ~$30B (April 2026), suggesting Meta's spending may have been a major driver of Anthropic's growth.
- Despite ideological opposition — Anthropic's Dario Amodei reportedly views open-source models like Llama as a "betrayal to America" and sees Meta as a near-existential risk — the two companies became closely intertwined through this spending relationship.
- In roughly 9 months, Meta assembled a new team and shipped "Muse Spark," described as its first genuinely good model in years, scoring 52 on the Artificial Analysis intelligence benchmark — competitive with Claude Opus 4.6, after prior flops (Llama 4) and delays (Avocado).
- The piece frames this trajectory as evidence that Meta's reliance on Anthropic's models was both a sign of its internal AI weakness and, paradoxically, a catalyst for its subsequent catch-up.
