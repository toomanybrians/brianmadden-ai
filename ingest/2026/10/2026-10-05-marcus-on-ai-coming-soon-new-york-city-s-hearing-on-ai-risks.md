# Coming soon: New York City’s hearing on AI risks

## Insights

- Marcus testified before NYC City Council in support of an "FDA-like" regulatory regime for AI, requiring independent third-party review of AI systems before market release, with developers bearing the burden of proving benefits outweigh risks.
- He frames the proposal as filling a federal vacuum: he claims the current US administration is "deeply tied to big tech" and unwilling to regulate, and that he made the same FDA-style pitch to the Senate in 2023 without result.
- Draws an analogy to California's role in clean energy standards, arguing a large city/state market (NYC) can set de facto national AI policy even without federal action.
- Cites concrete harm categories to justify regulation: tens of thousands of hacking incidents tied to agentic AI with internet access, deepfake-driven propaganda and nonconsensual porn, and chatbots implicated in suicides and a mass murder.
- Notably tempers his own alarmism — explicitly states he doubts imminent human extinction from AI despite headline fears, while still arguing serious risk exists.
- Also backs a separate NYC whistleblower-protection measure, referencing recent reports that OpenAI may have fired whistleblowing employees as justification.

## Quote

> We believe that the world should accept some bad things happening for the benefits of this technology.
