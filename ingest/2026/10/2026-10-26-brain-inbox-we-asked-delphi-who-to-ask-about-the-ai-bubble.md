# ⚙️ We asked Delphi who to ask about the AI bubble

## Insights

- Several AI investors/builders queried via "Digital Mind" AI personas converge on a split view: infrastructure/compute buildout is real, but hype and overvaluation are concentrated in applications and individual companies, not the underlying technology shift.
- Specific framing from Katyal (Milana): the current bubble is "of pilots, not compute" — enterprise AI pilots are getting false validation because buyers are eager to try AI, producing signals that look like adoption but aren't designed to convert to real deployment.
- Oxford's Carl Benedikt Frey draws a historical parallel to railroads and dot-com overbuilding, but flags a specific present-day risk: much data-center investment is off-balance-sheet and credit-financed, so a correction could spread financial damage beyond tech companies themselves.
- Cited data point: workplace AI adoption reportedly declined from ~46% (June 2025) to ~37% (September), even as capital expenditure on AI infrastructure rose — a divergence between spending and actual usage.
- Cited estimate: roughly half of ~$3 trillion in planned data-center spending through 2028 must come from financing outside hyperscalers' own cash flow (per Morgan Stanley), underscoring leverage/credit exposure in the buildout.
- Partovi (Neo) argues chip/power scarcity alone won't trigger a bubble burst — the real danger is a scenario where demand fails to materialize relative to built-out supply.

## Quote

> "The danger is financial, not technological." — Carl Benedikt Frey's Digital Mind
