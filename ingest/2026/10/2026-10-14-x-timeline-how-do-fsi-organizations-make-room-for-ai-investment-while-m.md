# How do #FSI organizations make room for #AI investment while managing costs, res

## Insights

- Financial services firms are being advised to fund AI investment not through new budget but by freeing capital from existing operations: vendor consolidation, centralized application management, and reduced tool sprawl across NetOps/EUC/SecOps are positioned as the primary levers.
- Downtime is framed as a major cost driver justifying infrastructure investment (citing Splunk research pegging FSI downtime costs at $151M annually), with resilience reframed as continuous architecture rather than periodic disaster-recovery planning.
- Consumption-based cloud/DaaS pricing models are presented as a way to eliminate "compute and licensing waste," with workload-level telemetry (e.g., distinguishing browser-only users from heavy-processing users) used to right-size spend before migration decisions.
- Compliance (DORA, NIS2) is described as requiring real-time, granular visibility across the whole workspace, not just centralized management — observability is positioned as a precondition for policy enforcement, not a substitute for it.
- Security and employee experience are presented as compatible rather than tradeoffs: zero-trust/context-aware access controls in the browser are claimed to reduce friction while maintaining strict data-loss-prevention and audit requirements.
- A past example (RPA deployment for retail customer support) is cited as delivering "massive operational expenditure reductions," used as evidence that automation can directly redirect capital toward AI governance and innovation.

## Quote

> Consolidate to innovate: Streamline tool sprawl across NetOps, EUC, and SecOps to unlock the capital needed to fund critical AI and digital banking initiatives.
