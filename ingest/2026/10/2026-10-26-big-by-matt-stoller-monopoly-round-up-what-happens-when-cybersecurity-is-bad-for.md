# Monopoly Round-Up: What Happens When Cybersecurity is Bad for Business

## Insights

- AI has lowered the cost of exploiting software vulnerabilities, producing a "step change" in systemic risk — exemplified by PHP2Shell, a flaw in PHP (used by ~70% of websites) discovered using AI tools from Wiz, Calif, and OpenAI.
- The underlying fragility predates generative AI: cyberattack statistics, hospital ransomware shutdowns, and warnings from JPMorgan's CEO cited as "current" in the piece's opening were actually from 2019, suggesting AI is accelerating an existing structural problem rather than creating a new one.
- Private equity's standard playbook — acquiring companies, then offshoring or cutting cybersecurity/engineering/IT spending to boost margins — is identified as a recurring root cause (PowerSchool/Bain, SolarWinds, Pulse Connect Secure, Kaseya, CDK Global, Change Health).
- Cybersecurity is framed as a market failure: companies externalize risk onto the public because customers can't assess security quality until after a breach occurs (citing Bruce Schneier), unlike regulated domains such as construction or food safety.
- U.S. policy has consistently favored voluntary over mandatory cybersecurity standards since the 2013 NIST framework fight, due to industry lobbying and lack of progressive engagement; binding rules (e.g., 2022 CIRCIA) remain stalled or unenforced as of late 2026.
- Google's $32B acquisition of security firm Wiz means a single Big Tech company now sits as a chokepoint for cybersecurity intelligence across much of the internet.
