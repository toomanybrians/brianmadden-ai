# Google bid $10 million for an airline's old work emails to train AI. Much of what made that work matter never showed up in an email.

## Insights

- Google bid $10 million (pending bankruptcy court approval) for Spirit Airlines' internal business records — emails, Teams messages, spreadsheets — explicitly for AI training and product development, prompting the question of what working data actually captures.
- The piece argues records of work often miss what made the work valuable: a document's revision history shows word changes, not the realization, disagreement-resolution, or reframing that drove them.
- Presentations and documents are framed as tools for alignment, not the "real work" itself — the actual value is in people changing their minds, dropping bad plans, or avoiding unnecessary projects, which often leaves little or no trace in records.
- AI's ability to quickly reproduce outputs (documents, presentations) can feel threatening because people use effortful artifacts to measure their own contribution — the discomfort is proposed as a prompt to distinguish genuine problem-understanding from mere performance of expected effort.
- The piece reframes AI's opportunity: faster output creation can free time not just for more of the same output, but for testing/prototyping ideas with end users before committing resources — potentially making previously out-of-reach projects (e.g., underserved recurring customer problems, small business services) viable.
- Proposes that AI should prompt revisiting assumptions about what requires significant time/money/people, surfacing shelved ideas now within reach, rather than just defending the value of past work.
