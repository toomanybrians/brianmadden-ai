# The Age of Programmable Biology

## Insights

- The piece frames biology as newly "programmable" due to the convergence of AI and synthetic biology: AI can model the effect of a genomic edit before it's made, and multiplex editing can write dozens of changes simultaneously, enabling organisms to be designed in silico before being made real.
- Cites the cost of sequencing a human genome falling from ~$100M (2001) to ~$200 today — a 500,000-fold drop — as the underlying exponential curve enabling this shift, explicitly compared to (and faster than) Moore's Law.
- Notes a $1.8B commitment from Chan Zuckerberg Biohub, DOE, NIH, Google DeepMind, Isomorphic Labs, and Meta to generate data for an AI "virtual cell" — signaling major institutional and AI-lab investment in modeling biology computationally.
- Colossal Biosciences is presented as having built an integrated, end-to-end platform (read genome → AI-model a change → write via multiplex editing → grow → scale) that previously required separate disciplines/labs/timelines, demonstrated publicly via the woolly mouse and de-extincted dire wolves.
- Author announces NeoGenesis Capital, a new investment vehicle concentrating capital in the Colossal platform and its spinouts (software, plastic-digesting microbes, an evolution-trained biology model, plus stealth companies in longevity, fertility, agriculture, AI-native genetic design) rather than diversifying across the broader synthetic bio field.
- Advises different audiences to prepare: entrepreneurs should consider programmable-biology applications across food/materials/medicine/pets/beauty; executives should add synthetic biology to five-year roadmaps now; students should learn biology and AI jointly as complementary literacies.

## Quote

> We spent the last half century learning to read life. The next decade is about designing it, writing it and producing it.
