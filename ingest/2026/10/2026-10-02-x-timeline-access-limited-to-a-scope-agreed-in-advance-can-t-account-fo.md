# Access limited to a scope agreed in advance can't account for unforeseen risks,

## Insights

- Pre-agreed, fixed-scope access for AI safety assessments is inadequate because it can't anticipate novel risks, such as new training methods producing unforeseen failure modes.
- Proposes that sensitive concerns (e.g., protecting proprietary data or algorithms) don't require limiting assessor access — data can remain on developer infrastructure with blocked egress, and algorithmic details can be covered by NDAs instead of being walled off.
- Argues that a full safety assessment requires persistent, deep access across multiple domains: training techniques (including experimental ones), training data, safety measures, internal deployment traffic, incident history/existing evidence, internal tools, and staff interviews.
- Frames the four assessment components (implied: techniques, data, safety measures, deployment) as interdependent — none can be meaningfully evaluated in isolation or through narrow, one-time access grants.

## Quote

> Access limited to a scope agreed in advance can't account for unforeseen risks.
