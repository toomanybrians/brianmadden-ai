# Supply chain resilience starts with adaptability.

When disruption impacts opera

## Insights

- The piece argues resilient organizations should plan around capabilities needed regardless of disruption cause (continuous system access, real-time visibility, secure collaboration, rapid supplier/worker onboarding, flexible infrastructure scaling, governance) rather than predicting specific disruption events.
- It reframes scenario planning around decision points rather than event probabilities — e.g., instead of asking which supplier might fail, ask how fast a backup supplier can be onboarded.
- Cites Unilever's supply chain chief claiming agility investment yields roughly 10x the return of forecasting/scenario-planning spend.
- Frames supply chain resilience as fundamentally an access/continuity-of-work problem: performance depends on whether people can reach applications, workflows, and data regardless of location, device, or office/network dependency.
- References WEF language describing current supply chains as facing "structural volatility" — disruption as a permanent condition rather than a temporary one — and notes 74% of supply chain leaders (per WEF 2026 research) now see resilience as a growth driver rather than just risk management.
- Positions this as a CIO-level business continuity question, not purely an IT one: can operations continue if people can't work the way they do today.

## Quote

> Every dollar we spent on agility has probably got a 10x return on every dollar spent on forecasting or scenario planning.
