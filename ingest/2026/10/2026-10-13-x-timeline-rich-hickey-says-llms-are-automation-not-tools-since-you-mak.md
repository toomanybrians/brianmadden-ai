# Rich Hickey says LLMs are automation, not tools, since you make a request and th

## Insights

- Rich Hickey argues LLMs are fundamentally "automation," not "tools," based on a specific definition: a tool is something you wield with your hands, with control, continuous direction, and sensory feedback; automation is a process that works on its own with little direct human control.
- He contrasts the two experientially: tool use builds expertise through continuous technique and feedback, teaching you about the world, the task, and yourself; automation use instead means trying to "divine" and game an opaque system's behavior (e.g., crafting the right prompt/"incantations").
- Hickey warns that knowledge gained from working with automation is fragile — it can be rendered obsolete by a single model/algorithm update, unlike durable real-world skill built through tool use.
- Applied to reading/writing: using an LLM to summarize a paper isn't "using it as a tool" — the LLM did the reading, so the user misses learning to write, argue, notice detail, and think, calling this "abdication" rather than tool use.
- He rejects anthropomorphizing language like "misaligned," "rogue," or "conspiring" for tools, implying such language is only coherent because LLMs are automation, not tools — and that treating AI as "just a tool" ("what you make of it") is a category error.
- A commenter pushes back, framing AI as a "foundry" of tools that elevates the user from individual craftsperson to organizational director of tool-users — raising the question of whether this is abdication or a shift in required skillset.

## Quote

> LLMs are not tools. LLMs are automation.
