# Nubank walks away from £10B Monzo deal after 10% share drop 👋📉; Did AmEx and Perplexity just launch an AI CFO for small businesses? 🤖📊

## Insights

- American Express and Perplexity launched roughly 10 "Skills" inside Perplexity Computer that use AmEx Small Business Card data (via read-only Plaid links) to generate cash-flow forecasts, reconciliation, tax prep, vendor benchmarking, and scenario planning — but the tool cannot execute payments, a deliberate limit since AmEx keeps payment authority in-house via its own Agentic Commerce Experience (ACE) kit.
- The deal is structured so Perplexity gains the clearer near-term win (paid seats plus metered usage revenue from a high-intent customer channel), while AmEx gives up little — it isn't exclusive, isn't liable for outputs, and retains the card, credit line, and payment tooling.
- Compared to other small-business AI agents, Perplexity/AmEx is described as the narrowest offering: Anthropic's Claude for Small Business has 43 workflows, connectors to QuickBooks/Xero/Gusto, 900K+ plugin installs, and already stages payments for approval; Meta's Muse is free with usage limits and draws on Instagram/ad data.
- The piece flags that none of the three competing small-business AI agent products (Perplexity/AmEx, Claude, Muse) yet holds authority to actually pay on a business owner's behalf — all stop at drafts/approvals — but whichever party controls payment authority is positioned to capture the most value.
- A potential future inflection point identified: if a Skill flags a cash shortfall and simultaneously offers an AmEx credit line in the same output, the forecasting tool effectively becomes a lending channel, raising questions about whether a lender-curated "AI CFO" ranks options neutrally.
- Separately noted: Barclays is expanding use of Anthropic's Claude Code, targeting 50% of its developer population by end of 2026 and a majority of engineers by 2027, as part of a push toward £2B in AI-driven efficiency savings; it already has a Claude-based colleague knowledge assistant (16,000+ staff, 1M searches) and email-processing that classifies/routes ~120,000 emails daily.
- JPMorgan Chase topped Evident's AI Index as the most AI-advanced bank for another year (with Capital One and RBC rounding out the top three); across 50 banks assessed, AI capability advanced nearly 3x faster than prior years, and the number of banks reporting realized/projected ROI from AI rose from 8 to 12.
