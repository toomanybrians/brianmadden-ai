# 💯. And as predicted. 

1. Better and better open models with no controls on them

## Insights

- Predicts a three-stage trajectory for open-weight AI models: (1) increasingly capable open models released with no safety controls, (2) proliferation of "harnesses" built to weaponize these models for harm (cybercrime, organized crime), (3) governments eventually criminalizing use of open models.
- Cites a real-world data point as validation: a single hacker used AI agents to attack 1,200+ websites, compromising 100+ of them for roughly $5,000 in total token/compute cost — about $50 per successful hack.
- Frames this as part of a broader shift termed "breachpocalypse," with incident-response (IR) firms reportedly saturated and turning away work.
- Implies AI agents are now operationally effective at autonomously executing and winning offensive cyber campaigns at very low cost, not just assisting human attackers.
