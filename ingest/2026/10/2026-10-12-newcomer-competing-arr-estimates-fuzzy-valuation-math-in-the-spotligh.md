# Competing ARR Estimates & Fuzzy Valuation Math in the Spotlight as Markets Get Edgy

## Insights

- OpenAI's reported ARR confusion stemmed from gross vs. net revenue accounting: Anthropic reports gross (including partner resales via Amazon/Microsoft, who take a cut), while OpenAI reports net — making OpenAI's figure look artificially weaker in comparisons despite similar underlying business scale.
- A funding-round document leak led the Financial Times to report OpenAI's ARR as $20B below the widely-cited $70B figure, triggering an AI stock selloff even though the discrepancy was largely a reporting-methodology issue, not a business decline.
- Tranched funding rounds at multiple valuations are becoming normalized in AI investing, partly as an optics strategy — raising transparency concerns for investors, employees, and founders about what a startup's "true" valuation actually is.
- Three new open-source LLMs (Reflection's Beam, Mistral's Large 4, Aleph Alpha's Kolibri) launched from the US, France, and Germany explicitly marketed around "AI sovereignty" and countering Chinese models like DeepSeek — but investors report portfolio companies mostly don't care about a model's national origin unless handling sensitive government/trade data.
- Despite the sovereignty framing, Chinese open models (GLM 5.3, Kimi K3, DeepSeek V4.1) remain the most-used on inference platforms Baseten and Fireworks, and DeepSeek is reportedly close to raising $12B co-led by Tencent and CATL.
- Open-source's cost advantage is contested: while compute-per-query may be cheaper, customizing open models for specific tasks can be expensive, and some companies (e.g., Databricks) have already stepped back from building their own foundation models due to cost and fast obsolescence.
- Bill Gurley argued open models are more secure due to community stress-testing and warned that regulating open source based on misuse risk would effectively create a government-enforced monopoly for closed frontier labs.
