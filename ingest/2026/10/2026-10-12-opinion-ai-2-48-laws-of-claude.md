# 48 Laws of Claude

## Insights

- Recommends matching model tier to task difficulty (Haiku for cheap repetitive work, Sonnet for everyday tasks, Opus for high-stakes/complex decisions) and chaining them into multi-model workflows to control cost.
- Advocates structuring Claude around persistent "Projects" with saved instructions, uploaded writing samples, and example documents so tone/format/standards don't need re-explaining each session.
- Pushes techniques to reduce hallucination and shallow output: requiring sourced answers from uploaded files, explicit "not found" instead of invented figures, self-critique passes, and having Claude ask clarifying questions before starting ambiguous work.
- Describes agentic/automation features — scheduled recurring tasks, cloud-based jobs running without a laptop open, connectors to Gmail/Drive/Slack, parallel or subagent review in Claude Code — as ways to offload ongoing work rather than one-off prompting.
- Emphasizes maintaining continuity across sessions via handover notes, decision logs, and memory review, treating AI work as persistent institutional process rather than disposable chats.
- Closes with a governance principle: let the model handle research, drafting, and preparation, but require explicit human approval before sending, deleting, spending, or making permanent changes.

## Quote

> You can research, organize, calculate, prepare files, and draft messages. Ask for my approval before sending anything.
