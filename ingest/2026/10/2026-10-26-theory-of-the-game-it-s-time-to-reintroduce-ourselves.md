# It’s time to reintroduce ourselves

## Insights

- Hoffman cites concrete examples of AI matching or exceeding human intelligence in specialized domains: pushing math research frontiers, Stanford AI agents analyzing ~50,000 clinical trials to find a lung cancer therapy later given FDA breakthrough designation, and AI tutoring in Nigeria producing 1.5–2 years of learning gains in six weeks.
- He argues these developments undermine the premise that raw intelligence is what makes humans unique or valuable, prompting a search for alternative definitions of human identity beyond "Homo sapiens."
- He surveys a series of alternative human identities drawn from philosophy/social science (Homo faber/maker, ludens/player, aestheticus/expressive, festivus/celebratory, donator/giving, curans/caring, viator/traveler, prospectus/anticipatory), framing each as a quality AI cannot replace.
- Central claim: these identities aren't standalone — they must interweave (e.g., "making" needs "caring" to determine what's worth building; "anticipating futures" needs play, art, and giving to know which futures are worth pursuing).
- Proposes a practical evaluative test for AI systems: whether they help people exercise judgment, deepen relationships, and actively participate (make, give, play, care) — or merely raise output while turning people into spectators.
- Notes agency over technology is not fixed or granted once — it's continually claimed or ceded — and cites that nearly half of American adults now use AI chatbots, up from one-third two years prior.

## Quote

> Intelligence is now something we can build. Wisdom is knowing what intelligence is for and whom it serves. — Reid Hoffman
