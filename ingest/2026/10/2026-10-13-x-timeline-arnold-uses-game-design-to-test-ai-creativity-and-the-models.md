# Arnold uses game design to test AI creativity, and the models come up with odd m

## Insights

- Raemon runs a personal "taste eval" on AI models by asking them to design a novel game with never-before-seen mechanics, using his own game-design expertise as the judge of genuine creative novelty.
- Early models defaulted to recycling existing tropes (e.g., repeatedly proposing "time manipulation mechanics" echoing the 20-year-old game Braid); reasoning models began producing more original ideas, and later models became more reasonably fun.
- o1 produced a "scratch and sniff" card game concept that was evocative but, on inspection, didn't functionally differ from standard power-card mechanics with powers simply written on the card.
- Opus 4.6, prompted explicitly to generate a novel *concept* rather than just a novel *mechanic*, produced "Tabula Rasa" — a game where pieces are placed before rules exist, and later-defined rules retroactively determine the value of already-placed pieces — judged as notable though not fully novel in the way intended.
- The model's raw reasoning trace shows it generating and discarding dozens of candidate concepts (shadow-based "Umbra," wave-interference "Resonance," timeline-cascade "Causality," vibration-based "Tremor," etc.), repeatedly self-critiquing that ideas were derivative, overcomplicated, or not demonstrably fun.
- A key secondary eval criterion mentioned: whether a game concept "still helps you think after you forget the game" — a bar for conceptual usefulness beyond mere mechanical novelty.

## Quote

> His better test is whether a concept still helps you think after you forget the game.
