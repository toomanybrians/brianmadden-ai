# Anthropic’s Pending IPO Has Silicon Valley on the Edge of Its Seat

## Insights

- Anthropic's pre-IPO fundraising forced major VCs (Menlo Ventures, Lightspeed, ICONIQ) to raise new "megafunds," solicit LP co-investment, and market special-purpose vehicles just to write checks large enough to participate — a new dynamic in Silicon Valley VC.
- Anthropic's upcoming S-1 is expected to show a cap table dominated by corporate investors and late-stage megafunds, with smaller VCs holding modest but highly lucrative stakes.
- OpenAI is reportedly in talks for a $30 billion raise at a $1.25 trillion valuation as a bridge to its own IPO, reinforcing the trend of ballooning capital requirements in AI.
- The megafund model has structural limits: it depends on a small pool of large institutional LPs who haven't seen much cash returned over the past decade and will eventually demand stronger returns; a large pool of unicorns still has no viable IPO or M&A exit path.
- A parallel investor class — wealthy individuals and smaller funds (e.g., Sound Ventures, Factorial Funds) who made money in earlier tech waves — is capturing outsized stakes in deals like Anthropic and SpaceX when traditional VC giants pass or get priced out.
- Separately, a data-center capacity debate emerged: SemiAnalysis projects 50GW online by 2027 based on signed contracts, while Altimeter's Brad Gerstner initially argued for a lower ~25GW estimate, with the dispute centering on whether physical buildout can keep pace with prepaid/debt-financed GPU purchases.
