# 🪄 Google Breaks Open Quantum's Magic Bottleneck

## Insights

- Google demonstrated "magic state cultivation" working on real hardware (its Willow chip), a technique developed on paper in 2024, cutting errors ~40x and reaching 99.99% logical fidelity — addressing what's expected to be one of quantum computing's biggest overheads (estimates put up to 95% of a machine's capacity into producing magic states).
- The demonstration remains small-scale: the cultivation filter keeps only ~8% of attempts, and the hardest step of the original theory (moving states into a ~500-qubit code) doesn't yet fit on current hardware.
- Interpretability researcher Nick Cammarata (co-author of the original "Zoom In" paper on neuron superposition) now argues the field misread its own evidence — a preliminary audit of Qwen3-32B found ~90% of neuron signals have one main interpretable job, versus ~1.7% judged legible by earlier (2023) automated tools.
- Anthropic's IPO pricing discussions have reportedly dropped toward ~$1.5 trillion from an expected ~$2 trillion, despite 45% quarterly growth and adjusted profitability, as growth decelerated from quarter to quarter; context includes a selloff in AI infrastructure financing (CoreWeave).
- Nvidia released an open-weights multimodal model (Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, 120B parameters/12B active, 1M token context) explicitly tested for integration into existing coding agent tools like Claude Code and Codex.
- Taiwan plans to criminalize AI chip smuggling to China directly (previously prosecuted only via forgery/breach-of-trust charges), aligning its export law with U.S. restrictions amid large-scale smuggling cases involving Nvidia and Super Micro hardware.
