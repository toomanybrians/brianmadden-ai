# The Agentic Bank Run is coming for banking’s cheapest money 🤖🏦; Robinhood Agents is live, and it earns from your trades, not the AI 🤖📈

## Insights

- Apollo's Torsten Slok warns of an "agentic bank run": AI agents (e.g., Meta's Muse) could systematically sweep household cash from low-yield checking accounts (0.1% national average) into higher-yield fintech accounts (3.3%–5%), raising banks' cost of deposits even if total deposits in the system stay flat.
- The shift mirrors the Amazon/Shopify split: incumbents with profit pools dependent on customer inertia (big banks, Amazon) are blocking or restricting agents, while challengers that gain from rate-comparison (SoFi, Wealthfront, Shopify) are welcoming them — framed via a16z's Alex Immerman's two-question test (does the agent bring new demand, and does profit depend on owning the customer relationship).
- Permissioning is the key threshold: agents today mostly read accounts (via Plaid) and require approval to move money, but features like Rocket Money's "Rowan" or standing instructions ("keep $2,000, sweep the rest") would let agents act autonomously across millions of accounts simultaneously, a qualitatively different risk.
- Robinhood Agents reframes the business model: Robinhood resells OpenAI/Anthropic model access at cost and makes its money from trade execution/transaction revenue (already 44% of Q2 revenue), not from the AI itself — it wants the account and execution layer, not AI margins.
- Liability for agentic trading is structurally unclear: Robinhood's AI software comes from a non-broker-dealer entity, placing risk on the user; regulators (House Democrats questioning the SEC) and FINRA's "technology neutral" stance suggest enforcement cases, not new rules, will define accountability.
- Both stories point to the same mechanism: software gaining "standing authority" over money (recurring instructions, automated sweeps, autonomous trading) is the real inflection point, more consequential than current agent usage levels (e.g., average Robinhood agent balances are still only ~$1,000).

## Quote

> What agents change is who gets paid for holding it, and banks and fintechs are splitting over agents the same way Amazon and Shopify split when Muse launched.
