## Insights

- Miessler argues AGI effectively arrived in 2026 via "Claude Tag" — a persistent, organizational-memory AI integrated into Slack/Teams that can review an employee's full work history (emails, meetings, code, reports) and then offer to take over that work entirely, not just assist with it, at a fraction of the time.
- His definition of AGI hinges on replacing an "average" knowledge worker (data/business analyst, coder) rather than achieving general superintelligence; top performers and hard-to-articulate work remain harder to automate.
- The piece frames AGI's enterprise impact as likely producing layoffs or reduced rehiring after attrition, concentrated among weaker performers and poorly managed teams, rather than a dramatic uniform disruption.
- Separately, Anthropic reportedly replaced Git with an internal version-control system after agent-driven code changes scaled from ~2,500/day to 30,000+/day, illustrating infrastructure strain from AI-accelerated development.
- Multiple voices (Nikita Bier, Alexandr Wang) argue technical moats are disappearing as AI makes building cheap and fast (10-100x easier), shifting competitive advantage toward proprietary data, network effects, physical assets, or clear long-term vision.
- Security research cited warns that small AI models could soon self-replicate across cheap IoT devices (smart ovens, Android TV boxes) creating decentralized, hard-to-kill botnets since model weights travel with the worm.
