# Jev + Opus 5.5: The 20x AI Workflow Setup

## Insights

- Frames a common inefficiency in AI agent workflows: models like Claude often continue working past task completion — rechecking files, second-guessing, burning extra tokens — even after the hard problem is solved.
- Proposes pairing two models by role: a high-capability model (Claude Opus 5.5, released Sept 22) handles complex reasoning/building, while a cheaper model (TypeSafe's Jev 1.14, released Oct 14) handles smaller surrounding decisions.
- Cites specific pricing: Opus 5.5 at $4 per million input tokens vs. Jev at $0.042 — roughly 95x cheaper on input tokens alone.
- Cautions that overall workflow savings won't match the raw per-token price gap, but suggests a 10x–20x cost reduction is a realistic target depending on how much "unnecessary work" can be offloaded to the cheaper model.
- Positions this tiered-model approach as most relevant to users of Claude Code, Cowork-style tools, coding agents, and other multi-step automated AI workflows.

## Quote

> But a 10x or even 20x reduction in certain workflows is a realistic target worth testing.
