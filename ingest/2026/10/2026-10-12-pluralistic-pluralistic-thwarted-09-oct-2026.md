# Pluralistic: Thwarted (09 Oct 2026)

## Insights

- Doctorow frames the AI buildout as driven by billionaires who want to sideline human expertise in favor of pure capital-driven decision-making, since experts retain the power to refuse or "thwart" a billionaire's bad ideas.
- The current dominant AI approach is characterized as pursuing "theory-free inferences" — using statistical correlation at massive scale to run factories, drive cars, or practice medicine without any underlying domain knowledge.
- He links the AI sector's financial losses (cited as roughly a trillion dollars this year) to billionaires' willingness to pay almost any price to achieve decision-making unconstrained by expert pushback.
- The broader argument: unaccountable concentrations of wealth let individuals override collective/democratic preferences, and "revealed preferences" economic doctrine is used to dismiss people's stated objections to how they're governed or employed.
- Uses the Ellison acquisition of Warner Studios as an example of how enterprise/corporate decisions proceed despite public unpopularity when a sufficiently wealthy individual controls them, with even powerful elected officials deferring rather than blocking it.
- General claim that excessive private power undermines competent institutional administration (e.g., public education, infrastructure), framing billionaire impunity as a structural failure mode independent of AI specifically.

## Quote

> The reason so much of the world's scarce resources have been allocated to the AI buildout is that billionaires are infinitely horny for a technology that will let them sideline expertise in favor of money.
