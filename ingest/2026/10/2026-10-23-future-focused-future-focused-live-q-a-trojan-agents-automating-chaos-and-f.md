# Future-Focused Live Q&A: Trojan Agents, Automating Chaos and Failure, and Hollowing Talent

## Insights

- Agentic AI tools (Meta Muse, OpenAI Dots, Grokbot) are being marketed with friendly, mascot-like personas specifically to lower users' psychological guardrails before handing over account access and autonomy — framed as a "Trojan horse" dynamic.
- Most current agentic AI use is described as a net-negative waste of time/compute/money; the author argues that if a task is trivial enough to hand to an unmonitored bot, the better question is why it's being done at all.
- Shadow AI use by employees (unsanctioned bots for client updates, scheduling) often stems from slow or unclear official tooling and lack of open conversation with management — companies often already own approved tools employees don't know about.
- Vendor lock-in risk with ecosystem-driven agentic tools is real; the recommended mitigation is building an abstraction/interface layer so the underlying AI model (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google) can be swapped without rebuilding workflows.
- When automation fails, leaders should baseline/map the existing manual process and track hours lost to cleanup *before* claiming AI efficiency gains — several cited cases found the fix was a broken data pipeline, not a missing AI agent.
- On layoffs and surveillance: job losses are attributed partly to companies offsetting unexpected metered AI costs via headcount cuts, and workplace surveillance (screen/keystroke monitoring) is portrayed as unstoppable — advice given is to keep deep thinking off company devices and only bring finished output to company systems.
- Freezing junior hiring in favor of agents is flagged as a long-term talent-pipeline risk (no one to promote into senior roles later); recommended countermeasure is pairing junior staff with seniors to build judgment while using AI, documenting risks, and letting mandated failures play out visibly rather than absorbing the cleanup silently.
