# Google Has More Data Than Almost Anyone. So Why Is It Bidding $10 Million On Old Emails?

## Insights

- Google reportedly bid $10 million for Spirit Airlines' old work emails and Teams records specifically to train AI agents, signaling that corporate communication archives are now treated as valuable AI training data.
- A core tension raised: most workplace records (emails, chats) may capture the "performance of work" rather than the work itself, raising doubts about whether this data can actually teach AI agents to do real jobs.
- The piece distinguishes between verifiable work artifacts (like invoices) and ambiguous ones, framing this as a "PRD problem" — defining what counts as evidence that a job was actually done.
- Mercor is named as a player involved in judging/defining when a job is considered complete, tying into how AI agent training pipelines decide what "done" looks like.
- Data brokers are described as a "data of last resort" for AI training, suggesting a market hierarchy where company archives and broker-sourced data are being tapped once better sources run out.
- Workers are beginning to push back against companies selling their work data/communications to AI companies, an emerging friction point between labor and AI data markets.
- Raises the idea that AI agents can "simulate work" (e.g., recognizable AI-generated outputs like a "Claude PowerPoint"), implying detectable gaps between genuine and simulated work product.

## Quote

> AI agents can take real drudgery off your plate, and the people building them still need a credible answer to what your work is.
