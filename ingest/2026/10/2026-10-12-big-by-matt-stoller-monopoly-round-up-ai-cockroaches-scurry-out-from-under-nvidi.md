# Monopoly Round-Up: AI Cockroaches Scurry Out from Under Nvidia, the Most Valuable Company in the World

## Insights

- Nvidia practices "circular financing" — investing in customers (sometimes through multiple layers, e.g., OpenAI buying from Oracle, which buys from Nvidia) so they can afford to buy its chips, inflating demand signals for its own products.
- A major test case: Firmus, an Australian "neocloud" (data center company renting AI compute, backed by Nvidia, Jane Street, and Blackstone) had to scrap what would have been Australia's largest-ever IPO ($30B valuation) after investors balked at terms letting insiders sell shares immediately post-listing.
- Firmus's co-founder has a prior insider-trading conviction; the company pivoted from bitcoin mining to AI, a pattern described as common across a list of similarly structured "neocloud" firms (TeraWulf, Sharon AI, Applied Digital, Coreweave, Nebius, Crusoe, and others).
- The described mechanism: prestigious AI players (Nvidia, Google, Anthropic) backstop or do business with a neocloud, which inflates its private-market valuation, enabling further fundraising, more data center announcements, and more chip orders — a self-reinforcing cycle independent of underlying business viability.
- Explicit analogy drawn to 2008 subprime mortgage lending: fly-by-night originators were enabled by Wall Street banks' warehouse credit lines and prestige, masking weak underlying economics until losses were passed to end investors (then pension funds, now public market IPO buyers).
- Frames this as part of a recurring pattern of capital chasing speculative sectors (SPACs, China reverse mergers, biotech, shale, crypto/FTX) with the same promoters moving between bubbles, enabled by lack of regulatory appetite to impose capital controls.
