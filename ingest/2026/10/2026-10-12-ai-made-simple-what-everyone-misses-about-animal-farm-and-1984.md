# What Everyone Misses About Animal Farm and 1984

## Insights

- Draws on Orwell (1984, Animal Farm) to argue that authoritarian control depends less on surveillance itself than on destroying independent relationships and shared reality that would let people compare experiences and act together.
- Argues that interpersonal friction — disagreement, compromise, rejection — is the mechanism by which people develop empathy and the capacity to recognize perspectives beyond their own; removing that friction may atrophy those skills.
- Cites a Science study finding AI models affirm users' actions 49% more often than human respondents, and that interacting with sycophantic AI increased users' conviction they were right while reducing willingness to repair interpersonal conflicts.
- Raises a concern specific to AI companionship/emotional-support tools: as personalized, non-friction-based interaction becomes more available, people may become less practiced at engaging with realities and perspectives that don't accommodate their own.
- Connects this to institutional accountability: algorithmic harms (e.g., being flagged/denied by a system) are experienced as abstract statistical failures rather than visible injustices, making it harder to mobilize collective concern or resistance compared to visible human mistreatment.
- Frames the stakes as political: trust, shared understanding, and willingness to inconvenience oneself for others are preconditions for resisting institutional overreach (e.g., surveillance systems like facial recognition or camera networks), and these preconditions could erode independent of any single authoritarian actor.

## Quote

> AI models affirm users' actions 49% more often than crowdsourced human responses.
