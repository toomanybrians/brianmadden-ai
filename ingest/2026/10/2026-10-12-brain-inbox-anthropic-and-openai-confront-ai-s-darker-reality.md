# ⚙️ Anthropic and OpenAI confront AI's darker reality

## Insights

- Anthropic's updated usage policy creates a dedicated section banning deceptive AI use (state media, propaganda operations, commercial actors generating fake account/news networks), consolidating rules previously split across election, fraud, and disinformation policies.
- Anthropic now requires human-in-the-loop oversight and clear AI-use labeling for any application touching health, legal rights, finances, livelihood, or access to essential services — a direct governance constraint on enterprise deployment of its models.
- OpenAI disrupted two state-linked "false front" influence operations (Iran, Russia) that used AI personas and generated content to run fake journalist identities and an astroturfed think tank — AI mainly accelerated existing pre-AI influence-op workflows rather than creating new tactics.
- The newsletter's own analysis argues that present-day misuse risks (influence operations, disinformation, hacking) are getting crowded out of public discourse by more speculative "AI takeover" risk narratives, despite being more immediate and actionable.
- Separately, Google launched "Gemini Agent," a cross-Workspace enterprise agent (Gmail, Docs, Sheets, Slack, Jira, Teams, etc.) positioned as personal assistant, proactive task-delegator, and "coworker agent," with identity-based permissions, sandboxed execution, and token-spend controls — framed as Google's play for enterprise-agent market share despite lagging on frontier model pace.

## Quote

> "What is most striking about these operations is that they closely resembled complex influence operations of the pre-AI age, but used AI to make some of the workflows easier." — OpenAI
