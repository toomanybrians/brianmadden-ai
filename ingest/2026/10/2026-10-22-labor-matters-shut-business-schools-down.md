# Shut business schools down?

## Insights

- A Cambridge Judge Business School professor's viral NYT op-ed argued for shutting down business schools, citing research (Acemoglu et al.) that firms led by CEOs with business degrees pay lower wages without producing more output, investment, or employment.
- Data from the Burning Glass Institute shows MBA/business-master's enrollment within five years of graduation has been falling sharply among graduates of the most selective U.S. colleges for nearly 15 years — from 7.3% (class of 2006) to 4.7% (class of 2020) at the top 2.5% of colleges.
- The decline is concentrated at elite institutions; the bottom 80% of colleges by selectivity saw enrollment rates barely move, and rates at the least selective colleges have actually been rising since 2015.
- The author offers two possible (unconfirmed) explanations: elite graduates may be finding they can advance careers without a business degree, or the degree may be losing signaling value to employers.
- The piece frames this as a market-driven unwinding of business school demand among top talent, independent of the debate over whether schools should be forcibly "shut down."

## Quote

> At least among graduates of the most selective colleges, they're already emptying out on their own.
