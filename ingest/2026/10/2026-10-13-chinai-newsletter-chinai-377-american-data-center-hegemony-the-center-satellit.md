# ChinAI #377: American Data Center Hegemony - the Center-Satellite Structure

## Insights

- A 2024 Beijing Cultural Review essay by Yanhui He argues U.S. AI dominance rests less on chips/algorithms and more on large-scale control of physical resources: compute, electricity, cooling, and land.
- The essay describes a "center-satellite" structure: U.S. hyperscalers (Google, Microsoft, Amazon) own more than half of global hyperscale data centers, with countries like Ireland and the Netherlands serving as infrastructure hubs due to their internet connectivity.
- As of 2023, the U.S. hosted 5,000 data centers versus fewer than 2,000 combined for China, Germany, and the UK — not counting U.S. companies' overseas facilities.
- The dependency structure is most pronounced in developing countries: 80% of Nigeria's public health data sits on Google/Amazon/Microsoft servers, which the essay argues boosts providers' profits and data access without building local data sovereignty capacity.
- China is framed as a "special case" — its domestic market is dominated by local firms (Amazon is the only foreign top-five player, at 8.1% share) due to licensing restrictions, yet Chinese firms expanding overseas (NetEase, Bilibili) rely on Amazon for nearly 80% of their international data processing.
- Even sovereign-cloud efforts in France and Germany still depend on Microsoft/Google infrastructure, and small European enterprises largely oppose stricter cross-border data flow regulation due to cost concerns — illustrating the stickiness of U.S. infrastructural dominance.

## Quote

> The current AI race can also be seen as "a competition over the strategic deployment of data centers."
