# Flying Blind

## Insights

- Meta's Muse AI personal assistant hit #1 in App Store downloads and added roughly $400 billion to Meta's market cap, with early use cases including auto-canceling forgotten subscriptions and quitting services (e.g., a gym) without human interaction.
- Analysts warn Muse-style AI agents could disrupt bank profitability by moving consumer savings from near-zero-interest accounts (avg. 0.1%) to high-yield accounts, and could gut subscription-based businesses since roughly half of subscription revenue allegedly comes from forgotten/unused subscriptions.
- AI threatens jobs built on inefficiency: medical billing staff, customer service (2.8M workers), and insurance claims processing (2.9M workers, including ~400K claims adjusters) are cited as vulnerable to automation of routine verification and information-passing tasks.
- A banking executive described two parallel but asynchronous trends: AI models improving rapidly, and organizations' slower progress operationalizing them — the gap between the two is currently masking corporate layoffs/productivity shifts, but convergence is expected to trigger significant staff reductions within 1-2 years.
- Anthropic's leaked S-1 shows 2025 revenue of $4.59B and $518B in compute commitments to Google, Amazon, and Microsoft, targeting a $2 trillion IPO valuation — despite annualized revenue growth of 14.3x, this would imply a ~25x revenue multiple versus the S&P 500's 3.6x, which the author calls unjustifiable "froth."
- Investors report early signs of corporate pullback on per-employee AI spending (both Claude and ChatGPT) due to unclear ROI, raising doubts about whether AI vendors can sustain the growth needed to justify current/expected valuations, especially ahead of a possible OpenAI IPO.

## Quote

> When the updraft ends, gravity remains. It may not feel like it, but we're in the air right now.
