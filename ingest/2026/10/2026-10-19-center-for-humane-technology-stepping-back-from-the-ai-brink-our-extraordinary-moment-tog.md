# Stepping Back From the AI Brink: Our Extraordinary Moment Together In Iceland

## Insights

- Icelandic President Halla Tómasdóttir invited CHT's Tristan Harris and Aza Raskin to help lead a national and global summit on the AI arms race, held October 11 — chosen to mark 40 years since the 1986 Reagan-Gorbachev Reykjavík Summit on nuclear arms.
- The framing explicitly draws a parallel between today's AI race and Cold War nuclear brinkmanship, including the idea that a single film ("The Day After" then, "The AI Doc" now) can shift a national leader's risk perception enough to drive diplomatic action.
- Iceland held a preceding national AI dialogue (broadcasting "The AI Doc" on national TV) before the summit itself, suggesting a model of public pre-education ahead of elite negotiation.
- CHT presented a new talk, "The Humanity Dilemma," to thousands of world leaders at the summit.
- CHT's concrete policy ask: a verified "red line" prohibiting the use of compute for recursive self-improvement in AI systems, with verification mechanisms and treaties to be worked out afterward.
- The piece frames the core obstacle as a psychological/narrative one — breaking the sense of "inevitability" that follows each new AI model release and associated harms — rather than purely a technical or diplomatic one.

## Quote

> We need to break this spell of inevitability that follows each fresh cycle of model releases and uncontrollable harms.
