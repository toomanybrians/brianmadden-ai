# The Home Stretch

## Insights

- A16Z laid off most of its 34-person in-house talent/recruiting team, which Yang frames as a signal that portfolio companies have stopped hiring senior people — attributed to some mix of AI, slower growth, or capital constraints.
- VC-backed startups are characterized as leading indicators ("canaries in the coal mine") for the broader economy, since they're first to feel hiring and funding headwinds when conditions turn.
- Anthropic's quarterly revenue grew 45% to $16.8 billion, but that growth rate is decelerating from the prior quarter, and its private-market valuation fell 10% (from $2 trillion to $1.8 trillion).
- The dominant AI narrative has been pure growth ("to the moon"), but as growth visibly slows, valuation multiples (e.g., 18x revenue) become vulnerable to sharp compression — a risk Yang says markets haven't fully priced in.
- AI is described as simultaneously driving economic growth and public anxiety about the future, a tension Yang says didn't exist in prior cycles.
- Yang criticizes political leadership for allowing AI companies to "self-regulate" rather than establishing oversight, lumping this in with other policy failures (Iran-related cost increases, tariffs) contributing to economic strain.
