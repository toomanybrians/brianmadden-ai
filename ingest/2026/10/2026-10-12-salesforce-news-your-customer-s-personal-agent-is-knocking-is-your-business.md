# Your Customer’s Personal Agent Is Knocking. Is Your Business Ready to Answer?

## Insights

- Personal AI agents (Meta's Muse, OpenAI's Dots, the invite-only "Instinct AI" reportedly near $1B in annual transactions, built on an open-source project called OpenClaw) are framed as a fast-emerging consumer platform shift, with businesses currently split on whether to allow them: Amazon blocked Muse quickly, Shopify built official connectors, and Walmart's Meta partnership reportedly hit technical failures at checkout/bot-verification steps.
- Core argument: websites and APIs are inadequate interfaces for personal agents — sites are built to screen out non-humans (captchas), and APIs execute requests without judgment, negotiation, or policy awareness, forcing a visiting personal agent to infer business rules and potentially get them wrong.
- The piece proposes "business agents" (specifically framed around Salesforce's Agentforce) as the necessary counterpart: company-side agents running on internal data/workflows that can authoritatively answer questions, validate policy compliance, and use deterministic logic for high-risk actions (refunds, payments, escalations) to prevent manipulation by a customer's personal agent.
- Strategic framing: blocking personal agents preserves control but loses traffic; opening raw website/API access wins traffic but cedes the customer relationship and decision-making to the personal agent. A business agent at the "front door" is positioned as the way to capture both traffic and relationship ownership.
- Underlying business logic: since the business bears fulfillment risk and operational cost regardless of how an order arrives, the article argues it should also retain the customer relationship and data — contingent on whether it fields personal-agent interactions with its own agent.

## Quote

> The truth is, personal agents are only as reliable as the business agents waiting to greet them at the front door.
