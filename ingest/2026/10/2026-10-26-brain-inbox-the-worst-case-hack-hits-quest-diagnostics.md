# 🧪 The Worst-Case Hack Hits Quest Diagnostics

## Insights

- Quest Diagnostics disclosed hackers accessed its Quanum Lab Services Manager portal, exposing patient health and identity data; the company calls it unrelated to the week's separate PHP2Shell exploit wave affecting ~70% of websites.
- CrowdStrike CEO George Kurtz argues open-weight models with guardrails stripped now give low-level attackers capabilities once limited to nation-states, and intruders move laterally inside breached networks in as little as 27 seconds (29 minutes average).
- Anthropic says it used Claude to prove security properties across 100+ open-source libraries in weeks — work it frames as previously feasible only for small codebases or with heavy expert time, positioning AI as a defensive tool matching attacker speed.
- HIPAA Journal data shows hacking/IT-related health record exposures rising sharply since 2014, driven by breaches at centralized "hub" entities (clearinghouses, billing vendors, shared portals) like Change Healthcare (192.7M in 2024) and Conduent (62.2M in 2025) — a pattern Quest's portal breach resembles.
- DHS ordered civilian federal agencies to remove DeepSeek and any third-party products built on DeepSeek's open-weight models, citing risk of data flowing to Chinese state access and possible embedded vulnerabilities in the models themselves; China's foreign ministry called the move politicized overreach.
- MiniMax released open weights for its M3.1 Flash agent model (428B total/23B active parameters, 1M token context), letting companies self-host rather than rely on MiniMax's cloud.
