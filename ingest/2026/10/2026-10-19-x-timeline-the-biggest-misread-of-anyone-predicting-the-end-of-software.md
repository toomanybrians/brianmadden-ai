# The biggest misread of anyone predicting the end of software engineering was ima

## Insights

- The common prediction that AI-driven software automation would shrink the need for engineering labor assumes a fixed, saturable demand for software — a premise the author argues is false.
- Instead, automation appears to be expanding demand: as software becomes cheaper/faster to produce, the appetite for more of it grows rather than shrinks.
- Supporting data cited (via @fchollet): there are more software engineers now than in 2025, and demand for them continues to grow.
- Despite AI agents reportedly writing close to 100% of code, engineers are described as working harder than ever — their role has shifted away from writing code itself, not away from work.
- This reframes "automation reduces labor need" as a category error: automation can increase total labor demand when it unlocks previously uneconomical output.

## Quote

> The biggest misread of anyone predicting the end of software engineering was imagining that the world only needed a certain amount of software.
