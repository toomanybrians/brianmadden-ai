# The Agentic Bank Run is coming for banking’s cheapest money 🤖🏦; Anthropic’s IPO prospectus asks public investors to pay a $518 billion compute bill 😳📈

## Insights

- Apollo's Torsten Slok warned of an "agentic bank run": AI agents (e.g., Meta's Muse) could automatically sweep household cash from checking accounts paying ~0.1% interest into fintech accounts paying 3.3%-5%, eroding banks' cheap deposit base.
- The risk framing is distinct from a traditional bank run — most swept cash stays within the banking system overall, but control over who profits from holding deposits shifts away from traditional banks toward fintechs.
- Market reaction suggests investors are already pricing this risk: Goldman Sachs' "consumer inertia" stock basket fell over 7% in six sessions, Schwab fell 6.1%, and JPMorgan Chase and Wells Fargo each dropped more than 3% in a single session as Muse gained app-store traction.
- The dynamic is compared to the earlier Amazon/Shopify split triggered by Muse's launch, implying a bifurcation where some banks/fintechs will gain and others lose as agents mediate where money sits.
- The piece frames this as a profit-margin threat (net interest margin/deposit cost) rather than a liquidity or deposit-flight threat, since the money largely stays in-system.

## Quote

> Banks "could lose a large share of the cheap deposits they rely on to make loans."
