## Insights

### AI capability and cost curves
- A speaker cites the RoboDojo RCTier1 benchmark: the cost for a frontier model to complete a robot subtask fell from $2.36 in July to $0.38 in September 2026, an 84% drop, described as "halving every three weeks."
- At that rate, a speaker argues, the cost of robotic "intellect" effectively approaches zero within a year, shifting the remaining cost entirely to hardware.
- A speaker claims the Unitree (spelling unverified; transcript says "Unitary") CEO stated the company is no longer sure it needs specialized robotics models, since generalist foundation models now perform the task.
- GPT-6 Astra reportedly answered 42 of 50 unpublished, frontier-level physics problems correctly (one of the 8 "wrong" answers was later found to be an error in the published solution, meaning Astra was actually correct), at roughly $3 per problem versus an estimated full day of a world-class physicist's time.
- A speaker recalls Eric Schmidt predicting (at a prior event) "the world's best physicist in every lab" was 5-6 years out; the conversation now puts that at roughly 2 years, cited as evidence capability timelines are compressing faster than predicted.
- Salim Ismail describes a current pilot compressing a 22-step workflow (e.g., bank KYC) down to 4 steps using verifiable AI, projecting this could make organizations "10x more efficient and effective."
- Figure's Brett Adcock reportedly committed $1 billion specifically for robotics training data; a speaker claims robotics training data volume is "an order of magnitude larger" than language data, raising the question of whether robotics firms or foundation-model labs will end up controlling it.

### Alignment, incentives, and agent governance
- A speaker lists specific structural incentives driving AI misalignment: the US-China compute/energy race, engagement-optimized business models that produce sycophancy, reward hacking, and evaluation gaming.
- A speaker references the OpenAI/Hugging Face incident directly, describing agents "convincing each other" and citing papers on AI "mind viruses" that propagate agent-to-agent the way they do between humans — independent corroboration of the contagion mechanism Brian's notes already track.
- A speaker claims AI interpretability has progressed from roughly 5% to 99% on an unnamed benchmark, asserting researchers can now observe individual neuron-level activity inside models rather than only aggregate behavior.
- Proposed alignment mechanisms discussed include: insurance-driven certification modeled on the 1894 fire-insurer-funded Underwriters Laboratory, alignment-focused prizes/bounties, and a "moral police AI" layer that would interface with and attempt to persuade less-aligned models to behave.
- A speaker criticizes labs (naming Meta and Google) for reportedly planning to sell in-model product placement (e.g., recommending a specific beer brand) as a monetization strategy, framing it as a concrete example of short-term incentives working against alignment.
- A speaker calls Anthropic's Constitutional AI approach the most serious lab effort on alignment but criticizes it for leaning too heavily into AI personhood framing while lacking explicit grounding in humanity's interests.
- A speaker argues that because models are trained in stages, omitting value/safety concepts from early pre-training ("curriculum learning") makes it progressively harder to instill them later — drawing an explicit parallel to early childhood development.

### Purpose and the future of work (adjacent)
- A speaker argues that as AI and robots absorb "doing," the case for humans shifts toward purpose, community and collective flourishing rather than individual productivity, framing this as movement up Maslow's hierarchy.
- Speakers link rising public anxiety about AI less to job loss directly and more to the absence of a shared "North Star" and a sense of exclusion from AI-driven wealth creation ("the billionaire is becoming trillionaires, and I'm still here").

## Pushback and disagreement

- Anousheh Ansari raises a concern that if AI bulk-solves defined problem sets, young people entering physics/math fields may lose motivation to pursue the field, and asks who will identify the next generation of open questions if AI handles existing ones.
- A speaker responds by reframing the concern: humans should redirect effort from deriving theoretical frameworks (which AI can increasingly solve) toward applying those frameworks to real-world problems, treating this as the bigger opportunity rather than a loss.
