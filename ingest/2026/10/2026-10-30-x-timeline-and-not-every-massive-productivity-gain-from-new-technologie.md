# And not every massive productivity gain from new technologies in the Industrial

## Insights

- Challenges the common "electricity took 30 years to show productivity gains" analogy frequently applied to AI, arguing it's not a universal pattern for transformative technologies.
- Cites Ford's assembly line as a counterexample: full deployment took only 3 years and cut car-building time by 88%, showing rapid productivity gains are possible.
- Draws a historical analogy between steam engine makers (Boulton & Watt) and today's AI chipmakers (NVIDIA) as the visible "gold rush" winners.
- Argues that for every famous technology pioneer, many lesser-known firms ("General Amalgamated Hosiery Corporation") also benefited significantly — suggesting broad-based gains rather than winner-take-all dynamics.
- Frames this as the "opposite of Gold Rush dynamics" — implying technology adoption benefits are often more widely distributed than popular narratives suggest.

## Quote

> For every Boulton & Watt... there were a hundred firms... that did at least as well.
