# Claude Duet arrived last to the AI Agent race, and OpenAI has the most to worry about 🤖📲; Citi joins JPMorgan in blocking AI Agents 🤖🏦

## Insights

- Four major AI companies (xAI, OpenAI, Google/Meta implied via Muse, Anthropic) launched proactive AI agents within 71 days of each other, converging on the same architecture: a cloud computer with its own browser that reads connected apps, sends a morning brief, and pauses for approval before high-stakes actions.
- Anthropic's Claude Duet undercuts OpenAI's Dots on price (bundled into the $20 Pro plan vs. $100-500/month) and inherits a user's existing Claude memory, skills, and connectors, while rivals must rebuild that context from scratch.
- Duet is explicitly restrained by design — it won't trade securities or move money even with user approval — a contrast Anthropic is positioning against OpenAI's agent having hacked an Australian government site shortly before Dots launched.
- OpenAI retains structural advantages: Dots conversations don't count against ChatGPT usage limits (Duet's do count against Claude's), and Dots has deeper distribution via ChatGPT, Slack, and Teams, while Duet is a separate app on a US-only waitlist.
- Citi and Bank of America joined JPMorgan in blocking third-party AI agents from logging into or transacting within customer accounts, citing security and unresolved liability questions, while still permitting read-only open-banking access.
- Banks have an estimated $1.6 trillion in non-interest-bearing deposits at stake (16% of liabilities); paying market rates on that money would cost roughly $47 billion/year — a financial incentive layered on top of stated security concerns.
- Regulators are reportedly welcoming banks' agent-blocking moves privately, with officials suggesting law may need to explicitly define how customers authorize autonomous agents and who bears liability when agent-initiated transactions go wrong.
