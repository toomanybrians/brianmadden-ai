# Sam Altman Wants Us To Accept “Bad Things”—Just So Long As He Doesn’t Have To

## Insights

- Sam Altman publicly frames AI harms as an acceptable trade-off ("the world should accept some bad things happening for the benefits of this technology"), articulated amid OpenAI's IPO prep requiring ~$30B in additional fundraising to support a $1.4T valuation.
- The piece argues this "trade-off" rhetoric is a consistent, deliberate talking point across AI leadership (Altman, Dario Amodei, Mark Zuckerberg), not an isolated remark — framed as leaders positioning themselves as uniquely qualified to judge others' acceptable risk.
- Draws a line to effective altruism/longtermism as the ideological scaffolding behind claims that "superintelligent" actors are best positioned to act "altruistically" for humanity's survival.
- Notes the US government's executive order requiring DOJ lawyers to use "superintelligence" instead of "artificial intelligence," arguing this rebranding is a rhetorical move to make AI seem more serious and its controllers more credible/rational.
- Cites concrete harms presented as already occurring without public consent: AI-assisted military targeting decisions (Anthropic models in airstrike contexts), location data fed to government agencies, and vulnerable ChatGPT users experiencing mental-health harms tied to chatbot interactions.
- Reports that an OpenAI publicist cut off a Vanity Fair interview when Altman was asked about lawsuits from families of users who died by suicide allegedly linked to ChatGPT use.
- Item also flags unrelated industry notes: Anthropic co-founders doubled their salaries this year; Paramount-Warner merger clearance may cost ~4,500 LA film/TV jobs over three years; tech workers adopting "loud working" behavior amid layoff anxiety (illustrated by a Meta employee comparing the environment to "the Hunger Games").

## Quote

> We believe that the world should accept some bad things happening for the benefits of this technology.
