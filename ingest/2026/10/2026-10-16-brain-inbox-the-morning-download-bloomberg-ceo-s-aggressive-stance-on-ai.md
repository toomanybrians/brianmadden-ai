# The Morning Download: Bloomberg CEO’s ‘Aggressive’ Stance on AI

## Insights

- Bloomberg CEO Vlad Kliatchko argues it's premature to measure ROI on AI investment; the company is investing "aggressively" out of strategic conviction rather than near-term payback, a stance he says mirrors many CEOs he talks to, in contrast to public-market jitters.
- Bloomberg rolled out ASKB, its conversational AI interface, to all Terminal users, with a new version letting users blend proprietary content (watchlists, chat logs) with Bloomberg's financial data in personalized workflows.
- Kliatchko frames original journalism/research as newly vindicated strategic assets in the AI era, since LLMs and AI tools depend on unique, actionable underlying information — justifying past investment in Bloomberg's newsroom.
- Top AI labs face a growing insider-risk problem: rapid hiring and frequent employee movement between rival firms (OpenAI, Apple, Meta) are increasing leaks and IP theft, including a case of an ex-Apple employee at OpenAI allegedly exfiltrating confidential device files, and an OpenAI employee charged with planting a covert recording device.
- Separately, AI is also an external security threat: Bank of America CEO Brian Moynihan described deploying 1,000 contractors (on top of 3,000 existing staff) to patch vulnerabilities after AI accelerated attackers' ability to find and exploit software flaws.
- Context data points: Anthropic's Q3 revenue hit $16.8B (up from $11.6B in Q2), ahead of a planned IPO; TSMC posted a 69% profit jump on AI chip demand; banks are projected to earn $8.5B globally this year in AI-deal advisory fees.

## Quote

> We believe it's premature to focus on the ROI. We are acting out of a strategic conviction that now is the time to invest in AI aggressively. — Vlad Kliatchko
