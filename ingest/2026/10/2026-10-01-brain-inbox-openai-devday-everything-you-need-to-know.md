# 🎤 OpenAI DevDay: Everything You Need to Know

## Insights

- OpenAI's DevDay emphasized price/speed over raw capability: GPT-6.1 Sol reaches near-parity with GPT-6 Astra (52 vs 53 on Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index) at roughly a fifth of the cost ($0.72 vs $3.26 per task), while Anthropic's Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 still lead the index.
- OpenAI withheld its next flagship model (Astra successor) over internal researcher safety concerns, having also paused training on another model the prior week — Altman framed this as a deliberate policy of slowing releases when safety cases aren't solid.
- "Dots" — always-on agentic assistants built on GPT-6 Astra, each with a dedicated cloud computer and access to 4,000+ apps — launched for Pro/Business Premium users, positioned as a premium product ahead of a planned mass-market version.
- A voluntary, legally non-binding White House AI safety accord (signed by Anthropic, OpenAI, Nvidia, and others) introduces independent/outside auditors for cyber, bio, chemical, and misalignment risks; Trump called it "morally binding" and the text itself flags future codification as a possibility.
- Anthropic's red team found the open-weight model GLM-5.3 (Zhipu AI) can build working cyberattacks nearly as well as Anthropic's own gated Claude Mythos Preview, with safeguards easily stripped — a capability previously confined to vetted access now freely downloadable.
- A new multi-agent benchmark (AgentWorld) found even the best AI agent team (Gemini 3 Flash) completed only 52% of coordinated tasks, dropping to 24% on harder variants, with less than a third of actions actually contributing to success — suggesting coordination, not just model intelligence, is a distinct unsolved problem for agent teams.
