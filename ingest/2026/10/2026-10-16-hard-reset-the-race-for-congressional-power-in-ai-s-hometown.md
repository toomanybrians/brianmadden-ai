# The Race for Congressional Power in AI’s Hometown

## Insights

- Both leading candidates for Pelosi's SF House seat (Wiener and Chan) support federal AI regulation, taxing AI-derived wealth for public redistribution, and holding parent companies liable when AI agents break the law — signaling AI regulation has become a mainstream, bipartisan-adjacent campaign issue rather than a fringe one.
- Wiener's legislative track record (SB 53, California's AI safety law requiring safety-framework disclosure, incident reporting, whistleblower protections, and creation of CalCompute) shows industry-backed "safety" regulation can win support from frontier labs themselves — both Anthropic and OpenAI endorsed SB 53 after opposing his earlier, stricter SB 1047.
- Chan's platform frames AI policy explicitly through a labor lens: mandatory union/worker seats in automation decisions, pre-deployment government inspection/approval of AI systems rather than after-the-fact "kill switches," and skepticism that competitiveness-framed regulation (vs. China) ultimately serves workers rather than industry profit.
- Chan backs structurally aggressive federal proposals: Sanders' bill for a one-time 50% tax on frontier AI company stock to fund a public sovereign wealth fund (~$7T estimate) and public ownership, plus Jayapal's National AI Charter Act (monopoly prevention, labor protections, public fund from tax/penalties) — and wants the tax extended to any company using AI to displace workers, not just AI vendors.
- A real-world test case surfaced: after a Hugging Face hack, SB 53's mandatory safety-incident reporting requirement reportedly did not trigger — an early, concrete data point on whether such disclosure laws function as intended.
- Industry money's indirect influence on politics was illustrated by Anthropic's $40M donation to an "education"-only nonprofit PAC that in turn funneled $3.5M to a pro-Wiener PAC, raising questions about how earmarked restrictions on political spending hold up in practice.
- A candidate's campaign (Wiener's) built and deployed an AI chatbot impersonating an opponent that produced racist/sexist output before public backlash forced its removal — a concrete example of AI misuse within a political campaign context.
