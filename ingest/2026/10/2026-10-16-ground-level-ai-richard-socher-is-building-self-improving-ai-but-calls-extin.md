# Richard Socher is building self-improving AI—but calls extinction fears ‘ridiculous’

## Insights

- Richard Socher, cofounder of Recursive Superintelligence (with former Google/Meta/OpenAI researchers), is building AI designed to improve itself, aiming to accelerate scientific discovery rather than pursue AGI for its own sake.
- Socher rejects AI extinction risk outright, stating his probability estimate of AI-caused extinction is zero, and explicitly disagrees with Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio on this point.
- He characterizes much of the AI safety discourse as "fear marketing," arguing it distorts public perception relative to actual risks.
- His regulatory stance distinguishes between labs that can't control their own systems (who should slow down) and the broader field of legitimate scientific AI work (which shouldn't be held back by others' failures).
- He frames his new book, The Eureka Machine, around AI-driven scientific discovery, including ambitions around virtual cells and drug discovery, while acknowledging obstacles like data access, biology complexity, and human trials.
- On jobs, he argues that outcomes (not job preservation per se) should be the primary lens for evaluating AI's labor impact, per the episode's closing topic.

## Quote

> If you can't control AI from committing felonies, maybe that organization should slow down.
