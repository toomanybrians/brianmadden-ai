# Responsible AI governance: How AWS positions customers to align with ISO/IEC 42005:2025

## Insights

- AWS frames the new ISO/IEC 42005:2025 standard as guidance for conducting "AI system impact assessments" — documented risk reviews covering privacy, discrimination, and performance impacts that feed into organizational risk management decisions.
- The standard offers two integration paths: Annex D lets organizations with existing impact-assessment processes (legal, security, privacy, procurement) fold AI assessments in to avoid duplication, while Annex E provides a standalone template for organizations without that infrastructure.
- ISO/IEC 42005 defines a full assessment lifecycle (scoping, execution, analysis, ongoing monitoring) plus reassessment triggers tied to legal changes, system changes, or shifts in deployment context, and includes a lightweight "triage" step to decide if a full assessment is even needed.
- Assessment documentation is expected to cover system description, foreseeable misuse, training data/components, deployment environment, and affected individuals/communities — with explicit encouragement to consult diverse stakeholder groups.
- AWS positions ISO/IEC 42005 as directly supporting ISO/IEC 42001 (AI Management System) certification, and ties its own Well-Architected Responsible AI Lens to the standard's structure (benefits/harms identification, risk integration, lifecycle embedding).
- AWS cites existing ISO/IEC 42001 accredited certification for Bedrock, Q Business, Textract, and Transcribe as proof points, alongside references to a "missing middle" of AI facilitator roles (per an external "AI Labor Stack" framework) responsible for translating AI capability into practical deployment.

## Quote

> "AI facilitators" are often "the missing middle" in national AI strategies, "translat[ing] AI capability into practical deployment across firms, sectors, and public services."
