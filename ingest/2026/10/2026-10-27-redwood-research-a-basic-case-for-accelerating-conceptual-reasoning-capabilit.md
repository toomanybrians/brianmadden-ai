# A basic case for accelerating conceptual reasoning capabilities

## Insights

- Defines "conceptual reasoning" as tasks resembling philosophy: not verifiable by empirical/mathematical feedback, lacking established methodology, and progressed via argumentation rather than trained intuition or experimentation (e.g., ethics, decision theory, normative questions).
- Argues AI safety/alignment work depends heavily on conceptual reasoning, while AI R&D and takeover capability are much less conceptual (closer to verifiable, benefiting from RLVR) — meaning improvements in conceptual reasoning would differentially help safety work over generic capabilities or takeover risk.
- Claims current AI training pipelines (especially RLVR-heavy posttraining) poorly cultivate conceptual reasoning because such tasks resist verifiable reward signals, and that AI companies have weak commercial incentive to train for it — making it a neglected area relative to its importance.
- Reports empirical results: basic elicitation techniques (few-shot prompting) and small-scale targeted training moved models forward by roughly 3–12 months-equivalent on their internal conceptual-argumentation benchmark (LMCA), suggesting real tractability despite neglect.
- Frames stakes in terms of timing relative to "takeoff": how early AI gains strong conceptual reasoning (relative to scheming onset and automation of AI R&D) may determine whether humans can get enough quality-adjusted safety/philosophical work done in time, and could shape path-dependent societal views on ethics/decision theory.
- Notes a key risk/tension: better conceptual reasoning could also somewhat improve AI's capacity for scheming, evading control measures, or misaligned self-succession — though the authors argue this downside is likely smaller than the upside and can be managed by choosing which conceptual subdomains to accelerate (e.g., avoiding control red-teaming).

## Quote

> We expect improved conceptual reasoning to be differential, i.e., to help much more with safety work than with generic capabilities.
