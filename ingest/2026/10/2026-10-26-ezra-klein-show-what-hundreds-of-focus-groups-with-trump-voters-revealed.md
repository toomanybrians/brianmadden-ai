## Insights

- A political consultant (Sarah Longwell) reports that the data center/AI issue first surfaced organically in 2025 races (Sherrill, Spanberger) and has risen in salience through 2026, now central in Senate races in Michigan, Iowa, and Ohio.
- She says the issue "activates voters across multiple vectors": rising energy prices from data center power demand, data centers as a physical "blight" on communities, and fear that the same AI buildout is "setting up to take our jobs from us."
- Voters reportedly ask "Is this technology going to replace me?" as a now-common refrain across focus groups — described as a new, spreading anxiety rather than a fringe concern.
- The guest claims Trump's public messaging on data centers is purely promotional ("if you want to get rich... you're going to want a data center") with no real attempt to explain trade-offs or walk voters through the technology, which she frames as a missed communicative opportunity on both sides.
- She argues Democrats risk a similar failure if they simply oppose data centers reflexively rather than engaging substantively, and that voters are hungry for a politician who can credibly "walk them through" a complicated, fast-changing economic shift rather than offering certainty.
