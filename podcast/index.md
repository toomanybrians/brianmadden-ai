# Citrix AI Hotsheet

Brian's podcast, co-hosted with Dave Brear (account technology strategist, Citrix). Monthly, ~45 minutes per episode. The show is for IT leaders, architects, and practitioners responsible for bringing AI into real enterprise environments at scale.

Brian and Dave come at every topic from two angles: Brian focuses on where AI is heading and what it means for the future of enterprise work; Dave focuses on what actually works inside complex enterprise environments today.

The show covers what's actually changing for enterprise AI: agentic systems and computer use, identity and governance for non-human personas, the economics of AI at scale, and what it takes to bring AI into environments that can't be ripped out and replaced.

## Episodes

- **EP 1: AI agents, second brains, and the enterprise AI gap** (May 18, 2026) — `podcast/ep1.md`
  Brian's thesis that AI will enter the enterprise by using the same desktops and applications human workers already use, and Dave's introduction to context vaults (second brains) and why this practice is already happening quietly across knowledge workers.
- **EP 2: The Last Chapter of EUC** (June 13, 2026 — special solo edition) — `podcast/ep2.md`
  Brian shares his EUCTech keynote. Where the end-user computing industry is in 2026 (capabilities vs. diffusion), the invisible 80% of knowledge work, the updated seven-step roadmap for how AI enters work, an audit of every EUC primitive translated into its AI-era successor, and why this is the last chapter of book one — not the end of the story.
- **EP 3: Second brains hit the enterprise wall — and why AI automations won't save you** (June 19, 2026) — `podcast/ep3.md`
  Brian makes the case that AI automations and workflows are the wrong frame for knowledge workers — that's RPA thinking applied to the wrong problem. The real move is connecting the second brain into all enterprise apps and data sources via the same front door humans use. Dave explains the glass ceiling he hit on a personal system, what changed when he moved his second brain into a sanctioned Citrix VDI, and why the CIO message is: treat your data as a product the business can consume.
- **EP 4: OSWorld 2.0, AI Reconciliation Maps, & the Futurist's Playbook** (July 15, 2026) — `podcast/ep4.md`
  Four topics. OSWorld 2.0 raises the computer-use benchmark now that the original is saturated, and cost is a first-class metric alongside score. Dave introduces the reconciliation map — the new workflow stage he needs when a second brain pulls in contradictory enterprise data sources. Brian argues organizational AI maturity is a treehouse, not the ladder every consulting firm ships. And the futurist's playbook: don't predict one future, map many; then find what you can do today that pays off across all of them. The takeaway: open-weight Sonnet-class models will exist no matter what, so do the second-brain and knowledge-factory work now.
- **EP 5: AI Knowledge Factories — Company-Wide Second Brains** (September 1, 2026) — `podcast/ep5.md`
  Brian admits he was wrong: wiring his and Dave's personal second brains together through a shared GitHub folder was a disaster — second brains are too bespoke to merge, even between two AI nerds. What works instead is the knowledge factory: the second brain applied at company scale, with three tiers instead of two — raw inputs, a curated canonical layer, and outputs — where canon acts as a firewall so hallucination-prone slurry never touches output generation directly. Covers why underspecified questions (not conflicting data) cause AI to guess, two-hour sprints, provenance from output back to source, canon managed like source code on Citrix SecurSpaces behind a NetScaler AI Gateway, Google's Open Knowledge Format as external validation, and forward-deployed engineers vs. today's service-owner role. Plus a bonus: new details on OpenAI's agents reverse-engineering a cybersecurity test and going after the (non-existent) monitoring system to cover their tracks, including a mass agent die-off nobody can explain.
